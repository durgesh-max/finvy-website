/ Section 54F core rule

The exemption.

  • Available to individual + HUF only (not companies, firms, LLPs).
  • LTCG on sale of ANY long-term asset EXCEPT residential property.
  • Covers: shares + mutual funds + gold + unlisted shares + commercial property + land + specific non-residential assets.
  • Full exemption if net consideration (not just gain) invested in ONE residential property in India within:
  •   — 1 year back from sale OR
  •   — 2 years forward from sale (purchase) OR
  •   — 3 years forward (construction).
  • If part of net consideration invested: proportional exemption.

/ Section 54F specific conditions

The strict rules.

  • Taxpayer must NOT own more than ONE residential property on date of sale (other than the new property being purchased).
  • New residential property must NOT be sold within 3 years of purchase (else exemption clawed back).
  • Only ONE residential property allowed as reinvestment destination (not multiple).
  • Property must be in INDIA (not foreign).
  • Must be residential (not commercial / land).
  • If LTCG asset is shares: specific application of Section 112A rules (12.5% post Finance Act 2024).

/ Section 54 vs 54F

The difference.

  • Section 54: for LTCG on sale of RESIDENTIAL property reinvested in residential property.
  • Section 54F: for LTCG on sale of NON-residential asset (shares, MF, gold, commercial, land) reinvested in residential property.
  • Section 54: exempts the GAIN (if gain invested).
  • Section 54F: exempts the full LTCG if full NET CONSIDERATION invested (strict).

/ Common 54F mistakes

Where returns trip.

  • Net consideration vs gain confusion: 54F requires full net consideration invested; 54 requires gain invested. Different standards.
  • Multiple residential property on sale date: owning 2+ residential properties (other than reinvestment target) disqualifies 54F.
  • Investment in commercial / land: 54F requires RESIDENTIAL. Commercial reinvestment doesn't qualify.
  • Foreign residential property: 54F requires property in India.
  • Sale of new property within 3 years: clawback - prior-year exemption added back + tax at 54F recapture rate.
  • Multiple residential properties as reinvestment: only ONE allowed under 54F.
  • Timing - 2 vs 3 year window: purchase 2 years forward; construction 3 years forward. Must complete within window.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What's Section 54F exemption?
LTCG on sale of any long-term asset (NOT residential property) is EXEMPT if net consideration (not just gain) invested in ONE residential property in India within specified timelines. Powerful tax-saving provision for sale of shares / MF / gold / commercial property / land + reinvestment in residential property.
Can I claim Section 54F for sale of shares?
Yes - shares are 'long-term asset' (held > 12 months for listed; > 24 months for unlisted). LTCG on share sale invested in one residential property in India qualifies for 54F. Common tax-saving strategy for retail investors + ESOP exercise + startup secondary sales.
Can I invest in multiple residential properties under 54F?
No - 54F allows reinvestment in ONLY ONE residential property. Section 54 (for residential property sale) allowed TWO residential properties under specific conditions in some older interpretations - but even that has restrictions. 54F is strictly ONE residential property.
What if I sell the new residential property within 3 years?
Clawback: prior-year 54F exemption is added back to taxable income in the year of new property sale. Taxed as LTCG. Additional interest + penalty may apply. 3-year lock-in is strict.
Can NRI claim Section 54F?
Yes - NRI individual can claim Section 54F for LTCG on sale of non-residential Indian asset (shares, MF, gold, commercial property) reinvested in Indian residential property. Same conditions apply. Combined with LDC under Section 197 for TDS reduction.
