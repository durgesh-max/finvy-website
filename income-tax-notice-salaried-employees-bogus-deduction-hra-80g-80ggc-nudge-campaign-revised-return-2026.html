/ Salaried employees, including NRIs and returning residents · Deduction and exemption claims · Updated 2026-11-27

Income Tax Notice for Salaried Employees on Bogus Deductions (2026): HRA, 80C, 80G and 80GGC Claims, the CBDT NUDGE Campaign and How to Correct or Reply, fix the claim before it becomes a penalty.

Since December 2025 the Income Tax Department has been using data analytics to flag salaried taxpayers whose deductions look unsupported - especially donations under sections 80G and 80GGC and HRA exemptions - and has sent SMS, e-mail and portal messages asking them to review and correct their returns. A claim you can prove is safe; a claim you cannot support should be withdrawn through a revised or updated return before a notice turns into a demand with penalty. Ignoring the message can lead to scrutiny or reassessment.

  • Employers too: the CBDT said employees of multinationals, PSUs, government bodies and academic institutions were among those flagged, so claims passed through payroll are not immune.

/ How to respond

Correct, or defend with papers.

  • If the claim is genuine: keep it, collect the proof, and reply to any query or notice with the documents.
  • If it cannot be supported: withdraw it - a revised return for AY 2026-27 is free until 31 December 2026 (with a fee up to 31 March 2027); for earlier years an updated return (ITR-U) within 48 months, paying the additional tax.
  • If a notice has already come: reply within the time given; correcting voluntarily before an addition is made generally reduces exposure.
  • Do not argue a claim you cannot document: under-reporting attracts a penalty of 50% of the tax under section 270A, and misreporting such as fake receipts 200%.

/ For NRIs and returning residents

Common traps.

  • HRA and most deductions are available only under the old regime, and many NRIs have no Indian salary to claim HRA against.
  • NRIs cannot claim the section 87A rebate or some resident-only deductions; a refund claimed on that basis is likely to be questioned.
  • Returning residents should check that Form 130 from an Indian employer matches the return and the AIS.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Why are salaried employees getting income tax notices for deductions?
The department's data analytics has flagged returns where deductions look unsupported - especially donations under sections 80G and 80GGC and HRA exemptions - and since December 2025 it has sent SMS, e-mail and portal messages under a NUDGE campaign asking taxpayers to review and correct them, followed by queries or notices where needed.
What should I do if I claimed a deduction I cannot prove?
Withdraw it before the department makes an addition: file a revised return for AY 2026-27 (free until 31 December 2026, with a fee up to 31 March 2027) or, for earlier years, an updated return (ITR-U) within 48 months, paying the additional tax. Correcting voluntarily generally reduces penalty exposure.
Can I get a notice for HRA claimed on rent paid to my parents?
Rent paid to a parent can be valid if the rent is actually paid, there is an agreement and receipts, payments go through the bank, and the parent shows the rent as income in their return. Claims without real payment, or without the landlord's PAN where annual rent exceeds Rs 1 lakh, are the ones being questioned.
Why are donations under 80GGC being checked?
The CBDT has flagged claims for donations to political parties linked to cash-back arrangements. The deduction is available only for contributions to registered political parties made other than in cash, backed by a proper receipt.
What is the penalty for a bogus deduction claim?
If income is under-reported the penalty under section 270A is 50% of the tax on it; if it is misreported - for example through fake rent or donation receipts - the penalty is 200% of the tax, in addition to the tax and interest.
