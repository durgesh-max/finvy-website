/ Japanese companies and NRIs in Japan · DTAA · Updated 2026-11-24

India-Japan DTAA (2026): Tax Treaty Rates for Dividends, Interest, Royalties and Technical Fees, Article 12 and How to Claim, 10% on the main payment streams, if you can show treaty residence.

The India-Japan double tax avoidance agreement, signed in 1989 and amended by protocol, caps Indian tax on dividends, interest, royalties and fees for technical services paid to a Japanese resident at 10%, against India’s domestic withholding of around 20% plus surcharge and cess. Interest paid to the governments or central banks is exempt, and unlike the India-US treaty, Article 12 has no “make available” test, so most technical service fees fall within the 10% rate. The treaty is modified by the Multilateral Instrument, including the principal purpose test. To get the rate, the Japanese recipient gives the Indian payer a tax residency certificate, Form 41 (formerly Form 10F) and usually a PAN; otherwise TDS is deducted at the domestic rate.

/ Quick answers

What Japanese companies and NRIs ask.

  • What is the dividend rate? 10% of the gross dividend under Article 10, if the Japanese resident is the beneficial owner.
  • What does Article 12 cover? Royalties and fees for technical services, both capped at 10%; there is no make-available clause, so managerial, technical and consultancy fees are generally covered.
  • Is interest taxed? At up to 10% under Article 11, with exemptions for interest paid to the governments, central banks and certain government-backed lenders.
  • Are capital gains covered? Yes, under Article 13; whether India can tax a particular share sale depends on that article and the facts, so confirm before relying on an exemption.
  • Does the MLI apply? Yes. Both countries listed the treaty under the Multilateral Instrument, so the principal purpose test can deny benefits to arrangements made mainly to obtain them.

/ Claiming the rate

Documents and credit.

  • Tax residency certificate from the Japanese tax office for the relevant period; see tax residency certificates.
  • Form 41 (formerly Form 10F) with the details the TRC does not give, and a PAN, or the alternative documents allowed for non-residents without one.
  • Beneficial ownership and PPT: keep evidence that the Japanese entity has substance and a business reason, not only a treaty reason.
  • Credit in Japan: Indian tax withheld at the treaty rate is generally creditable against Japanese tax on the same income.
  • Indian residents with Japanese income claim foreign tax credit in India for Japanese tax, filing the foreign tax credit form before the return.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What are the India-Japan DTAA rates?
Dividends, interest, royalties and fees for technical services paid by an Indian payer to a Japanese resident are taxed in India at up to 10%, with interest to the governments and central banks exempt. Without the treaty, Indian withholding is usually 20% plus surcharge and cess.
What does Article 12 of the India-Japan DTAA cover?
Royalties and fees for technical services, both taxable in India at up to 10% of the gross amount. Unlike the India-US treaty, it has no make-available test, so most managerial, technical and consultancy fees are covered.
How does a Japanese company claim the India-Japan treaty rate?
Give the Indian payer a tax residency certificate from Japan, Form 41 (formerly Form 10F) and a PAN or the documents allowed instead, and keep evidence of beneficial ownership and substance, because the MLI principal purpose test applies to this treaty.
Does the MLI apply to the India-Japan DTAA?
Yes. Both countries listed the treaty under the Multilateral Instrument (Japan in 2018, India in 2019), so the principal purpose test can deny benefits to arrangements whose main purpose is to obtain them.
