Got an Income Tax Email About Foreign Assets (FAST-DS)? What It Means and What to Do, an advisory, not a notice, but do not ignore it.

The Income Tax Department is emailing taxpayers whose AIS shows foreign bank accounts, shares, custodial accounts or property, based on data received from other countries under CRS and FATCA. The email says the Department's records indicate you may have overseas financial interests and points you to FAST-DS 2026, the one-time disclosure scheme that closes on 31 December 2026. It is an advisory, not a legal notice, but the Department now has a record that you were told.

/ First, check the facts

Before you do anything else.

  1. Log in to the e-filing portal yourself (type the address; do not use links in the email).
  2. Open your AIS and the Foreign Assets Information section.
  3. For each year, compare the entries with Schedule FA (foreign assets) and Schedule FSI (foreign income) in the returns you filed.
  4. Check your residential status for each year: Schedule FA is required only in years you were resident and ordinarily resident.

/ Then pick the right route

Depending on what you find.

What you findWhat to do
Everything was reported correctlyNo action; keep your working papers
You hold nothing abroad, or the AIS entry is wrongGive feedback on the AIS entry; do not file anything just to be safe
Asset bought while non-resident or from taxed income, but missing from Schedule FAFAST-DS Category 2: flat INR 1 lakh fee (assets up to INR 5 crore)
Foreign income or assets never taxed in IndiaFAST-DS Category 1: 60% of the value (up to INR 1 crore in aggregate)
Error only in the AY 2026-27 returnA revised return before 31 December 2026 may be simpler

Foreign assets (other than immovable property) with an aggregate value up to INR 20 lakh fall below the Black Money Act penalty threshold for non-reporting; check your exposure before paying anything.

/ How FAST-DS filing works

Four forms.

  • Form 1: your declaration, filed on the e-filing portal by 31 December 2026. One form can cover several assets.
  • Form 2: the Department's order stating the amount payable.
  • Form 3: your payment details.
  • Form 4: the certificate confirming the declaration.
  • Payment is due within two months from the end of the month in which you receive Form 2; a further two months is allowed with 1% simple interest a month. After that, the declaration is void.
  • The 31 December date is for declaring, not paying. Assets are valued as on 31 March 2026.

/ Watch for phishing

Stay safe.

  • The genuine email's links pass through an email-tracking domain before reaching the Income Tax site, so you cannot judge it by the link alone.
  • Never upload documents or enter your password through an emailed link; go to the portal directly.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Is the FAST-DS email from the Income Tax Department a notice?
No. It is an advisory based on foreign-account data in your AIS. But the Department has a record that you were informed, so check your returns against the data.
I received the email but reported everything. Do I need to do anything?
No. If your Schedule FA and FSI already show the assets and income, keep your working papers and take no further action.
What if the AIS entry is wrong?
Submit feedback on the AIS entry on the portal. Do not file a FAST-DS declaration just to be safe.
When do I have to pay under FAST-DS?
After you receive the Form 2 order: within two months from the end of that month, or two more months with 1% interest a month. The 31 December 2026 deadline is for filing the declaration.
