/ Private limited companies, OPCs, LLPs and foreign subsidiaries · Companies Act 2013 · Updated 2026-11-26

ROC compliance and late filing fees (2026-27), what to file with the Registrar, and when.

ROC compliance means the filings a company or LLP must make with the Registrar of Companies on the MCA portal, plus the meetings and records the law requires. A private limited company files AOC-4 within 30 days and MGT-7 / MGT-7A within 60 days of its AGM, DPT-3 by 30 June, MSME-1 half-yearly and event-based forms such as DIR-12 and PAS-3. An LLP files Form 11 by 30 May and Form 8 by 30 October. Late company forms cost an additional fee of ₹100 a day with no cap; late LLP forms cost a multiple of the normal fee that rises with the delay.

/ What ROC compliance means

Three kinds of obligation.

  • Annual forms: financial statements, annual return and other yearly returns filed with the Registrar of Companies (ROC) through the MCA V3 portal.
  • Event-based forms: filed when something changes, such as a new director, a share allotment, a special resolution or a change of auditor.
  • Meetings and records: board meetings, the annual general meeting (AGM), statutory registers and minutes that must be kept even though they are not filed.
  • Who it applies to: every company registered in India, including foreign-owned subsidiaries, and every LLP, whether or not it has done any business in the year.

/ Late fees, penalties and fees

What missing a date costs.

  • Additional fee: ₹100 a day of delay on top of the normal filing fee for company forms, with no cap, unless a waiver applies. For LLP Form 11 and Form 8 the additional fee is a multiple of the normal fee that rises with the delay, up to 15 times (small LLPs) or 30 times (other LLPs) plus ₹10 / ₹20 a day beyond 360 days.
  • Penalties: separate penalties can apply to the company and its officers for failing to hold meetings or file returns.
  • Director disqualification: directors of a company that has not filed financial statements or annual returns for three continuous financial years can be disqualified under section 164(2).
  • Strike-off: the Registrar can strike off companies that stop filing; a company with no business can instead apply to become dormant or close through STK-2.
  • Government filing fees: the normal MCA fee for most forms depends on the company's authorised share capital (or the LLP's contribution), so it differs from company to company.

/ Foreign-owned subsidiaries

Extra reporting beyond the ROC.

  • RBI reporting: FC-GPR within 30 days of allotting shares to a foreign investor, and the annual FLA return.
  • Resident director: at least one director must stay in India for 182 days or more in the financial year.
  • Calendar: see the annual compliance calendar for foreign-owned subsidiaries.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is ROC compliance?
ROC compliance is the set of filings a company or LLP makes with the Registrar of Companies on the MCA portal, such as the annual financial statements and annual return, plus event-based forms and the meetings and registers the law requires.
What are the ROC compliances for a private limited company?
At least four board meetings, a statutory audit, an AGM by 30 September, AOC-4 within 30 days and MGT-7 / MGT-7A within 60 days of the AGM, ADT-1 within 15 days of appointing the auditor, DPT-3 by 30 June, MSME-1 half-yearly if dues to micro and small suppliers are outstanding, DIR-3 KYC every three years, and event-based forms such as DIR-12 and PAS-3.
What is the ROC compliance calendar for 2026-27?
DIR-3 KYC and DPT-3 by 30 June, AGM by 30 September, AOC-4 within 30 days and MGT-7 / MGT-7A within 60 days of the AGM, MSME-1 by 31 October and 30 April, and for LLPs Form 11 by 30 May and Form 8 by 30 October.
What are the ROC compliances for an LLP?
Form 11 (annual return) by 30 May, Form 8 (statement of account and solvency) by 30 October, an audit if turnover exceeds Rs 40 lakh or contribution exceeds Rs 25 lakh, DIR-3 KYC of designated partners every three years, and Form 3 / Form 4 within 30 days of changes.
How many board meetings must a private limited company hold?
At least four a year with no more than 120 days between two meetings. A small company or OPC needs at least one meeting in each half of the calendar year, at least 90 days apart.
Is ROC compliance needed if the company has no business?
Yes. Annual filings are due even with no transactions. A company with no business can apply for dormant status or close through strike-off instead of filing indefinitely.
What is the penalty for late ROC filing?
For company forms, an additional fee of Rs 100 a day of delay with no cap (for LLP forms, a multiple of the normal fee), plus possible penalties on the company and officers; directors can be disqualified if financial statements or annual returns are not filed for three continuous years.
What is the ROC late filing fee for AOC-4 and MGT-7, and is there a waiver in 2026?
The additional fee is Rs 100 for every day of delay for each form, on top of the normal filing fee, with no upper cap. LLP Form 8 and Form 11 work differently: their additional fee is a multiple of the normal fee that rises with the delay, up to 15 times (small LLPs) or 30 times (other LLPs) plus Rs 10 / Rs 20 a day beyond 360 days. Under MCA General Circular 05/2026, the additional fee for AOC-4 is waived for filings made by 31 December 2026 and for MGT-7 / MGT-7A by 31 January 2027, so companies with overdue FY 2025-26 accounts or annual returns should file inside those windows. LLP Form 8 is not covered. Separately, the Registrar can levy penalties on the company and its officers for continuing default.
