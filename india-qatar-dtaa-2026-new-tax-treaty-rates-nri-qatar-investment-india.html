New India-Qatar Tax Treaty from 1 April 2026: Rates, Capital Gains, PE and Anti-Abuse Rules for Qatar-Based NRIs and Qatari Investors, what changed from the 1999 treaty and who it affects.

India and Qatar signed a new tax treaty on 18 February 2025 to replace the 1999 treaty. It entered into force on 10 September 2025 and was notified by CBDT Notification No. 154/2025. It applies in India to income arising on or after 1 April 2026, and in Qatar from 1 January 2026. The key changes: the 5% dividend rate now needs a 25% shareholding (it was 10%), with 10% in other cases. Interest now expressly covers Islamic finance, and interest to the Qatar Investment Authority and Qatar Holding is exempt in India. India can tax gains on shares of property-rich companies (more than 50% of value from Indian immovable property). A service PE arises after 90 days of services in 12 months. A principal purpose test denies benefits to arrangements set up mainly for treaty access. Qatar-based NRIs claiming treaty rates on Indian income need a Qatari tax residency certificate and Form 10F (Form 41 under the new rules).

/ For Qatar-based NRIs

Practical points.

  • NRO interest and dividends: give your Indian bank or company a Qatari tax residency certificate and Form 10F to get treaty rates instead of 30% or 20% plus surcharge and cess; claim refunds of excess TDS in your Indian return; see which ITR for NRIs.
  • NRE and FCNR interest: exempt in India while you are non-resident; Qatar has no personal income tax on it.
  • Deemed residence: an Indian citizen with Indian income above INR 15 lakh who is not liable to tax in any country can be treated as resident in India; see deemed residence for Gulf NRIs.
  • Property and shares in India: capital gains on Indian property and most Indian shares remain taxable in India; see selling property from abroad.

/ For Qatari companies and investors

Before investing or contracting.

  • Investing in India: FDI route and sector caps apply as for any foreign investor; see working with an Indian CA as a foreign company and acquiring an Indian company.
  • Holding structure: to get the 5% dividend rate, the Qatari company must beneficially own at least 25% and pass the principal purpose test.
  • Projects in India: track staff days; more than 90 days of services in 12 months can create a PE and Indian tax on profits.
  • Financing: Islamic finance structures now fit the interest article; check withholding on each payment.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

When does the new India-Qatar tax treaty apply?
It entered into force on 10 September 2025 and applies in India to income arising on or after 1 April 2026 (in Qatar from 1 January 2026), replacing the 1999 treaty.
What is the dividend tax rate under the new India-Qatar treaty?
5% where the beneficial owner holds at least 25% of the paying company (previously 10%), and 10% in other cases; dividends paid to the other state itself are exempt in the source state.
Does the new treaty change tax on Indian shares sold by Qatar residents?
India can tax gains on shares that derive more than 50% of their value from Indian immovable property, and most other Indian share gains remain taxable in India under domestic law.
What documents does a Qatar-based NRI need for treaty rates?
A Qatari tax residency certificate and Form 10F (Form 41 under the new rules), given to the Indian payer before payment; excess tax can be claimed back in the Indian return.
Is interest paid to Qatar Investment Authority taxed in India?
No. The protocol lists Qatar Investment Authority and Qatar Holding LLC among the bodies whose interest is exempt in the source state.
