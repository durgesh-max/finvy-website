Rule 96(10) Omitted Retrospectively: Exporters Can Fight Old IGST Refund Demands (Circular 264/10/2026), relief back to October 2017.

CBIC Circular 264/10/2026, dated 26 October 2026, accepts the Supreme Court's 6 August 2026 judgment in Goodluck India Limited and treats the omission of Rule 96(10) of the CGST Rules as effective from 23 October 2017. Show cause notices and demands that relied on Rule 96(10) cannot be sustained, and the same treatment extends to Rules 89(4A) and 89(4B).

/ What Rule 96(10) did

Background.

  • Rule 96(10), inserted from 23 October 2017, stopped exporters from claiming a refund of IGST paid on exports if they had bought inputs under certain concessional schemes, such as advance authorisation or EOU benefits.
  • Thousands of exporters received demands to repay IGST refunds, often with interest and penalty.
  • The rule was omitted from 8 October 2024 without a saving clause, and the dispute was whether pending cases survived.

/ What changed

Judgment and circular.

  • The Supreme Court held that, without a saving clause, the omission applies to all proceedings pending on the date of omission.
  • CBIC accepted the judgment without reservation and directed field officers to apply it: notices and demands founded on Rule 96(10) cannot be sustained at notice, adjudication or appeal stage.
  • The circular applies the same treatment to Rules 89(4A) and 89(4B), which reduced refund amounts in similar situations.

/ Who benefits and how

Order passed, appeal pendingFile an additional submission with the circular; the demand should be set aside.
Paid under protest during pending proceedingsClaim a refund of the amount paid.
Refunds reduced under Rule 89(4A)/(4B), still in disputeSeek recomputation.
Matter closed without contest, or finalNot automatically reopened; whether relief is available depends on whether proceedings were pending on 8 October 2024. Needs case review.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What does Circular 264/10/2026 say?
That the omission of Rule 96(10) applies retrospectively from 23 October 2017, following the Supreme Court's Goodluck India judgment of 6 August 2026, and that demands based on the rule cannot be sustained. The same applies to Rules 89(4A) and 89(4B).
Can exporters get back IGST they repaid under Rule 96(10)?
Amounts paid under protest or recovered while proceedings were pending can be claimed as refunds. Cases that were closed or became final are not automatically reopened.
Does it apply to cases in appeal?
Yes. Appellate authorities should set aside demands based on Rule 96(10); a short additional submission with the circular and judgment is usually enough.
Is there a deadline to claim?
The limitation period for refund claims arising from the circular is not settled, so claims should be filed promptly.
