/ NRIs and OCI cardholders taking up work in India · Indian or foreign employer · Updated 2026-11-28

Can an NRI Work in India? (2026) FEMA Residence on Arrival for a Job, NRE / NRO Re-Designation, Tax Day Count, Remote Work and OCI Cardholders, yes - but your status changes.

An Indian citizen living abroad needs no permit to work in India, and OCI cardholders can also take up employment. The catch is status: under FEMA you become resident from the day you arrive to take up a job, and for income tax the day count decides - while salary for work done in India is taxed here whatever your status. Plan the bank accounts, investments and the year of the move before the first payslip.

/ Two laws, two tests

FEMA and income tax work differently.

When do you become resident?From arrival, if you come to India to take up employmentBy day count for the financial year (182 days, or 60 days plus 365 days in the previous four years)
What changes?NRE / NRO accounts re-designated; resident rules for new investmentsIndian salary always taxable; foreign income taxable once resident and ordinarily resident
TransitionRFC account can hold foreign currency brought backRNOR years can keep foreign income outside Indian tax
  • OCI cardholders: can work in India; special permission is needed only for specified activities such as research, journalism, missionary work and mountaineering, and for protected areas.
  • Salary for work in India: taxable in India from day one, even while you are still non-resident for the year.
  • Both countries taxing: the tax treaty's tie-breaker decides residence, and foreign tax credit is claimed with Form 67.

/ Remote work and the move

Common situations.

  • Working remotely from India for a foreign employer: a long stay can make you resident for tax, and the salary for days worked in India is taxable here - the employer may also face payroll and permanent establishment questions.
  • Joining an Indian employer: the employer deducts TDS from salary; tell your banks so accounts are re-designated.
  • Investments made as an NRI: NRE deposits can run to maturity or move to RFC; shares and funds bought as an NRI need the broker and depository records updated.
  • Foreign pensions and accounts: report them in Schedule FA once you are resident and ordinarily resident.
  • Timing: the date of arrival and the days in India in the year of the move decide whether that year counts as resident.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Can an NRI work in India?
Yes. An Indian citizen living abroad needs no permit to work in India. Under FEMA, coming to India to take up employment makes you resident from arrival, so NRE and NRO accounts must be re-designated, and for income tax the day count decides your status for the year.
Can an OCI cardholder work in India?
Yes. OCI cardholders can take up employment in India; special permission is needed only for specified activities such as research, journalism, missionary work and mountaineering, and for visits to protected or restricted areas.
Is my salary taxable in India if I am still an NRI?
Salary for work done in India is income earned in India and taxable here whatever your residential status. Your status decides whether your foreign income is also taxed in India.
Can I work remotely from India for a foreign company?
You can, but a long stay can make you resident for tax, and the salary for the days you work in India is taxable here. The foreign employer may also need to look at Indian payroll and permanent establishment risk. Plan the stay and the contract first.
What happens to my NRE account when I start working in India?
It must be re-designated as a resident account once you become resident under FEMA. Foreign-currency money can move to a Resident Foreign Currency (RFC) account, and FCNR deposits can run to maturity.
