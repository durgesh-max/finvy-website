US green card + Indian property, the dual-residence puzzle.

US green card holder (lawful permanent resident / LPR) is a US tax person on worldwide income. If also physically present in India > 182 days or qualifying under Section 6 Indian tests, becomes dual tax resident. Form 1040 US + ITR India both. India-US DTAA + Foreign Tax Credit (FTC) prevent double tax but require careful execution. Plus FBAR, Form 8938, Section 877A exit tax. Here's the complete 2026 framework.

/ The dual-residence problem

US + India both.

US tax residence for green card holder:

  • LPR is 'US person' per IRC Section 7701(b) — taxed on worldwide income regardless of physical location.
  • Must file Form 1040 reporting global income.
  • Must file FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) if aggregate foreign financial accounts > USD 10,000 at any time in year.
  • Must file Form 8938 (FATCA) if foreign financial assets > USD 50,000 (single) / 100,000 (joint) at year-end OR 75K / 150K any time.
  • State tax: if domiciled in a state (CA, NY, NJ), state income tax on worldwide income unless state residency broken.

Indian tax residence for green card holder:

  • Section 6: physically in India 182+ days in FY, OR 60+ days plus 365+ days in preceding 4 FYs.
  • If resident: global income taxable in India (subject to FTC and DTAA).
  • If RNOR: India-source + foreign business controlled from India + professional set up in India.
  • If NRI: India-source only.

Dual resident: possible to be US tax person (LPR) AND Indian tax resident (182+ days India) in same year. India-US DTAA Article 4 tie-breaker applies.

/ India-US DTAA tie-breaker + relief

The treaty framework.

Article 4 India-US DTAA tie-breaker rules (for individuals resident of both):

  1. Permanent home: where available — that country is residence.
  2. Centre of vital interests: closer personal / economic relations — that country is residence.
  3. Habitual abode: country where person habitually resides — that country is residence.
  4. National: country of citizenship.
  5. Mutual agreement: competent authorities of two countries decide.

Green card holder physically settled in India after moving back: likely Indian treaty residence under centre of vital interests + habitual abode.

Foreign Tax Credit (FTC):

  • Form 1116 US: credit for Indian income tax paid on same income.
  • Section 90 India: credit for US tax paid on US-source income (for Indian ITR).
  • Example: Green card holder in India sells US stock. US capital gain tax at long-term rate (0/15/20%). India also taxes under Section 48 (post-2024 LTCG 12.5% + reinvestment exemptions). FTC in each ITR for other country's tax.

/ Common cross-tax scenarios

What to plan.

  • Green card holder returning to live in India: becomes Indian tax resident. Form 1040 + Indian ITR both required. Prior-year RNOR relief possible for 2-3 years. See RNOR returning guide.
  • Green card holder with Indian rental income: taxable both countries. Indian ITR with Section 24 standard deduction (30%) + interest on home loan (full deduction if let-out). US Schedule E with GAAP depreciation. FTC claim.
  • Green card holder with India mutual fund: Indian LTCG 12.5% post Finance Act 2024. US treats as PFIC (Passive Foreign Investment Company) — complex taxation (QEF election or Mark-to-market). FTC available but PFIC rules may erode benefit.
  • Indian employer salary + US green card: Indian employer withholds Section 192 TDS. US treats as US-source foreign wages (requires separate reporting). FTC relief under DTAA.
  • Indian dividend to green card holder: Section 195 TDS at 20% (10-15% under DTAA with TRC + Form 10F). US taxes as ordinary dividend (unless qualifying for lower rate). FTC relief.

/ Social Security - no totalization

The gap.

  • India and US have NOT signed a Social Security Totalization Agreement (both have pending negotiations — no agreement as of 2026-10-15).
  • Green card holder working for Indian employer in India: contributes to Indian PF/EPF. No US Social Security contribution. Years do NOT count toward US SS eligibility.
  • Green card holder working for US employer in India (secondment): US SS withheld (W-2) + Indian PF obligation on Indian company. Double contribution.
  • Planning: for dual residents / returning green card holders, structure employment to avoid double social security where possible.

/ Section 877A exit tax

If giving up green card.

  • Section 877A: expatriation tax applies if LPR "covered expatriate" — defined as LPR who:
  •   — Average net income tax for 5 years > USD 206,000 (2026 threshold), OR
  •   — Net worth > USD 2 million, OR
  •   — Fails to certify 5-year tax compliance.
  • Mark-to-market tax: all worldwide assets treated as sold at fair value on day before expatriation. Deemed gain / loss subject to tax (with USD 866,000 exclusion in 2026).
  • Specific IRA / 401k / deferred compensation: special rules.
  • Indian property + Indian company shares: deemed sold — US capital gains tax on built-in gain.
  • Planning: pre-expatriation review of worldwide assets, Section 1014 step-up planning, Indian legal / tax structuring.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Does a US green card holder need to file Indian ITR?
Only if (a) physically Indian tax resident (182+ days in FY, or 60+/365+ test), OR (b) India-source income exceeds basic exemption limit (currently INR 3 lakh for NRIs). Most green card holders residing in US are Indian NRIs - file Indian ITR only if India rental / Indian capital gains / Indian bank interest triggers filing threshold. Green card holders physically in India for extended periods become Indian residents - full worldwide income disclosure in Indian ITR.
How does the India-US DTAA help a green card holder?
Prevents double taxation via Foreign Tax Credit (FTC) + reduced treaty rates on specific income types. Article 4 tie-breaker determines treaty residence if dual resident. Treaty rates: dividend 15% (25% ownership) / 25% (otherwise); royalty 10-15%; FTS 10-15%. Both India and US must honour treaty rates per their domestic law. TRC + Form 10F required for Indian treaty claim.
Is Indian PF (Provident Fund) taxable to a US green card holder?
Complex. US generally treats Indian PF as foreign pension - taxable in US on contributions + growth + distributions unless specific DTAA treatment applies. India-US DTAA has no specific PF article - US tax applies. Needs year-by-year reporting (Form 8938, FBAR). Specialised cross-border tax advice essential; many green card holders unknowingly misreport Indian PF.
Can I keep US green card while living in India long-term?
Technically yes - LPR status maintained if you don't abandon residence. But: (a) physical absence from US > 1 year without re-entry permit creates presumption of abandonment; (b) USCIS may question continued LPR status at port of entry if India stay prolonged; (c) US tax obligations continue regardless of physical location. Many long-term India residents either surrender green card (triggering Section 877A exit tax if covered expatriate) or obtain re-entry permit periodically.
What is Section 877A exit tax for giving up US green card?
If covered expatriate (net worth > USD 2M, or 5-year avg tax > USD 206K in 2026, or fails 5-year certification), worldwide assets deemed sold at fair value on day before expatriation. Capital gains tax applies on built-in gain (above USD 866K exclusion in 2026). Specific rules for IRA / 401k / deferred compensation. Pre-planning essential: step-up strategies, gifting out assets before trigger date, Indian legal / tax integration.
