/ Section 149(3) requirement

The statutory rule.

  • Every Indian company (Pvt Ltd + Public Ltd) must have minimum directors (2 for Pvt Ltd; 3 for Public Ltd).
  • At least ONE director must be resident of India - stayed in India 182+ days in preceding FY.
  • Non-resident Indians (NRIs) with Indian passport but non-resident status: do NOT qualify.
  • Foreign citizens who are tax-residents of India (182+ days physical): qualify.
  • Non-compliance: Section 164 director disqualification risk + ROC filing blockage + Section 450 default penalty.

/ When nominee director needed

Common scenarios.

  • Foreign parent incorporating Indian WOS: 2 foreign directors appointed; need 1 India-resident nominee.
  • India-resident director resigns: company left without resident director; 30 days to appoint replacement.
  • Expat executive relocates out of India: director who was India-resident loses that status after 180+ days abroad.
  • Dormant Indian subsidiary: not actively operated but still needs 1 resident director for compliance.

/ BQP nominee director service workflow

Step-by-step.

  1. Nominee Director Agreement: signed between foreign parent + BQP nominee partner. Defines scope + fee + indemnity.
  2. Pre-signed resignation: nominee signs undated DIR-11 resignation form; held in escrow (can be used immediately if nominee needs to be replaced).
  3. DIN application: nominee's Director Identification Number if not already (BQP partners have DIN).
  4. DSC: Digital Signature Certificate for ROC filings.
  5. Board resolution: company's board appoints nominee as director + specific disclosure of nominee relationship.
  6. Form DIR-12: filed with ROC within 30 days of appointment.
  7. Indemnity: foreign parent indemnifies nominee against claims arising from acts of other directors / company's operations. Standard legal protection.
  8. D&O insurance (optional): foreign parent may purchase D&O insurance covering Indian subsidiary directors.

/ Nominee director's scope + what they do NOT do

Clear boundaries.

Nominee DOES:

  • Named as director in Indian company's records (MCA / ROC filings).
  • Holds valid DIN + DSC.
  • Signs required ROC filings: AOC-4, MGT-7, DIR-12, PAS-3, MGT-14 as applicable.
  • Attends board meetings (video / telephonic) when quorum needs resident director.
  • Signs resolutions requiring resident director signature.
  • Files DIR-3 KYC (every three years).
  • Signs required bank KYC / banking documents.

Nominee does NOT:

  • Participate in business operations / strategy / commercial decisions.
  • Sign commercial contracts / customer agreements / vendor contracts.
  • Represent the company publicly.
  • Access operational funds / treasury / payroll.
  • Make day-to-day business judgments.

Role is purely statutory governance - satisfying Section 149(3), not running the business.

/ Replacement + transition workflow

If nominee changes.

  1. Reasons for replacement: nominee's own change of circumstances; foreign parent prefers different nominee; appointment of new India-resident director from parent group.
  2. Pre-signed resignation (DIR-11) activated - specific date entered.
  3. Successor nominee's DIR-12 (within 30 days of appointment).
  4. Section 164 disqualification check: successor must not be disqualified.
  5. Bank KYC refresh: new signatory authorisation.
  6. MCA records updated: company's director list updated.
  7. Updated Nominee Director Agreement with new nominee.
  8. Standard replacement timeline: 30-45 days end-to-end.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Why do foreign-owned Indian subsidiaries need nominee director?
Section 149(3) Companies Act 2013: every Indian company must have at least one director resident in India (stayed 182+ days in preceding FY). If foreign parent has no India-resident person on leadership team: nominee resident director required. Standard arrangement for 90%+ of foreign WOS incorporations.
What's the legal risk of being a nominee director?
Directors owe fiduciary duties (Section 166) + liable for non-compliance by company (Section 164 disqualification, Section 450 default penalties). Mitigated by: scope restriction (nominee doesn't participate in operational decisions), foreign parent's indemnity, regular DIR-3 KYC + DIR-8 + MBP-1 compliance, D&O insurance (optional). BQP structuring includes standard protective framework.
Can my Indian employee be the resident director instead?
Yes if they are tax-resident in India + willing. However: directors have personal liability under Companies Act; many employees refuse. Also creates employer-director conflict in HR matters. External CA-qualified nominee (like BQP partners) is cleaner structurally. Many foreign parents choose BQP nominee specifically for professional separation.
