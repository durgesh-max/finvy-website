/ Reassessment Season Alert · Updated 2026-10-22

Section 148 reassessment season, Nov-Dec 2026 wave is here.

November-December is India's traditional reassessment-notice season. AOs rushing to issue Section 148A(b) show-cause notices for AY 2019-20 (standard 4-year window closing March 2027) and AY 2015-16 / 2016-17 (10-year window for asset-form escaped income above INR 50 lakh). If you've received a Section 148A(b) notice, the response window is 7-30 days. Here's exactly what to do.

/ The current season context

Why November-December.

  • AY 2019-20 standard 4-year limit: closing on 31 March 2027. AOs issuing 148A(b) before year-end to preserve option to proceed to Section 148 before limitation.
  • AY 2015-16 / 2016-17 10-year extended limit: applicable where escaped income is INR 50 lakh+ in asset form. AOs reviewing CBDT risk-based selection + FTA (Foreign Tax Authority) exchange data + search cases.
  • Data sources driving notices: SFT (Statement of Financial Transactions), AIS / TIS mismatches, international tax exchange, specific industry audits, informant reports.
  • Volume: significant volume of 148A(b) notices issued each November-December season. Many are mechanical and quashable.

/ Section 148A procedure recap

Finance Act 2021 framework.

  1. AO has information suggesting income escaping assessment (SFT, AIS mismatch, international exchange, search output).
  2. Section 148A(a) enquiry: optional - AO may make preliminary enquiry.
  3. Section 148A(b) show-cause notice: AO issues formal show-cause with the 'information' it relies on + proposed grounds. Response window 7-30 days.
  4. Taxpayer's response: can refute information, present mitigating facts, argue jurisdictional defects.
  5. Section 148A(d) order: AO decides whether to proceed. Must consider taxpayer's response.
  6. Section 148 formal notice: if AO proceeds, formal reassessment notice issued. Taxpayer must file return.
  7. Section 147 reassessment: regular assessment follows.
  8. Section 147 order appealable to CIT(A) within 30 days.

/ Response strategy

Step-by-step playbook.

  1. Immediate action (Day 1): acknowledge notice; note exact response deadline; preserve all records relevant to the year in question.
  2. Information review (Day 1-3): analyse exactly what information AO relies on. RTI request for complete file if needed.
  3. Classification review (Day 3-5): is this a mechanical notice (easy to quash) or substantive issue (need defence)?
  4. Legal grounds assessment:
  5.   — Jurisdictional: 4-year vs 10-year limit, Section 148A procedure defects.
  6.   — Substantive: income was already disclosed, SFT misreporting, duplicate counting, non-taxable receipt (gift, inheritance, exempt).
  7.   — Treaty: DTAA relief available, foreign tax credit, treaty-residence tie-breaker.
  8. Documentary refutation: ITR extract showing disclosed income, bank statements, agreements, Section 50C compliance docs, Section 56(2)(x) relative gift docs.
  9. Section 148A(b) response drafting: comprehensive written response with annexures.
  10. Submission on income tax portal: electronic filing before deadline.
  11. Follow-up: await Section 148A(d) order. If favourable: closure. If adverse: Section 148 notice follows.
  12. Writ in High Court: if Section 148A procedure fundamentally defective, consider writ petition.

/ Specific issues this season

What AOs are focused on.

  • Foreign asset disclosure gaps: FTA exchange data + Schedule FA mismatches. US + UK + Singapore + UAE financial institutions sharing with Indian tax authority under CRS / FATCA.
  • Real estate transactions: SFT reports of large property purchases / sales (Form 61A) not reflected in ITR capital gain.
  • Share transactions: Form 61A reports of large securities transactions not reflected in capital gain disclosure.
  • Cash deposits: large cash deposits reported via SFT.
  • Business receipt gaps: TDS / TCS discrepancy with ITR revenue.
  • Trust / family structures: foreign trust distributions, private trust income allocation.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Why is November-December reassessment-notice season in India?
AO's 4-year standard reassessment limitation closes on 31 March for the AY that becomes 4 years old. For AY 2019-20, limitation closes 31 March 2027. AOs issue Section 148A(b) show-cause notices in late Q4 2026 and Q1 2027 to preserve the option to proceed to Section 148 within the window. Same pattern applies to 10-year extended limit for high-value asset-form escaped income cases.
What's the response window for Section 148A(b)?
Typically 7-30 days from receipt of show-cause notice. Specific days specified in notice. Extensions sometimes granted on reasoned request. Critical to respond within window - no response = AO proceeds to Section 148A(d) order with only AO's version considered.
Can Section 148A(b) notice be quashed?
Yes - via writ in High Court if procedure fundamentally defective (no proper 'information', mechanical satisfaction, pre-decided outcome, no genuine opportunity to respond). Several High Court rulings have struck down defective 148A proceedings. For substantive but weak notices, Section 148A(b) response itself can achieve dismissal at 148A(d) stage without High Court involvement.
What if the 'escaped income' is actually already disclosed?
This is one of the most common defences. Produce ITR Schedule showing the income reported. Cross-reference with Form 26AS (Form 168 from tax year 2026-27) / AIS / TIS. Documentary proof of disclosure in prior year ITR. If AO's SFT data duplicates already-disclosed income, response conclusively establishes no escape.
What's the penalty if Section 148 reassessment confirms escaped income?
Section 270A: 50% penalty for under-reporting (good faith) OR 200% penalty for misreporting (fraud / wilful concealment). Classification is a key battle in reassessment. Section 270AA voluntary disclosure provision can avoid 200% rate. CIT(A) + ITAT appeal paths available.
