/ Criterion 1: ICAI-qualified CA partner

Non-negotiable.

Indian audit reports + tax audit + Form 15CB + Form 3CEB can only be signed by Chartered Accountant (ICAI member). Verify:

  • Partner / principal is ICAI member (member ID verifiable at icai.org).
  • Firm is ICAI-registered (practice registration number verifiable).
  • Statutory audit sign-off goes to CA (not just operations managed by non-CAs).
  • UDIN (Unique Document Identification Number) generated for every audit report / certificate - ICAI verifiable.

Many 'CA firms' are actually accounting / book-keeping firms without CA at helm. Avoid for anything requiring CA signature.

/ Criterion 2: Remote delivery capability

For foreign clients.

Foreign client should not need to visit India. Verify firm offers:

  • Zoom / Google Meet calls at client-friendly time zones.
  • Cloud workspace (Google Drive / Dropbox / Microsoft 365 / Box) for document exchange.
  • Electronic signing (DocuSign / HelloSign / Adobe Sign) for engagement letters + deliverables.
  • Deliverables in client's preferred format (PDF / Word / Excel / Google Docs).
  • No office visit required at any engagement stage.

/ Criterion 3: Multi-standard competence

Beyond Ind AS alone.

Foreign-parent Indian subsidiary audit needs:

  • Ind AS (Indian) statutory audit.
  • IFRS reconciliation for foreign parent's consolidation.
  • US GAAP reconciliation if parent is US-based (ASC 606 Revenue + ASC 842 Leases + ASC 718 SBC + ASC 326 CECL + ASC 740 Taxes).
  • UK FRS 102 if parent is UK-based.
  • Australian AASB if parent is Australian.

Firm's track record of serving US / UK / Singapore / UAE foreign parents indicates competence.

/ Criterion 4: Scope-based + transparent fee

No hourly surprises.

Scoped engagement letter should include:

  • Fixed scope: specific deliverables + specific exclusions.
  • Fixed fee: lump-sum OR clearly-defined retainer.
  • Timeline: delivery milestones.
  • Change-order mechanism: if additional work needed, pre-approval + fee adjustment.

Avoid hourly billing without caps - unlimited exposure for scope expansion.

/ Criterion 5: Specialist depth in your area

Not generalist.

If your need is specific (OIDAR GST, DPDP compliance, PLI application, Section 148 response, Form 15CB urgent, Rule 9B demat remediation, Section 56(2)(viib) legacy), look for specialist firm with:

  • Prior mandates in the specific area.
  • Published practitioner guides / articles / case studies.
  • Specific sector expertise (SaaS / Fintech / Healthtech / D2C / Manufacturing).
  • Partner-level personal involvement in the specific matter.

Generalist firms spread thin across many services - acceptable for standard compliance; often weak on specialist matters.

/ Criterion 6: Published practitioner library

Transparency signal.

Firm's website / blog / published guides demonstrate:

  • Technical depth: specific section numbers, rule references, timelines, forms.
  • Current coverage: 2026 updates reflecting Finance Act 2024, DPDP Act, Pillar 2, FDI reforms.
  • Breadth: 100+ guides suggests practitioner depth; 10 guides may suggest marketing site.
  • Author credentials: specific CA partner authored (not anonymous content).
  • Update frequency: regularly refreshed vs stale.

BQP's 300+ guides at bharatquantumprospera.com are a transparency signal of practitioner depth.

/ Criterion 7: Co-ordination capability with foreign counsel

For cross-border mandates.

For cross-border work (US / UK / EU / Singapore counsel + Indian CA):

  • Firm's prior work with foreign counsel - specific firm references.
  • Deliverables formatted for foreign counsel's SPA / term sheet integration.
  • Weekly / bi-weekly status calls across time zones.
  • Document version control + simultaneous editing capability.
  • Partner-level direct communication with foreign counsel.

Firm's track record on cross-border mandates matters more than any marketing claim.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What's the single most important criterion?
ICAI-qualified CA partner with personal involvement in your matter. Everything else flows from that. ICAI qualification is India's statutory accounting body credential (established 1949, equivalent to US CPA / UK ICAEW / Canadian CPA). Non-CA firms can't sign Indian audit / tax audit / Form 15CB / Form 3CEB.
How do I verify ICAI qualification?
ICAI member search at icai.org. Enter partner's name or membership number. Verifies: membership status (active / lapsed), practice registration, firm name. If partner is not ICAI member or firm not ICAI-registered: avoid for anything requiring CA signature.
Should I pick Big-4 or specialist firm like BQP?
Depends on stage + budget + specific need. See our BQP vs Big-4 comparison. For startup / SMB / mid-market / foreign client wanting specialist + scoped fee + partner attention: specialist firm. For listed Indian company / Fortune 500 subsidiary / mega-cap group / Pillar 2 complex / global network needs: Big-4.
How do I verify a firm's foreign-client track record?
Ask for specific client references (anonymised OK): what countries have they served, what services, over what period. Published case studies on firm's website. LinkedIn: partner's work history + prior foreign-client mandates. Industry references: Big-4 or law firms who've worked alongside the firm on cross-border mandates.
How much should Indian CA engagement cost?
Varies by scope. For reference ranges (indicative, scoped per actual mandate): standard annual compliance (Pvt Ltd + audit + ITR + GST + ROC): INR 1.5-5 lakh per year. Form 15CB: INR 5,000-25,000 per certificate. India market entry (WOS + GST + GST + initial compliance): INR 2-5 lakh one-time. M&A DD: INR 10-50 lakh per target. Compared to Big-4: typically 50-70% lower for specialist firm comparable scope.
