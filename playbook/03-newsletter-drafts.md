**This week in regulation:** FinCEN has not re-activated the Corporate Transparency Act (CTA / BOIR) for domestic US entities since the March 2025 interim rule. Delaware LLCs owned by Indian founders are currently not filing BOIR. Monitor for a final-rule change — we track it and will flag.

---

**Pattern we're seeing:** three Indian founders this quarter with Stripe Atlas Delaware LLCs that have never filed Form 5472. Each had 2-4 missed years at USD 25,000 per year of potential penalty exposure.

Form 5472 applies to any 25%+ foreign-owned US entity with reportable transactions — which includes almost every active LLC owned by a non-US person (the initial capital contribution to open the US bank account counts as a reportable transaction).

The cleanup path is voluntary back-filing with a reasonable-cause statement, usually before the IRS correspondence lands. If you have a Delaware LLC and don't recall seeing a Form 5472 in your filings, worth a check this week.

## Newsletter #2

**Subject:** BQP #2 — Flipping to Delaware: the timing trap

Hi,

---

**This week in regulation:** RBI issued updated Overseas Investment Rules clarifications affecting Indian individuals making outbound equity investments under the LRS route. Form ODI continues to be mandatory within 30 days of each remittance; the clarification affects documentation standards for the pricing certificate.

---

**Pattern we're seeing:** founders arriving at Series A conversations with US VCs, and only then discovering they need to flip from Indian Pvt Ltd to Delaware C-Corp.

The flip itself is routine (60-90 days). The expensive part is the Indian capital gains on the share-for-share swap — calculated on the FMV uplift from original cost to today's valuation.

Flipping at pre-seed FMV (USD 2-5M) → trivial LTCG, often under INR 10 lakh.
Flipping at Series A FMV (USD 50M+) → INR 3-5 crore across the cap table.

If US VC is in your 24-month plan, flipping early at low FMV saves multiples of what late-flipping costs. The one-time INR 5 lakh at pre-seed replaces INR 5 crore at Series B.

If you're not sure where you'll end up, flipping at seed (USD 10-15M FMV) is the "cheap optionality" moment.

## Newsletter #3

**Subject:** BQP #3 — The India-UAE DTAA after UAE Corporate Tax

Hi,

---

**This week in regulation:** The UAE Federal Tax Authority (FTA) has issued further clarifications on Qualifying Free Zone Person (QFZP) status under UAE Corporate Tax. The "qualifying income" definition continues to be the operative test for 0% vs 9% treatment. Entities relying on QFZP status should refresh their substance pack annually.

---

**Pattern we're seeing:** Dubai holdcos set up by Indian founders between 2016-2022 under the pre-2023 UAE zero-tax assumption. Many were run on nominee director + outsourced accounting + minimal UAE physical presence.

Post-2023, those structures:
→ Face 9% UAE Corporate Tax (above AED 375K profit) unless QFZP
→ Need ESR notification + report filed annually for Relevant Activities
→ Need genuine substance for QFZP qualification (local directors, local employees, local operating expenditure, Qualifying Income analysis)
→ Face POEM (India Section 6(3)) risk if the Indian founder's physical presence has shifted back to India

The India-UAE DTAA is still valuable (10% dividend cap, 10% royalty cap vs higher domestic rates), but the structure now needs real work.

For 2016-vintage Dubai holdcos we're currently running substance uplifts and QFZP qualifications as 60-90 day engagements.

## Newsletter #4

**Subject:** BQP #4 — NRI returning to India: the RNOR window

Hi,

---

**This week in regulation:** CBDT clarified via a response to taxpayer representations that the UAE Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) continues to be acceptable evidence for India-UAE DTAA treaty-benefit claims post-UAE Corporate Tax. Document the physical-presence support alongside TRC each year.

---

**Pattern we're seeing:** NRIs who were in Dubai, Singapore, or the US for 7-12 years and are now permanently returning to India discovering the RNOR window too late.

Residential status post-return: typically RNOR for 2-3 financial years, then ROR.

During RNOR, foreign-source income (US brokerage gains, UAE real estate rental, Singapore business income, UK pension) is NOT taxable in India. After RNOR hits ROR, worldwide income becomes Indian-taxable.

The returnee's one-time planning window is RNOR. Standard moves during RNOR:
→ Sell appreciated US / UAE / UK equity → no Indian tax on the gain
→ Convert US traditional IRA to Roth → US tax, no Indian tax; subsequent Roth withdrawals clean
→ Sell overseas real estate → no Indian tax on the gain
→ Close out foreign business distributions → simpler ROR filings later

Discovered too late (after return + 3-4 years), the ROR tax applies to all of this.

For NRIs considering permanent return within 24 months, pre-return scoping is the single most leveraged planning conversation.
