/ OCI cardholders and families · Immigration and tax · Updated 2026-11-25

OCI Card Rules (2026): Eligibility, What OCIs Can and Cannot Do, Passport Reissue at 20 and 50, Children and the 2025 Cancellation Rules, a lifelong visa with conditions that tightened in 2025.

An Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) card is a lifelong, multiple-entry visa for foreign citizens of Indian origin and foreign spouses of Indian citizens or OCIs; it is not dual citizenship. Rules that matter in 2026: since August 2025 an OCI registration can be cancelled if the holder is sentenced to two years or more, or charge-sheeted for an offence punishable with seven years or more, in India or abroad. Under the 2021 guidelines, a card needs reissuing only once, when a new passport is issued after age 20 to someone registered before 20; otherwise the new passport and a photo are uploaded online, each time up to age 20 and once after age 50, within three months.

/ Quick answers

What OCI holders ask most.

  • What changed in 2025? The government added cancellation grounds: a prison sentence of two years or more, or being named in a chargesheet for an offence punishable with seven years or more. It applies to offences in India or abroad that are recognised under Indian law.
  • Do I need a new OCI card with every passport? No. If you registered before 20, the card is reissued once, when you get a new passport after turning 20. If you registered after 20, no reissue is needed; upload the new passport details online.
  • What are the rules for children? Each time a child gets a new passport, up to age 20, upload the new passport and a recent photo on the OCI portal within three months. Reissue the card once after the child turns 20 and gets a new passport.
  • What can’t OCIs do? Vote, hold constitutional or most government posts, or buy agricultural land, plantation or farmhouse property. Research, missionary or journalism work and visits to protected areas need special permission.
  • Who cannot get OCI? Applicants who, or whose parents or grandparents, have been citizens of Pakistan or Bangladesh, and serving or retired foreign military personnel.
  • Does OCI decide my Indian tax? No. Tax residence depends on days spent in India, not on OCI status; see OCI cardholder tax

/ Money and tax for OCIs

What the OCI card does not cover.

  • Bank accounts: OCIs are treated like NRIs for accounts; see can an OCI open NRE / NRO accounts.
  • PAN: needed for investments and property; see PAN for NRIs and OCIs.
  • Investing: demat, mutual funds and digital KYC from abroad; see digital KYC for NRIs and OCIs.
  • Agricultural land: OCIs cannot buy it but can inherit it; see agricultural land rules.
  • Giving up Indian citizenship first: surrender and the renunciation certificate come before OCI; see renouncing Indian citizenship.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What are the new OCI card rules from 2025?
Since August 2025, OCI registration can be cancelled if the holder is sentenced to imprisonment of two years or more, or is charge-sheeted for an offence punishable with seven years or more, in India or abroad, where the offence is recognised under Indian law.
Do I need to renew my OCI card when I get a new passport?
Only once, if you registered before age 20: the card is reissued when a new passport is issued after you turn 20. If you registered after 20, no reissue is needed. In other cases upload the new passport and a photo on the OCI portal within three months.
What are the OCI card rules for children?
Each time a child gets a new passport up to age 20, the new passport and a recent photo must be uploaded on the OCI portal within three months, and the card is reissued once after the child turns 20 and gets a new passport.
Can an OCI cardholder buy property in India?
Yes, residential and commercial property, but not agricultural land, plantation property or a farmhouse. Agricultural land can be inherited.
Does an OCI card make me an Indian tax resident?
No. Tax residence depends on the days you spend in India in the tax year, not on OCI status. OCIs are generally taxed like NRIs on Indian income if they are non-resident.
Can BQP help OCI cardholders?
Yes, on the tax and money side: residence, Indian income, NRE / NRO accounts, property and repatriation. Immigration questions should go to the Indian mission.
