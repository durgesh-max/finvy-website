    / How to use the draft

    From draft to submission.

    • Quote the notice exactly: section or form, DIN or reference number, date and year, so the reply is matched to the right proceeding.
    • Answer every query: one point per query, with figures that match your return and books.
    • Attach evidence: each annexure the draft lists should be a clear PDF: bank certificates, ledgers, invoices, reconciliations.
    • Submit online: income tax replies go through e-Proceedings on the e-filing portal; GST replies are filed on the GST portal in the form the notice names (ASMT-11, DRC-06 and so on).
    • Check before you submit: a submitted reply stays on record. If you need more time, ask for an adjournment before the due date.

    FAQ

    Common questions, answered.

    Is there a free tool to draft an income tax notice reply?
    Yes. The generator on this page builds a point-by-point draft for income tax notices under sections 143(1), 139(9), 142(1), 143(2), 133(6) and 148A, which you can copy into Word and save as a PDF.
    Can it draft a GST notice reply?
    Yes, for ASMT-10 (reply in ASMT-11), DRC-01A, DRC-01 (reply in DRC-06) and GSTR-3A notices. The figures still need to match your returns and books.
    Is this an AI tool?
    No. It is a template builder that runs in your browser, so nothing you type is uploaded. It gives you a clean structure; the content of each reply has to come from your facts and documents.
    Is there a prescribed format for a notice reply?
    No. A clear letter that quotes the notice, answers each query and lists the annexures is enough. Income tax replies go through e-Proceedings; GST replies use the form the notice names.
    Should a CA check the draft?
    It is safer. A submitted reply cannot be edited, and a wrong admission or missed point can lead to an adjustment, demand or penalty.
