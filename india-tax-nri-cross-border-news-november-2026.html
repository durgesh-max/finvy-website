The tax and regulatory changes this month that matter to NRIs, Indian founders and foreign companies with Indian operations, each with the action to take. Compiled by CA Durgesh Chavda, Bharat Quantum Prospera and updated through the month.

Newest first.

  • 25 Nov 2026: GST on rent, who pays
    Residential rent to a GST-registered tenant and commercial rent from an unregistered landlord are taxed at 18% under reverse charge, paid by the tenant; composition tenants are excluded for commercial rent. Read more
  • 25 Nov 2026: TDS on rent now sits in section 393
    For tax year 2026-27, tenants deduct TDS on rent above ₹50,000 a month under section 393 (old 194-IB and 194-I), and property buyers deduct 1% under Sl. No. 3(i) (old 194-IA). Read more
  • 25 Nov 2026: AIS mismatch? Five weeks left to revise for free
    If your FY 2025-26 return misses income shown in your Annual Information Statement, a revised return is free until 31 December 2026; wrong AIS entries need feedback on the portal. Read more
  • 25 Nov 2026: 54EC bonds now sit in section 85
    Capital gains bonds for long-term gains on land or buildings keep the six-month window, ₹50 lakh yearly cap and five-year lock-in under the Income-tax Act, 2025. Read more

  • 24 Nov: GST on insurance for NRIs. Individual life, term and health premiums carry nil GST since 22 September 2025; NRI GST refunds matter only for earlier premiums paid from NRE or abroad. Details.
  • 24 Nov: managing Indian property from abroad. Whoever manages the flat, the tenant deducts about 31.2% TDS, rent should go to your NRO account and you file the Indian return; Form 128 can cut the TDS. Checklist.
  • 24 Nov: dormant companies. An inactive Indian company can take dormant status under section 455 (MSC-1, then MSC-3 yearly), but income tax and FLA returns continue; strike-off may be simpler if there is no plan to restart. Details.
  • 24 Nov: GST registration for startups. Required above INR 20 lakh turnover for services (INR 40 lakh for goods-only in most states), and from the first rupee for inter-state goods, reverse charge and a few other cases; startup recognition gives no GST exemption. Details.
  • 24 Nov: angel tax abolished, what remains. Section 56(2)(viib) was omitted from 1 April 2025 (FY 2024-25 onwards) for all investors, but FEMA fair value pricing, investor-side tax on below-value issues and Companies Act valuation still apply. Details.
  • 24 Nov: getting paid by foreign clients. Use the right RBI purpose code (P0802, P0807, P1006 and others), keep the FIRA, file an LUT for GST and realise within nine months under the 2026 FEMA export rules. Guide.
  • 24 Nov: NRI karta of an HUF. Allowed; the HUF is non-resident only if its control and management is wholly outside India, otherwise resident, with ordinary residence following the karta. Details.
  • 24 Nov: unlisted (pre-IPO) shares. Legal as a private transfer, but SEBI warned on 17 June 2026 against unauthorised platforms; gains after 24 months are taxed at 12.5%, and NRI buyers must pay fair value and file FC-TRS. Details.
  • 24 Nov: ESOP buybacks. Shares bought back on or after 1 April 2026 give a capital gain over the exercise-date value (12.5% for unlisted shares held over 24 months); cash for unexercised options is usually salary, though a 2026 Bangalore tribunal held it a capital gain. Details.
  • 24 Nov: Australian residents selling property in India. India deducts TDS first; Australia taxes half the gain if held 12 months, with cost at market value when you became resident and a foreign income tax offset; temporary visa holders are generally outside Australian tax on it. Steps.
  • 24 Nov: UK residents selling property in India. India deducts TDS first (12.5% plus surcharge and cess, reduce with Form 128); HMRC charges 18% or 24% above GBP 3,000 with foreign tax credit relief, through Self Assessment. Steps.
  • 24 Nov: US citizens selling property in India. Allowed, including inherited property and by power of attorney; India deducts TDS first, and the US taxes the dollar gain with a stepped-up basis for inherited property and a foreign tax credit. Steps.
  • 24 Nov: Canadians selling property in India. TDS of 12.5% plus surcharge and cess on long-term gains (reduce it with Form 128), up to USD 1 million a year to Canada from NRO, and Canadian tax on half the gain with credit for the Indian tax. Steps.
  • 24 Nov: registering a US company from India. The US filing is quick, but a resident must report the investment as ODI through the bank, file the APR by 31 December and, for a single-member LLC, Form 5472 in the US. Steps and Indian side.
  • 24 Nov: gift money from parents in India. Not taxed as income in India, the US, UK, Canada or Australia; US persons file Form 3520 above USD 100,000, and parents pay 20% TCS on gift remittances above INR 10 lakh, refundable in their return. Country by country.
  • 24 Nov: accuracy pass on capital gains and residence. Corrected: the 20%-with-indexation option on pre-23 July 2024 assets is only for land or buildings of resident individuals and HUFs (LTCG guide; inherited property); RNOR needs non-residence in 9 of 10 preceding years or 729 days or less in 7 (residential status); the INR 10 crore cap on Section 54 and 54F added to the 54 vs 54F vs 54EC guide.
  • 23 Nov: correction on 54EC bonds. Section 54EC bonds exempt only long-term gains on land or buildings (since 1 April 2018), not gains on shares, mutual funds or ESOPs; our 54 vs 54F vs 54EC guide and two ESOP exit pages have been corrected. Also new: is Dubai or other foreign salary taxed in India for residents, leavers and deemed residents.
  • 23 Nov: NRI senior citizens. India’s senior-citizen tax benefits (higher exemption, INR 50,000 interest deduction, advance tax exemption, Form 15H, the 75+ no-return rule) are for residents only; NRIs get INR 2.5 lakh at any age under the old regime. What still works and what changes on retiring to India.
  • 23 Nov: GST on export of services. A valid service export is zero-rated: file the LUT (RFD-11) before the first export invoice of the year and invoice without IGST, or pay 18% IGST and claim it back; payment under the LUT must arrive within one year (Rule 96A). Conditions, invoice wording and refunds.
  • 23 Nov: transfer pricing safe harbour vs APA. From tax year 2026-27, Indian IT, ITeS, KPO and software R&D subsidiaries can opt for a 15.5% margin on operating expenses (up to INR 2,000 crore, Form 49 for five years); APAs now cost a flat INR 20 lakh and unilateral IT services APAs have a two-year timeline. Which to choose.

  • 23 Nov (accuracy): the OCI cardholder tax guide is corrected. For an OCI holder visiting India the 60-day residence test becomes 182 days, or 120 days where Indian income exceeds INR 15 lakh; deemed residency under section 6(1A) applies only to Indian citizens, not OCI holders; the basic exemption is INR 4 lakh under the new regime with no rebate for non-residents; and the PAN form for foreign citizens is now Form 95.

    The Finance Act, 2026 extended the Section 139(5) window from nine to twelve months. Revising until 31 December 2026 stays free; a revision from 1 January to 31 March 2027 attracts a fee under section 234-I of INR 1,000 or INR 5,000 depending on total income.     Sole traders and landlords in the first Making Tax Digital for Income Tax cohort send their update for 6 July to 5 October 2026 by 7 November. No penalty points for late quarterly updates in 2026-27, but returns and payments are still penalised. Read more · Source: Xero

    Notification 03/2026-Central Tax (26 October 2026) brings Sections 153 to 155 of the Finance Act, 2026 into force from today. Discounts given after a supply can be adjusted through a credit note (Sections 15(3)(b) and 34(1)), 90% of inverted-duty refund claims can be paid provisionally (Section 54(6)), and the INR 1,000 minimum no longer applies to refunds on goods exported with payment of tax. / Already in force

Changes from earlier in 2026 that still catch people out.

  • Income-tax Act, 2025 section numbers and new form numbers (15CA is now Form 145).
  • No TAN needed to buy property from an NRI from 1 October 2026.
  • The amended India-Oman treaty (Protocol notified as Notification 69/2025) applies from 1 April 2026: royalties and technical fees fall from 15% to 10%. See also the India-Saudi Arabia treaty rates.
  • The new India-Qatar tax treaty (signed 18 February 2025, Notification 154/2025) applies in India from 1 April 2026: 5% dividends now need a 25% holding, Qatar Investment Authority interest is exempt, and a principal purpose test applies.
  • NRIs: GIFT City options (dollar FDs, IFSC funds, insurance) are tax-free in India for non-residents in many cases; most useful for Gulf-based NRIs.
  • New tool: India entry structure chooser for foreign companies.
  • New calculator: NRI rent TDS refund (31.2% tenant TDS vs tax on actual rental income).
  • New tool: FEMA health check for foreign-owned Indian companies (FC-GPR, FLA, Form DI, ECB, valuation).
  • New tool for foreign companies: permanent establishment risk checker (offices, staff, agents, services, projects).
  • Trade: India’s trade agreements in 2026 - UK CETA in force from July 2026, EFTA TEPA since October 2025, EU FTA concluded in January 2026 and awaiting signature.
  • NRI wills: probate is no longer compulsory in Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata after the Repealing and Amending Act, 2025, though registrars and banks may still ask for it.
  • NRIs and OCIs: PAN applications now use Form 93 or Form 95 (formerly 49A / 49AA) from 1 April 2026; an inoperative PAN blocks refunds.
  • NRIs with Indian shares: capital gains tax and TDS refunds (12.5% long-term above INR 1.25 lakh, 20% short-term; claim excess TDS by 31 December 2026).
  • Employees of foreign companies: RSU taxation in India (salary at vesting, 12.5% on long-term gains after 24 months, Schedule FA every year).
  • Raising from abroad: a FEMA valuation report is still needed for every issue or transfer with a non-resident, even though angel tax was abolished from FY 2024-25.
  • Foreign parents: a single FY 2026-27 compliance calendar for Indian subsidiaries, the first year under the Income-tax Act, 2025.
  • RBI’s June 2026 deposit amendment opened SNRR rupee accounts to any non-resident (no business-interest test, seven-year limit deleted). Draft FEMA (Foreign Investment) Rules issued in July 2026 would rework downstream investment rules; not yet in force.
  • Foreign parents: buybacks are taxed as capital gains from 1 April 2026 (promoter levy applies); compare with dividend and capital reduction in getting money back from an Indian subsidiary. New founders: registering a company as a foreign national.
  • RBI’s October 2025 draft rules for foreign liaison and branch offices (no net worth test) were still not notified at last report; the 2016 rules apply. Also new: franchising a foreign brand in India.
  • US companies: paying Indian contractors needs W-8BEN / W-8BEN-E, not Form 1099-NEC; watch misclassification and PE risk.
  • Also for foreign companies: China plus one (new manufacturers pay 25.168%; the 15% regime closed after 31 March 2024), royalty payments and types of due diligence.
  • For foreign companies: acquiring an Indian company (CCI deal value threshold INR 2,000 crore; escrow up to 25% for 18 months), joint ventures, distributors and partners and sell-side due diligence.
  • Which ITR for NRIs: ITR-2 or ITR-3; FY 2025-26 belated returns and refund claims until 31 December 2026.
  • NRE interest abroad: tax-free in India but taxable for UK residents since the remittance basis ended on 6 April 2025 (unless four-year FIG relief applies), and in the US, Canada and Australia.
  • NRIs buying property in India: pay by remittance or from NRE to keep the price repatriable.
  • FEMA export rules: nine months to realise export proceeds.
  • Press Note 2 (2026) on investment with China or other land-border links.
  • NRIs starting a business in India: Indian citizens abroad can form an OPC (since 1 April 2021); LLP resident designated partner test is 120 days.
  • Revised ECB framework (in force 16 February 2026): NRIs and other non-resident individuals can lend to Indian companies; event-based ECB 2 returns.
