GST Refund for Exporters (2026): Refund of IGST Paid on Exports or Unused ITC Under LUT - RFD-01, Shipping Bill Route, Time Limit, Documents RFD-08 Notice Reply and What to Do If the Refund Is Rejected (RFD-06), pick the route, file in time, match every document.

Exports are zero-rated under GST, so an exporter should not end up bearing GST: either you pay IGST on the export and get it refunded, or you export under a letter of undertaking (LUT) without paying IGST and get the unused input tax credit refunded. Most refund delays come from mismatches between invoices, shipping bills, GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B, or from missing proof of foreign payment for services. The application must be made within two years of the relevant date.

/ The two routes

With payment of IGST, or under LUT.

RouteHow the refund worksForm

Export of services with payment of IGSTApply for a refund of the IGST paid on the export invoicesRFD-01

/ Time limits and rules

What the law says.

  • Two years: the refund application must be filed within two years of the relevant date (section 54(1)).
  • Provisional refund: for zero-rated supplies, 90% of the claim can be refunded provisionally (section 54(6)), with the balance after verification.
  • Order: the officer should pass the order within 60 days of a complete application (section 54(7)).
  • LUT every year: the LUT in Form RFD-11 is furnished for each financial year, before exporting without IGST.
  • Services: an export of services needs the payment received in convertible foreign exchange (or in rupees where the RBI permits); keep the FIRC or e-BRC for each invoice.
  • Old demands: rule 96(10) was omitted retrospectively, which helps exporters fighting old IGST refund demands - see our rule 96(10) guide.

/ Why refunds get stuck

The usual mismatches.

  • Export invoice values or numbers in GSTR-1 Table 6A not matching the shipping bill.
  • GSTR-3B not filed, or IGST paid shown differently from GSTR-1.
  • Missing FIRC / e-BRC for services, or payment received in a way that does not count as export proceeds.
  • Unused ITC claim not reconciled with GSTR-2B, or turnover figures in the rule 89(4) formula inconsistent with returns.
  • Deficiency memo (RFD-03) not answered with a corrected fresh application.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

How do exporters get a GST refund?
Either export with payment of IGST and claim it back (for goods the shipping bill works as the refund application once GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B are filed; for services file RFD-01), or export under an LUT without paying IGST and claim a refund of the unused input tax credit in RFD-01.
Do I have to pay GST on exports?
Exports are zero-rated: you either pay IGST and get it refunded, or export under an LUT without paying IGST. Either way the exporter should not finally bear GST, provided the conditions and paperwork are in order.
What is the time limit for a GST export refund?
The refund application must be filed within two years of the relevant date under section 54(1) of the CGST Act. For zero-rated supplies, 90% can be refunded provisionally, and the officer should pass the order within 60 days of a complete application.
How do I file an LUT for exports under GST?
File Form GST RFD-11 on the GST portal for each financial year before exporting without paying IGST. With an LUT you then claim a refund of the unused input tax credit, not of IGST.
Why is my GST export refund stuck?
Usually a mismatch: invoice details in GSTR-1 not matching the shipping bill, GSTR-3B not filed or inconsistent, missing FIRC / e-BRC for services, or ITC figures not matching GSTR-2B. A deficiency memo needs a corrected fresh application.
How do I check my GST refund status?
For a refund filed in Form RFD-01 (unused credit under an LUT, or IGST paid on services), track it with the ARN on the GST portal's refund status facility. For IGST paid on exported goods, the shipping bill itself is the refund claim and its status is tracked with customs on ICEGATE. Once a refund is sanctioned, the bank credit is processed through PFMS, so a sanctioned refund that has not reached your account is checked there. If the status has not moved for weeks, look for a deficiency memo or RFD-08 notice in your account.
How do I reply to a GST refund show cause notice in Form RFD-08?
Under rule 92(3) of the CGST Rules, an officer who thinks all or part of a refund is not admissible must first issue a notice in Form RFD-08, and you reply in Form RFD-09 within fifteen days of receiving it (or any other period the notice allows). Answer each ground point by point with the supporting documents - shipping bills, export invoices, bank realisation, the LUT, the ITC working - and ask for a personal hearing. The officer then passes a reasoned order in RFD-06, and a refund cannot be rejected without giving you a hearing. File the reply before the order is issued, as the portal does not accept an RFD-09 once an RFD-06 has been passed; if the claim is still rejected, the remedy is an appeal in APL-01.

My GST refund was rejected in Form RFD-06. What can I do next?
First read the order: it should say whether the claim is rejected in full or only in part, and the reason for each part. If the application was only returned with a deficiency memo (RFD-03), that is not a rejection on merits - file a fresh, corrected RFD-01 within the two-year time limit. If the refund was rejected on merits, the remedy is an appeal to the Appellate Authority under section 107 in Form APL-01, filed within three months of the date the order was communicated (the authority can allow one more month for sufficient cause). A rejection passed without first issuing an RFD-08 notice and giving a hearing can be challenged on that ground as well. Where the claim was a refund of unused input tax credit debited from the electronic credit ledger, the rejected amount is re-credited to the ledger by an order in PMT-03 once the rejection becomes final, so decide on the appeal before that point.
