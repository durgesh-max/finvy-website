/ NRIs in Kuwait and Kuwaiti companies · DTAA · Updated 2026-11-25

India-Kuwait DTAA (2026): Tax Treaty Rates of 10%, TRC for NRIs in Kuwait, Deemed Residence and NRO Interest, 10% on paper, but NRIs in Kuwait must prove treaty residence and watch the deemed-residence rule.

The India-Kuwait double tax avoidance agreement caps Indian tax on dividends, interest, royalties and fees for technical services paid to a Kuwaiti resident at 10%, about half India’s domestic withholding of 20% plus surcharge and cess, with no shareholding tiers. For the large Indian community in Kuwait, the catch is proof: the treaty rate needs a Kuwaiti tax residency certificate and Form 41 (formerly Form 10F), and because Kuwait has no personal income tax, an Indian citizen with Indian income above INR 15 lakh who is not liable to tax anywhere can be treated as resident in India under the deemed-residence rule. In every case the lower of the treaty rate and the Income-tax Act rate applies.

/ Quick answers

What NRIs in Kuwait ask.

  • What TDS applies to my NRO interest? 30% plus surcharge and cess by default; with a Kuwaiti TRC and Form 41 the bank can apply the 10% treaty rate.
  • Is NRE interest taxed? No. NRE interest is exempt in India while you are a non-resident, so the treaty is not needed for it.
  • Can I get a tax residency certificate in Kuwait? Kuwait issues residency certificates for treaty purposes through its tax authorities; apply well before the Indian payment date, since requirements and timing vary.
  • Does the deemed-residence rule affect me? Possibly: an Indian citizen with Indian income (excluding foreign sources) above INR 15 lakh who is not liable to tax in any country can be deemed resident (RNOR) in India; see deemed residence for Gulf NRIs.
  • What about rent and property sales? Rent and capital gains on Indian property remain taxable in India; the treaty does not usually reduce them.

/ What to do

A short checklist for NRIs in Kuwait.

  • Get a Kuwaiti TRC for the relevant year and give it to your Indian bank or payer with Form 41 and your PAN.
  • Count your days in India and your Indian income, to check non-resident status and the deemed-residence rule.
  • Keep NRE and NRO separate: foreign earnings in NRE (tax-free interest), Indian income in NRO.
  • File an Indian return where TDS was deducted above your actual tax, to claim a refund.
  • Report correctly if you become resident again on return, including your Kuwaiti end-of-service benefits and accounts.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What are the India-Kuwait DTAA rates?
Dividends, interest, royalties and fees for technical services paid by an Indian payer to a Kuwaiti resident are taxed in India at up to 10%, with no shareholding tiers. If the domestic rate is lower, the domestic rate applies.
How can an NRI in Kuwait reduce TDS on NRO interest?
Give the Indian bank a Kuwaiti tax residency certificate, Form 41 (formerly Form 10F) and your PAN so it can deduct 10% under the treaty instead of 30% plus surcharge and cess, and file an Indian return to claim any excess back.
Does Kuwait having no income tax affect my Indian residence?
It can. An Indian citizen whose Indian income (excluding foreign sources) is above INR 15 lakh and who is not liable to tax in any country can be deemed resident in India (as RNOR), so check your income and your status each year.
Is NRE interest taxable for an NRI in Kuwait?
No. Interest on NRE accounts is exempt in India while you are a non-resident, so the treaty is not needed for it.
