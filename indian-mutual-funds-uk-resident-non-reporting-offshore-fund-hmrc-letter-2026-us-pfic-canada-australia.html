/ NRI · Mutual funds · UK offshore funds · HMRC · Updated 2026-11-20

Indian Mutual Funds While Living Abroad (2026): UK Non-Reporting Offshore Fund Rules and HMRC's October 2026 Letters, US PFIC, Canada T1135, Australia, Singapore, UAE, the tax in your country can be far higher than the Indian tax.

For UK residents, Indian mutual funds are usually non-reporting offshore funds. Indian funds generally do not appear on HMRC’s list of reporting funds, but check each fund. When you sell, the gain is an offshore income gain taxed as income at your marginal rate, up to 45%, not as a capital gain. The capital gains allowance does not apply and capital losses cannot be set off. Distributions are taxed as dividends. In October 2026 HMRC began writing to investors in offshore funds, asking them to check that past and current returns include this income and these gains. HMRC says it is not a compliance check, but errors found should be corrected. Since 6 April 2025 the remittance basis has gone, although qualifying new arrivals may use the 4-year FIG exemption. US persons face the PFIC rules on Indian funds. Canadians report them on T1135 once the cost of foreign property exceeds CAD 100,000. Australians pay CGT with a credit for Indian tax. Singapore and UAE residents generally pay no tax there on the gains. India also taxes NRI gains, with TDS.

/ If you are UK resident

Practical steps.

  • Check status: look up each fund on GOV.UK’s list of approved offshore reporting funds; assume non-reporting if it is not listed.
  • Report correctly: offshore income gains go on the Self Assessment return as income; distributions as foreign dividends.
  • Past years: if gains or income were missed, consider correcting them; your UK adviser can advise on HMRC’s disclosure routes.
  • Indian side: the fund house deducts TDS on NRI redemptions; file an Indian return to reconcile and claim any excess; see NRI mutual fund tax in India.
  • Credit: the UK generally gives credit for Indian tax on the same gain under the India-UK treaty, subject to UK rules; see the India-UK treaty guide.
  • Status changes: see changing NRI status in funds; US persons see PFIC explained.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Are Indian mutual funds reporting funds for UK tax?
Generally not; Indian funds do not usually appear on HMRC's list of approved offshore reporting funds, so they are treated as non-reporting funds. Check each fund on the GOV.UK list.
How are gains on Indian mutual funds taxed in the UK?
For non-reporting funds, gains on disposal are offshore income gains taxed as income at your marginal rate, without the capital gains allowance or loss relief; distributions are taxed as dividends.
What is HMRC's October 2026 offshore funds letter?
An educational one-to-many letter asking investors in offshore funds to check that income and gains are included in their Self Assessment returns for current and past years; HMRC says it is not a compliance check.
Do UK residents pay Indian tax on Indian mutual fund gains too?
Yes, India taxes the gains of non-residents with TDS; the UK generally gives credit for the Indian tax under the treaty, subject to UK rules.
How are Indian mutual funds taxed for US, Canada and Australia residents?
US persons face PFIC rules; Canadians report on T1135 above CAD 100,000 and pay tax on gains; Australians pay CGT with a foreign income tax offset for Indian tax.
