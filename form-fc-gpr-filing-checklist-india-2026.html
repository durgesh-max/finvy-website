/ FEMA Form Checklist · Updated 2026-10-22

Form FC-GPR Filing (2026): Due Date, FIRMS Procedure, Documents and Checklist, 30-day deadline + checklist.

Form FC-GPR (the FEMA return on issue of shares to a person resident outside India) is mandatory FEMA filing by Indian company receiving foreign inward FDI remittance: within 30 days of share allotment post remittance. Non-filing attracts FEMA compounding (section 15). Here's the complete 2026 filing checklist.

/ Who must file + when

The core rule.

  • Indian company receives foreign inward remittance for share subscription (FDI).
  • Share allotment made to foreign subscriber.
  • Form FC-GPR filed within 30 days of allotment via FIRMS portal (RBI).
  • Late filing: within three years, regularised by paying a late submission fee (LSF); beyond that, or for other breaches, compounding under section 15 of FEMA - otherwise a penalty under section 13 of up to three times the amount involved.

/ Required documents

The attachments.

  • FIRC (Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate) from AD bank.
  • KYC of foreign subscriber (passport + address proof).
  • Board resolution authorising allotment.
  • Share allotment letter.
  • Pricing certification: CA / Merchant Banker certificate confirming price per Rule 21 Pricing Guidelines (internationally accepted pricing methodology for unlisted shares; SEBI-guideline price for listed shares).
  • Non-resident declaration of eligibility.
  • CS certificate of compliance (where applicable).

/ Filing workflow

Step-by-step.

  1. Collect foreign shareholder KYC + apostilled documents pre-allotment.
  2. Verify FDI route: automatic vs approval; sector cap check.
  3. Press Note 3 (eased for non-controlling stakes within 10% by Press Note 2 (2026)) beneficial ownership check for land-bordering country investments.
  4. Receive foreign remittance in AD-I bank account. Obtain FIRC.
  5. Allot shares via board resolution within 60 days of receiving the money (FEMA; section 42 of the Companies Act sets the same 60 days for private placements).
  6. Issue share certificates + file Form PAS-3 within 15 days of allotment (private placement under section 42; 30 days applies only to other allotments).
  7. Obtain pricing certificate from CA / Merchant Banker.
  8. File Form FC-GPR via FIRMS portal within 30 days of allotment.
  9. AD bank reviews + confirms successful filing.
  10. Preserve filing acknowledgement + all supporting documents 8+ years.

/ Common mistakes + fixes

What goes wrong.

  • Missed 30-day deadline: pay a Late Submission Fee (INR 7,500 plus 0.025% of the amount for each year of delay, capped at the amount involved) within three years of the due date; after that the contravention goes to compounding.
  • Pricing below Rule 21 guidelines: FEMA breach - compounding + possible unwinding.
  • Press Note 3 breach: beneficial ownership from land-bordering country without approval - serious compounding + unwinding risk.
  • Share allotment delay beyond 60 days: separate FEMA breach: shares must be issued within 60 days of receiving the money (the earlier FEMA limit was 180 days), or the money refunded within 15 days after that.
  • FIRC not obtained: cannot file FC-GPR without FIRC. Chase AD bank.
  • Wrong pricing method: unlisted shares need a fair value on an internationally accepted pricing methodology, and listed shares the SEBI-guideline price; the issue price cannot be below it.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

When is Form FC-GPR due?
Within 30 days of share allotment to foreign subscriber. Allotment itself must be made within 60 days of receiving the money (FEMA Non-Debt Instrument Rules, and section 42(6) of the Companies Act for private placements), failing which the money must be refunded. So typical timeline: remittance → 60 days → allotment → 30 days → FC-GPR filing. Total 90 days from remittance to FC-GPR.
How do I file FC-GPR online on the RBI FIRMS portal?
FC-GPR is filed in the Single Master Form on the RBI's FIRMS portal. The company first needs an Entity User (approved by the RBI) to complete the Entity Master, and then a Business User approved through its AD bank - only a Business User can file forms. The Business User fills FC-GPR with the allotment details and uploads the board resolution for the allotment, the valuation certificate, the KYC of the foreign investor and proof of the inward remittance from the AD bank, along with the declarations the form asks for. The AD bank checks the form and forwards it to the RBI; reply to any query it raises within the time given.
Is there a fee to file FC-GPR?
No fee is charged for filing FC-GPR on time. If it is filed late, the RBI allows the delay to be regularised by paying a late submission fee (within three years of the due date), and longer delays go through FEMA compounding under section 15.

When is Form PAS-3 due after allotting shares to a foreign investor?
Where the shares are issued by private placement under section 42, the return of allotment in Form PAS-3 is due within 15 days of allotment, and the money received cannot be used until PAS-3 is filed. FC-GPR is a separate FEMA filing due within 30 days of allotment.
What's the penalty for late FC-GPR?
Reporting delays within three years are usually settled by a Late Submission Fee of INR 7,500 plus 0.025% of the amount for each year of delay (within three years of the due date); otherwise FEMA compounding applies. For prolonged non-filing or sector-cap breach: significantly higher, up to 3x amount involved.
What's Rule 21 pricing guidelines?
FEMA Rule 21 (Non-debt Instrument Rules 2019 + predecessors): share issue to non-resident must be at fair value. Unlisted: fair value on an internationally accepted pricing methodology on an arm's length basis, certified by a chartered accountant, SEBI-registered merchant banker or practising cost accountant. Listed: the price worked out under the SEBI guidelines. Issue below Rule 21 pricing is FEMA breach.
