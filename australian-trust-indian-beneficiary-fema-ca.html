/ Australian trust framework

The vehicles.

  • Discretionary trust: trustee has discretion over distributions among specific class of beneficiaries. Common Australian family wealth vehicle.
  • Unit trust: fixed entitlements based on units held. Common Australian investment vehicle.
  • Australian trust taxed on undistributed income at 47% (top marginal); distributed income taxed in beneficiary's hands at beneficiary's rate.
  • Trust can be settlor-settled or deed-created with specific structure.
  • Specific Australian anti-avoidance rules + specific disclosure requirements.

/ Indian tax on trust distribution

The receipt analysis.

  • Residential status: Indian beneficiary's ROR / RNOR / NR determines Indian taxability.
  • ROR: worldwide income taxable in India - trust distribution taxed under specific head-wise characterisation (business / interest / dividend / capital gain).
  • RNOR: foreign-source trust income NOT received in India AND NOT accruing in India - typically not taxable in India.
  • NR: foreign-source trust income not taxable in India.
  • Section 56(2)(x): gift received from non-relative taxable as 'Income from Other Sources' if aggregate specific threshold INR 50K per year.
  • Trust distribution characterisation: specific analysis - is it distribution of income (taxable) or distribution of corpus (gift / capital receipt)?
  • India-Australia DTAA: specific head-wise relief + FTC under Section 90.

/ FEMA reporting

The exchange control.

  • Trust distribution received by Indian resident: inward remittance via AD-I category bank.
  • Specific FEMA LRS not applicable to inward (LRS is for outward).
  • Inward trust distribution: FIRC (Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate) issued.
  • Specific disclosure in Indian ITR: specific foreign income head + Schedule FA (if specific thresholds).
  • Specific Schedule FA requirements: specific foreign assets + specific beneficial interests + specific trust beneficiary disclosure.
  • Specific Black Money (Undisclosed Foreign Income and Assets) and Imposition of Tax Act 2015 compliance: Schedule FA accurate reporting critical.

/ India-Australia DTAA

The treaty overlay.

  • India-Australia DTAA (operational since 1991; MLI covered agreement).
  • Residence tie-breaker: specific tests applied.
  • Dividend: 15% treaty rate.
  • Interest: 15% treaty rate.
  • Royalty / FTS: 10-15% depending on specific scenarios.
  • Business profits: taxable only via PE (Article 7).
  • Capital gain: taxable in state of residence subject to specific real estate / substantial holding carve-outs.
  • Specific trust distribution + beneficiary residency analysis.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Is Australian trust distribution to Indian beneficiary taxable in India?
Depends on beneficiary's residential status: ROR = yes, worldwide income taxable + head-wise characterisation. RNOR / NR = foreign-source trust income generally not taxable in India unless received/accruing in India. Specific analysis: is distribution income (taxable) or corpus (gift / capital receipt)?
Does Schedule FA need to disclose Australian trust beneficial interest?
Yes - Indian resident with foreign assets / beneficial interests above specific thresholds must disclose Schedule FA. Trust beneficial interest + specific trust assets + specific income from trust = Schedule FA disclosure. Black Money Act 2015 strict penalties for non-disclosure.
What's Section 56(2)(x) impact on trust distribution?
Section 56(2)(x): gift received from non-relative above INR 50K/year taxable as 'Income from Other Sources'. Trustee / trust is typically not 'relative' under Section 56. Specific trust distribution analysis: if characterised as gift from non-relative + aggregate > INR 50K = taxable under Section 56. Analysis case-by-case.
Can FTC be claimed for Australian tax paid?
Yes - Section 90 FTC with Form 67. Credit limited to lower of (a) Australian tax on same income, (b) Indian tax on same income. Rule 128 governs computation. Form 67 filed before Indian ITR due date. India-Australia DTAA Article 24 governs.
