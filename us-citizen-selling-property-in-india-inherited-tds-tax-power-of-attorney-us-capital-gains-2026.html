US Citizen or NRI in the US Selling Property in India (2026): Tax Implications in India and the US, Inherited Property, TDS, Power of Attorney and Repatriation, India taxes first; the US credits it.

Yes, a US citizen can sell property in India, including inherited property: as a person of Indian origin (with or without an OCI card) you can sell a house, flat or commercial property to a resident Indian, an NRI or an OCI holder; agricultural land only to a resident Indian citizen. India taxes the gain first: the buyer deducts TDS, 12.5% plus surcharge and cess on long-term gains, unless you get a lower deduction certificate (Form 128). You can send up to USD 1 million a year to the US from your NRO account. The US taxes the gain in dollars at 0%, 15% or 20% (plus 3.8% net investment income tax for higher incomes), with a foreign tax credit for the Indian tax; for inherited property, your US cost is the value on the date of death, which often makes the US gain small.

/ Quick answers

What US citizens selling in India ask.

  • Can a US citizen sell property in India? Yes, residential or commercial property, to a resident Indian, an NRI or an OCI. Agricultural land, plantations and farmhouses can be sold only to an Indian citizen resident in India.
  • Can I sell property I inherited in India? Yes. For Indian tax, your cost and holding period include the previous owner’s; for US tax, your cost is the fair market value at the date of death.
  • Can I sell without travelling to India? Yes, through a power of attorney signed in the US, notarised and apostilled (or attested at an Indian consulate), then adjudicated for stamp duty in India within the time allowed.
  • How much TDS? 12.5% plus surcharge and cess on long-term gains (held over 24 months); 30% plus surcharge and cess on short-term gains. Apply for Form 128 before the sale so TDS matches the real tax.
  • What do I report in the US? The sale on Schedule D and Form 8949, the Indian tax on Form 1116 for credit, the NRO account on the FBAR and Form 8938 where thresholds are met, and Form 3520 if you inherited over USD 100,000 from a non-US person.

Because the US uses the stepped-up value for inherited property and measures in dollars, the Indian tax is often larger than the US tax on the same sale; unused foreign tax credit can be carried back one year and forward ten. See the India-US treaty rates.

/ Steps

Selling from the US without surprises.

  • 1. Documents: title deed or will, probate or succession certificate where needed, mutation in your name, tax receipts, PAN (Form 95 for foreign citizens).
  • 2. Work out both gains and the expected Indian and US tax.
  • 3. Apply for the lower deduction certificate (Form 128); see the TDS and LDC calculator.
  • 4. Sign in person or by power of attorney; the buyer deducts TDS (a resident buyer can deposit it under their own PAN in Form 141 from 1 October 2026).
  • 5. Receive the money in your NRO account and remit with Forms 145 and 146, up to USD 1 million a year.
  • 6. File the Indian return to claim back excess TDS, then report in the US and claim the foreign tax credit.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Can a US citizen sell property in India?
Yes. A US citizen of Indian origin, with or without an OCI card, can sell residential or commercial property in India to a resident Indian, an NRI or an OCI. Agricultural land, plantations and farmhouses can be sold only to an Indian citizen resident in India.
Can a US citizen sell inherited property in India?
Yes. Indian tax uses the previous owner's cost and holding period; US tax uses the fair market value at the date of death as your cost, which often leaves little US gain. Title must first be in your name through mutation, and a succession certificate or probate may be needed.
How is the sale of Indian property taxed for a US citizen?
India taxes the gain first (12.5% plus surcharge and cess for long-term gains). The US taxes the gain in dollars at 0%, 15% or 20%, plus 3.8% NIIT for higher incomes, and allows a foreign tax credit for the Indian tax under the India-US treaty.
Can a US citizen sell property in India with a power of attorney?
Yes. Sign a power of attorney in the US, have it notarised and apostilled or attested at an Indian consulate, and have it adjudicated for stamp duty in India within the time allowed; your attorney can then sign the sale deed.
How do I transfer the sale proceeds to the US?
Through your NRO account, up to USD 1 million per financial year, with Form 145 and a chartered accountant's certificate in Form 146.
