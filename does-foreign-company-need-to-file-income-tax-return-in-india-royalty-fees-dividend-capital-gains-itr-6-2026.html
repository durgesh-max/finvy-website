Does a Foreign Company Have to File an Income-Tax Return in India? (2026) Royalty, Service Fees, Dividends, Share Sales, Branches and the 31 December Belated Date, when TDS is the end of the matter, and when it is not.

A foreign company must file an Indian income-tax return for any year in which it has income taxable in India, unless a narrow exemption applies. The exemption covers a company whose only Indian income is dividends, interest, royalties or technical service fees, and only where tax was deducted at the full domestic rate. If tax was deducted at a lower treaty rate, the cautious and usual practice is to file. A return is also needed for a branch, project office or other permanent establishment, for capital gains on Indian shares or assets, and to claim a refund of excess TDS. TDS deducted by the Indian customer does not by itself remove the duty. For the year to 31 March 2026, a company that has not yet filed can still file a belated return by 31 December 2026.

/ How filing works

Forms, dates and what you need.

  • Form: companies, including foreign companies, file ITR-6 online.
  • Due date: 31 October after the financial year ends on 31 March. It is 30 November if a transfer-pricing report is required. For FY 2025-26, audit cases were extended to 21 November 2026.
  • Late filing: a belated return can be filed until 31 December following the year, with a late fee of INR 5,000 (INR 1,000 if total income is up to INR 5 lakh) and interest on any tax due. See belated returns.
  • What you need: an Indian PAN, registration on the income-tax portal, a digital signature for the authorised signatory, the TDS statement (Form 26AS / AIS), contracts and invoices, and a tax residency certificate with Form 41 if a treaty is claimed.
  • Transfer pricing: a foreign company that earns royalty, fees or interest from its own Indian subsidiary is itself within the transfer-pricing report requirement for that income. See the transfer-pricing report.
  • Rates: royalties, service fees, interest and dividends at the treaty or domestic rate on the gross amount; business income of a branch or permanent establishment at 35% plus surcharge and cess on net profit.

/ Practical points

What goes wrong.

  • “Our customer deducted tax, so we are done”: true only if the full domestic rate was used and that is your only Indian income.
  • Dividends from an Indian subsidiary at the treaty rate: the parent is usually expected to file.
  • Mismatch notices: the tax department sees payments to you in the payer’s TDS filings. A PAN with reported income and no return draws a notice.
  • Refunds: where a customer deducted at 20% plus surcharge and cess although a 10% or 15% treaty rate applied, the excess comes back only through a return.
  • Past years: if returns were missed, review the position before a notice arrives; options narrow with time.
  • Liaison offices file an annual statement and not an income return. See liaison and branch office annual filings.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Is a foreign company required to file an income-tax return in India?
Yes, for any year in which it has income taxable in India, unless its only Indian income is dividends, interest, royalties or technical service fees and tax was deducted at the full domestic rate. A branch, permanent establishment, capital gains or a refund claim always needs a return.
If TDS has been deducted, does a foreign company still need to file?
Often yes. TDS alone does not remove the duty. The exemption applies only to specified income taxed at source at the full domestic rate; where a lower treaty rate was used, the usual practice is to file.
Which ITR form does a foreign company file in India?
ITR-6, filed online with a digital signature. The company needs an Indian PAN and registration on the income-tax portal.
What is the due date for a foreign company's return in India?
31 October after the financial year, or 30 November where a transfer-pricing report is required. A belated return can be filed until 31 December; for FY 2025-26 that is 31 December 2026.
Does a foreign parent receiving dividends from its Indian subsidiary have to file?
If tax was deducted at a treaty rate lower than the domestic rate, the parent is usually expected to file a return. If the full domestic rate was deducted and that is its only Indian income, the exemption applies.
Does a liaison office file an income-tax return?
A liaison office earns no income in India, so it files an annual statement of its activities and not an income return.
