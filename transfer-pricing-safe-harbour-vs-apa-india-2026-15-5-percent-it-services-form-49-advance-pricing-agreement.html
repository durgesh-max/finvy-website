Transfer Pricing Safe Harbour vs APA in India (2026): 15.5% IT Services Margin, Form 49 and Advance Pricing Agreements, certainty for your Indian subsidiary, two ways.

An Indian subsidiary can lock in its transfer pricing in two ways: opt for the safe harbour, or sign an advance pricing agreement (APA) with the tax department. From tax year 2026-27, a single IT services safe harbour (software development, ITeS, KPO and software contract R&D) accepts an operating profit of at least 15.5% of operating expenses, for eligible companies up to INR 2,000 crore, chosen once in Form 49 for five tax years. It is cheap and quick, but one-sided: the parent’s country is not bound and treaty dispute resolution is not available. An APA covers up to five future years plus four years back, can be bilateral with the parent’s country, and suits larger or complex transactions; the fee is INR 20 lakh and unilateral IT services APAs now have a two-year timeline. CBDT signed a record 219 APAs in FY 2025-26.

/ Quick answers

The questions foreign parents ask first.

  • What is the transfer pricing safe harbour in India? A set of margins and rates the tax department must accept for specified related-party transactions if the company opts in (section 167 of the Income-tax Act, 2025; Rules 86 to 102 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026).
  • What is the safe harbour margin for IT services in 2026? 15.5% of operating expenses, for software development, ITeS, KPO and software contract R&D together. Earlier margins were 17% to 24% with a INR 300 crore limit.
  • What is an advance pricing agreement? A binding agreement with the Central Board of Direct Taxes on the pricing method (and often the margin) for future years, and optionally past years (section 168).
  • Which is better? Safe harbour for a cost-plus captive whose margin is already at or above 15.5% and whose parent’s country will accept that price. APA for bigger, unusual or two-country problems: royalties, distribution, intangibles, or where double tax is the real risk.
  • When must Form 49 be filed? On or before the due date of the return for the first year of the option (for companies with transfer pricing reports, 30 November after the tax year).

  • Operating expenses include salaries, ESOP costs, reimbursements and depreciation, and exclude interest, exchange losses and income tax. Cost reimbursed by the parent without mark-up still counts in the base.
  • Not available where the associated enterprise is in a notified jurisdiction or a territory with no or low tax.
  • No treaty relief: a transaction accepted under safe harbour cannot go to the mutual agreement procedure, so if the parent’s tax authority says 15.5% is too high, India will not adjust.
  • Paperwork continues: transfer pricing documentation and the accountant’s report (Form 48, formerly Form 3CEB) are still required.
  • Check the rule text for your facts: commentators differ on how the INR 2,000 crore limit is measured, and the non-IT categories have their own procedure before the Assessing Officer.

/ Which to choose

Safe harbour, APA or annual benchmarking.

  • If your captive already earns 15.5% or more on cost and the parent’s country accepts that mark-up, the safe harbour removes Indian audit risk for five years at almost no cost.
  • If your benchmark supports less than 15.5%, safe harbour means paying tax on a higher profit than needed; compare the extra tax with the cost of defending a lower margin.
  • A US parent should check the US side: a higher Indian mark-up is a higher US deduction, which the IRS can question. A bilateral APA solves both sides.
  • Groups near the global minimum tax threshold should model the effect on the Indian effective rate; see Pillar Two in India.

/ Steps

Electing the IT services safe harbour.

  • 1. Check eligibility: activity within the IT services definition, associated enterprise not in an excluded territory, within the INR 2,000 crore limit.
  • 2. Compute the margin on the rules’ operating expense base for the current year and forecast the next four.
  • 3. Align the intercompany agreement so the parent pays cost plus at least 15.5%, with year-end true-ups.
  • 4. File Form 49 electronically by the return due date of the first year, certified as the rules require.
  • 5. Keep documentation and file Form 48 and, where applicable, the master file each year.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is the safe harbour margin for IT services in India from 2026?
At least 15.5% of operating expenses for software development, ITeS, KPO and software contract R&D, from tax year 2026-27, for eligible companies up to INR 2,000 crore. One Form 49 covers five consecutive tax years.
What is an advance pricing agreement in India?
A binding agreement between the taxpayer and the Central Board of Direct Taxes on the transfer pricing method for up to five future tax years, with an option to roll it back to four earlier years. It can be unilateral, bilateral or multilateral.
What is the APA fee in India in 2026?
INR 20 lakh flat under the Income-tax Rules, 2026, regardless of transaction value.
How long does an APA take in India?
Unilateral APAs for IT services are now on a fast track: if not concluded within two years from the end of the quarter of application (extendable by six months), the application is treated as closed. Other APAs have historically taken several years.
Can we use the mutual agreement procedure if we opt for safe harbour?
No. Transactions accepted under safe harbour cannot be taken to the mutual agreement procedure, so the parent's country is not bound by the Indian price. A bilateral APA gives two-country certainty.
Is safe harbour or APA better for a GCC in India?
For a cost-plus captive already earning 15.5% or more on cost, safe harbour is usually simplest. For large captives, mixed functions, intangibles or where double tax is the concern, an APA is usually better.
