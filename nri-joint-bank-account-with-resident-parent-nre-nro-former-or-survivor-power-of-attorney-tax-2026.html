/ NRIs with parents or close relatives in India · FEMA rules on joint accounts · Updated 2026-11-28

NRI Joint Bank Account With a Resident Parent (2026): NRE, NRO and Resident Savings Accounts, Former or Survivor, Power of Attorney and Tax on Interest, who can operate, and who owns the money.

An NRI can hold an NRE or FCNR(B) account jointly with a resident close relative, and a resident parent can add an NRI child to a resident savings account - but only on a ‘former or survivor’ basis. In each case the second holder cannot operate the account while the first holder is alive, except a resident acting under a power of attorney on an NRE account. Interest is taxed in the hands of the person whose money it is, not simply the first name on the passbook.

  • Close relative: as defined in the Companies Act - parents, spouse, children, siblings and certain in-laws.
  • Bank practice: banks can apply stricter terms; check the mode of operation in writing when opening.

/ Tax, control and succession

What families get wrong.

  • Whose money is it: interest is taxed in the hands of the person who contributed the funds; adding a name does not move income for tax.
  • NRE interest: exempt only while the account holder is non-resident; a resident joint holder does not change that.
  • TDS: banks deduct TDS based on the first holder’s status; NRO interest for non-residents carries higher TDS, which can be adjusted in the return or reduced under a DTAA.
  • Operating for a parent: a power of attorney or bank mandate is often better than a joint account if the aim is paying bills.
  • On death: the survivor gets access, but other legal heirs may still claim the money under succession law - a will and nominations keep it clean.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Can an NRI open a joint account with a resident parent?
Yes. An NRI can hold an NRE or FCNR(B) account jointly with a resident close relative such as a parent on a former or survivor basis, and an NRO account can also be held jointly with a resident. The resident can operate the NRE account during the NRI's lifetime only as a power of attorney holder.
Can an NRI be a joint holder in a parent's resident savings account?
Yes, banks may add an NRI close relative as joint holder on a former or survivor basis. The NRI cannot operate the account while the parent is alive, and if the NRI becomes the survivor, the account is converted into an NRO account.
What does former or survivor mean?
Only the first-named holder (the former) operates the account during their lifetime. The second holder can operate it only after the first holder's death.
Who pays tax on interest in a joint account?
The person whose money is in the account. Adding a joint holder does not shift the income. NRE interest is exempt only while the holder is non-resident, and NRO interest for non-residents carries TDS.
Is a joint account better than a power of attorney for helping parents?
Not always. A joint account affects ownership and succession; a power of attorney or bank mandate lets you operate without changing ownership. The right choice depends on what you want to achieve.
