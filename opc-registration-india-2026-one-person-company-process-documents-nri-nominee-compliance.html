OPC Registration in India (2026): One Person Company Process, Documents, Nominee, NRI Owners and Compliance, one owner, limited liability, and a company you can grow.

A One Person Company (OPC) is a private company with a single shareholder, registered online with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs through the SPICe+ form on the MCA V3 portal. Since 1 April 2021 any Indian citizen, whether resident in India or not, can form one; you must name an Indian-citizen nominee who takes over if you die or become incapable, and appoint at least one director. The old caps of INR 50 lakh paid-up capital and INR 2 crore turnover were removed, so an OPC can grow and convert into a private or public company at any time. It needs a statutory audit and yearly filings, but no annual general meeting.

/ Quick answers

What solo founders ask.

  • Who can form an OPC? One natural person who is an Indian citizen, resident in India or not. A person can be a member of only one OPC, and foreign nationals cannot form one.
  • Can an NRI register an OPC? Yes, since 2021. But every company needs at least one director who stays in India for 182 days in the financial year, so an NRI owner usually appoints a resident director as well.
  • What is the nominee? An Indian citizen named in the memorandum who becomes the member if the owner dies or cannot act; the nominee gives written consent in Form INC-3 and can be changed later.
  • Is there a minimum capital? No minimum capital, and since 2021 no upper limit on capital or turnover that forces conversion.
  • OPC, LLP or private limited? An OPC suits one owner; for two or more founders or outside investors, a private limited company or LLP fits better. See LLP vs private limited.

/ After registration

What an OPC must keep up.

  • Board meetings: at least one in each half of the calendar year, with a gap of at least 90 days; an OPC with only one director is exempt and records its decisions in the minutes book instead.
  • No AGM: an OPC is exempt from holding an annual general meeting.
  • Audit: a statutory audit by a chartered accountant every year, whatever the turnover.
  • Filings: financial statements in Form AOC-4 within 180 days of the year end, the annual return in Form MGT-7A, and the director KYC.
  • Tax: the OPC files a company income tax return and is taxed as a company, about 25% under the concessional regime; the owner pays tax on salary or dividends taken out.
  • Growing: convert into a private limited company when you add shareholders; see founder salary vs dividend.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

How do I register a One Person Company in India?
Get a DSC, reserve the name through SPICe+ Part A or RUN, file SPICe+ Part B on the MCA V3 portal with the e-MOA, e-AOA, nominee consent in Form INC-3 and the AGILE-PRO-S form, and receive the certificate of incorporation with CIN, PAN and TAN.
What documents are needed for OPC registration?
PAN (or passport for documents from abroad), address proof and a photograph of the member, director and nominee, the nominee's consent in Form INC-3, proof of the registered office with the owner's no-objection or a rent agreement, and DSCs for those signing.
Can an NRI register an OPC in India?
Yes. Since 1 April 2021 any Indian citizen, resident or not, can form an OPC. The company still needs at least one director who stays in India for 182 days in the financial year, so an NRI owner usually appoints a resident director.
Is there a capital or turnover limit for an OPC?
No. The INR 50 lakh paid-up capital and INR 2 crore turnover caps were removed in 2021, there is no minimum capital, and an OPC can convert into a private or public company at any time.
What are the annual compliances of an OPC?
At least one board meeting in each half of the calendar year (unless the OPC has only one director), a statutory audit, financial statements in Form AOC-4 within 180 days of the year end, the annual return in Form MGT-7A, director KYC and the company income tax return. No annual general meeting is needed.
