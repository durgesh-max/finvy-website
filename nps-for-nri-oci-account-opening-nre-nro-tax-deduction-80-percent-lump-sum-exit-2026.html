NPS for NRIs and OCI Cardholders (2026): Opening an Account From Abroad, NRE or NRO Funding, Tax, and the New 80% Lump Sum at Exit, useful only if you have Indian income to save tax on, or plan to retire in India.

NRIs and OCI cardholders can open a National Pension System (NPS) Tier I account from abroad, funded from an NRE or NRO account. Contributions from an NRE account can later be sent back abroad. Since December 2025, a non-government subscriber can take up to 80% of the corpus as a lump sum at exit, with at least 20% buying an annuity. But Indian tax law still exempts only 60%, so the extra 20% is taxable at slab rates until the law changes. The annuity is taxed as income. The extra INR 50,000 deduction for your own contributions works only under the old tax regime and only if you have taxable income in India. Your country of residence may tax the account too. NPS suits an NRI with Indian income or plans to retire in India; for others, check the home-country tax first.

  • Before you choose 80%: taking the full 80% adds the extra 20% to your Indian income in that year. Spreading the exit or taking less can cost less tax.
  • Old or new regime: the deduction for your own contributions is old-regime only; see which benefits NRIs can use.
  • US residents: NPS is a foreign pension arrangement for US tax and may need annual reporting; take advice before contributing.
  • UK residents: NPS is not a UK-registered pension, so UK relief does not apply; growth and withdrawals need checking under UK rules.
  • Returning to India: the account continues; your status changes on record with the bank and NPS. See returning to India.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Can an NRI open an NPS account?
Yes. NRIs can open an NPS Tier I account, funded from an NRE or NRO account, and complete KYC from abroad. Tier II is not available to NRIs.
Can OCI cardholders invest in NPS?
Yes, under current PFRDA rules OCI cardholders can join NPS Tier I, funded from an NRE or NRO account.
How much can I withdraw from NPS at 60 now?
A non-government subscriber can take up to 80% of the corpus as a lump sum, with at least 20% used to buy an annuity, under PFRDA's December 2025 amendment. Government subscribers remain at 60:40.
Is the NPS lump sum tax-free?
Only 60% of the corpus is exempt under current Indian tax law. Commentary indicates the extra 20% now allowed is taxable at slab rates until the law is amended. The annuity is taxed as income.
Can an NRI claim tax deduction on NPS?
Yes, if the NRI has taxable income in India and uses the old regime, including the extra INR 50,000 deduction for own contributions. Under the new regime that deduction is not available.
What happens to NPS if an NRI becomes a foreign citizen?
If the person gives up Indian citizenship and does not hold an OCI card, the account is closed and the balance paid to the NRO account.
Can BQP advise on NPS while I live abroad?
Yes, on tax and FEMA, not on product choice.
