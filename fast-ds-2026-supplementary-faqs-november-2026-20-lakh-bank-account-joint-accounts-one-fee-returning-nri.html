/ FAST-DS 2026 · New FAQs · Updated 2026-11-21

FAST-DS 2026 Supplementary FAQs (November 2026): Bank Accounts up to INR 20 Lakh, Joint Accounts, One INR 1 Lakh Fee and Returning NRIs, 43 new answers, and what they change for you.

The Income Tax Department has published 43 more FAQs on FAST-DS 2026 (questions 51 to 93), reported on 20 and 21 November 2026. The headline points: foreign bank accounts that together never held more than INR 20 lakh in the year, funded only from taxed money, need no declaration. Each joint holder of a foreign account must declare for themselves, even if only one person put money in. The INR 1 lakh fee is paid once, for all eligible assets and all years. An asset bought partly from untaxed money goes under the 60% route. People who are now non-resident can still declare what they should have reported while resident. The last date has not moved: 31 December 2026, and only one Form 1 can stand, so it has to be right.

/ Valuation and filing

Details that affect the amount.

  • Dollar rate: values in US dollars are converted at INR 94.6543, the RBI reference rate of 30 March 2026, because none was published on 31 March 2026 (Q55).
  • Joint accounts and the INR 5 crore limit: the limit is tested separately for each person on their own Form 1 (Q59).
  • Property and the 20% tolerance: if you use a recognised valuer’s report and the tax officer later arrives at a value up to 20% higher, the declaration stays valid. If you use indexed cost instead, that figure is final (Q76 to Q78).
  • Gifted or inherited assets: the cost is what the donor paid (Q80).
  • Fractional shares: valued in proportion to a full share (Q73).
  • No double counting: untaxed income and the asset bought with it are not both charged (Q72).
  • One Form 1: all assets and income go in a single consolidated form. It can be refiled only until Form 2 is issued (Q84, Q86).
  • Company accounts: a director, employee or authorised signatory may have to declare a company’s foreign account where the Black Money Act reporting conditions are met (Q60, Q61).
  • Search or survey: does not bar the scheme unless a Black Money Act assessment is already complete or money-laundering proceedings apply (Q88).
  • Confidentiality: a declaration is treated like a tax return, and declared assets are not added to income in any assessment (Q74, Q75).

/ What to do now

The window closes on 31 December.

  • List every foreign account, share, plan and property you held while resident in India, and check each against Schedule FA of your returns.
  • For bank accounts, find the highest balance in each year and where the money came from. That decides between no declaration, the INR 1 lakh route and the 60% route.
  • Joint holders should each plan their own Form 1.
  • Order valuation reports early for property and unlisted shares.
  • Compare with the alternatives where the 60% route applies: see FAST-DS vs revised or updated return and no foreign tax credit under the 60% route.
  • File well before 31 December 2026 so there is time to correct Form 1 before Form 2 is issued.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Do I need a FAST-DS declaration for a foreign bank account below INR 20 lakh?
Not if the aggregate balance never exceeded INR 20 lakh at any time in the year and all deposits came from disclosed, taxed sources (supplementary FAQ 67). A declaration is needed if the account holds undisclosed income or unexplained deposits.
In a joint foreign account, who has to declare under FAST-DS?
Each joint holder who was required to report the account declares separately, even if only one of them funded it. The value is split by ascertainable share, or equally where shares are not fixed (FAQs 56 to 58).
Is the FAST-DS fee of INR 1 lakh charged per asset or per year?
Neither. It is a single flat fee for all eligible assets and all years in one declaration, up to an aggregate value of INR 5 crore (FAQs 82 and 83).
Can an NRI use FAST-DS?
Yes, if the person was resident in India in the years when the foreign asset should have been reported. A person who has since become non-resident remains eligible (FAQ 62).
What is the dollar conversion rate for FAST-DS?
INR 94.6543 per US dollar, the RBI reference rate of 30 March 2026, since no rate was published on 31 March 2026 (FAQ 55).
Can I correct Form 1 after filing?
Yes, by filing a fresh Form 1 before Form 2 is issued. Once Form 2 has been issued accepting the declaration, another Form 1 cannot be filed (FAQs 84 to 86).
Has the FAST-DS last date been extended?
No. Declarations close on 31 December 2026 and no extension had been announced as of 21 November 2026.
