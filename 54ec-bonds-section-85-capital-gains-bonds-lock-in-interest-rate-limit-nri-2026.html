/ Property sellers and NRIs · Capital gains · Updated 2026-11-25

54EC Bonds (2026): Capital Gains Bonds Under New Section 85, Lock-in, Interest Rate, Rs 50 Lakh Limit and NRIs, six months to invest, five years to hold.

54EC bonds, called capital gains bonds, let you save tax on a long-term gain from selling land or a building. From 1 April 2026 the exemption sits in section 85 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (it was section 54EC). You invest the gain, not the full sale price, in specified bonds within six months of the sale, up to ₹50 lakh in a tax year, and hold them for five years. The interest, reported at about 5.25% a year on current issues, is taxable. Residents and NRIs can both use it.

/ Quick answers

What sellers ask most.

  • What is the lock-in period? Five years from the date of allotment. Selling, transferring or taking a loan against the bonds in that time brings the exempt gain back to tax.
  • What is the time limit? Invest within six months of the date of transfer of the property. The six months run from the sale, not from the end of the year or the return due date.
  • How much can I invest? Up to ₹50 lakh in a tax year, across all issuers together. Bonds are issued in units of ₹10,000.
  • What is the interest rate? Current issues are reported at about 5.25% a year, paid annually. The interest is fully taxable at your slab rate.
  • Which gains qualify? Only long-term capital gains on land, a building or both. Gains on shares, mutual funds, gold or short-term property sales do not.
  • Can NRIs invest? Yes, NRIs can claim the exemption on property sold in India. Tax is deducted on the bond interest paid to NRIs.

Exemption is proportionate: if you invest only part of the gain, only that part is exempt. A gain above ₹50 lakh can be covered by combining section 85 with the house exemption in section 82 (formerly 54); see section 54 vs 54F vs 54EC.

/ For NRIs selling property in India

Bonds, TDS and timing.

  • Buyer’s TDS: the buyer deducts tax on the gain at the time of sale whether or not you plan to buy bonds. To deduct less, apply for a lower TDS certificate (Form 128, formerly Form 13) before the sale and show the planned bond investment.
  • Refund route: if full TDS was deducted, invest in bonds within six months and claim the exemption and the refund in your Indian return.
  • Paying for the bonds: NRIs usually invest from their NRO account, where the sale proceeds land; check the issuer’s NRI application rules.
  • Repatriation: on maturity, the principal and interest can be moved abroad within the NRO repatriation rules with Forms 145 and 146 (formerly 15CA and 15CB). See NRI property sale tax.
  • Residence country: the gain may also be taxable where you live; check the treaty and your foreign tax credit.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is the lock-in period for 54EC bonds?
Five years from the date of allotment. If you sell or transfer the bonds, or take a loan against them, within five years, the capital gain that was exempted becomes taxable.
What is the time limit to invest in 54EC bonds?
Within six months of the date of transfer of the land or building. From 1 April 2026 the exemption is in section 85 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, with the same six-month window.
What is the maximum investment in 54EC bonds?
Rs 50 lakh in a tax year across all issuers together. A gain above that can be covered by also buying a house under section 82 (formerly 54) where the conditions are met.
Is the interest on 54EC bonds taxable?
Yes. Current issues are reported at about 5.25% a year, and the interest is taxable at your slab rate. Only the capital gain invested is exempt.
Can an NRI invest in 54EC bonds?
Yes. An NRI selling land or a building in India can invest the long-term gain in section 85 bonds within six months and claim the exemption. Tax is deducted on interest paid to NRIs, and a lower TDS certificate can reduce the buyer's TDS on the sale.
