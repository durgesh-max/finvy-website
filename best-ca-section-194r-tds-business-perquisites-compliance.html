/ Section 194R core rule

The 10% + aggregation.

  • Any person (business / profession) giving benefit / perquisite to resident recipient (business / profession receiver) must deduct 10% TDS.
  • Threshold: INR 20,000 per FY per recipient (aggregated).
  • Perquisite / benefit forms: free samples, free equipment / vehicles / electronics, conference / travel sponsorship, hospitality, awards, prizes, specific free services.
  • Deductor: payer (business providing benefit).
  • TDS at 10% on fair market value of benefit (not just cash cost).
  • Form 26Q quarterly return + Form 16A to recipient.
  • Section 206AA: 20% rate if recipient PAN unavailable.

/ Common 194R trigger scenarios

Where it applies.

  • Pharma marketing to doctors: samples + conference sponsorship + equipment loans + hospitality. Primary 194R target when introduced 2022.
  • Tech / SaaS to beta customers: free API credits, free POCs, free equipment loans, hackathon prizes.
  • Consumer goods to influencers / content creators: free products, sponsored travel, event tickets.
  • Banking + financial services to intermediaries: referral rewards, free training programmes, travel incentives.
  • Insurance to agents: incentive trips, equipment grants, free training.
  • Platform / marketplace to sellers: free promotional campaigns, equipment grants, specific free services.
  • Agencies to clients: free upgrades, promotional additions beyond contracted scope.

/ Who's exempt

The carve-outs.

  • Benefits to resident SALARIED employee: Section 192 TDS applies instead (salary perquisite route).
  • Benefits in cash: Section 194R applies if cash > INR 20K / FY. For non-business cash gifts: Section 56(2)(x).
  • Discounts + rebates in course of trade: typically not 194R (reduces sale value, not separate benefit).
  • Specific entity benefits: company to subsidiary own-group benefits may not be 194R-triggered (specific CBDT clarifications).
  • Below INR 20K / recipient / FY aggregate threshold: no 194R.

/ Compliance workflow

The operating playbook.

  1. Identify all benefit-giving activities + recipients.
  2. Fair market value estimation for non-cash benefits (equipment, hospitality, travel, prizes).
  3. Recipient PAN collection + verification.
  4. Section 206AA 20% rate if PAN missing - decide: collect PAN or absorb higher rate.
  5. TDS deposit via Challan ITNS 281 within 7 days of month-end.
  6. Form 26Q quarterly return.
  7. Form 16A to each recipient quarterly.
  8. Recipient-side: Section 194R benefit treated as income under 'Business / Profession' or 'Other Sources' depending on nature; must disclose in ITR.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What's Section 194R and when does it apply?
10% TDS on business perquisites / benefits given by payer to resident recipient during business / profession. Threshold: INR 20,000 per FY per recipient. Applies to free samples, equipment loans, conference sponsorship, hospitality, awards, prizes above threshold. Introduced Finance Act 2022, effective 1 July 2022.
Does Section 194R apply to employee bonus / perquisite?
No - employee perquisites are under Section 192 TDS (salary perquisite route). Section 194R specifically for business / profession benefits (payer giving benefit to another business or professional person) - NOT employer giving to employee.
How to value non-cash benefits for 194R?
Fair market value. For equipment: current purchase price or market rate. For hospitality: actual cost. For travel: actual cost of ticket + accommodation. For prizes: declared / announced value. Deductor's responsibility to document FMV methodology; defensible to AO scrutiny.
What if recipient refuses to provide PAN?
Section 206AA applies: 20% TDS rate (vs 10% standard). Can either collect PAN or absorb higher rate. For material benefit values, PAN collection usually easier than 20% hit.
Does 194R apply to discounts and trade margins?
Generally no - discounts / rebates in course of ordinary trade reduce sale consideration, not separate benefit. CBDT clarifications (Circulars 2022-2023) specifically address this. Documentary trail (sale invoice reflecting discount) important to defend non-194R classification.
