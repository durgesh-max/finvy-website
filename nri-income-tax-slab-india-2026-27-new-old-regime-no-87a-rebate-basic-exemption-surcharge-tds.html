/ NRIs and OCI cardholders · Indian income tax · Updated 2026-11-25

NRI Income Tax Slab in India 2026-27: New and Old Regime Rates, Why There Is No 87A Rebate, Basic Exemption, Surcharge and TDS, same slabs as residents, but no Rs 12 lakh rebate.

NRIs pay income tax in India at the same slab rates as residents, but they cannot claim the section 87A rebate, so the tax-free income of up to ₹12 lakh under the new regime does not apply to them. Under the new regime (the default), tax starts above ₹4 lakh of Indian income; under the old regime it starts above ₹2.5 lakh, whatever the NRI’s age. Capital gains are taxed at their own rates, and the TDS deducted on NRI income is often more than the final tax, which is why many NRIs file a return to claim a refund.

/ What is different for NRIs

Rebate, exemption, special rates and TDS.

  • No 87A rebate: the rebate (section 156 of the Income-tax Act, 2025) is for resident individuals only, so an NRI with ₹10 lakh of Indian income pays tax under both regimes; see what NRIs are eligible for.
  • No senior-citizen uplift: the higher old-regime exemptions of ₹3 lakh (60 to 80) and ₹5 lakh (80+) are for residents; NRIs stay at ₹2.5 lakh.
  • Only Indian income is taxed: income earned and received outside India is not taxed in India for an NRI; check your status with the residential status calculator.
  • Special rates: capital gains on shares, mutual funds and property are taxed at their own rates, not the slabs; see NRI mutual fund tax.
  • NRE interest is exempt; NRO interest is taxable; see NRE, NRO and FCNR rules.
  • TDS is often higher than the tax: payers deduct TDS under section 195 (now section 393), often at flat rates; see section 195 to 393 and the NRO interest TDS refund calculator.

/ Filing and treaty relief

When to file and how to cut the tax.

  • File a return if Indian income exceeds the basic exemption, to claim a refund of excess TDS, or to carry forward losses; NRIs use ITR-2 or ITR-3, not ITR-1; see which ITR an NRI should file.
  • Treaty rates: a DTAA can cap tax on interest, dividends and royalties with a tax residency certificate; see what is a DTAA.
  • Choose the regime: the old regime still allows 80C, 80D and home-loan interest; run the new vs old regime calculator.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is the income tax slab for NRIs in India?
The same as for residents: under the new regime nil up to Rs 4 lakh, then 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25% and 30% above Rs 24 lakh; under the old regime nil up to Rs 2.5 lakh, 5%, 20% and 30% above Rs 10 lakh, plus surcharge and 4% cess.
Is income up to Rs 12 lakh tax-free for NRIs?
No. The Rs 12 lakh tax-free limit comes from the section 87A rebate, which is available only to resident individuals. An NRI pays tax on Indian income above Rs 4 lakh under the new regime.
Do senior citizen NRIs get a higher basic exemption?
No. The higher old-regime exemptions of Rs 3 lakh and Rs 5 lakh apply only to resident senior and super senior citizens; NRIs of any age have a Rs 2.5 lakh basic exemption under the old regime.
Which income of an NRI is taxable in India?
Only income that arises, accrues or is received in India, such as rent, NRO interest, dividends, capital gains on Indian assets and salary for work done in India. NRE interest is exempt.
Can an NRI choose the old tax regime?
Yes. The new regime is the default, but an NRI can opt for the old regime in the return if deductions such as 80C, 80D and home-loan interest make it cheaper.
Why is TDS on NRI income higher than the tax?
Payers deduct TDS on payments to NRIs at prescribed rates without considering the slabs or the basic exemption, so the TDS is often more than the final tax; the excess is refunded when the NRI files ITR-2 or ITR-3.
