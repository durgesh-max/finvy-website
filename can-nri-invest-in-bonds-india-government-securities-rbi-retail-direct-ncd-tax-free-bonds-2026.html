Can an NRI Invest in Bonds in India? (2026) Government Securities and T-Bills via RBI Retail Direct, Corporate Bonds and NCDs, Tax-Free Bonds - and What Is Not Allowed, what you can buy, through which account, and how the interest is taxed.

Yes, mostly. NRIs can buy government securities, state development loans and treasury bills, including directly through RBI Retail Direct. That needs a Retail Direct Gilt account linked to an Indian bank account, usually NRO, plus a PAN and KYC. NRIs can also buy listed corporate bonds and NCDs, subject to FEMA and the offer terms, and tax-free bonds on the stock exchange. Tax-free bond interest is exempt. Not allowed: RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds, which are for residents only; new sovereign gold bonds; and new NSCs. Certificates bought while you were resident can continue to maturity. Interest is taxed in India, TDS is deducted for non-residents, and treaty rates can reduce it if you give a TRC and Form 10F (Form 41 from 1 April 2026). Listed bonds held for more than 12 months are taxed at 12.5% on the gain, while gains on unlisted bonds and debentures are taxed at slab rates.

/ Tax and practical points

Before you invest.

  • Repatriation: investments made from an NRE account are generally repatriable; NRO-funded investments go back to NRO and abroad within the USD 1 million facility.
  • TDS on interest: payers deduct tax on interest paid to non-residents; give a tax residency certificate and Form 10F for the treaty rate (for example 12.5% for UAE residents on interest, as summarised in the India-UAE guide).
  • Capital gains: listed bonds held over 12 months: 12.5% without indexation; unlisted bonds and debentures: slab rates whatever the holding period.
  • Refunds: excess TDS comes back only through an Indian return; see NRO interest refund calculator.
  • US persons: individual bonds are generally not PFICs, unlike Indian mutual funds, but report accounts in FBAR and Form 8938.
  • GIFT City: dollar bonds and deposits in GIFT IFSC are another route; see GIFT City for NRIs.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Can an NRI invest in government bonds in India?
Yes. NRIs eligible under FEMA can buy government securities, state development loans and treasury bills, including directly through RBI Retail Direct.
Can an NRI open an RBI Retail Direct account?
Yes. Non-resident retail investors eligible to invest in government securities under FEMA can open a Retail Direct Gilt account linked to an Indian bank account (usually NRO), with PAN and KYC.
Can an NRI invest in RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds?
No. The Floating Rate Savings Bonds 2020 (Taxable) are open to resident individuals and HUFs only.
Can an NRI invest in corporate bonds or NCDs?
Generally yes for listed corporate bonds and NCDs, subject to FEMA and the terms of the issue; check whether a public issue accepts NRI applications.
Can an NRI buy NSC?
No new NSC can be bought by an NRI; certificates bought while resident can be held to maturity.
