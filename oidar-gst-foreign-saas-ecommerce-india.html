OIDAR GST, what every foreign SaaS selling to India needs.

If you're a US / UK / EU / Singapore SaaS company and you have paying Indian B2C customers (individuals, not GST-registered businesses), you likely need an OIDAR GST registration. 18% IGST on every rupee. Netflix, Spotify, Zoom, Adobe, Microsoft, Shopify — all registered. Many smaller SaaS companies miss this and accumulate liability. Here's the complete OIDAR framework.

/ What is OIDAR

Section 2(17) + 2(118).

OIDAR = Online Information Database Access and Retrieval services. Defined under Section 2(17) of the IGST Act:

  • Services whose delivery is mediated by information technology over the internet or electronic network.
  • Essentially automated with minimal human intervention.
  • Impossible to supply without information technology.

Examples of OIDAR services:

  • SaaS subscriptions (Zoom, Dropbox, Figma, Notion, Slack).
  • Online advertising (Google Ads, Meta Ads, LinkedIn Ads purchased by Indian B2C customer).
  • Cloud storage + computing (AWS, GCP, Azure B2C).
  • E-books, music, video streaming (Spotify, Netflix, YouTube Premium).
  • Mobile app subscriptions (gaming, productivity).
  • Online learning platforms (Coursera, Udemy B2C).
  • Digital advertising services, website hosting, online data retrieval.

NOT OIDAR: online consultancy with real human CA / consultant on the other end (that's regular Section 2(118) service), remote training with live instructor, physical goods shipped internationally, B2B services where the Indian recipient is GST-registered (reverse charge applies — no OIDAR obligation on foreign supplier).

/ Who must register

B2C to Indian customers.

OIDAR GST registration is mandatory (no threshold) if ALL of these are true:

  • You are a foreign supplier (incorporated / based outside India).
  • Services qualify as OIDAR under Section 2(17).
  • Recipient is a non-taxable online recipient — i.e., an Indian B2C customer (individual not registered for GST, or Indian entity not registered for GST, or Indian government body).

B2B exception: if your Indian customer is GST-registered (business), they are deemed to self-assess under reverse charge mechanism (Section 5(3) IGST). No GST obligation on foreign supplier for B2B. But practically, most SaaS companies have mix of B2B + B2C — and must register once any B2C paying customer exists.

Finance Act 2023 broadened OIDAR definition (removed 'essentially automated' requirement for some sub-categories) — even more foreign suppliers now in scope.

/ Registration + compliance workflow

What foreign SaaS must do.

  1. Appoint representative in India (recommended, not mandatory): a GST-registered Indian consultant / CA firm as 'authorised representative' for the OIDAR registration. BQP handles this role.
  2. Register under OIDAR: online via GST portal (ggsp.gst.gov.in or regular GST portal, specific OIDAR category). Documents: company incorporation, passport of directors, bank account, business address proof.
  3. GSTIN issued: specific format for OIDAR registrants (begins with '9900' or similar).
  4. Charge 18% IGST on every invoice to Indian B2C customer. Shown on customer invoice; collected via payment gateway.
  5. Monthly / quarterly return: Form GSTR-9A (OIDAR annual return) + monthly statement. Pay collected IGST to Indian government by 20th of next month.
  6. IGST payment: via NEFT/RTGS to designated government account. In practice, Indian representative handles payment on behalf of foreign supplier.

/ Common OIDAR mistakes + risks

What foreign SaaS gets wrong.

  • Not registering at all: assuming 'we don't have an Indian entity, so no GST applies'. Wrong — OIDAR applies even without Indian presence. Non-registration = tax demand under Section 74 (fraud / wilful suppression) up to 5 years back + 100% penalty.
  • Not distinguishing B2B vs B2C: Indian B2B customer should provide their GSTIN for self-assessment (reverse charge). Foreign supplier should capture GSTIN at checkout. Without GSTIN capture, every sale defaults to B2C → 18% OIDAR GST obligation.
  • Charging INR-denominated vs USD-denominated: GST is on supply value. If invoice is in USD, convert at RBI reference rate for the day. Document FX conversion rate.
  • Credit note / refund processing errors: refunds reduce taxable supply but need proper credit-note documentation under GST invoice rules.
  • Missed Form OIDAR-9 / GSTR-9A filing: annual return separate from monthly compliance. Late fee + penalty.
  • No Indian representative: makes tax officer communication / notice response very difficult. Appointment of Indian authorised representative is standard best practice.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is OIDAR GST?
Online Information Database Access and Retrieval service tax under GST. Foreign suppliers of digital services (SaaS, streaming, cloud, online ads, e-books) to Indian B2C customers (non-GST-registered individuals / businesses) must register, charge 18% IGST, and remit to Indian government. Introduced under Section 2(17) IGST Act. Netflix, Spotify, Zoom, Adobe are all OIDAR-registered.
Is there a threshold for OIDAR registration?
No threshold - mandatory registration for every foreign supplier of OIDAR services to Indian B2C customers from the first rupee. Unlike regular GST which has INR 20 lakh / 40 lakh thresholds, OIDAR has zero threshold for foreign suppliers.
Does OIDAR apply to B2B sales to Indian businesses?
No - if the Indian customer is GST-registered (business), reverse charge mechanism applies (Section 5(3) IGST Act). Indian recipient self-assesses GST; no obligation on foreign supplier. BUT: foreign supplier must capture Indian customer's GSTIN at checkout to prove B2B status. Without GSTIN, every sale defaults to B2C and attracts OIDAR GST obligation.
Do I need an Indian representative for OIDAR?
Not strictly mandatory, but highly recommended. The Indian authorised representative handles: representation with tax officer, monthly return filing, IGST payment execution, response to notices, annual return (GSTR-9A). Without an Indian representative, every compliance step is harder for foreign supplier. BQP acts as authorised representative for several foreign SaaS clients.
What happens if I don't register for OIDAR?
Section 74 GST demand (fraud / wilful suppression) can go back 5 years with 100% penalty. Section 73 (non-fraud) 3 years back with 10% penalty / INR 10,000 whichever higher. Payment gateway blocking is also possible (RBI can direct). Reputational risk if major customer tax audit surfaces the issue.
