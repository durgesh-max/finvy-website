/ Individuals and HUFs selling a house · Capital gains · Updated 2026-11-25

Section 54 of Income Tax Act (Now Section 82): Exemption on Sale of House Property - Conditions, Rs 10 Crore Limit, Two Houses, CGAS, NRIs, New Regime (2026), reinvest in a house, on time, and keep it for three years.

Section 54 exempts the long-term capital gain on selling a residential house if the gain is invested in another residential house in India: bought within one year before or two years after the sale, or constructed within three years. From tax year 2026-27 the same relief is in section 82 of the Income-tax Act, 2025. It is available to individuals and HUFs, resident or non-resident, and under both the old and the new tax regime. The exemption is the lower of the capital gain and the cost of the new house, with anything above ₹10 crore ignored. Money not invested by the return due date must be parked in the Capital Gains Account Scheme.

/ Quick answers

What people search for.

  • What are the section 54 conditions? The house sold was a residential house held for more than 24 months; the seller is an individual or HUF; the new residential house is in India and is bought or built within the time limits.
  • What is the section 54 exemption limit? The exemption is limited to the cost of the new house, and cost or gain above ₹10 crore is not counted.
  • Can I buy two houses? Once in a lifetime, if the capital gain does not exceed ₹2 crore, the gain can be invested in two residential houses.
  • Is section 54 available in the new tax regime? Yes. The new regime removes deductions such as 80C and HRA, but capital gains exemptions under sections 54, 54F and 54EC remain available.
  • What if I cannot buy before filing my return? Deposit the unused gain in the Capital Gains Account Scheme with an authorised bank before the return due date and claim the exemption; use it within the time limit or it becomes taxable.
  • Section 54 on sale of land? Section 54 applies only to a residential house sold. For land or other assets use section 54F (invest the net sale consideration in a house) or 54EC bonds; see the 54 vs 54F vs 54EC decision tree.

/ Traps that cost the exemption

Watch these.

  • Selling the new house within three years: the exemption is reversed by reducing the cost of the new house, so the gain becomes taxable on that sale.
  • House abroad: the new house must be in India.
  • Missing the CGAS deposit: without a deposit before the return due date, the uninvested part is taxable.
  • Over the cap: amounts above ₹10 crore get no exemption; consider 54EC bonds for part of the gain, see 54EC bonds.
  • NRIs: the exemption applies, but the buyer deducts TDS on the sale; a lower deduction certificate can reflect the reinvestment, see NRI capital gains on property.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is the section 54 exemption?
An exemption from tax on the long-term capital gain from selling a residential house, if the gain is invested in another residential house in India within the time limits. From tax year 2026-27 it is section 82 of the Income-tax Act, 2025.
What are the conditions for section 54?
The seller is an individual or HUF; the house sold was held for more than 24 months; the new residential house in India is bought within one year before or two years after the sale, or constructed within three years; and the new house is not sold within three years.
Is section 54 available to an NRI?
Yes. Section 54 (now section 82) applies to any individual or HUF, whatever the residential status, so an NRI selling a house in India can claim it on the same conditions - including that the new house must be in India, not abroad. The exemption does not stop the buyer deducting TDS on the whole price under section 393 (old 195): either get a Form 128 (old Form 13) lower TDS certificate before the sale, showing the planned reinvestment, or claim the excess TDS back as a refund in your ITR-2 for the year.
What is the maximum exemption under section 54?
The lower of the capital gain and the cost of the new house, with any cost or gain above Rs 10 crore ignored.
Is section 54 available under the new tax regime?
Yes. Capital gains exemptions under sections 54, 54F and 54EC (and their 2025 Act counterparts) are available under both the old and new regimes.
Can I claim section 54 for two houses?
Yes, once in a lifetime, if the long-term capital gain does not exceed Rs 2 crore. Otherwise the exemption is for one residential house.
What is the Capital Gains Account Scheme and how does it work?
It is a 1988 government scheme run through authorised bank branches for gains you have not reinvested by the return due date. You deposit the unused amount before the due date (31 July for most individuals not needing an audit) and claim the exemption in the return. Account A works like a savings account for withdrawals as you pay for the house; Account B is a term deposit. The money must be used for the new house within two years of the sale for a purchase, or three years for construction; whatever is unused then becomes taxable as long-term capital gain in the year the period ends. Withdrawals and closure follow the forms prescribed under the scheme, and closing with an unused balance needs the Assessing Officer's approval.
Can an NRI open a Capital Gains Account Scheme account?
Yes. The scheme is open to any taxpayer claiming exemption under sections 54, 54F and similar provisions (now sections 82 and 86), including non-residents. Open it at an authorised branch of a bank that runs the scheme (rural branches are not authorised) and deposit from the sale proceeds before the return due date; check the bank's KYC requirements for non-residents first. Keep the deposit receipt with your ITR papers, and remember the buyer's TDS on the sale is separate - a Form 128 lower TDS certificate before the sale avoids excess tax being withheld.
