/ Salaried employees, contractors and landlords whose TDS is missing from Form 168 (old 26AS) · Updated 2026-11-27

TDS Deducted by Employer but Not Deposited (2026): Section 205, the CBDT 2016 Instruction, Claiming Credit and Answering the Demand, the tax was cut from your salary - you should not pay it twice.

If your employer deducted TDS from your salary but did not deposit it, the tax does not show in your Form 168 (old 26AS) and the return gets a demand or a reduced refund - but section 205 bars the department from collecting that tax from you again, and a CBDT instruction of 11 March 2016 tells officers not to enforce such demands against the employee. The fix is to prove the deduction (salary slips, Form 16), press the employer to deposit and file a correction, and answer any demand citing section 205 and the instruction.

/ What happens

Why the demand appears, and why it should not stick.

PointWhat it means for you
TDS missing from Form 168 / AISThe return's TDS claim is reduced when processed, creating a demand or a smaller refund
Section 205No direct demand on you for tax that was deducted from your income
CBDT office memorandum, 11 March 2016Demands arising only because the deductor did not deposit TDS are not to be enforced against the deductee
Credit for the taxOften allowed on the strength of Form 16 / salary slips, but tribunal decisions are not uniform - evidence matters
The employerLiable for the tax, interest for late deposit and penalty or prosecution provisions
  • Not the same as a late filing: sometimes the employer deposited the tax but filed the TDS return late or with the wrong PAN - a correction statement fixes that, and the credit then appears.
  • Contractors, landlords and NRIs paid after TDS face the same issue when the payer does not deposit; the same protections apply.

/ What to do

Step by step.

  • Check Form 168 (old 26AS) and AIS to see whether the TDS is missing, or present under a different PAN or quarter.
  • Collect proof of deduction: salary slips, Form 130 (old Form 16), bank statements showing net salary credited.
  • Ask the employer in writing to deposit the tax and file or correct the TDS return; keep the correspondence.
  • File your return claiming the TDS shown in Form 16, with the proof ready.
  • If a 143(1) demand comes: respond on the portal disagreeing with the demand, citing section 205 and the CBDT office memorandum of 11 March 2016, and attach the proof; raise a grievance if it is still pursued.
  • If recovery is pressed or a refund is adjusted against it, write to the Assessing Officer with the evidence; an appeal is the next step if credit is refused.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What happens if TDS is deducted by the employer but not deposited?
The tax does not appear in your Form 168 (old 26AS), so when your return is processed the TDS claim is cut and a demand or smaller refund follows. But section 205 bars a direct demand on you for tax already deducted from your income, and a CBDT office memorandum of 11 March 2016 tells officers not to enforce such demands against the employee. The employer remains liable for the tax, interest and penalties.
Do I have to pay the tax again if my employer did not deposit my TDS?
No - not for the tax that was actually deducted. Prove the deduction with salary slips and Form 130 (old Form 16), and answer any demand citing section 205 and the 2016 CBDT instruction. Credit for the tax itself is often allowed on that evidence, though tribunal decisions are not uniform, so keep the proof.
How do I reply to a demand for TDS my employer did not deposit?
Respond to the 143(1) demand on the portal by disagreeing with it, cite section 205 and the CBDT office memorandum F.No. 275/29/2014-IT(B) of 11 March 2016, and attach the salary slips, Form 16 and the employer correspondence. If the demand is still pursued or a refund is adjusted, write to the Assessing Officer and, if needed, appeal.
Why is my TDS not showing in Form 26AS?
Either the deductor did not deposit it, filed the TDS return late, or quoted a wrong PAN, amount or quarter. Check AIS and ask the deductor - a correction statement fixes errors and late filing; non-deposit is the employer's default, covered by section 205.
What action can be taken against an employer who does not deposit TDS?
The employer is liable for the tax with interest under section 201(1A), can face penalty and, for deducted tax not deposited, prosecution provisions. Employees can also report the default to the TDS jurisdictional officer with their evidence.
