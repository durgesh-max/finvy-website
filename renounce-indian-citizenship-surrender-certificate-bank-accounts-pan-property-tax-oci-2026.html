/ NRIs becoming foreign citizens · Updated 2026-11-24

Renouncing Indian Citizenship (2026): Surrender Certificate, and What Changes for Your Bank Accounts, PAN, Property and Tax, the passport is the easy part; update the money side too.

India does not allow dual citizenship: Indian citizenship ends when you voluntarily take another country’s citizenship, and you must then surrender your Indian passport and get a surrender certificate through the Indian mission, applying online on the Passport Seva portal. Some missions also ask for a renunciation declaration on the Ministry of Home Affairs portal; keeping or using an Indian passport after becoming a foreign citizen is an offence. The surrender certificate is then needed for an OCI card or Indian visa. On the money side, very little has to be closed, but a lot has to be updated: bank and demat KYC with your new passport, PAN details, how you hold Indian property, and your tax position, where losing citizenship ends the deemed-residence rule that catches some Indian citizens.

/ Quick answers

What people giving up Indian citizenship ask.

  • Do I have to renounce Indian citizenship? It ends automatically when you take foreign citizenship voluntarily (section 9 of the Citizenship Act); you then surrender the passport and, where your mission asks, file a renunciation declaration.
  • How do I get a surrender certificate? Apply online on Passport Seva, then submit at the mission or its service provider with your foreign passport, naturalisation certificate and the original Indian passport; fees and processing vary by country.
  • Can I keep my Indian bank accounts? Yes as a non-resident of Indian origin or OCI: NRE, NRO and FCNR accounts can continue, but update KYC with the foreign passport and OCI details.
  • Can I keep my property in India? Yes. Property you already own stays yours; as an OCI you can also buy residential and commercial property, but not new agricultural land, plantations or farmhouses.
  • Does it change my Indian tax? Residence still depends on days in India, but the deemed-residence rule for Indian citizens with high Indian income and no tax elsewhere no longer applies.

/ Order of steps

A practical sequence.

  • 1. Surrender the Indian passport soon after naturalisation and get the surrender certificate (and renunciation certificate if your mission requires it).
  • 2. Apply for OCI using the surrender certificate, so you can live, work and hold property in India without a visa.
  • 3. Update banks, PAN, demat and insurers with the new passport and OCI number.
  • 4. Review tax: your residence days, Indian income, treaty position and home-country reporting; see deemed residence for Indian citizens.
  • 5. Plan property: what to keep, sell or gift, and how to repatriate; see foreign citizens owning Indian property.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

How do I renounce Indian citizenship?
Indian citizenship ends automatically when you voluntarily take another country's citizenship. You then surrender your Indian passport and get a surrender certificate by applying online on Passport Seva and submitting at the Indian mission or its service provider; some missions also require a renunciation declaration on the Ministry of Home Affairs portal.
What is a surrender certificate?
The certificate the Indian mission issues when you surrender your Indian passport after becoming a foreign citizen. You need it to apply for an OCI card or an Indian visa; using or keeping an Indian passport after taking foreign citizenship is an offence.
Can an OCI cardholder become an Indian citizen again?
Yes, by registration under section 5(1)(g) of the Citizenship Act: a person registered as an OCI for five years who has lived in India for a year before applying (usually read as the 12 months immediately before) can apply online to the Ministry of Home Affairs in Form VII. Because India does not allow dual citizenship, the foreign citizenship has to be given up as part of the process. Processing takes months and is case by case, so plan bank, tax and property changes for the switch back to resident Indian citizen status.
Can I keep my Indian bank accounts after giving up Indian citizenship?
Yes. As a person of Indian origin or OCI you can keep NRE, NRO and FCNR accounts; update KYC with your foreign passport and OCI details. Resident savings accounts should already have been converted to NRO when you became non-resident.
Can I keep property in India after renouncing citizenship?
Yes. Property you already own stays yours. As an OCI you can buy residential and commercial property but not new agricultural land, plantations or farmhouses, and sale proceeds are repatriated within FEMA limits.
Does renouncing Indian citizenship change my Indian tax?
Tax residence still depends on days spent in India, and Indian income stays taxable in India. But the deemed-residence rule, which can treat Indian citizens with high Indian income and no tax elsewhere as resident, no longer applies.
