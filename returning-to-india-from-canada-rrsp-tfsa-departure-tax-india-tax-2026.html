Returning to India From Canada: Departure Tax, RRSP, TFSA and What India Taxes (2026), two tax systems, one move.

Leaving Canada triggers Canada's departure tax: most investments are treated as sold at market value on the day you leave, though RRSPs, TFSAs, cash and Canadian real estate are excluded. On the Indian side, your first years back are usually non-resident or RNOR years in which Canadian income is not taxed in India. Once you are resident and ordinarily resident, the RRSP, TFSA, bank and brokerage accounts all go in Schedule FA. An RRSP can use Form 40 to defer Indian tax until withdrawal; a TFSA has no such relief.

/ Use the RNOR years well

Planning points.

  • Find your RNOR years with the residential status calculator; foreign income and gains received outside India are generally not taxed in India then.
  • Restructure before you are resident and ordinarily resident: decide what to keep in Canada, what to close and what to bring back while Indian tax does not reach it.
  • TFSA: since India gives it no shelter and no new contributions are allowed, many returning NRIs review whether to keep it beyond the RNOR years.
  • NRE and FCNR accounts in India must be redesignated once you return for good; foreign currency can sit in an RFC account. See NRE and NRO conversion on return.
  • CPP and OAS paid to you in India bear Canadian non-resident tax; check the India-Canada treaty position for your case before filing in India.

/ If you returned years ago

Fix it before 31 December 2026.

  • Canadian accounts and investments built from salary earned while you were an NRI, not shown in Schedule FA after you became resident: FAST-DS Category 2, INR 1 lakh (foreign assets up to INR 5 crore in total).
  • TFSA income, interest or dividends earned while resident and ordinarily resident and never offered to tax: Category 1, 60% within INR 1 crore.
  • Declare by 31 December 2026, pay after the Form 2 order, and report every year after.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is Canada's departure tax?
When you emigrate, Canada treats most of your property as sold at fair market value on the day you leave and taxes the gain. RRSPs, RRIFs, RESPs, TFSAs, cash and Canadian real estate are excluded. The tax can be deferred by posting security.
Can I keep my RRSP after moving back to India?
Yes. Withdrawals later bear Canadian non-resident withholding. In India, list it in Schedule FA once you are resident and ordinarily resident; Form 40 can defer Indian tax on the income inside until you withdraw.
Is a TFSA tax-free in India?
No. India does not recognise the TFSA, so income inside it is taxable in India once you are resident and ordinarily resident, and it goes in Schedule FA. Canada also bars contributions while you are non-resident.
When does India start taxing my Canadian income?
Usually not in the non-resident or RNOR years after you return, if the income is received outside India. Once you are resident and ordinarily resident, your worldwide income is taxed in India.
I came back from Canada years ago and never reported my accounts. What now?
If they were built from salary earned while you were an NRI, FAST-DS Category 2 settles it for a flat INR 1 lakh if you declare by 31 December 2026.
