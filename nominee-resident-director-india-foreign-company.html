/ What Section 149(3) requires

The core rule.

  • Every company incorporated under Companies Act 2013 must have minimum 2 directors (Pvt Ltd) or 3 directors (Public Ltd).
  • At least ONE director must be a 'resident of India' — defined as a person who has stayed in India for at least 182 days during the preceding FY.
  • Non-resident Indians (NRIs) with Indian passport but non-resident status do NOT qualify.
  • Foreign citizens who are tax-residents of India (physically present 182+ days) qualify.
  • Non-compliance consequences: Section 164 director disqualification risk, ROC filing blockage, inability to incorporate / sign filings.

/ When foreign companies need nominee director

Common scenarios.

  • Foreign parent incorporating Indian WOS: parent appoints 2 foreign directors from its own leadership team. Needs 1 India-resident director — BQP provides.
  • India-resident director resigns: company left without resident director. 30 days to appoint replacement — BQP provides temporary / long-term.
  • Expat executive relocates out of India: director who was India-resident loses that status after 180+ days abroad. Replacement needed.
  • Dormant Indian subsidiary: subsidiary not actively operated but still needs 1 resident director for compliance.

/ Scope of nominee director's role

What they do and don't do.

What nominee director DOES:

  • Named as director in Indian company's records (MCA / ROC filings).
  • Holds valid DIN (Director Identification Number) + DSC (Digital Signature Certificate) issued in India.
  • Signs required ROC filings: AOC-4, MGT-7, DIR-12, PAS-3, MGT-14 as applicable.
  • Attends board meetings (video / telephonic) when quorum needs resident director.
  • Signs resolutions requiring resident director signature.
  • Files DIR-3 KYC (every three years).
  • Signs required bank KYC / banking documents for Indian company.

What nominee director does NOT do:

  • Participate in business operations / strategy / commercial decisions.
  • Sign commercial contracts / customer agreements / vendor contracts.
  • Represent the company publicly.
  • Access operational funds / treasury / payroll.
  • Make day-to-day business judgments.

Nominee director's role is purely statutory governance — satisfying Section 149(3), not running the business.

/ Legal structuring + indemnity

What protects everyone.

  • Nominee Director Agreement: between foreign parent + BQP nominee — defines scope, fee, indemnity.
  • Director's Letter of Appointment: standard Indian company board resolution appointing nominee as director.
  • Letter of Resignation: pre-signed, undated, held in escrow — can be used immediately if nominee needs to be replaced.
  • Indemnity from foreign parent: foreign parent indemnifies nominee against claims arising from acts of other directors / company's operations. Standard legal protection.
  • Nominee's indemnity from BQP: as a firm, we indemnify our nominee partners.
  • D&O insurance (optional): foreign parent may purchase D&O insurance covering Indian subsidiary directors.
  • Section 164 disqualification protection: nominee director complies with DIR-3 KYC (every three years), DIR-8, MBP-1 to avoid Section 164 risk from the Indian company's non-compliance.

/ Replacement + transition

When nominee changes.

  1. Reasons for replacement: nominee's own change of circumstances; foreign parent prefers different nominee; appointment of new India-resident director from parent group.
  2. Resignation: nominee submits DIR-11 within 30 days of resignation.
  3. Appointment of successor: board resolution + DIR-12 within 30 days.
  4. Section 164 disqualification check: successor must not be disqualified.
  5. Bank KYC refresh: Indian bank requires KYC update for new signatory.
  6. MCA records updated: company's director list updated.

Standard replacement timeline: 30-45 days end-to-end.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is a nominee / resident director in India?
A person named as director of an Indian company to satisfy Section 149(3) Companies Act 2013 requirement that at least one director must be an India resident (stayed in India 182+ days in preceding FY). Nominee's role is statutory governance - signing filings and resolutions - not operational business decisions. Standard arrangement for foreign-owned Indian subsidiaries without an India-resident person on their leadership team.
What is the legal risk of being a nominee director?
Directors owe fiduciary duties to the company (Section 166 Companies Act) + liable for non-compliance by the company (Section 164 disqualification, Section 450 default penalties). Mitigated by: (a) scope restriction (nominee doesn't participate in operational decisions), (b) foreign parent's indemnity, (c) regular DIR-3 KYC + DIR-8 + MBP-1 compliance, (d) D&O insurance (optional). BQP's nominee program bundles standard protective structuring.
Can a Non-Resident Indian (NRI) be the resident director?
Depends on actual residence, not citizenship. If the NRI has stayed in India 182+ days in the preceding FY, they qualify as 'resident' for Section 149(3). If the NRI is a tax non-resident (less than 182 days), they do NOT qualify - even with Indian passport. Common misunderstanding. Nominee director must be assessed by physical residence, not citizenship.
How much does nominee director service cost?
Scope-based annual fee. Includes: DIN + DSC maintenance, DIR-3 KYC (every three years), signing of standard ROC filings (AOC-4, MGT-7, DIR-12 as applicable), participation in required board meetings. Typically scope-based engagement priced per annum. Pre-signed resignation + indemnity + replacement workflow included.
Can I use my Indian employee / consultant as director?
Yes if they are tax-resident in India and willing. However: directors have personal liability under Companies Act. Many employees refuse. Also, employee director creates employer-director conflict of interest in HR matters. External CA-qualified nominee (like BQP's partners) is cleaner structurally.
