Can the Income Tax Department See My Bank Account? (2026) What Banks Report, SFT Limits, Your AIS and When Statements Are Called For, not live - but it sees a lot.

The income tax department does not watch your account in real time, but banks report interest and large transactions against your PAN, and all of it appears in your Annual Information Statement (AIS). When those entries do not match your return, you get a compliance e-mail or a notice - and in an assessment or search, the department can ask the bank for full statements.

/ What banks and others report

Statement of Financial Transactions (SFT) and TDS.

TransactionReported when it reaches
Cash depositsINR 10 lakh or more in a year in savings accounts (a higher limit for current accounts)
Fixed / time depositsINR 10 lakh or more in a year
Credit card bill paymentsINR 1 lakh or more in cash, or INR 10 lakh or more by any mode, in a year
Shares, mutual funds, bondsPurchases of INR 10 lakh or more in a year
Foreign currencyPurchases (including forex card loads) of INR 10 lakh or more in a year
Interest on depositsReported through TDS statements, whatever the amount when TDS applies
  • Linked to your PAN: that is why every account, deposit and investment asks for it.
  • Where you see it: the AIS and Form 26AS (now Form 168) show what has been reported; check them before filing.
  • Property deals are reported by registrars above their own limit, and show up in the AIS too.

/ When it becomes a problem, and what to do

Mismatch, e-mail, notice.

  • A mismatch: an AIS entry that is not in your return - for example a cash deposit, FD or share purchase - is the usual trigger.
  • First step from the department: a compliance e-mail or message asking you to explain or file; reply with your feedback on the AIS entry and the source of the money.
  • If not resolved: a notice asking you to explain the source, with possible addition of unexplained money as income.
  • During an assessment or search: the officer can call for full bank statements from you or the bank.
  • Keep a paper trail: gifts with gift deeds, loans with agreements, sale proceeds with deeds and contract notes, cash from business with books.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Can the income tax department see my bank account?
Not in real time, but it sees a great deal. Banks report interest through TDS statements and large transactions through the Statement of Financial Transactions - such as cash deposits of INR 10 lakh or more in a year in savings accounts and fixed deposits of INR 10 lakh or more - all linked to your PAN and shown in your AIS.
How much cash can I deposit in my bank account without the tax department knowing?
Banks report cash deposits that add up to INR 10 lakh or more in a year in savings accounts, with a higher limit for current accounts. Smaller deposits are not automatically safe if they are unexplained income - what matters is that every deposit has a source you can show.
Can the tax department see my credit card spending?
Yes, above the reporting limits: credit card bill payments of INR 1 lakh or more in cash, or INR 10 lakh or more by any mode, in a year are reported and appear in the AIS.
How do I see what the department knows about my bank transactions?
Check your Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Form 26AS (now Form 168) on the income tax e-filing portal. If an entry is wrong, submit feedback on it; if it is right but missing from your return, correct the return.
What happens if my AIS shows a large deposit that is not in my return?
You may get a compliance e-mail asking you to explain it, and later a notice. Reply with the source - salary savings, a gift with a gift deed, a loan agreement or sale proceeds - and revise or update the return if income was left out.
