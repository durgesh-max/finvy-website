/ Foreign investors · FEMA · Valuation · Updated 2026-11-19

FEMA Valuation Report for Foreign Investment in India (2026): Fair Value Rules, Who Can Sign, Methods, 90-Day Validity and Income-Tax Overlap, for foreign investors, buyers and Indian companies raising from abroad.

When an Indian company issues shares to a foreign investor, or shares move between a resident and a non-resident, FEMA requires a price at fair value. For an unlisted company, the price for a non-resident investor must be no less than fair value, and a non-resident selling to a resident must receive no more than fair value. Fair value is worked out by an internationally accepted method on an arm's-length basis and certified by a chartered accountant, a SEBI-registered merchant banker or a practising cost accountant. Listed companies follow SEBI pricing. The rules fix no validity period, but banks and standard practice expect the report to be no more than 90 days old on the date of allotment or transfer. The same deal may also need an income-tax fair market value report (Rule 11UA) and, for a preferential allotment, a registered valuer's report under the Companies Act. Plan all three together so the numbers do not conflict.

/ Methods, validity and format

What the report should contain.

  • Methods: discounted cash flow is common for operating companies; net asset value for holding or asset-heavy companies; market multiples or recent transaction prices as cross-checks. The report must explain why the method suits the company.
  • Validity: FEMA does not set a period, but authorised dealer banks and practice treat a report older than 90 days at allotment or transfer as stale; a request to standardise at 180 days has been made but is not law. Re-date or refresh if the deal slips.
  • Contents: purpose and transaction, valuation date, information relied on, method and key assumptions, fair value per share, and the valuer's certification and registration details.
  • Convertibles: record the conversion formula or price at issue; the conversion price cannot be below fair value at the time of issue.
  • Signatories: a chartered accountant, a SEBI-registered merchant banker, or a practising cost accountant.

/ Income-tax and company-law overlap

Avoid three different numbers.

  • Angel tax: the income-tax charge on share premium above fair value (section 56(2)(viib)) was abolished from FY 2024-25, so issue prices above fair value no longer trigger it.
  • Transfers below fair market value: income-tax still deems fair market value (Rule 11UA) for the seller and can tax the buyer on a shortfall, so FEMA and income-tax values should be consistent.
  • Preferential allotment under the Companies Act: needs a registered valuer's report (IBBI-registered); one engagement can often cover FEMA, income-tax and Companies Act purposes if planned.
  • Late or missing valuation: an allotment without a valid report is a FEMA contravention; see FEMA compounding.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is a FEMA valuation report?
A certificate of the fair value of an Indian company's shares, prepared under an internationally accepted method, needed when shares are issued to or transferred between residents and non-residents.
Who can sign a FEMA valuation report?
A chartered accountant, a SEBI-registered merchant banker or a practising cost accountant; share swaps need a SEBI-registered merchant banker or an investment banker regulated abroad.
How long is a FEMA valuation report valid?
The rules do not fix a period, but banks and practice expect the report to be no more than 90 days old on the date of allotment or transfer.
Must a foreign investor pay at least fair value?
Yes, for unlisted shares issued or sold to a non-resident; when a non-resident sells to a resident, the price cannot exceed fair value.
Is angel tax still a concern for foreign investment?
No. The angel tax on share premium above fair value was abolished from FY 2024-25, though income-tax fair market value rules still apply to transfers.
