/ Charitable and religious trusts, societies and section 8 companies · Registration, renewal and 80G · Updated 2026-11-27

Trust Registration Under 12AB (Now Section 332) and 80G Approval (2026): Provisional and Regular Registration, Renewal, Form 104 / 105 and Compliance, no registration, no exemption.

A trust, society or section 8 company is taxed like any other entity unless it is registered under section 12AB (from 1 April 2026, section 332 of the Income-tax Act, 2025); donors get a deduction only if it also holds 80G approval (now section 354). A new entity first gets provisional registration for three years and must then apply for regular registration in time; registered entities renew every five years. Existing valid registrations continue under the new Act, but missing a renewal date can cost the exemption.

/ How registration works

Provisional, regular, renewal.

StageWhat happens
Provisional registrationFor a new entity, granted for three years; apply before or soon after starting activities
Regular registrationApply at least six months before provisional registration expires, or within six months of starting activities if earlier; generally granted for five years
RenewalApply at least six months before the current registration expires
80G approvalFollows the same provisional / regular / renewal path; lets donors claim a deduction
Forms (2025 Act)Form 104 for provisional and Form 105 for regular registration or renewal, replacing Forms 10A and 10AB
  • Existing registrations under 12A / 12AB / 80G remain valid under the 2025 Act until their expiry - no fresh registration just because the Act changed.
  • Documents: trust deed, society registration or section 8 licence, PAN, activity details, accounts and the existing orders.
  • Objects matter: the officer checks that activities are genuine and match the objects, and may ask questions before granting regular registration.

/ After registration

The yearly compliance that keeps it.

  • Books and audit: accounts audited and the audit report filed before the return when income before exemption exceeds the basic exemption limit.
  • Return: the trust files its own return every year, showing income applied to its objects and any accumulation.
  • Donation statements: an 80G-approved entity files the annual statement of donations (old Form 10BD) by 31 May and issues donor certificates - late filing attracts a fee and donors lose their deduction.
  • Cash donations: donors cannot claim a deduction for cash donations above Rs 2,000.
  • Missed renewal or cancellation: the exemption is lost and the trust can be taxed on its accreted income, so diary the expiry dates.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is trust registration under 12AB?
It is the income tax registration that lets a charitable or religious trust, society or section 8 company claim exemption on income applied to its objects. From 1 April 2026 it sits in section 332 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, but registrations granted under 12A / 12AB stay valid until they expire. Without it, the entity is taxed like any other.
How does a new trust get 12AB registration?
It first applies for provisional registration, which is granted for three years. It must then apply for regular registration at least six months before the provisional registration expires, or within six months of starting its activities if that is earlier. Under the 2025 Act the forms are Form 104 (provisional) and Form 105 (regular), replacing Forms 10A and 10AB.
How long is 12AB registration valid, and when must it be renewed?
Regular registration is generally granted for five years. The renewal application is due at least six months before it expires. Missing the date can cost the exemption, so diary the expiry date shown in the order.
Is 80G approval the same as 12AB registration?
No. 12AB (now section 332) gives the trust its own exemption; 80G (now section 354) lets donors deduct their donations. Both follow a provisional / regular / renewal path and are usually applied for together. An 80G-approved trust must file the annual statement of donations by 31 May and issue donor certificates.
Does a trust registered under 12AB need fresh registration under the new Income-tax Act?
No. A valid registration or approval under 12AB or 80G continues under the Income-tax Act, 2025 until its expiry; renewal then follows the new sections and forms.
What is the difference between audit report Form 10B and Form 10BB for a trust?
Both are the tax audit reports a registered trust, society or section 8 company must file, signed by a chartered accountant, when its total income before claiming the exemption exceeds the basic exemption limit. Since assessment year 2023-24, Form 10B (old numbering) applies where that total income exceeds INR 5 crore, where the trust received any foreign contribution, or where it applied any income outside India; every other trust files the shorter Form 10BB, which is also used by institutions approved under section 10(23C). Both forms ask for detailed disclosures - application of income, accumulation, corpus donations, anonymous donations, payments to related persons and investments - so the books must be ready well before the deadline, which is one month before the return due date (normally 30 September for a trust whose ITR-7 is due by 31 October). A missing or late audit report can cost the trust its exemption for that year. How does an NGO get FCRA registration to receive foreign donations?
Any trust, society or section 8 company must have FCRA registration or prior permission from the Ministry of Home Affairs before it accepts foreign contribution - including donations from NRIs who are foreign citizens and from foreign companies or foundations. Full registration is generally for organisations that have existed for at least three years and have spent a minimum amount on their objects over that period, shown in audited accounts filed with the application in Form FC-3; a newer organisation applies for prior permission for a specific donor and amount instead. Foreign contributions must first be received in the designated FCRA account at State Bank of India, New Delhi Main Branch, and can then be moved to separate utilisation accounts; no local money can be mixed in. Registration is granted for a fixed period and must be renewed in time, an annual return in Form FC-4 is filed with the accounts, and the 2020 amendment tightened the rules on passing foreign funds to other organisations and on administrative spending.
