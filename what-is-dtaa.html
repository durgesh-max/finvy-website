Definition

What is DTAA (Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement)?

A DTAA (Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement) is a bilateral tax treaty between two countries that prevents the same income from being taxed twice and sets lower withholding rates on cross-border payments. India has DTAAs with 90+ countries.

How it works

How DTAA works.

  • Two countries agree on which has primary taxing right on each income type
  • Resident-state typically taxes worldwide income; source-state taxes its source
  • DTAA provides Foreign Tax Credit (FTC) — credit for tax paid abroad
  • Lower withholding rates on cross-border payments (royalty, FTS, interest, dividends)
  • Tie-breaker rules if both countries claim residency

Common India DTAAs

Common Indian DTAAs.

Important ones:

  • US: Withholding on royalty/FTS reduced to 15% (down from 20%+); business profits taxed where PE exists
  • UK: Similar 15% withholding; tighter PE definition
  • Singapore: 10-15% withholding; widely used for fund vehicles
  • Mauritius: Used to be very advantageous for capital gains; reformed in 2017
  • UAE: 10-12% withholding; ground for Dubai-based businesses
  • Netherlands: 10% withholding; old favorite for IP holding companies

DTAA misuse risks

DTAA misuse.

  • GAAR can disregard structures with no commercial substance
  • MFN (Most Favoured Nation) clauses sometimes inapplicable
  • Treaty shopping increasingly contested by tax departments
  • Need to maintain substance — real offices, employees, decision-making in treaty country
  • Beneficial Ownership rules increasingly enforced

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

How do I claim DTAA benefit?
File Form 67 with Indian Income Tax Return for foreign tax credit. For a lower treaty rate on payments from India, the non-resident gives the payer a Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) and Form 10F (Form 41 from tax year 2026-27).
What documents are needed to claim DTAA benefits in India?
A Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) from the country where the recipient is resident, Form 10F (Form 41 from tax year 2026-27) filed on the income tax portal, the recipient's PAN where available, and usually declarations that the recipient is the beneficial owner and has no permanent establishment in India. The Indian payer keeps these on file before deducting tax at the treaty rate.
Can DTAA benefits be claimed without a TRC or without a PAN?
Not without a TRC: Indian law makes the certificate a condition for any treaty rate. Without a PAN, tax is normally deducted at a higher rate (at least 20%), but a non-resident can avoid that for interest, royalty, technical fees, dividends and payments for capital assets by giving the payer its name, email, contact number, address, TRC and foreign tax identification number.
What does DTAA benefit mean?
It is the lower tax rate or exemption a tax treaty gives on income such as interest, dividends, royalties or capital gains earned in one country by a resident of the other, plus credit in the home country for tax paid abroad so the same income is not fully taxed twice.
Is Indian DTAA list publicly available?
Yes — incometaxindia.gov.in lists all 90+ DTAAs. Each treaty has specific rates and articles. Read carefully — they vary significantly.
With how many countries does India have a DTAA?
India has comprehensive double tax avoidance agreements with more than 90 countries, about 94 by commonly cited counts, plus a handful of limited agreements that cover only items such as shipping or air transport income. Counts differ because some sources include treaties signed but not yet in force. The current list and treaty texts are on the Income Tax Department website (incometaxindia.gov.in). Major partners include the US, UK, UAE, Singapore, Mauritius, Canada, Australia, Germany, Japan and the Netherlands.
Does DTAA cover capital gains?
Sometimes. India's DTAA with US/UK doesn't exempt capital gains for Indian residents — India taxes them. India-Mauritius (pre-2017) did exempt; reform changed this. India-Singapore: similar to Mauritius. We check before structuring.

Investing or paying between India and France? French parent or investor? The India-France protocol signed 23 February 2026 moves dividends to 5% or 15%, gives India full rights on share gains and removes the MFN clause once notified: India-France DTAA changes.
Paying or receiving income between India and Switzerland? Swiss parent or investor? Switzerland suspended the MFN clause for income from 1 January 2025, so Indian dividends to Swiss residents are taxed at the 10% treaty rate, not 5%: India-Switzerland DTAA rates.
Paying or receiving income between India and Italy? Italian parent or client? The India-Italy DTAA has higher rates than most treaties (about 15% on dividends and interest, 20% on royalties and technical fees), so compare with the domestic rate: India-Italy DTAA rates.
Paying or receiving royalties or fees between India and Spain? Spanish licensor or service provider? Notification 33/2024 cut Indian tax on royalties and technical fees to 10% from tax year 2023-24, with refunds for excess withholding: India-Spain DTAA rates.
Paying or receiving income between India and Ireland? Irish parent, lessor or client? The India-Ireland DTAA caps Indian tax on dividends, interest, royalties and technical fees at 10%, with Article 8 on shipping and air transport: India-Ireland DTAA rates.
Living in Kuwait with income in India? Living in Kuwait? The India-Kuwait DTAA caps tax on NRO interest and dividends at 10% with a Kuwaiti TRC and Form 41, but no income tax in Kuwait can trigger India’s deemed-residence rule: India-Kuwait DTAA for NRIs.
Paying or receiving income between India and China? Chinese parent, licensor or supplier? The India-China DTAA caps Indian tax on dividends, interest, royalties and technical fees at 10%, but investment with Chinese ownership still needs approval: India-China DTAA and FDI rules.
Living in Malaysia with income in India? Living or investing in Malaysia? The India-Malaysia DTAA caps Indian tax at 5% on dividends and 10% on NRO interest, royalties and technical fees with a Malaysian TRC: India-Malaysia DTAA rates.
Paying or receiving income between India and Korea? Korean parent, licensor or supplier? The revised India-Korea DTAA taxes royalties, technical fees and interest at 10%, but gains on Indian shares stay taxable in India: India-Korea DTAA rates.
Paying or receiving income between India and Hong Kong? Hong Kong company or holding structure? The India-Hong Kong DTAA caps Indian tax at 5% on dividends and 10% on interest, royalties and technical fees, with an anti-abuse article and FDI approval still required: India-Hong Kong DTAA.
Paying or receiving income between India and Thailand? Thai company or NRI in Thailand? The revised India-Thailand DTAA caps Indian tax on dividends at 10% and reports 10% on interest and royalties; service fees need a treaty check: India-Thailand DTAA.
