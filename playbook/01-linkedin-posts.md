## Post 1 (Week 1) — India-US DTAA Form 10F process

Most Indian founders paying a US vendor for services deduct 25% TDS and call it a day.

The India-US DTAA caps most FTS at 15% (and in many cases, Article 7 business profits means zero Indian withholding if there's no US PE).

The claim is mechanical, not negotiable:
→ US vendor obtains US TRC from the IRS
→ US vendor files Form 10F electronically on the Indian income tax portal
→ Indian payer deducts at the treaty rate
→ CA signs Form 15CB on the characterisation

Most Indian CAs default to 25% out of caution. 10% of gross contract value stuck as working capital for 12-18 months before refund.

If you're paying US vendors recurring amounts, worth the 2-day setup to do it right.

## Post 2 (Week 2) — Form 5472 USD 25,000 penalty

The single most-missed US filing for Indian founders:

Form 5472.

Required for any Delaware single-member LLC with 25%+ foreign ownership and any reportable transaction with the foreign owner. "Reportable transaction" includes the initial capital contribution to open the US bank account — so effectively every active foreign-owned LLC.

Penalty for missing: USD 25,000 per form, per year. Automatic under IRC Section 6038A.

A founder I spoke to last month had three missed years on their Stripe Atlas LLC. USD 75,000 exposure. We cleaned it up via back-filing with a reasonable-cause statement — most of the penalty was abated — but the stress and time cost were real.

If you have a Delaware LLC and never heard of Form 5472, check your filings this week.

## Post 3 (Week 3) — India-to-Delaware flip timing

The flip math is non-obvious.

Many Indian founders assume "we'll flip to Delaware when the US VC term sheet is close."

That's the expensive version.

The Indian capital gains at the share-for-share swap are calculated on the FMV UPLIFT from original cost to today's valuation. Flipping at:

→ Pre-seed (USD 2M FMV) → trivial LTCG, often under INR 5 lakh total.
→ Seed (USD 10M FMV) → INR 20-40 lakh across the cap table.
→ Series A (USD 50M+ FMV) → INR 3-5 crore across the cap table.
→ Series B (USD 200M+ FMV) → INR 20-50 crore. Hence PhonePe-scale tax outlays.

If US VC is anywhere in your 24-month plan, flip early. The one-time INR 5 lakh at pre-seed saves INR 50 crore at Series C.

## Post 4 (Week 4) — UAE Corporate Tax 2023 reality check

For Indian founders with Dubai holdcos: the "0% UAE tax" era is over.

UAE Federal Corporate Tax effective 1 June 2023: 9% above AED 375,000 profit. Free Zone Qualifying Free Zone Person (QFZP) 0% on qualifying income, 9% on everything else.

The QFZP qualification is technical:
→ Core income-generating activities in UAE Free Zone
→ Adequate substance (local employees, operating expenditure, physical office)
→ Qualifying Income definition (distribution to Free Zone persons, specific IP income, treasury financing to related parties)
→ Audited financial statements
→ Transfer pricing documentation

What used to be a lifestyle structure (shell Free Zone + nominee director + no UAE presence) now fails on both QFZP and ESR substance tests.

If your Dubai holdco was set up pre-2023, re-price the economics with 9% baseline + plan the QFZP substance work.

## Post 5 (Week 5) — SAFE vs CCPS for Indian company seed round

Indian founders raising from US angels keep asking: "Can we use a SAFE?"

If the company issuing is a Delaware C-Corp: yes, use a YC post-money SAFE. Standard.

If the company issuing is an Indian Pvt Ltd: no, SAFE is not clearly recognised under FEMA. Use CCPS (Compulsorily Convertible Preference Shares) instead.

CCPS delivers the SAFE equivalent:
→ Preference-stock economics (1x non-participating liquidation preference)
→ Mandatory conversion at next priced round at pre-money valuation
→ No immediate equity dilution
→ FEMA-compliant for inbound FDI
→ Form FC-GPR within 30 days of issue

Legal cost similar (1-2 weeks to close), investor comfortable, Indian regulator comfortable.

For hybrid rounds (US angel + India angel into Indian company), CCPS works for all investors.

## Post 6 (Week 6) — Mercury vs Brex opening order

For Indian founders opening a US bank account, order matters.

Mercury first. Brex second (if at all).

Mercury: fully-remote, pre-revenue friendly, Treasury yield on idle balance, FDIC via partner banks. Approves earlier-stage founders.

Brex: better for funded companies (post seed/Series A) that need corporate cards + spend management for a 10+ person team. Brex has periodically declined pre-revenue applicants.

Opening Brex first and getting declined for insufficient revenue signal becomes a KYC flag on your file. Opening Mercury first, operating for 6 months, then adding Brex is a cleaner progression.

For most Indian founders at seed stage: Mercury is sufficient. Add Brex at Series A.

## Post 7 (Week 7) — ESOP exercise perquisite tax

The hidden tax on ESOP exercises in India:

Under Section 17(2)(vi), the perquisite = (FMV at exercise − strike price). Taxable in the employee's hands as salary income, at slab rate (up to 42.7% including surcharge).

Employer deducts TDS. Employee pays through salary cycle. The ESOP holder HAS NOT yet sold the shares — they only exercised. But they owe tax now.

For a successful startup, this is often the moment where the employee discovers they need INR 10-50 lakh cash to execute the paper exercise. Many never exercise because they can't fund the tax.

The pattern that works: company-funded exercise + sell-to-cover arrangement at Series B+, or cashless exercise where the company processes the net cash to the employee.

Design the ESOP plan with the exercise-year tax in mind. Not just the vesting schedule.

## Post 8 (Week 8) — India-Singapore DTAA grandfathering

The 2016/2017 India-Singapore DTAA protocol closed the capital gains exemption on Indian equity.

For Singapore holdcos still exiting Indian portfolio companies with pre-April-2017 holdings, grandfathering preserves the exemption — but ONLY if:

→ Shares were acquired before 1 April 2017
→ The Singapore entity passes the LOB substance test (SGD 200K+ annual Singapore expenditure in the 12 months preceding, genuine operations, no "shell" profile)
→ MLI Principal Purpose Test (PPT) satisfied
→ Indian GAAR not triggered

Three sequential filters. Many 2010-vintage Singapore holdcos were set up with minimal substance (nominee director, outsourced accounting, no local employees) and fail at least one filter today.

Pre-exit diagnostics 60-90 days before the sale is the standard working-CA mandate. Audit substance, remediate gaps, document position before closing.

## Post 9 (Week 9) — Delaware franchise tax authorised shares trap

Why many Delaware C-Corps get a USD 180,000 franchise tax bill in year 1:

The Delaware "authorised shares method" calculates franchise tax at USD 400 base + USD 85 per 10,000 authorised shares.

Standard VC-track C-Corps authorise 10,000,000 shares to allow for ESOP, future rounds, etc. Authorised shares method: USD 400 + (10M / 10K) × USD 85 = USD 85,400 per year.

The alternative: "Assumed Par Value method" — calculates based on gross assets, usually produces a much smaller number for early-stage companies. Default minimum: USD 400.

Delaware allows you to use whichever method produces the LOWER tax. Most CAs file the lower one automatically, but the state's own calculator defaults to the authorised shares method unless you switch.

Free calculator (BQP built it): https://bharatquantumprospera.com/delaware-franchise-tax-calculator.html

Check which method applies to your entity before the 1 March deadline.

## Post 10 (Week 10) — QSBS Section 1202 for US-resident founders

The USD 10M+ US capital gains exclusion most Indian founders don't realise applies to them:

Section 1202 QSBS (Qualified Small Business Stock).

If a US-tax-resident founder holds C-Corp stock for 5+ years, up to USD 10M (or 10× basis, whichever is greater) of capital gains at exit can be fully excluded from US federal tax.

For an Indian founder who moves to the US on an H-1B or L-1 and holds Delaware C-Corp stock, this is live planning.

Key conditions:
→ Must be C-Corp stock (not LLC member interest, not S-Corp stock, not warrant)
→ Must be acquired at original issue (not secondary purchase)
→ 5-year holding period from acquisition
→ Company gross assets ≤ USD 50M when stock was issued
→ Active business (80%+ of assets in qualified trade or business)

For founders flipping to Delaware before US relocation: the QSBS clock starts at the flip date, not the Indian company incorporation date. Flip early, start the clock early.

## Post 11 (Week 11) — Transfer pricing documentation first-year decision

The first year of your inter-company arrangement sets the pattern for every year after.

If your Delaware C-Corp (customer) is being serviced by your Indian subsidiary (service provider), the cost-plus markup you choose in year 1 defines the Indian subsidiary's profitability and tax liability for every year after.

Market standards for routine services (back-office, engineering support): cost-plus 10-15%.

For IP-generating activities (R&D, product engineering with original work): higher markup (18-25%) often defensible, backed by functional analysis.

What's weak: cost-plus 5% ("minimising Indian tax") — Indian authorities routinely challenge below-market markups, retro-adjust the markup, raise demand with interest and penalty.

What's weak the other direction: cost-plus 30%+ ("defending Delaware tax") — hard to justify under US transfer pricing arm's-length, risks IRS adjustment.

Set the markup once, document it with a functional analysis under Section 92D India + US contemporaneous documentation under Section 482. Review every 3 years.

## Post 12 (Week 12) — BOIR / CTA current status for Indian owners

Quick status check on BOIR / CTA for Indian founders with Delaware entities:

The Corporate Transparency Act required beneficial-ownership reporting from most US entities. In 2024 the implementation was challenged in multiple federal courts.

March 2025 FinCEN interim final rule: domestic US entities (standard Delaware LLC / C-Corp owned by Indian founder) are currently EXEMPT from BOIR filing. The obligation now applies only to foreign reporting companies with non-US-citizen beneficial owners.

Current practical position for Indian founders:
→ Delaware LLC owned by Indian individual: domestic entity, not filing right now
→ Delaware C-Corp with Indian founder shareholders: same, not filing
→ Keep beneficial-ownership documentation internally so you can file within 30 days if the rule reverts

Monitor FinCEN updates. The final rule may restore domestic obligations. Entities that filed under the pre-2025 rule do not need to withdraw — filed BOIRs remain on record.
