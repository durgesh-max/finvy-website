Transfer pricing India, when parent is foreign.

Any Indian subsidiary of a foreign MNC with cross-border related-party transactions is in India's transfer pricing regime: Section 92 to 92F, Rules 10 to 10TE. Form 3CEB (Form 48 from tax year 2026-27) mandatory. Benchmarking study required. Scrutiny likelihood is high — India is one of the most TP-active jurisdictions globally with ~INR 50,000 crore+ in adjustments annually. For groups above the threshold, Master File + CbCR + BEPS Pillar 2. Here's the practitioner's framework.

/ When Indian TP applies

Associated Enterprise + International Transaction.

India's TP applies when:

  • An Indian company has transactions with an Associated Enterprise (AE), AND
  • The transaction qualifies as an International Transaction or Specified Domestic Transaction.

Associated Enterprise (Section 92A):

  • Direct or indirect participation in management, control, or capital (26%+ shareholding, 26%+ voting, etc.).
  • Common management / control of both enterprises.
  • Guarantee / loan providing > 51% total.
  • Appointment of > 50% of board members.

Indian subsidiary of US parent: AE. Indian subsidiary of UK parent via Singapore holdco: both US / UK / Singapore are AE.

International Transaction (Section 92B): any transaction between AEs that affects profits, income, losses, or assets — purchase/sale of goods, services, intangibles, lending/borrowing, guarantees, cost sharing, business restructuring.

Threshold for TP documentation: aggregate international transactions > INR 1 crore in the FY → Section 92D documentation required.

/ Form 3CEB (annual audit)

The core compliance.

Form 3CEB is a transfer pricing audit certificate issued by a Chartered Accountant, filed as part of the Indian company's annual tax return. Required for:

  • Every taxpayer with international transactions above INR 1 crore in the FY (effectively every foreign-subsidiary).
  • Due date: 31 October of the AY (vs 31 July for non-TP taxpayers).

Form 3CEB covers:

  • List of all international transactions with each AE — by nature (goods, services, intangibles, financing, cost allocation, business restructuring).
  • Transaction value in INR + description.
  • Arm's-length method chosen (CUP, Resale Price Method, Cost Plus Method, TNMM, PSM, Other Method).
  • Benchmarking analysis supporting arm's-length conclusion.
  • Material facts and specific adjustments / documentation.

Underlying documentation: Section 92D requires detailed TP documentation to be maintained (not filed, but produced if asked): entity profile, industry analysis, functional analysis, economic analysis / benchmarking study. Must be contemporaneous (prepared by the time of filing Form 3CEB, not after a scrutiny notice).

/ Master File + CbCR (for large groups)

BEPS compliance.

Finance Act 2016 introduced BEPS-aligned Master File and Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) requirements.

Master File (Form 3CEAA): required for any Indian entity which is part of an international group where:

  • Consolidated group revenue in preceding year > INR 500 crore (~USD 60 million), AND
  • Aggregate value of international transactions > INR 50 crore OR international transactions pertaining to intangibles > INR 10 crore.

Contents: group overview, business strategy, group's intangibles, group's intercompany financing, group's financial + tax position.

CbCR (Form 3CEAD): required for any ultimate parent of an international group with consolidated revenue > INR 6,400 crore (~EUR 750 million, aligned with BEPS Action 13).

  • Primary filing obligation on the ultimate parent (if India).
  • Secondary filing (Form 3CEAC & 3CEAD) on Indian subsidiary of foreign parent if parent country has no CbCR regime or no information-sharing MoU.

Contents: country-by-country breakdown of revenue, profit, tax paid, employees, tangible assets.

BEPS Pillar 2 (Global Minimum Tax, 15%): India implementing from FY 2025-26 for groups with consolidated revenue > EUR 750M. Separate GloBE compliance alongside TP. See our Pillar 2 guide.

/ APA (Advance Pricing Agreement)

Certainty for 5+5 years.

For taxpayers wanting certainty on TP position: Section 92CC Advance Pricing Agreement with CBDT.

  • Unilateral APA: with CBDT only.
  • Bilateral APA: between CBDT and competent authority of the other country (US, UK, Japan, Singapore — most useful).
  • Multilateral APA: three or more jurisdictions.

Duration: 5 years prospective + 4 years rollback = up to 9 years of certainty.

Benefits:

  • No TP scrutiny for covered transactions during APA period.
  • Removes double-taxation risk (bilateral APA).
  • Reduces litigation cost / management time.

Timeline: typically 24-36 months from filing to signing. Expensive upfront but valuable for groups with large recurring intercompany transactions.

/ Common TP mistakes foreign MNCs make in India

What triggers assessment.

  • Low operating margin on contract services: Indian subsidiary providing IT / BPO services to foreign parent at 10% net margin. Indian comparables typically show 15-18%. Addition likely at ~5% of cost base.
  • Royalty rate on licensed brand / technology: too low (parent extracting value) or too high (shifting profit to parent). Benchmarking against royalty databases (e.g., RoyaltyStat, ktMINE) + comparability adjustments.
  • Guarantee fee for parent's bank guarantee: Indian subsidiary paying nil or symbolic guarantee fee to parent for parent-provided guarantee. CBDT expects fee at ~0.5-1.5% of guaranteed amount.
  • Marketing intangibles (AMP / brand building): Indian subsidiary spending significantly on brand marketing in India but not getting marketing-service fee / IP reward from parent. 'Bright Line' test ADPCO cases → adjustment risk.
  • Cost allocation without substance: parent allocating group HQ costs to Indian subsidiary without proper allocation key / benefit test. CBDT typically disallows allocations lacking benefit test.
  • Weak benchmarking: using too few comparables, including non-comparable sized companies, cherry-picking inter-quartile range.
  • Not filing Master File / CbCR when applicable: group revenue above threshold but local Indian team not aware of global filing status.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Is Form 3CEB mandatory for every foreign-subsidiary in India?
Effectively yes - any Indian subsidiary of a foreign parent that has international transactions (parent charges management fee, royalty, interest, service fee, or buys/sells goods to Indian subsidiary) crosses the INR 1 crore threshold quickly. Form 3CEB mandatory alongside Form 3CD and ITR-6. Due 31 October of the AY.
What is Master File and when do I need it?
Form 3CEAA - detailed documentation of the entire international group (business, intangibles, intercompany financing, tax position). Required if (a) group consolidated revenue > INR 500 crore in preceding year AND (b) aggregate international transactions > INR 50 crore OR intangible-related > INR 10 crore. Filed by Indian subsidiary; prepared based on group data from parent.
What is CbCR and when does India require it?
Country-by-Country Report (Form 3CEAD) under BEPS Action 13 - country-wise breakdown of revenue, profit, tax paid, headcount, tangible assets for the whole international group. Required for groups with consolidated revenue > INR 6,400 crore (~EUR 750M). Primary filing by ultimate parent; secondary filing by Indian subsidiary if parent country has no CbCR regime.
Is APA worth the cost for a foreign MNC in India?
For groups with large recurring intercompany transactions (e.g., annual royalty > USD 5M, or IT services engagement with 1000+ Indian headcount), yes - 5+4 years of certainty + removal of double-taxation risk + reduced litigation outweigh the 24-36 month filing process + professional fees. For smaller / variable transactions, standard annual Form 3CEB may suffice.
How much is a typical TP adjustment in India?
Varies widely by industry and transaction type. For IT services with low Indian margin: adjustment at 2-8% of cost base. For royalty: often 100% disallowance if not benchmarked. For guarantee fee: 0.5-1.5% of guaranteed amount addition. Across India, CBDT makes ~INR 50,000 crore+ in TP adjustments annually across all taxpayers.
