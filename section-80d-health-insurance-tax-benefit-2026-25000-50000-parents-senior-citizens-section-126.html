/ Families and senior citizens · Income tax · Updated 2026-11-25

Health Insurance Tax Benefit 2026 (Section 80D, Now Section 126): Rs 25,000 / Rs 50,000 Limits, Parents, Senior Citizens and New Regime, up to Rs 1 lakh a year, but only in the old regime.

Health insurance premiums paid for yourself, your spouse and children are deductible up to ₹25,000 a year under section 80D (₹50,000 if you are a senior citizen), with a further ₹25,000 for your parents (₹50,000 if a parent is a senior citizen), so the maximum is ₹1 lakh. The deduction is available only under the old tax regime. From tax year 2026-27 it sits in section 126 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, with the same limits.

/ Worked example (illustrative)

  • Family floater premium for self, spouse and children: ₹30,000, deduction ₹25,000 (cap).
  • Parents’ senior citizen policy: ₹55,000, deduction ₹50,000 (cap).
  • Total deduction ₹75,000; tax saved at 30% plus 4% cess = ₹23,400.

/ Rules and common questions

What counts and what does not.

  • Payment mode: premiums must be paid by any mode other than cash; only the preventive check-up can be paid in cash.
  • Multi-year policies: a premium paid upfront for several years is deducted proportionately each year.
  • Who can pay: you can claim for premiums you pay for your parents even if they are not dependent on you; premiums for in-laws, siblings or grandparents do not qualify.
  • Employer group cover: health insurance bought by your employer for you is generally not a taxable perquisite, but you cannot also claim it under 80D; top-ups you pay yourself do qualify.
  • New regime: no 80D deduction; Budget 2026 did not change this.
  • In the return: report the insurer and policy details the ITR form asks for and keep the premium receipts.
  • NRIs and returning seniors: see NRI senior citizen tax and the 80C deduction list.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

How much tax can I save on health insurance?
Under the old regime you can deduct up to Rs 25,000 for yourself, spouse and children (Rs 50,000 if you are a senior citizen) and a further Rs 25,000 for parents (Rs 50,000 if they are senior citizens), up to Rs 1 lakh a year in total.
Is health insurance deductible under the new tax regime?
No. Section 80D (section 126 of the Income-tax Act, 2025) is available only under the old regime.
Is employer-provided medical insurance a taxable benefit?
Generally no. Premium paid by your employer for group health cover is not taxed as a perquisite, but you cannot claim 80D for it; premiums you pay yourself, such as a top-up or parents' policy, can be claimed.
Can I claim 80D for my parents' health insurance?
Yes, even if they are not dependent on you: up to Rs 25,000, or Rs 50,000 if either parent is 60 or older. Premiums for in-laws do not qualify for you, though your spouse can claim for their own parents.
Is a preventive health check-up deductible?
Yes, up to Rs 5,000 a year within the overall 80D limit, and it can be paid in cash.
