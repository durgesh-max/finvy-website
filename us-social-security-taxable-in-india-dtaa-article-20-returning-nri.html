Is US Social Security Taxable in India? Article 20(2) of the India-US Tax Treaty for Returning NRIs (2026), the US taxes it; India does not.

No. Under Article 20(2) of the India-US tax treaty, US Social Security paid to a resident of India is taxable only in the United States, even after you become resident and ordinarily resident in India. What changes is the US side: if you are no longer a US citizen or green card holder, the Social Security Administration usually withholds 30% on 85% of each payment, an effective 25.5%. In your Indian return you show it as exempt under the treaty, and the US bank account it is paid into still goes in Schedule FA.

The account it lands in still counts

India does not tax the benefit, but once you are resident and ordinarily resident the US bank account it is paid into belongs in Schedule FA, and the interest on it is taxable in India. / In your Indian return

What to do every year.

  • Show the benefit as exempt income under the India-US treaty; do not add it to taxable income. Keep the SSA-1042S or SSA-1099 for the year.
  • No foreign tax credit for it in India: India does not tax the benefit, so there is nothing to set the US tax against. Form 67 is needed only for foreign income India does tax, such as US bank interest or dividends.
  • Schedule FA: once you are resident and ordinarily resident, report the US bank account the benefit is credited to (calendar-year basis), along with any other US accounts, IRAs or 401(k)s.
  • RNOR years: in your first years back you may be resident but not ordinarily resident; Schedule FA does not apply then, and foreign income outside India is generally not taxed. Check with the residential status calculator.
  • A refund of over-withheld US tax (for example on a government-work benefit) is claimed from the IRS on Form 1040-NR, not in India.

/ Points people miss

Before you assume it is all simple.

  • Social Security is not the same as a 401(k) or IRA. Withdrawals from a 401(k) or IRA are not covered by Article 20(2) and can be taxable in India once you are resident and ordinarily resident; Form 40 may defer it.
  • There is no US-India totalization agreement, so work years in India do not count towards the 40 US credits, and Indian Provident Fund rules are separate.
  • US state tax is a separate question from federal tax and the treaty.
  • Interest earned on the benefit after it reaches a US or Indian bank account is ordinary income and taxable in India when you are resident.

Sources: Convention between India and the United States for the avoidance of double taxation, Article 20(2) (and Article 19); US Social Security Administration, nonresident alien tax withholding guidance; IRS Publication 915. FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Is US Social Security taxable in India?
No. Under Article 20(2) of the India-US tax treaty, Social Security paid by the US to a resident of India is taxable only in the US. Show it as exempt income in your Indian return.
How much US tax is withheld from Social Security if I live in India?
If you are not a US citizen or green card holder, the SSA usually withholds 30% on 85% of the benefit, an effective 25.5%. US citizens and green card holders are taxed under normal US rules instead.
Can I claim the US tax as a credit in India?
No credit is needed for the benefit itself, because India does not tax it. Form 67 and foreign tax credit apply to foreign income India does tax, such as US interest or dividends.
Do I report the US bank account in Schedule FA?
Yes, once you are resident and ordinarily resident. Report the account the benefit is paid into, on a calendar-year basis. Missed accounts can be fixed through FAST-DS until 31 December 2026.
Is a 401(k) or IRA treated the same way?
No. Article 20(2) covers Social Security and public pensions, not 401(k) or IRA withdrawals, which can be taxable in India once you are resident and ordinarily resident. Form 40 may defer the tax.
