NRI Capital Gains Tax on Indian Shares (2026): LTCG 12.5% Above INR 1.25 Lakh, STCG 20%, TDS by the Broker, Treaty Relief and Getting Refunds, for NRIs trading or investing in Indian stocks from abroad.

NRIs pay the same capital gains rates on listed Indian shares as residents. Gains on shares held over 12 months are long-term and taxed at 12.5% above INR 1.25 lakh a year; shorter holdings are taxed at 20%, plus surcharge and cess in both cases. The big difference is TDS: for NRIs the broker deducts tax on the gain at the time of sale, often more than the final tax. NRIs also cannot use the basic exemption limit against these gains, which residents can. Most tax treaties let India tax gains on Indian shares, so treaty relief is limited. Older shares bought by Singapore and Mauritius residents before 1 April 2017 are the main exception. Your country of residence may also tax the gain and give credit for Indian tax. To recover excess TDS and carry forward losses, file an Indian return. For FY 2025-26 that means a belated or revised return by 31 December 2026.

/ Accounts, treaty and home-country tax

What else changes for NRIs.

  • Accounts: repatriable investing is done through NRE-linked accounts designated for portfolio investment; non-repatriable investing through NRO accounts; proceeds follow the source account's repatriation rules; see NRE, NRO and FCNR.
  • KYC from abroad: from 3 December 2026 intermediaries can complete KYC digitally for NRIs and OCIs in FATF-compliant countries; see digital KYC from abroad.
  • Treaties: India generally keeps the right to tax gains on Indian shares; grandfathering for Singapore and Mauritius residents covers only shares bought before 1 April 2017; mutual fund units can be treated differently under some treaties; see NRI mutual funds and India-UAE treaty.
  • Home country: US, UK, Canadian and Australian residents report the gain at home and claim credit for Indian tax; Gulf residents pay no tax at home; see how home countries tax Indian income.
  • Trading derivatives: futures and options profits are business income, taxed at slab rates, with audit thresholds to watch.

/ Getting excess TDS back

Before 31 December 2026.

  • Download Form 26AS and AIS and match TDS with the broker's statement.
  • File ITR-2 (ITR-3 if you trade derivatives) for FY 2025-26 as a belated or revised return by 31 December 2026, claiming treaty or slab benefits and set-offs.
  • After 31 December only an updated return is possible, which cannot claim a refund.
  • Keep a lower deduction certificate in mind for large planned sales.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is the capital gains tax for NRIs on Indian shares?
Listed shares held over 12 months: 12.5% on gains above INR 1.25 lakh a year; held 12 months or less: 20%; plus surcharge and cess, the same rates as residents.
Is TDS deducted on an NRI's share sales?
Yes. The broker deducts TDS on the capital gain for NRIs at the time of sale, which can exceed the final tax; file a return to claim the excess back.
Can NRIs use the basic exemption limit against share gains?
No. Only residents can adjust the unused basic exemption against short-term and long-term gains taxed at special rates.
Do tax treaties exempt NRI share gains from Indian tax?
Generally not; most treaties let India tax gains on Indian shares. Singapore and Mauritius grandfathering covers only shares bought before 1 April 2017.
How can an NRI get back excess TDS on share sales?
File ITR-2 (or ITR-3 for derivatives) for the year; for FY 2025-26 a belated or revised return is possible until 31 December 2026.
