/ Sellers of a house, land or other assets claiming section 54 / 54F / 54B · Residents and NRIs · Updated 2026-11-27

Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS) 1988: Time Limit, Account A vs Account B, Forms, Withdrawal, Closure and NRIs (2026), deposit before your return is due.

If you have sold a house or other asset and plan to claim a section 54, 54F or 54B exemption, but have not bought or built the new asset by the due date of your income tax return, the unused gain must be deposited in the Capital Gains Account Scheme before that due date - otherwise the exemption is lost for that amount. The money then has to be used for the new asset within the time the section allows; whatever is still unused at the end of that period is taxed as a capital gain in that year.

/ How the scheme works

Two accounts, one deadline.

PointRule
Who uses itAnyone claiming exemption under sections 54, 54B, 54D, 54F and related provisions who has not reinvested the full amount by the return due date
Deadline to depositBefore the due date of the return for the year of sale (section 139(1) due date)
Account-ASavings-type account for money you expect to draw soon
Account-BTerm-deposit account for money not needed for a while; withdrawals go through Account-A
WhereNotified branches of authorised banks that offer the scheme
InterestTaxable in the year it accrues
  • Forms: Form A to open the account; Form C to withdraw; Form D to show how the previous withdrawal was used; Form G to close (Form H for a nominee or legal heir).
  • Use within 60 days: money withdrawn must be spent on the new asset within 60 days of withdrawal.
  • Show it in the return: the deposit, with the account details, supports the exemption claimed in the return for the year of sale.

/ Time limits

What happens if the money is not used.

  • Section 54 (house to house): buy within two years or build within three years of the sale; the deposit only keeps that window open.
  • Unused balance: any amount not used by the end of the period is taxed as long-term capital gain of the year in which the period ends.
  • Closing the account: closure needs the Assessing Officer's approval, and the unused amount is taxed as above.
  • Late deposit: a deposit made after the return due date does not protect the exemption - check the date before you file.

/ NRIs and special cases

Where people get stuck.

  • NRIs selling Indian property can use the scheme in the same way to keep a section 54 or 54F claim open; the buyer still deducts TDS on the price unless a Form 128 lower deduction certificate is obtained.
  • Joint owners: each owner deposits and claims for their own share of the gain.
  • Death of the depositor: the nominee or legal heir closes the account with Form H; the unused amount is not taxed in their hands as the depositor's capital gain.
  • 54EC bonds are a separate route with their own six-month deadline - the scheme does not replace them.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is the Capital Gains Account Scheme?
A deposit scheme under the Income-tax Act that lets you park the part of a capital gain you have not yet reinvested, so that a section 54, 54F or 54B exemption is not lost just because the return is due before the new house or asset is bought or built. The money must later be used for the new asset within the time the section allows.
What is the time limit to deposit in the Capital Gains Account Scheme?
Before the due date of your income tax return for the year of the sale. A deposit made after that date does not protect the exemption for that amount.
What is the difference between Account A and Account B?
Account-A works like a savings account for money you expect to draw soon; Account-B is a term deposit for money you will not need for some time. To withdraw from Account-B, the amount is first moved to Account-A.
Which banks offer the Capital Gains Account Scheme?
Notified branches of authorised banks that run the scheme. Not every branch handles it, so check with the bank's branch before the deadline rather than on the last day.
What happens if I do not use the money in the capital gains account?
Whatever is still unused when the section's period ends - two years for buying or three years for building under section 54 - is taxed as long-term capital gain in the year the period ends. Closing the account needs the Assessing Officer's approval.
Can an NRI open a Capital Gains Account Scheme account?
Yes. An NRI selling Indian property can deposit the unused gain in the scheme to keep a section 54 or 54F claim open, on the same rules. TDS on the sale is separate and is deducted on the price unless a Form 128 lower deduction certificate is obtained.
