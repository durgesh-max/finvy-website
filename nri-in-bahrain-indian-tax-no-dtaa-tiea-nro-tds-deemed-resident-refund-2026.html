NRI in Bahrain: How India Taxes Your Indian Income Without a DTAA (2026) - NRO TDS, Refunds, Deemed Residency and the TIEA, no treaty, so plan the Indian side.

India and Bahrain have no double taxation agreement - only a Tax Information Exchange Agreement, in force since April 2013. So an NRI in Bahrain gets no treaty rate on Indian income: TDS is deducted at the full non-resident rate, and the excess comes back only through an Indian income tax return. Bahrain does not tax your salary, so there is usually no double tax to relieve - the work is on the Indian side: refunds, residency and reporting.

  • Refunds: TDS on NRO interest or rent is often more than the tax actually due once slab rates and deductions apply; the excess is refunded only when you file the Indian return.

/ Deemed residency: the INR 15 lakh rule

Why Bahrain residents should check it.

  • The rule: an Indian citizen whose Indian income (other than foreign-source income) is above INR 15 lakh in a year, and who is not liable to tax in any country, is deemed resident in India.
  • Why it matters in Bahrain: because Bahrain has no personal income tax, NRIs with large Indian income - rent, interest, gains - should check whether this applies.
  • What it changes: a deemed resident is treated as resident but not ordinarily resident (RNOR), so Indian income stays taxable; the CBDT clarified in 2020 that salary earned abroad by Indian citizens working there is not taxed because of this rule.
  • Below the threshold, or not an Indian citizen (for example an OCI cardholder): the rule does not apply.

/ How to handle it

Each year.

  • Keep your day count: passport stamps or travel history for the 182-day and 120-day tests.
  • Route income correctly: rent and Indian income to the NRO account; NRE for money brought from Bahrain.
  • File the Indian return (usually ITR-2) when there is Indian income or TDS to recover, by the due date or as a belated return by 31 December.
  • Before selling property: apply for a lower TDS certificate so the buyer deducts on the real gain, not the whole price.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Is there a DTAA between India and Bahrain?
No. India and Bahrain have only a Tax Information Exchange Agreement, signed on 31 May 2012 and in force from 11 April 2013, under which the tax authorities exchange information on request. There is no comprehensive double taxation agreement, so Indian income of an NRI in Bahrain is taxed at Indian domestic rates.
How much TDS is deducted on NRO interest for an NRI in Bahrain?
The full non-resident rate - 31.2% in most cases - because there is no treaty rate to apply. If your actual tax is lower, the excess is refunded when you file an Indian income tax return.
Is my Bahrain salary taxed in India?
Not while you are non-resident. Even if the deemed-residency rule applies because your Indian income is above INR 15 lakh, the CBDT has clarified that salary earned abroad by Indian citizens working there is not taxed in India because of that rule.
Do I need a tax residency certificate from Bahrain?
Not for treaty relief, because there is no treaty. Proof that you live in Bahrain, together with your day count, still helps show that you are a non-resident for Indian tax.
Are my Indian mutual fund gains taxable if I live in Bahrain?
Yes. Without a treaty, capital gains on Indian shares and mutual funds are taxed in India at Indian rates, and the fund house or broker deducts TDS on redemption. Any excess TDS is refunded through the Indian return.
