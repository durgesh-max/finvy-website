/ Foreign companies · Digital · Tax · Updated 2026-11-21

Equalisation Levy in India: Abolished (2026) - the 6% Ad Levy Ended 1 April 2025, the 2% E-Commerce Levy Ended 1 August 2024, and What Applies Now, India no longer has a digital services tax.

India has abolished the equalisation levy. The 2% levy on foreign e-commerce operators ended on 1 August 2024, and the 6% levy on online advertising paid to foreign companies ended on 1 April 2025. India therefore has no digital services tax today. That does not mean Indian revenue of a foreign digital business is tax-free. It now falls under the ordinary rules: whether the foreign company has a permanent establishment or a significant economic presence in India, whether the payment is a royalty or technical service fee, and whether the Indian customer must withhold tax. GST still applies: Indian businesses pay it under reverse charge on imported services, and foreign companies selling digital services to Indian consumers must register. Levy dues and filings for the years before abolition can still be examined.

/ What it was and when it ended

The two levies.

6% levy (online advertising)2% levy (e-commerce)
Started1 June 20161 April 2020
Charged onPayments by Indian businesses to non-residents for online advertising and ad spaceSales of goods and services into India by non-resident e-commerce operators
ThresholdPayments above INR 1 lakh a year to one non-residentIndian sales above INR 2 crore a year
Who paid it to the governmentThe Indian payer, by deducting it from the paymentThe foreign operator, every quarter
Ended1 April 2025 (Finance Act, 2025)1 August 2024 (Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024)

Income that bore the levy was exempt from Indian income tax. With the levy gone, that exemption has ended too, so the income is tested under the normal income-tax rules.

/ What applies now

For foreign sellers and Indian buyers.

  • Foreign company in a treaty country, no presence in India: business income is taxable in India only through a permanent establishment. To rely on the treaty it needs a tax residency certificate and Form 41 (formerly Form 10F), and customers usually ask for a no-PE declaration.
  • Foreign company with no treaty protection: the significant economic presence rule can tax it without any physical presence: payments from India above INR 2 crore a year, or 3 lakh Indian users. See significant economic presence.
  • Royalty or technical service fee: software licences, some cloud and platform fees and support services may be classed this way and taxed on the gross amount, usually at 10% to 15% under a treaty. See the TDS calculator for payments to foreign companies.
  • Indian business buying ads from a foreign platform: the 6% deduction has stopped for payments from 1 April 2025. The payer must still decide whether income-tax withholding applies and keep the vendor’s documents; the bank asks for Form 145 and, where needed, Form 146.
  • GST: an Indian business pays 18% GST under reverse charge on imported services and normally claims it as credit. A foreign company supplying online services to Indian consumers registers and pays GST itself.
  • Past years: levy for periods before abolition, the annual levy statement and any interest remain open to scrutiny. An Indian payer that did not deduct the 6% levy in those years can have the expense disallowed.

/ Practical points

Common questions from finance teams.

  • Is anything due on ad spend with foreign platforms in 2026? No equalisation levy. Check income-tax withholding once per vendor, and account for GST under reverse charge.
  • Did abolition make foreign platforms cheaper? Where the platform had added the 6% to invoices, that cost has gone. Contracts with a gross-up clause should be checked.
  • Does the old exemption still protect us? No. Income that used to be exempt because the levy was paid is now looked at afresh.
  • Selling to Indian consumers through an app or marketplace: income tax depends on treaty and presence; GST registration depends on what is supplied and to whom. See GST for foreign online service providers.
  • An Indian subsidiary or team changes the answer: people in India who sell or conclude contracts can create a permanent establishment. See the PE risk guide.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Is the equalisation levy still applicable in India?
No. The 2% levy on non-resident e-commerce operators was withdrawn from 1 August 2024 and the 6% levy on online advertising from 1 April 2025. No equalisation levy applies to transactions after those dates.
What was the equalisation levy rate in India?
6% on payments to non-residents for online advertising (from 1 June 2016 to 31 March 2025) and 2% on sales into India by non-resident e-commerce operators (from 1 April 2020 to 31 July 2024).
Does India have a digital services tax?
Not any more. With the equalisation levy abolished, foreign digital businesses are taxed in India only under the ordinary income-tax rules, such as permanent establishment, significant economic presence and royalty or technical service fees, plus GST on the supply.
Do Indian companies still deduct 6% on payments to Google or Meta entities abroad?
No, not for payments from 1 April 2025. The payer should still check whether income-tax withholding applies, keep the vendor's tax residency certificate and Form 41, and pay GST under reverse charge.
How is a foreign digital company taxed in India after the equalisation levy?
If it is resident in a treaty country and has no permanent establishment in India, its business income is generally not taxed here. Without treaty protection, significant economic presence can apply above INR 2 crore of Indian payments or 3 lakh users. Royalties and technical service fees are taxed on the gross amount at treaty or domestic rates.
Can old equalisation levy periods still be questioned?
Yes. Levy, statements and interest for periods before abolition can be examined, and an Indian payer that failed to deduct the 6% levy in those years risks disallowance of the expense.
