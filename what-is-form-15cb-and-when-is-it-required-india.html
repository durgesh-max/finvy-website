What Is Form 15CB and When Is It Required in India, the CA certificate for foreign remittances.

Direct answer: Form 15CB is a CA certificate under Section 195 of the Indian Income Tax Act, certifying that TDS on foreign remittance has been correctly computed per applicable Indian income tax law + DTAA treaty. It's required for most foreign remittances by Indian resident to non-resident where chargeable to Indian tax. Required by AD-I category bank before releasing SWIFT. / What Form 15CB certifies

The CA's attestation.

  • Nature of remittance (dividend / interest / royalty / FTS / rent / professional service / specific).
  • Chargeability under Section 9 (business connection / royalty / FTS / interest / dividend / capital gain).
  • Applicable Indian income tax rate (Section 115A for specific types).
  • Applicable DTAA treaty rate (if beneficial + TRC + Form 10F available).
  • Specific MLI PPT / GAAR applicability (if specific scenarios).
  • TDS amount deducted or to be deducted.
  • Beneficial ownership + treaty entitlement.
  • CA's professional assurance + UDIN.

/ When Form 15CB is required

The trigger conditions.

  • Default rule: for any remittance exceeding INR 5 lakh in aggregate during FY + chargeable to Indian tax: Form 15CB required.
  • Form 15CA Part A: aggregate remittance less than INR 5 lakh - 15CA Part A only + no Form 15CB needed.
  • Form 15CA Part B: specific LDC under Section 197 obtained - 15CA Part B + CA certification not needed (LDC covers it).
  • Form 15CA Part C: aggregate remittance > INR 5 lakh + no LDC - Form 15CB required + 15CA Part C filed.
  • Form 15CA Part D: remittance not chargeable to tax (specific exempt categories) - 15CA Part D + no Form 15CB needed.

/ Common scenarios requiring Form 15CB

What triggers CA involvement.

  • Dividend to foreign parent / shareholder.
  • Interest to foreign lender.
  • Royalty to foreign IP owner.
  • Fees for Technical Services (FTS) to foreign consultant.
  • Rent to foreign landlord (specific if NRI landlord selling / leasing Indian property).
  • Professional fees to foreign professional.
  • Software licence to foreign vendor (specific case-law on royalty vs business income).
  • Specific capital account remittance / repatriation.
  • NRI's rental income repatriation from Indian property.
  • Specific commissions + brokerages.
  • Specific insurance premia to foreign insurer.

/ Documents needed for Form 15CB

Fast collection.

  • Invoice / agreement supporting the remittance (commercial contract).
  • Payee's TRC (Tax Residency Certificate) from home country tax authority.
  • Payee's Form 10F (electronic filed via India e-filing portal) - critical for treaty rate.
  • Payee's Indian PAN (if available) - without PAN, Section 206AA 20% rate applies.
  • No-PE declaration from payee (if claiming business income exemption).
  • Board resolution authorising remittance.
  • FIRC (Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate) if money has flowed inward previously (for repatriation context).
  • Prior Form 15CA + 15CB history (if recurring remittance).

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is Form 15CB?
Form 15CB is a CA certificate under Section 195 Indian Income Tax Act. It certifies that TDS on foreign remittance has been correctly computed per Indian income tax law + applicable DTAA treaty. Signed by ICAI-qualified CA with UDIN. Required by AD-I category bank before releasing foreign currency SWIFT.
When is Form 15CB required?
For foreign remittances exceeding INR 5 lakh aggregate during FY + chargeable to Indian tax, Form 15CB is required (filed with Form 15CA Part C). If aggregate < INR 5 lakh: only Form 15CA Part A, no 15CB. If LDC under Section 197 obtained: Form 15CA Part B, no 15CB. If exempt category: Form 15CA Part D, no 15CB.
