Agricultural Income Tax in India 2026: Is It Taxable, Partial Integration Above Rs 5,000, Calculation and Farmland Sale, exempt itself, but it can raise the tax on everything else.

Agricultural income from land in India, such as income from growing and selling crops, is exempt from income tax, but if it is more than ₹5,000 and your other income is above the basic exemption limit, it is used to push your other income into higher tax slabs through partial integration. The tax is worked out as the tax on agricultural plus non-agricultural income, minus the tax on agricultural income plus the basic exemption limit, under whichever regime you use. Selling rural agricultural land is not taxed as capital gains, but urban agricultural land is.

/ Quick answers

What people search for.

  • Is agricultural income taxable in India? No, it is exempt, but it is included for rate purposes when it exceeds ₹5,000 and other income exceeds the basic exemption limit.
  • What counts as agricultural income? Rent or revenue from agricultural land in India, income from cultivation and the sale of produce in a form that is marketable, and income from farm buildings used for agriculture. Dairy, poultry, fisheries and processed products beyond basic marketability are generally not agricultural income.
  • Does partial integration apply under the new regime? Yes, it applies under both regimes, using that regime’s slabs and basic exemption (₹4 lakh in the new regime).
  • Where is it reported? In the exempt income schedule of the return; with agricultural income above ₹5,000, use ITR-2 or ITR-3 rather than ITR-1.
  • Is selling agricultural land taxable? Rural agricultural land (outside municipal limits and beyond 2, 6 or 8 km depending on population) is not a capital asset, so no capital gains tax; urban agricultural land is taxed as capital gains, with relief under section 54B on reinvestment in agricultural land.
  • NRIs: see NRIs and agricultural land for FEMA limits on buying and inheriting farmland.

/ Practical points

Avoid the common mistakes.

  • Keep evidence: land records, crop sale receipts (mandi slips or invoices) and expenses, because large agricultural income claims are scrutinised.
  • Rent from farmland: rent from agricultural land is agricultural income; rent from a farmhouse not used for agriculture is house property income.
  • Large claims draw notices: unusually high agricultural income compared with landholding is a common scrutiny trigger; see scrutiny notices.
  • State levies: agricultural income tax is a state subject; check whether your state levies any tax on plantation or agricultural income.
  • Selling farmland: confirm rural or urban status from municipal limits and distance before the sale; see capital gains exemptions.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Is agricultural income taxable in India?
No. Agricultural income from land in India is exempt from income tax, but if it exceeds Rs 5,000 and your other income is above the basic exemption limit, it is aggregated to decide the tax rate on your other income (partial integration).
How is tax on agricultural income calculated?
Compute tax on non-agricultural plus agricultural income, then subtract the tax on agricultural income plus the basic exemption limit. The difference, plus cess, is the tax payable. The method applies under both the old and new regimes.
Does partial integration apply in the new tax regime?
Yes. It uses the new regime slabs and its Rs 4 lakh basic exemption.
Is sale of agricultural land taxable?
Rural agricultural land outside municipal limits and beyond the 2, 6 or 8 km band is not a capital asset, so its sale is not taxed as capital gains. Urban agricultural land is taxed as capital gains, with relief under section 54B for reinvestment in agricultural land.
Which ITR do I file with agricultural income?
If agricultural income is up to Rs 5,000, ITR-1 may be used where otherwise eligible; above that, ITR-2 (or ITR-3 with business income), reporting it in the exempt income schedule.
