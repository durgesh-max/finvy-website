/ Green Card Returning to India · Updated 2026-10-23

CA for US Green Card Holder Returning to India (Tax Step-by-Step), RNOR + US retention + dual-tax coordination.

US green card holder returning to India faces specific dual-tax complexity: RNOR (Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident) status for 2-3 years + continuing US tax obligations (Form 1040 global income + FBAR + Form 8938) + specific DTAA FTC coordination + potential Section 877A exit tax if later surrendering green card. / Pre-return planning (6-12 months before)

Window of opportunity.

  • Final year of US-only tax residence: optimise US position (401k contributions, specific gains / losses, charitable giving timing).
  • Decision: keep green card or surrender before return (Section 877A exit tax implications).
  • If keeping: understand ongoing US tax obligations (worldwide income reporting + FBAR + Form 8938).
  • If surrendering: Section 877A exit tax if 'covered expatriate' (net worth > USD 2M, or 5-year avg tax > USD 206K in 2026, or fails 5-year certification).
  • Indian NRE/NRO account setup if not already.
  • Indian investment transfer planning: foreign to Indian structure optimisation.

/ Return year: RNOR qualification

The 2-3 year window.

  • Section 6 residence: physically in India 182+ days in return FY (or 60+ days + 365+ days in preceding 4 FYs) → Indian tax resident.
  • Section 6(6): RNOR if non-resident for 9+ of 10 preceding FYs OR physically in India ≤ 729 days in 7 preceding FYs. Returning NRI typically qualifies RNOR 2-3 years.
  • During RNOR: foreign-source investment income + overseas employment / profession income NOT taxable in India. Only India-source + foreign business controlled from India + foreign profession set up in India taxable.
  • Opportunity: large-value foreign-source income (US capital gains, US salary bonus, US pension distribution) remains India tax-free during RNOR.
  • Use RNOR to repatriate funds + convert accounts without Indian tax.

/ Account conversion workflow

FEMA residence shift.

  1. Notify bank: change of status declaration + supporting documents (Indian address + arrival date + employment letter).
  2. NRE/NRO/FCNR conversion:
  3.   — NRO → Regular Savings (same account, status re-designated).
  4.   — NRE → Regular Savings OR RFC (Resident Foreign Currency - preserves foreign currency).
  5.   — FCNR → continues till maturity; then transfer to RFC.
  6. PIS (Portfolio Investment Scheme) account conversion.
  7. Mutual fund + demat KYC update to resident.
  8. Insurance policy + credit card + loan account status update.

/ US tax continuity (if keeping green card)

The ongoing framework.

  • Form 1040 US: continue annual filing on worldwide income.
  • Foreign Tax Credit (FTC) via Form 1116: credit for Indian tax paid on same income - prevents double tax.
  • FBAR (FinCEN Form 114): all Indian financial accounts aggregating > USD 10K.
  • Form 8938 (FATCA): Indian financial assets > USD 50K/100K.
  • Indian NRE / NRO / FCNR / mutual fund / shares - all reportable.
  • Indian PF (Provident Fund) complex treatment - foreign pension classification.
  • State tax if US domicile not broken.

/ Section 877A exit tax (if later surrender)

If green card given up.

  • Section 877A covered expatriate: net worth > USD 2M, 5-year avg tax > USD 206K (2026), fails 5-year certification.
  • Mark-to-market: all worldwide assets deemed sold at FMV on day before expatriation.
  • Capital gain tax on built-in gain + USD 866K exclusion (2026).
  • Specific IRA / 401k / deferred compensation rules.
  • Pre-expatriation planning: gifting to US-citizen spouse / children, timing specific assets, specific trust structuring, Section 1014 step-up planning.
  • Form 8854 Initial and Annual Expatriation Statement.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Do I need to surrender green card when returning to India?
No - green card can be maintained even while living abroad long-term (with specific caveats: physical absence > 1 year without re-entry permit creates presumption of abandonment; USCIS may question continued LPR at port of entry if India stay prolonged). But US tax obligations continue regardless. Many choose to maintain LPR + handle US tax; others surrender via Section 877A.
What's RNOR and how long does it last?
Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (Section 6(6)): typically available 2-3 years post-return. Foreign-source investment income + overseas employment / profession income NOT taxable in India during RNOR. Only India-source + specific income taxable. Massive planning window for returning NRI.
How do I avoid double tax as returning green card holder?
India-US DTAA + Foreign Tax Credit (FTC). Pay Indian tax on India-source income + treaty rate on specific income types. US Form 1116 FTC credits Indian tax paid on same income. Net: higher of two tax rates on each income type - no double tax. Requires coordinated India + US tax filing.
Can I keep NRE/FCNR accounts after returning?
Not permanently - FEMA requires conversion within reasonable time of becoming resident. NRE → convert to Regular Savings or RFC (Resident Foreign Currency - preserves foreign currency + repatriable). FCNR → continues till maturity, then RFC. RFC specifically designed for returning NRIs who want foreign currency continuity.
Should I pre-plan Section 877A exit tax?
If net worth > USD 2M + considering surrendering green card: YES - pre-planning essential. Mark-to-market at expatriation; large tax on built-in gains. Strategies: gifting to US-citizen spouse (unlimited marital deduction), gifting under annual exclusion before trigger, specific trust structuring, Section 1014 step-up planning. 2-3 year planning window ideal.
