SEBI's SME IPO Overhaul: What the October 2026 Consultation Paper Proposes, ₹3 crore average profit, ₹1,000 crore market cap, 500 allottees.

On 14 October 2026 SEBI published a consultation paper proposing a major overhaul of SME IPO rules: a higher profit test (average operating profit of ₹3 crore over three years), eligibility based on market capitalisation, a lower ₹1 lakh minimum application, 500 minimum allottees and no compulsory market making. Comments are due by 4 November 2026. Until SEBI notifies final amendments, the March 2025 rules continue to apply.

/ What it means if you are planning an SME IPO

Practical points.

  • Companies that meet today's test (₹1 crore operating profit in 2 of 3 years) but not a ₹3 crore three-year average should consider filing before any amendment takes effect, or plan for the QIB route.
  • Companies converted from a partnership, proprietorship or LLP may find the proposed four-quarter rule shorter than today's full-year wait.
  • Larger companies (market cap above ₹1,000 crore) would need Ind AS restated consolidated financials if they choose the SME platform, and must go to the main board above ₹4,000 crore.
  • Restated, audited financials, clean GST and TDS records, and tidy share-capital history (demat under Rule 9B, FEMA filings for foreign holders) matter under either set of rules.
  • You can send comments to SEBI by 4 November 2026.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What did SEBI propose for SME IPOs in October 2026?
A consultation paper dated 14 October 2026 proposes an average operating profit of ₹3 crore over three years, market-cap-based eligibility (SME platform up to ₹1,000 crore), a ₹1 lakh minimum application, 500 minimum allottees, removal of compulsory market making, and a QIB route for issuers that miss the profit test.
Are the new SME IPO rules in force?
No. They are proposals; comments are due by 4 November 2026. The SEBI ICDR rules as amended in March 2025 apply until SEBI notifies amendments.
What is the current SME IPO profit requirement?
Operating profit (EBITDA) of at least ₹1 crore in at least two of the preceding three financial years.
What happens to the minimum application size?
It is proposed to fall from ₹2 lakh to ₹1 lakh, made up of four lots of at least ₹25,000 each.
