NRI Year-End Tax Checklist Before 31 December 2026: Claim Refunds, File Belated or Revised ITR, FAST-DS, Advance Tax and Your India Day Count, six things to do before the year closes.

Six things NRIs should check before 31 December 2026. First, file a belated or revised return for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27) to claim back excess TDS on NRO interest, rent or a property sale; after 31 December only an updated return is possible, which carries additional tax of 25% to 70% and cannot claim a refund. Second, pay advance tax by 15 December if your FY 2026-27 Indian tax after TDS will exceed INR 10,000, for example on capital gains. Third, returning NRIs who held unreported foreign assets while resident should consider FAST-DS 2026, open until 31 December. Fourth, count your India days if you are visiting for the holidays: 182 days, or 120 days for Indian citizens with Indian income above INR 15 lakh, can make you resident. Fifth, get your tax residency certificate and Form 10F ready for treaty rates. Sixth, apply for a lower deduction certificate before any property sale.

/ Refunds: why 31 December matters

Updated returns cannot get you money back.

  • Common refund sources: TDS on property sale (deducted on the full price unless a lower deduction certificate was used), TDS on rent (31.2% or more), TDS on NRO interest (30% plus surcharge and cess) where treaty or slab tax is lower.
  • Belated return: section 139(4), by 31 December 2026, with late fee and interest; see belated return.
  • Revised return: to correct a filed return, also by 31 December 2026; see revised return.
  • Updated return afterwards: allowed for up to 48 months after the assessment year since the Finance Act, 2025, but only to pay more tax, not to claim a refund; see ITR-U.
  • Which form: ITR-2, or ITR-3 with business income; see which ITR for NRIs.

/ Holiday visits and residency

Count before you fly.

  • Basic tests: resident if in India 182 days or more in the financial year, or 60 days in the year plus 365 days in the previous four years; for Indian citizens and persons of Indian origin visiting India, the 60-day limb becomes 120 days only if Indian income exceeds INR 15 lakh, otherwise 182 days applies.
  • Deemed residence: an Indian citizen with Indian income above INR 15 lakh who is not liable to tax in any country is treated as resident (not ordinarily resident); relevant for NRIs in the UAE and other no-tax countries; see deemed residence.
  • Check your status: use the residential status calculator.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is the last date for NRIs to file a belated ITR for FY 2025-26?
31 December 2026 for AY 2026-27, with a late fee of up to INR 5,000 (INR 1,000 if total income does not exceed INR 5 lakh) plus interest.
Can an NRI claim a TDS refund after 31 December 2026?
Not through the regular route. After 31 December only an updated return (ITR-U) is possible, which can be used to pay additional tax but not to claim a refund.
Do NRIs need to pay advance tax?
Only if the Indian tax for the year, after TDS, exceeds INR 10,000; the third instalment (75% cumulative) is due on 15 December.
Can a holiday visit to India make an NRI resident?
Yes, if total days in India reach 182 in the financial year, or 120 days for Indian citizens and PIOs whose Indian income exceeds INR 15 lakh and who meet the 365-day test; count before you travel.
What is FAST-DS 2026 and does it apply to NRIs?
A one-time window to declare undisclosed foreign assets and income from resident years; it mainly matters for returning NRIs and is open until 31 December 2026.
