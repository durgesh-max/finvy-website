/ NRI tax · Banking accounts

NRE, NRO, FCNR — the three accounts and their tax.

The NRE / NRO / FCNR account trio defines how an NRI holds money with respect to India. Each account has its own tax, repatriation and re-designation rules. Mix them up and you either pay unnecessary tax or face FEMA breach. Here is the full matrix.

/ The three account types

What each is for.

NRE (Non-Resident External) Account:

  • Rupee-denominated.
  • Funded from foreign-currency inward remittances or transfers from another NRE / FCNR account.
  • Interest is fully exempt from Indian income tax under Section 10(4)(ii) — a major incentive for NRIs.
  • Principal and interest are fully repatriable (no USD 1M cap).
  • Joint account only with another NRI (not with a Resident).

NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) Account:

  • Rupee-denominated.
  • Funded from India-source income (rent, dividend, pension, salary earned in India).
  • Interest is fully taxable in India as interest income — TDS at 30% + surcharge + cess deducted by the bank at source.
  • Repatriation capped at USD 1 million per financial year with Form 15CA (Form 145 from 1 April 2026) + 15CB.
  • Joint account allowed with Resident Indian relative.

FCNR (Foreign Currency Non-Resident) Deposit:

  • Foreign-currency denominated (USD, GBP, EUR, JPY, AUD, CAD) — no exchange risk for the NRI.
  • Only term deposit (1 to 5 years); no FCNR savings account.
  • Interest is exempt from Indian income tax for a non-resident or RNOR under section 10(15)(iv)(fa) of the 1961 Act (carried into the Income-tax Act, 2025), so it stays tax-free for a returning NRI until RNOR status ends.
  • Principal and interest are fully repatriable (no USD 1M cap).
  • Protects NRI corpus from INR depreciation.

/ Taxation summary table

What India taxes.

NRE Savings Account interest: Nil Indian tax (Section 10(4)(ii)).

NRE Fixed Deposit interest: Nil Indian tax (Section 10(4)(ii)). Important: this exemption applies only while the account holder is NRI. On becoming Resident, the exemption ends from the re-designation date.

NRO Savings Account interest: Fully taxable in India at the NRI's slab rate. The bank deducts TDS at 30% + surcharge + cess at source. If the actual slab rate is lower, refund via ITR filing.

NRO Fixed Deposit interest: Same as NRO Savings — fully taxable; TDS at 30% deducted at source.

FCNR interest: Nil Indian tax while you are non-resident or RNOR (section 10(15)(iv)(fa)). Foreign-currency protected.

DTAA relief on NRO interest: treaty rate (typically 10-15%) overrides 30% domestic withholding if TRC + Form 10F (Form 41 from 1 April 2026) are filed with the bank in advance. Many NRIs miss this and pay 30% unnecessarily.

/ Repatriation rules

What leaves India and how.

From NRE: freely repatriable; no cap. Bank processes outward remittance to the NRI's overseas account on request. Form 15CA / 15CB generally not required (NRE funds are considered foreign-origin).

From NRO: up to USD 1 million per financial year (April to March). Form 15CA + Form 15CB (Form 146 from 1 April 2026) required.

From FCNR: freely repatriable in the foreign currency of the deposit; no exchange, no cap.

Transfer between accounts: NRE to NRO is permitted (NRE loses its 'external' character once in NRO). NRO to NRE is permitted, but only within the USD 1 million per financial year repatriation limit, after tax is paid and with Forms 145/146 (formerly 15CA/15CB) and a FEMA declaration to the bank. NRE to FCNR and FCNR to NRE transfers are permitted.

/ On becoming Resident

The re-designation step.

When the NRI returns to India permanently and becomes Resident:

  • NRE and NRO accounts must be re-designated as Resident accounts immediately on return, as RBI directs; tell your bank as soon as you arrive.
  • NRE Fixed Deposits can be converted to RFC (Resident Foreign Currency) deposits — useful if the NRI wants to retain the foreign-currency corpus but is now Resident.
  • FCNR deposits can continue until maturity after you return; the interest stays exempt while you are RNOR and becomes taxable once you are ordinarily resident. NRE interest is different: it becomes taxable once you are resident.
  • RFC Account (Resident Foreign Currency): allows Returning NRIs to continue holding foreign currency balances; interest taxable but useful for ongoing foreign-currency obligations.

Failure to re-designate: FEMA breach; bank may freeze the account; penalty under FEMA Section 13 up to 3 times the amount involved.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Can I open an NRE account without visiting India?
Yes. Most Indian banks (SBI, HDFC, ICICI, Axis, Kotak) offer online NRE account opening with video KYC. Required: passport, visa / residence permit of host country, overseas address proof, Indian PAN, FATCA / CRS declaration. Processing time depends on the bank.
What is the full form of NRE account?
NRE stands for Non-Resident External. It is a rupee account in India for NRIs and OCIs to hold income earned abroad; the balance and interest are fully repatriable, and interest is tax-free in India while the holder is non-resident. NRO stands for Non-Resident Ordinary (for income earned in India), and FCNR(B) is a foreign currency non-resident term deposit.
What is an NRI account?
An NRI account is a bank account in India that a non-resident Indian or OCI cardholder may hold under FEMA. There are three main types: NRE (Non-Resident External, for foreign income, fully repatriable, interest tax-free in India), NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary, for Indian income such as rent or dividends, interest taxable, remittance abroad up to USD 1 million a financial year) and FCNR (B) (fixed deposits held in foreign currency). A resident savings account must be redesignated as NRO once you become an NRI.
How do I open an NRI bank account?
Choose NRE (for money earned abroad) or NRO (for Indian income), then apply online or at a branch with your passport, visa, residence permit or OCI card, overseas address proof, PAN (or Form 60 if you have none), photographs and a FATCA / CRS declaration; KYC is done by video or with attested copies. Fund an NRE account from abroad. If you already have a resident savings account, ask the bank to redesignate it as NRO.
What is the full form of NRI?
NRI stands for Non-Resident Indian: under FEMA, an Indian citizen who lives outside India. For income tax, residential status is decided separately by the number of days spent in India, so an NRI under FEMA can still be a resident for tax in some years.
Can an NRI keep a normal savings account in India?
No. Under FEMA a resident savings account must be redesignated as an NRO account once you become an NRI; you can also open an NRE account for money earned abroad.
What is the interest rate on an NRE account?
Each bank sets its own NRE savings and fixed deposit rates by tenure, and under RBI rules they generally cannot be higher than the bank's rates on comparable domestic rupee deposits, so check the bank's current rate card. The interest is exempt from Indian tax while you remain an NRI, but it may be taxable in your country of residence.
Is NRE Fixed Deposit interest tax-free in India and in my country of residence?
In India: yes, under Section 10(4)(ii), provided NRI status is maintained. In your country of residence: depends. US NRIs report NRE interest as taxable income to the IRS under worldwide-income rules. UK NRIs report under self-assessment. UAE residents post-Corporate-Tax may report as business income if applicable. The tax-free Indian treatment is specifically an Indian rule, not an international one.
Which NRI fixed deposit is best: NRE, NRO or FCNR?
It depends on where the money comes from and which currency you want. An NRE FD holds money earned abroad in rupees; interest is tax-free in India while you are an NRI and the deposit is fully repatriable, but you carry rupee exchange risk. An FCNR(B) FD holds the same foreign money in a foreign currency such as USD, GBP or EUR for 1 to 5 years, so there is no exchange risk, and interest is tax-free for non-residents. An NRO FD is for income earned in India, such as rent, dividends or pension; interest is taxable, with TDS at 30% plus surcharge and cess unless you give the bank a tax residency certificate for the treaty rate, and repatriation is capped at USD 1 million a year after tax. Interest rates differ by bank and tenure, so compare them on the post-tax figure, and remember your country of residence may tax the interest.
What fixed deposit schemes can an NRI open in India?
Three: an NRE term deposit (rupees, from money earned abroad, minimum tenor one year, interest tax-free in India for non-residents, fully repatriable); an NRO term deposit (rupees, for Indian income such as rent or pension, interest taxable with TDS at 30% plus surcharge and cess unless a treaty rate applies, repatriable within the USD 1 million a year limit); and an FCNR(B) deposit (held in a foreign currency such as USD, GBP or EUR for 1 to 5 years, no exchange risk, interest tax-free for non-residents). Interest rates are set by each bank.
Under which section is NRE interest exempt for NRIs?
For income up to FY 2025-26, section 10(4)(ii) of the Income-tax Act, 1961. From tax year 2026-27, the same exemption sits in section 11 read with Schedule IV of the Income-tax Act, 2025. The substance is unchanged: interest on an NRE account is exempt for a non-resident, and the exemption stops once you become resident.
What are the RBI rules for NRI bank accounts?
Once you become an NRI, resident savings accounts must be converted to NRO accounts. Money earned abroad can go into NRE or FCNR accounts and is freely repatriable; Indian income such as rent, dividends and pension goes into an NRO account, from which up to USD 1 million a financial year can be sent abroad after tax. NRE accounts can be held jointly with a resident close relative on a former-or-survivor basis, and NRO accounts jointly with residents.
Can I withdraw an NRE fixed deposit before maturity?
Yes. NRE term deposits of INR 1 crore or less held by individuals must offer premature withdrawal. But the RBI's minimum tenor for NRE term deposits is one year, so if you close one before 12 months no interest is paid at all. After one year, banks usually pay the rate for the period actually held minus a penalty, often around 1%, set by each bank's scheme.
Is interest on a prematurely closed NRE FD taxable?
No, for a non-resident: NRE interest stays exempt from Indian tax whether the deposit runs to maturity or is closed early. It may be taxable in your country of residence. Once you become resident in India, NRE interest is no longer exempt.
Is it better to break an NRE FD or take a loan against it?
If you need money for a short time, a loan against the deposit avoids losing interest for the period held and any penalty; banks lend against NRE deposits within RBI limits. If the money is needed permanently, or the remaining term is short, closing may cost less. Compare the penalty with the loan interest.
Can I transfer money from NRO to NRE account?
Yes. Transfers from NRO to NRE are permitted within the USD 1 million per financial year repatriation limit (shared with remittances abroad from NRO), after tax is paid on the funds and with a FEMA declaration and Forms 145/146 (formerly 15CA/15CB) to the bank. It is not automatic. Many NRIs mistakenly assume NRO-to-NRE is free — it is not; the transaction is treated as outward remittance from NRO.
What happens to my NRE account when I return to India?
RBI expects NRE accounts to be re-designated as resident accounts (or moved to an RFC account) immediately on your return; banks ask you to do it as soon as you arrive. Savings component converts to Resident Savings Account (interest fully taxable from conversion). Fixed Deposits can be converted to RFC (Resident Foreign Currency) Account to retain foreign-currency denomination, or to Resident Rupee Fixed Deposits. The Section 10(4)(ii) exemption ends on the re-designation date; interest earned before is retained tax-free.
Can I claim DTAA treaty rate on NRO interest instead of 30% domestic TDS?
Yes. Submit TRC + Form 10F to the bank before the TDS cycle. Bank then deducts at the treaty rate (typically 10-15% depending on your country of residence). Without it the bank deducts 30% plus cess; the excess can be claimed back through the ITR, but it comes back only after the return for that year is filed and processed, which can be a year or more after the deduction.
