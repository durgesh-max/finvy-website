/ Joint holders · Signing authority · Schedule FA · Updated 2026-11-11

Parents in India Added to Your Foreign Bank Account? Their Schedule FA Duty, Explained (2026), your account can be their reporting problem.

Many NRIs add a parent in India as a joint holder or signatory on a US, UK or other foreign account for emergencies. If that parent is resident and ordinarily resident in India, the income-tax return asks whether they held any asset outside India as beneficial owner, beneficiary or otherwise, or had signing authority in any account outside India. A joint holding or signing authority means the account has to appear in the parent's Schedule FA, even when every rupee in it belongs to the child. The same applies to spouses on joint accounts.

/ Who reports what

Resident and ordinarily resident holders only.

SituationSchedule FA for the person in India
Parent is a joint holder of the child's foreign accountYes: report the account (as legal holder, noting the funds are the child's)
Parent is only an authorised signatoryYes: in the Schedule FA table for accounts with signing authority
Parent is only a nominee or beneficiary on deathGenerally no holding during life; reporting starts if the account passes to them
Spouses hold a foreign account jointly, both resident and ordinarily residentEach should report it; the income is taxed in the hands of the spouse whose money it is
The holder in India is NRI or RNOR that yearNo Schedule FA for that year

“It is my son’s money” is not the test

The Black Money Act penalty of INR 10 lakh reaches a resident who held a foreign asset “as a beneficial owner or otherwise” and left it out of the return, unless foreign assets other than property total INR 20 lakh or less. A name on the account is enough to raise the question. If your parents’ returns do not show it, fix it before 31 December 2026: message CA Durgesh Chavda on WhatsApp.

/ Tax on the interest

Reporting is not the same as owning the income.

  • Whose income: interest follows the person who funded the account. A parent who only lends a name usually has no Indian tax on the interest, but still reports the account.
  • Keep proof: the child's salary credits, transfer records and the bank's account-opening papers show whose money it is.
  • If the parent funded it: then it is the parent's own foreign asset, its income is taxable in India, and it may be an LRS remittance with its own records.
  • Residential status: parents who spend long periods abroad may be NRI or RNOR in some years. Check with the residential status calculator.

/ If it was never reported

Options before 31 December 2026.

  • Revised return: adds the account to the FY 2025-26 return (free until 31 December 2026, with a fee until 31 March 2027).
  • FAST-DS: gives immunity for earlier years for what is declared. Whether it is worth using for an account a parent only lends a name to depends on the amounts and the years; the INR 20 lakh rule matters here.
  • Going forward: report it every year the parent is resident and ordinarily resident, or remove the parent from the account if the role is no longer needed.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

My parents are joint holders on my US bank account. Do they need to report it in India?
Yes, if they are resident and ordinarily resident. The return covers foreign assets held as beneficial owner, beneficiary or otherwise, and accounts with signing authority, so a joint holding belongs in their Schedule FA even if the money is yours.
Do my parents pay tax on the interest?
Usually not, if you funded the account: income follows the person whose money it is. They still report the account. Keep records showing the funds are yours.
My father is only a signatory. Does that count?
Yes. Schedule FA has a separate table for accounts outside India in which you have signing authority.
What if they never reported it?
The Black Money Act penalty of INR 10 lakh can apply to an asset held as beneficial owner or otherwise, unless foreign assets other than property total INR 20 lakh or less. A revised return for FY 2025-26 or a FAST-DS declaration by 31 December 2026 can fix it.
Does a nominee need to report the account?
A nominee or beneficiary on death generally does not hold the account during the owner's life. Reporting starts if the account passes to them.
