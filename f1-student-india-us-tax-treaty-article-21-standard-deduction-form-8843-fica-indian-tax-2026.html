Indian F-1 Students in the US (2026): India-US Tax Treaty Article 21, Standard Deduction, Form 8843, FICA and the Indian Side, the treaty gives a deduction, not a blanket exemption.

Indian students on F-1 visas usually file as US nonresident aliens, and Article 21 of the India-US tax treaty gives them something most other nonresidents do not get: the US standard deduction on Form 1040-NR. There is no USD 5,000 wage exemption under the India treaty, despite what many forums say. Money sent from India for maintenance and studies is not US income. F-1 students who are nonresident aliens are generally exempt from Social Security and Medicare (FICA) on campus and OPT wages, under US law rather than the treaty, and every F-1 student files Form 8843 each year, even with no income. On the Indian side, check your residence in the year you leave, keep Indian income in an NRO account, and note that parents pay no TCS on education remittances funded by an education loan and 2% above INR 10 lakh a year if self-funded.

/ Quick answers

What Indian students and parents ask.

  • Do F-1 students pay taxes in the US? Yes on US income such as on-campus wages, assistantships, taxable scholarships and OPT pay, usually as nonresident aliens on Form 1040-NR. Money from home is not US income.
  • What does the India-US treaty give F-1 students? Article 21(2) lets Indian students claim the standard deduction allowed to US residents on Form 1040-NR, and Article 21(1) keeps payments from abroad for maintenance and education tax-free.
  • Is there a USD 5,000 exemption for Indian students? No. That exemption exists in some other countries’ treaties, not India’s.
  • Do F-1 students pay FICA? Generally not while they are nonresident aliens (typically the first five calendar years); the exemption comes from US tax law, not the treaty. Ask the employer to stop withholding if they deducted it.
  • What is Form 8843? A statement every F-1 student files each year to exclude days from the US residence test, even with no income.

/ Indian side

What the student and parents should check in India.

  • Residence in the year you leave: leaving for studies (unlike employment) does not get the relaxed 182-day rule, so a student who spent 60 days or more in India in that year and 365 days in the previous four may still be resident for that Indian tax year.
  • Indian income: interest, rent or investments in your name stay taxable in India; switch savings accounts to NRO once non-resident and file an Indian return if needed.
  • Parents sending money: education remittances funded by an education loan from a specified financial institution carry no TCS; self-funded education carries 2% TCS above INR 10 lakh a year, claimable in the parent’s return. See parents paying for study abroad.
  • Gifts from parents are not taxed in India; see gift money from parents (Form 3520 applies only to US persons).
  • Coming back: foreign accounts go into Schedule FA once you are resident and ordinarily resident in India again.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Do F-1 students pay taxes in the US?
Yes, on US income such as on-campus wages, assistantships, taxable scholarships and OPT pay, usually as nonresident aliens on Form 1040-NR. Money sent from India for living and study costs is not US income.
What does the India-US tax treaty give F-1 students?
Under Article 21(2), Indian students can claim the standard deduction allowed to US residents on Form 1040-NR, which most nonresidents cannot. Article 21(1) keeps payments from abroad for maintenance and education tax-free.
Is there a USD 5,000 tax exemption for Indian F-1 students?
No. The India-US treaty does not have a USD 5,000 wage exemption; that appears in some other countries' treaties.
Do F-1 students pay Social Security and Medicare tax?
Generally not while they are nonresident aliens, typically for the first five calendar years, under US tax law rather than the treaty. If FICA was withheld by mistake, ask the employer to refund it.
Is Form 8843 required if I had no income?
Yes. Every F-1 student who is excluding days from the US residence test files Form 8843 each year, even with no income.
