Income Tax Search and Seizure Under Section 132 (2026): What Happens in an Income Tax Raid, Your Rights, Statements on Oath, Jewellery Limits and Block Assessment, what you sign now decides the next six years.

An income tax search under section 132 - often called a raid - is carried out under a warrant of authorisation. Officers can enter homes and offices, open lockers, seize books, cash and jewellery, and record statements on oath that can be used as evidence. For searches from 1 September 2024, a block assessment covers the six earlier years plus the current year, with undisclosed income taxed at 60%.

Powers and rights.

  • Stay calm and cooperative: do not destroy or hide anything, and do not obstruct the team.
  • Read before you sign: check the panchnama and inventory against what was actually taken, and ask for copies.
  • Answer only what you know: a statement on oath carries weight; say that records will be produced rather than guessing figures or offering income on the spot.

/ After the search

Block assessment and what to do.

  • Block assessment: for searches on or after 1 September 2024, the officer assesses a block period of six assessment years before the year of search plus the current year up to the search; undisclosed income is taxed at 60% under section 113.
  • Statements: a statement made under pressure or by mistake should be retracted promptly with reasons and evidence; delay weakens a retraction.
  • Seized cash and assets: explain the source with records - books, bank statements, gift deeds, sale deeds, earlier returns - and apply for release where they are explained.
  • Restraint orders: track their validity and seek release of items that are explained.
  • Plan the return for the block period: the figures you file and the explanations you give will be tested against seized material.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What happens during an income tax raid?
Officers arrive with a warrant of authorisation, search the premises named (homes, offices, lockers, vehicles), seize unexplained books, cash, jewellery and other valuables, record statements on oath and prepare a panchnama with an inventory signed in front of two witnesses.
What is the difference between an income tax survey and a search?
A survey under section 133A is limited to business premises, allows no seizure of cash, jewellery or stock, and statements are not on oath. A search under section 132 needs a warrant, can cover a residence, allows seizure, and statements are on oath.
How much jewellery can be kept during an income tax search?
Under CBDT instructions, jewellery up to 500 g per married woman, 250 g per unmarried woman and 100 g per male member of the family is normally not seized, even without proof. More can be left if its source is explained.
What is block assessment after a search?
For searches on or after 1 September 2024, the officer assesses a block period of six assessment years before the year of search plus the current year up to the date of search. Undisclosed income found is taxed at 60%.
Can I retract a statement given during a search?
Yes, but it must be done promptly, in writing, with reasons and evidence. A statement on oath is strong evidence, so a late or unsupported retraction carries little weight.
