BEPS Pillar Two in India (November 2026): India Has Not Enacted the 15% Global Minimum Tax - What Indian Subsidiaries of Foreign Groups Still Have to Do, status, who collects the top-up tax, and the data your head office will ask for.

India has not enacted Pillar Two. As of November 2026 there is no Indian qualified domestic minimum top-up tax (QDMTT), income inclusion rule or undertaxed profits rule, and there is no Indian GloBE return or top-up tax to file. That does not take an Indian subsidiary out of the rules. If the group has consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more and its parent or other group countries have Pillar Two in force, the Indian entity is still a constituent entity. Any top-up tax on low-taxed Indian profits is collected abroad, usually by the parent’s country. Most Indian companies pay 25.17%, well above the 15% floor. The exposure sits with entities using incentives, such as SEZ or GIFT IFSC units and tax holidays. For US-parented groups, the OECD’s side-by-side package of 5 January 2026 can switch off the income inclusion and undertaxed profits rules, though reporting continues. An earlier version of this page said India’s rules were already in force. That was incorrect and was corrected on 21 November 2026.

/ Where things stand

India and the rest of the group.

Is there an Indian GloBE return or form?No. Existing filings continue: Form 3CEB, the master file (Form 3CEAA) and country-by-country report (Form 3CEAD) where thresholds are met
Which groups are in scope globally?Consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more in at least two of the four preceding years
Who taxes low-taxed Indian profits?The parent’s country under its income inclusion rule, or other group countries under the undertaxed profits rule, because India has no QDMTT to collect it first

/ What an Indian subsidiary should do now

Practical steps.

  • Confirm scope with head office: is the group above EUR 750 million, and which countries in the group have Pillar Two in force?
  • Compute the Indian effective rate the GloBE way: covered taxes divided by GloBE income, after the substance-based exclusion for payroll and tangible assets. It will differ from the rate in your tax return.
  • Review incentives: an incentive that takes the Indian rate below 15% may simply move the tax to the parent’s country. Model this before choosing SEZ, GIFT IFSC or a holiday; see GIFT City section 10(4D).
  • Get the data ready: deferred tax, payroll and asset figures in the form the group’s information return needs; reconcile Ind AS numbers to the group’s consolidated accounts.
  • Keep Indian filings in order: Form 3CEB, master file and country-by-country report; see transfer pricing for foreign groups in India.
  • Watch the Budget: if India introduces a QDMTT, the top-up tax would be paid in India instead of abroad, and Indian filings would follow.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Has India implemented Pillar Two?
No. As of November 2026 India has not enacted a qualified domestic minimum top-up tax, an income inclusion rule or an undertaxed profits rule. A QDMTT is widely expected in a future Budget.
Does an Indian subsidiary have to file a GloBE return in India?
No. There is no Indian GloBE information return or top-up tax filing. The subsidiary still supplies data for the group's return filed abroad, and continues Indian filings such as Form 3CEB, the master file and the country-by-country report.
Who collects top-up tax on low-taxed Indian profits?
Because India has no domestic top-up tax, it is collected abroad: by the parent's country under its income inclusion rule, or by other group countries under the undertaxed profits rule.
Is India's corporate tax rate above the 15% minimum?
Usually yes. Most companies pay 25.17%. The risk is for entities using incentives such as SEZ or GIFT IFSC benefits or tax holidays, which can pull the GloBE effective rate below 15%.
How does the side-by-side package affect US groups with Indian subsidiaries?
Under the OECD package of 5 January 2026, US-parented groups can be exempt from the income inclusion and undertaxed profits rules from 1 January 2026. Domestic top-up taxes in countries that have them, and information reporting, continue.
