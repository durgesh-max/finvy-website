/ Swiss companies and NRIs in Switzerland · DTAA · Updated 2026-11-24

India-Switzerland DTAA (2026): Treaty Rates, Article 12 and the MFN Suspension From 1 January 2025 After the Nestle Ruling, 10% is the working rate again: the 5% MFN route is closed for income from 2025.

Under the India-Switzerland double tax avoidance agreement, as amended by the 2010 protocol, Indian tax on dividends, interest, royalties and fees for technical services paid to a Swiss resident is capped at 10%. The protocol’s most-favoured-nation clause once let Swiss investors claim a 5% dividend rate by pointing to India’s later treaties with OECD members such as Lithuania. After India’s Supreme Court held in October 2023 (the Nestlé case) that such benefits need a separate notification, Switzerland suspended the MFN clause for income accruing from 1 January 2025, so both countries now apply the treaty rates as written. Swiss recipients claim the 10% rate with a Swiss tax residency certificate, Form 41 (formerly Form 10F) and a PAN.

/ Quick answers

What Swiss investors and NRIs ask.

  • What is the TDS rate on dividends to a Swiss resident? 10% under the treaty, against 20% plus surcharge and cess without it.
  • What does Article 12 cover? Royalties and fees for technical services, both capped at 10% of the gross amount.
  • What changed on 1 January 2025? Switzerland stopped applying the MFN clause, so the 5% MFN dividend rate is no longer available for income accruing from that date; Swiss withholding on dividends to Indian residents also reverts to the treaty rate.
  • What about dividends from 2018 to 2024? The Swiss notice indicates the MFN position may still apply to that period on the Swiss side; Indian authorities did not apply the 5% rate, so pending claims need case-by-case review.
  • Does the MLI apply? Check the synthesised text; anti-abuse rules and beneficial ownership tests apply either way.

/ What to do

Claims, refunds and planning.

  • Claim the treaty rate with a Swiss TRC, Form 41 and a PAN before the Indian payer deducts tax; see tax residency certificates.
  • Stop MFN-based 5% claims on Indian dividends for income from 2025, and review any refund claims for earlier years.
  • Swiss parent companies should model Indian dividend tax at 10% and check credit in Switzerland.
  • Indian residents with Swiss income claim foreign tax credit in India for Swiss tax withheld at the treaty rate.
  • Service providers should check permanent establishment risk for staff working in India.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is the India-Switzerland DTAA rate on dividends?
10% of the gross dividend for a Swiss resident beneficial owner. The 5% rate some investors claimed under the MFN clause no longer applies to income accruing from 1 January 2025, after Switzerland suspended the clause.
Why did Switzerland suspend the MFN clause with India?
India's Supreme Court held in October 2023, in a case involving Nestle, that MFN benefits apply only after a separate notification by the Indian government. Switzerland responded by suspending the MFN clause for income accruing from 1 January 2025, citing lack of reciprocity.
What does Article 12 of the India-Switzerland DTAA cover?
Royalties and fees for technical services, taxable in India at up to 10% of the gross amount for a Swiss resident beneficial owner.
How does a Swiss company claim the treaty rate in India?
Give the Indian payer a Swiss tax residency certificate, Form 41 (formerly Form 10F) and a PAN or the documents allowed instead, before tax is deducted.
