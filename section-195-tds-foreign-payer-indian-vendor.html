/ Foreign Clients · Section 195 · Updated 2026-10-13

Section 195 TDS, when you're the foreign payer.

Foreign company making payment to Indian service provider (freelancer, consultant, vendor) — does Section 195 India TDS apply? The usual answer: yes, if the payment is 'chargeable to tax in India'. But most foreign payers get this wrong, often over-withholding on simple services or under-withholding on technical services. Here's the correct framework.

/ When does Section 195 apply

The chargeability test.

Section 195 Income Tax Act: any person (whether resident or non-resident) making payment to a non-resident that is 'chargeable to tax in India' must deduct TDS. Note: the payer can also be a non-resident — so foreign company paying Indian vendor could trigger Section 195.

But wait — Section 195 is about payment to a non-resident. If you're paying an Indian resident (Indian individual CA / Indian company), Section 195 does NOT apply. Sections 194C / 194J / 194A would apply if the payer has an India tax presence (PE / fixed place / branch).

So the question splits:

  • Foreign payer, non-resident payee: Section 195 applies IF the income is India-sourced (Section 9) or deemed to accrue in India.
  • Foreign payer, resident payee (Indian citizen / Indian company): Sections 194C / 194J etc. may apply IF foreign payer has an India tax presence. If foreign payer has NO India presence — usually no TDS obligation on foreign payer; Indian resident pays own tax on receipt.

Most foreign-payer-to-Indian-vendor scenarios are the second case: no TDS obligation on foreign payer. Indian vendor files own ITR and pays own tax.

/ The common misconception

Foreign payer over-withholds.

Many foreign companies pay their Indian vendors (freelance developer in Bangalore, consultant in Mumbai, agency in Delhi) and ask 'should we withhold Indian tax?'. If the Indian vendor is an Indian resident (Indian individual or Indian company), the standard answer is No. The foreign payer has no India TDS obligation (because the foreign payer has no India tax presence that would make Sections 194C/194J/194A applicable).

The Indian vendor:

  • Receives gross payment.
  • Reports it as income in their Indian ITR.
  • Pays own income tax (slab rate for individuals, 25% / 22% for companies).
  • Charges GST if their turnover crosses threshold (zero-rated if export of service under GST rules).

Confirming: foreign payer's obligation is to pay the agreed invoice amount. No India TDS. No Indian compliance obligation.

/ When foreign payer DOES have Section 195 obligation

The exceptions.

Foreign payer DOES have Section 195 obligation when:

  • Payee is a non-resident entity (not an Indian resident) and income is India-sourced. Example: US company pays UK consultant for work done in India — UK consultant is non-resident; income may be India-sourced; Section 195 applies.
  • Foreign payer has an Indian branch / fixed place / PE, and the payment is made through / attributable to that India presence. Section 195 then applies.
  • Payment is treated as 'representative assessee' matter under Section 160: if foreign payer has an Indian representative (agent, broker, Indian subsidiary), the representative can be assessed as 'representative assessee' for the foreign company's India-sourced income. Section 195 obligations shift to the Indian representative.

For a US SaaS company paying an Indian individual CA for consulting work: Section 195 does not apply (payee is Indian resident, payer has no India presence).

For an Indian subsidiary of a US company paying a UK consultant: Section 195 does apply (payee is non-resident; Indian subsidiary is the payer; payment chargeable to tax in India).

/ Practical scenarios + handling

Three common cases.

Case 1: US company pays Indian freelancer USD 10K/month for development work

  • Payee: Indian individual (resident).
  • Foreign payer: no India presence.
  • Section 195: not applicable.
  • Action: pay full USD 10K via wire / Deel / Wise. Indian freelancer handles own ITR + GST.

Case 2: UK company pays Indian company (Pvt Ltd) INR 50 lakh for development project

  • Payee: Indian company (resident).
  • Foreign payer: no India presence.
  • Section 195: not applicable.
  • Action: pay full INR 50 lakh. Indian company handles own corporate tax + GST (export of service under GST may apply → zero-rated with LUT bond).

Case 3: Indian subsidiary (of German parent) pays German consultant EUR 20K for technical services

  • Payee: German resident (non-resident of India).
  • Indian subsidiary: the payer; has India tax presence.
  • Section 195: applies.
  • Chargeability: Fees for Technical Services (FTS) under Section 115A → chargeable in India at 20% default; 10% under India-Germany DTAA with TRC + Form 10F (Form 41 from 1 April 2026).
  • Form 15CA (Form 145 from 1 April 2026) + Form 15CB (Form 146 from 1 April 2026) mandatory before remittance.
  • Indian subsidiary deducts 10% TDS, deposits by 7th of next month, files Form 27Q (Form 144 from 1 April 2026) quarterly.

/ What foreign companies should do

Practical steps.

  • Pay Indian residents gross (full invoice amount), no India TDS withholding. If you're a US company paying an Indian freelancer in Bangalore, pay USD X; the Indian freelancer handles their own India tax.
  • Insist on invoice + PAN: your Indian vendor should provide a GST-compliant invoice with their PAN. Keeps your audit trail clean.
  • GST awareness: Indian vendor's invoice may show GST at 18% (if their turnover crosses threshold AND they bill in INR) or zero (if export of service under GST rules). Either way, foreign payer pays the invoice; GST is Indian vendor's obligation.
  • If YOU have an Indian subsidiary: that subsidiary paying any non-resident vendor → Section 195 applies → 15CA + 15CB protocol. See 15CA/CB guide.
  • Beneficial Ownership + Treaty Shopping risk: avoid routing payment through third-country intermediaries just to reduce TDS; India has GAAR + treaty-shopping provisions.
  • Doubt → written advisor opinion: a CA opinion letter costs little and clarifies the position; much cheaper than mistaken TDS obligations.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

If I'm a US company paying an Indian freelancer, do I need to withhold Indian tax?
No. Section 195 India TDS applies only when: (a) payee is non-resident of India AND income is India-sourced, OR (b) payer has India tax presence. Indian freelancer is Indian resident; US payer has no India presence. No Section 195 obligation. Pay full invoice amount. Indian freelancer handles own ITR + GST on their side.
What if I'm paying an Indian company (Pvt Ltd) from abroad?
Same as above - Indian Pvt Ltd is Indian resident. Foreign payer with no India presence has no Section 195 TDS obligation. Pay full invoice. Indian company handles own corporate tax + GST (export of service may apply at zero rate with LUT bond).
When does Section 195 definitely apply to a foreign payer?
When payee is non-resident of India AND payment is chargeable in India (India-sourced income). Example: Indian subsidiary of foreign parent paying a non-resident consultant for FTS - Section 195 applies, Form 15CA/CB mandatory. Or: foreign payer with India branch/PE making payment through the India presence.
Can my Indian vendor charge me GST?
Yes - if their Indian turnover crosses GST threshold (INR 20 lakh / 40 lakh depending on state and type). But export of service to foreign client is typically zero-rated under GST (place of supply outside India + payment in forex + LUT bond). So many Indian vendors bill foreign clients at zero GST while still being GST-registered. Verify your vendor's invoice format.
What is 'representative assessee' under Section 160?
Section 160 Income Tax Act: an Indian agent, broker, trustee, or Indian subsidiary of a foreign principal can be treated as the 'representative assessee' for the foreign person's India-sourced income. The representative is liable for India tax + TDS on behalf of the foreign principal. Shifts Section 195 obligations to the Indian representative.
