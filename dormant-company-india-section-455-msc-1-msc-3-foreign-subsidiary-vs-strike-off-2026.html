Dormant Company in India (2026): Section 455 Status, MSC-1 and MSC-3, What Still Applies, and Dormant vs Strike-Off, pause the company without closing it.

An Indian company that is not trading can apply to the Registrar of Companies for dormant status under section 455 of the Companies Act, 2013, by filing Form MSC-1, and then file a short annual return (MSC-3) instead of the full set of filings. It suits a company formed for a future project, one that only holds an asset or intellectual property, or one with no significant accounting transaction in the last two financial years. Listed companies, and companies with outstanding loans, deposits, statutory dues or pending investigations, cannot apply. Dormant status does not switch off everything: the company still files its income tax return, and a foreign-owned company still files the FLA return with the RBI. If there is no plan to restart, striking off the company may be cleaner.

/ Quick answers

What founders and foreign parents ask.

  • What is a dormant company in India? A company that has obtained dormant status from the Registrar under section 455 because it has no significant accounting transactions, for example one formed for a future project or holding only an asset or IP.
  • What is an example of a dormant company? A subsidiary set up by a foreign group for a project that has not started, a company that owns only a property or a trademark, or a startup that has paused operations for two years.
  • How do I apply? Pass a special resolution (or get the required consent), then file Form MSC-1 with the Registrar; once approved, the Registrar issues a certificate in MSC-2.
  • What does a dormant company still have to do? File MSC-3 every year, hold at least one board meeting in each half of the calendar year, keep the minimum number of directors, file its income tax return and, if foreign-owned, the FLA return.
  • How does it become active again? By filing Form MSC-4 with the Registrar once it wants to start business again.

/ Before you apply

Conditions and practical points.

  • No significant accounting transaction: paying ROC fees, statutory payments, allotting shares and paying for office and records upkeep do not count as significant transactions.
  • Clear the blockers first: outstanding loans, deposits or statutory dues, pending prosecutions or investigations, and unfiled returns stop the application.
  • Keep minimum directors: two for a private company, three for a public company, one for a one person company, and the resident director requirement continues.
  • Foreign parents: keep FEMA reporting current (FLA return each year) even while dormant; plan whether to inject funds or repatriate before applying.
  • Tax: a dormant company with no income still files a nil or loss return; brought-forward losses follow normal rules when it restarts.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is a dormant company in India?
A company that has obtained dormant status from the Registrar of Companies under section 455 of the Companies Act, 2013, because it has been formed for a future project, holds an asset or intellectual property, or has had no significant accounting transaction in the last two financial years.
How do I make my company dormant in India?
Check the conditions, pass the required resolution, and file Form MSC-1 with the Registrar. If approved, the Registrar issues a certificate of dormant status in Form MSC-2.
What filings does a dormant company make?
An annual return of dormant company in Form MSC-3, plus its income tax return every year and, if it has foreign investment, the FLA return to the RBI. It must hold at least one board meeting in each half of the calendar year.
Can a foreign-owned subsidiary in India become dormant?
Yes, if it meets the conditions. FEMA reporting such as the FLA return continues, and the resident director requirement still applies.
Is it better to make a company dormant or strike it off?
Dormant status suits a pause with a plan to restart or a company holding an asset or IP. If there is no plan to restart and no assets or liabilities, striking off is usually simpler.
How do I close a private limited company in India?
If the company has no assets or liabilities, apply for voluntary strike-off under section 248(2) of the Companies Act in Form STK-2. It is available where the company never started business within a year of incorporation, or has carried on no business for the two preceding financial years. Settle all liabilities, close bank accounts and bring annual filings up to date, then file STK-2 with a special resolution or consent of 75% of shareholders, an indemnity bond (STK-3) and affidavit (STK-4) from every director, and a recent statement of accounts certified by a chartered accountant. Applications are processed centrally by the MCA's C-PACE office, which publishes a notice before striking the name off. Companies with assets, liabilities or disputes need winding up instead.
How do I close an LLP in India?
An LLP that has stopped business can apply to be struck off. Settle all liabilities and close the bank account, bring annual filings (Form 8 and Form 11) and tax returns up to date, then file Form 24 on the MCA portal with the consent of all partners, a statement of accounts showing nil assets and liabilities certified by a practising professional, and affidavits and indemnity bonds from the designated partners. The Registrar gives notice and allows objections, usually for a month, before striking the name off.
Can an LLP with liabilities or pending disputes use Form 24?
No. Form 24 is only for LLPs with nil assets and liabilities and no ongoing business, contracts, employees or disputes. Others have to settle these first or go through winding up, which is slower and involves the tribunal or a voluntary liquidation process.
