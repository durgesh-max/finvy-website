/ Foreign companies · Electronics · Semiconductors · Updated 2026-11-15

Presumptive Tax for Foreign Companies Serving Indian Electronics and Semiconductor Plants (Section 44BBD): 25% Deemed Profit, About 9-9.5% Effective, tax certainty for overseas equipment, technology and service providers.

A non-resident that provides services or technology to an Indian company setting up or operating an electronics manufacturing facility (or a connected facility) under a scheme notified by the Central Government is taxed on a presumptive basis: 25% of its gross receipts is deemed to be its business profit. For a foreign company that works out to roughly 9.1% to 9.55% of gross receipts after the 35% corporate rate, surcharge (nil, 2% or 5% depending on income) and 4% cess. The rule was introduced as section 44BBD of the 1961 Act by the Finance Act, 2025 (from assessment year 2026-27) and is carried into the Income-tax Act, 2025 alongside the other non-resident presumptive provisions. No expenses are deducted beyond the deemed 25%, and unabsorbed depreciation and past losses cannot be set off. It sits beside the treaty and fees-for-technical-services rules, so the contract still decides which applies.

/ What to check

Before signing and invoicing.

  • Confirm the Indian customer's facility is approved under a notified scheme (for example the semiconductor and display fab or electronics component schemes) and meets the prescribed conditions; see India's semiconductor incentives for foreign chip companies.
  • Map each service and technology fee in the contract: installation, commissioning, training, technical assistance, software and know-how licences are often bundled.
  • Agree the withholding rate with the customer; the Indian payer withholds under section 393(2) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (formerly section 195), and a lower deduction certificate can align withholding with the presumptive tax.
  • Compare the treaty position: if the treaty taxes the fee lower or not at all (for example a make-available clause), check which route is available and keep the tax residency certificate and Form 41.
  • File an Indian income-tax return for the receipts, even under the presumptive scheme.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is section 44BBD?
A presumptive tax rule for non-residents providing services or technology to a resident company setting up or operating an electronics manufacturing facility under a notified scheme: 25% of gross receipts is deemed profit. It was inserted by the Finance Act, 2025 from assessment year 2026-27 and carried into the Income-tax Act, 2025.
What is the effective tax rate under section 44BBD?
About 9.1% to 9.55% of gross receipts for a foreign company: 25% deemed profit taxed at 35% plus surcharge (nil, 2% or 5% depending on income) and 4% cess.
Does it apply to any electronics customer in India?
No. The customer must be a resident company establishing or operating an electronics manufacturing facility, or a connected facility, under a scheme notified by the Central Government and meeting the prescribed conditions.
Can losses or depreciation be set off?
No. Unabsorbed depreciation and brought-forward business losses cannot be set off against the presumptive income.
How does it interact with tax treaties?
Non-residents can generally rely on a treaty where it is more beneficial, for example where fees are not taxable as fees for technical services under a make-available clause. The contract and the customer's scheme status decide which route applies.
