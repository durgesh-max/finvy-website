HRA is exempt up to the least of three amounts: the HRA you actually receive, rent paid minus 10% of salary, and 50% of salary if you rent in Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune or Ahmedabad (40% elsewhere).     / How HRA exemption works

    The rules behind the numbers.

    • Three limits: actual HRA received; rent paid minus 10% of salary; 50% of salary in the eight listed cities or 40% elsewhere. The lowest is exempt and the rest is taxable.
    • Salary for HRA: basic salary plus dearness allowance forming part of retirement benefits plus commission as a fixed percentage of turnover, for the period you paid rent.
    • Eight cities at 50% from 2026-27: Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad, under rule 279 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026.
    • Old regime only: the new regime does not allow the HRA exemption.
    • Rent to parents: allowed if genuine, the parent owns the property, rent is actually paid and the parent reports it as income.

    FAQ

    Common questions, answered.

    How is HRA exemption calculated?
    HRA is exempt up to the least of the actual HRA received, rent paid minus 10% of salary, and 50% of salary in the eight listed cities or 40% elsewhere. The calculator on this page works it out from your figures.
    Which cities get 50% HRA exemption in 2026-27?
    Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad. All other cities use 40% of salary.
    Is HRA exemption available in the new tax regime?
    No. HRA exemption is allowed only in the old regime; under the new regime the full HRA is taxable.
    What counts as salary for HRA?
    Basic salary plus dearness allowance forming part of retirement benefits plus commission as a fixed percentage of turnover, for the period the house was rented.
    What if my rent is less than 10% of salary?
    Then rent minus 10% of salary is nil, so no HRA is exempt and the whole HRA is taxable.
