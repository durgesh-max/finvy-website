Buying Property From an NRI: No TAN Needed From 1 October 2026 (Form 141), TDS under your own PAN.

From 1 October 2026, a resident individual or HUF buying property from a non-resident Indian can deposit and report the TDS using their own PAN through Form 141, instead of first obtaining a TAN. The obligation to deduct tax, the rates and the penalties for getting it wrong have not changed.

/ What changed

Before and after.

  • Before: a buyer purchasing from an NRI needed a TAN, then had to deposit TDS and file a quarterly TDS return like a business.
  • From 1 October 2026: resident individual and HUF buyers can use their PAN and report the deduction in Form 141 (new Schedule E), reported to be under the Income-tax (Fifth Amendment) Rules, 2026.
  • Not covered: companies, firms and other buyers still need a TAN.
  • Timing: the date tax is deducted decides which route applies. An instalment where TDS was deducted before 1 October 2026 stays on the TAN route.

/ What the buyer still has to deduct

Rates are unchanged.

  • Long-term gain (property held more than 24 months): 12.5% plus surcharge and cess.
  • Short-term gain: 30% plus surcharge and cess (higher slab rates can apply for some sellers).
  • There is no INR 50 lakh threshold when the seller is an NRI; that limit applies only to resident sellers.
  • Without a lower deduction certificate, buyers usually deduct on the full sale price, which can be far more than the tax on the gain.
  • If the NRI seller has no PAN, higher rates can apply.

/ For the NRI seller

Get the deduction right first.

  • Apply for a lower deduction certificate well before registration so the buyer deducts only the tax actually due on the gain.
  • Plan Section 54/54F/54EC exemptions if you are reinvesting.
  • After the sale, file an Indian return to claim any excess TDS, then repatriate through Form 15CA/15CB.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Do I need a TAN to buy property from an NRI?
Not from 1 October 2026 if you are a resident individual or HUF: you can use your PAN and Form 141. Companies, firms and other buyers still need a TAN.
What is the TDS rate when buying from an NRI?
12.5% plus surcharge and cess on long-term gains (held over 24 months) and 30% plus surcharge and cess on short-term gains, unless the seller gives you a lower deduction certificate.
Is there a minimum property value for TDS from an NRI?
No. The INR 50 lakh threshold applies only when the seller is resident. TDS applies to any purchase from an NRI.
I paid an instalment in September 2026. Which route applies?
The TAN route for that instalment, because TDS was deducted before 1 October 2026. Later instalments can use the PAN route.
