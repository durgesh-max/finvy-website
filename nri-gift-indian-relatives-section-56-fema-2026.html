/ NRI Guide · Gifts · Updated 2026-10-15

NRI gifts, both directions, Section 56 and FEMA.

NRI gifting money / property to Indian parents or siblings — or receiving gift from Indian relatives — is governed by both Income Tax Section 56(2)(x) and FEMA. Properly structured: fully tax-exempt + FEMA-compliant. Mishandled: Section 56(2)(x) 'Income from Other Sources' at slab rate, or FEMA breach with compounding. Here's the complete practical framework.

/ Section 56(2)(x) - the tax layer

The relative exemption.

Section 56(2)(x) of Income Tax Act taxes gifts (money / property / specified movable property) received by an individual above INR 50,000 aggregate in FY as 'Income from Other Sources' at slab rate. EXCEPT gifts from 'relatives' as defined.

'Relative' under Explanation to Section 56(2)(x) for an individual:

  • Spouse
  • Brother or sister (own)
  • Brother or sister of spouse
  • Brother or sister of either parent
  • Any lineal ascendant or descendant (parents, grandparents, children, grandchildren)
  • Any lineal ascendant or descendant of spouse (in-laws)
  • Spouse of any of the above persons

Standard NRI scenarios:

  • NRI son gifts to Indian parent — parent is 'relative', fully exempt.
  • Indian parent gifts to NRI daughter — daughter's parent is 'relative', fully exempt.
  • NRI sibling gift / inheritance — sibling is 'relative', fully exempt.
  • NRI uncle / aunt / cousin / niece / nephew — uncle / aunt via blood (parent's sibling) is 'relative'; via marriage is NOT; cousin / niece / nephew is NOT 'relative'; taxable above INR 50K.
  • NRI friend gift — NOT 'relative'; taxable above INR 50K.

/ FEMA layer - outbound (NRI to Indian relative)

Resident recipient.

  • NRI remits money to Indian relative: foreign-source inflow. Credited to recipient's NRE / regular savings / NRO account depending on recipient's status.
  • For Indian resident recipient: cash gift credited to regular savings account. No FEMA restriction on receipt by resident Indian. Form A2 + supporting documentation (gift deed / affidavit) completed at AD bank.
  • Gift deed recommended: notarised gift deed from NRI donor to Indian recipient describing: donor's details + recipient details + amount + relationship + stamp duty on gift deed (varies by state, typically INR 500-5,000 for cash gift documentation).
  • For gifts above INR 2 lakh to resident Indian in cash: Section 269ST violation if cash; strongly prefer bank transfer. NRI gifting via wire = compliant.
  • Multiple remittances in a year: aggregate matters. Standard practice: gift via one or two consolidated remittances with clear gift deed documentation.
  • Immovable property gift from NRI to resident: via registered gift deed in India + stamp duty + registration. FEMA permits (gift of property to resident family member allowed).

/ FEMA layer - inbound (NRI receiving from Indian relative)

Resident donor.

  • Indian relative gifts money to NRI: outbound remittance from India. Governed by FEMA Current Account Rules + LRS (Liberalised Remittance Scheme).
  • LRS limit: USD 2,50,000 per Indian resident per FY. Covers all permitted current + capital account transactions.
  • LRS use for gift to NRI relative: specifically permitted under FEMA Master Direction on LRS — included in 'maintenance of close relatives abroad' + 'gift' as permitted purposes.
  • Documentation: Form A2 declaration + PAN + purpose code (CRG - current remittance for gift). Gift deed strengthens case.
  • TCS under Section 206C(1G): 20% TCS on LRS remittance above INR 7 lakh in FY (post Finance Act 2024). See our TCS LRS guide. Collected by remitting bank, creditable against Indian donor's income tax.
  • Immovable property gift from resident to NRI: Indian resident can gift immovable property (not agricultural land) to NRI relative via registered gift deed + stamp duty.
  • Shares / securities gift: resident can gift Indian securities to NRI relative via FC-TRS filing (shares transferred to NRI's NRO/NRE demat).

/ Documentation + best practice

The paper trail.

  • Written gift deed: for all material gifts, not just immovable property. Notarised at least; registered if immovable property.
  • Content: donor name + address, recipient name + address, nature of gift (money / property / securities), amount or description, date, acceptance by recipient, witness signatures, consideration clause (voluntary / without consideration), relationship statement (critical for Section 56(2)(x) relative exemption).
  • Bank route: wire transfer / NEFT / RTGS / SWIFT. No cash for gifts above INR 2 lakh (Section 269ST).
  • Form 15CA + 15CB: for remittance out of India above INR 5 lakh in FY (though for gift from Indian relative to NRI, often not required because gift is not 'chargeable to tax' under Section 195 - but AD bank practice varies).
  • ITR disclosure: both donor and recipient should disclose material gifts in their respective ITR (Schedule EI for exempt gifts; Schedule OS if taxable).
  • 5-year retention: all documentation preserved for 5+ years to support tax position if questioned.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Is NRI gift to Indian parents tax-free?
Yes - parents are 'relatives' under Explanation to Section 56(2)(x). Gift from NRI son/daughter to Indian parent is fully exempt in parent's hands, any amount, under Section 56(2)(x). On FEMA side: outbound remittance from abroad to India is inbound into Indian resident's bank account - no FEMA restriction on receipt. Gift deed documentation recommended.
Can Indian parents gift money to NRI daughter / son abroad?
Yes - via LRS (Liberalised Remittance Scheme) up to USD 250,000 per Indian resident per FY (combined for all current + capital account purposes). Documented with Form A2 + gift purpose code + PAN. Gift deed strengthens the case. Section 56(2)(x) exempts the recipient NRI (parent is 'relative'). TCS 20% under Section 206C(1G) on LRS above INR 7 lakh/FY applies on remitting side.
Can Indian resident gift Indian property to NRI son / daughter?
Yes - via registered gift deed in India with stamp duty + registration (state-specific; varies 2-7% of circle rate / market value). Permitted under FEMA (gift of immovable property to NRI relative allowed; agricultural land excluded). NRI holds the property with NRO-linked income; eventual sale subject to Section 195 TDS + USD 1M/year repatriation.
Can an NRI give gift of Indian shares / securities to an Indian relative?
Yes - via FC-TRS filing (transfer of shares between non-resident and resident). Transfer at nil consideration (gift) permitted between relatives. Stamp duty on share transfer applicable. Section 56(2)(x) exempts recipient (donor is relative). Capital gain for NRI = nil (gift, no consideration). Cost of acquisition for Indian recipient = original cost in NRI's hands (Section 49(1)).
Does TCS apply to gift from Indian parent to NRI child?
Yes - under Section 206C(1G), 20% TCS on LRS remittance exceeding INR 7 lakh in FY (post Finance Act 2024). Collected by Indian remitting bank on Indian donor's side. Donor can claim TCS credit against own income tax or claim refund in own ITR. Does not affect recipient NRI's position.
