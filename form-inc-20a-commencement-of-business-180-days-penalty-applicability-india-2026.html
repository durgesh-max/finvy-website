Form INC-20A (2026): Commencement of Business Declaration, 180-Day Deadline, Penalty and Who Must File, file it before the company starts trading or borrowing.

Form INC-20A is the declaration for commencement of business that a company with share capital must file with the Registrar of Companies within 180 days of incorporation, confirming that every subscriber to the memorandum has paid for the shares they agreed to take. It comes from section 10A of the Companies Act, 2013, which applies to companies incorporated on or after 2 November 2018. Until the declaration is filed, the company cannot commence business or exercise borrowing powers. If it is not filed, the company faces a penalty of ₹50,000 and each officer in default ₹1,000 per day up to ₹1,00,000, and the Registrar can start striking off the company if it believes no business is being carried on.

/ Quick answers

What people search for.

  • What is INC-20A? A one-time e-form declaring that subscribers have paid the subscription money for their shares, so the company can start business.
  • Who must file it? Every company having share capital incorporated on or after 2 November 2018, including private limited companies and foreign-owned subsidiaries. Companies without share capital are outside section 10A.
  • What is the deadline? Within 180 days of the date of incorporation shown on the certificate of incorporation.
  • What is the penalty for not filing? ₹50,000 on the company and ₹1,000 per day on each officer in default, up to ₹1,00,000 per officer; late filing also attracts additional fees on the MCA portal.
  • What is attached? Proof that subscription money was received, usually the company bank statement, and the form must be certified by a practising chartered accountant, company secretary or cost accountant.
  • Is a certificate issued? No separate certificate is issued; the filed form and its acknowledgement are the record.

/ If the deadline has passed

Fix it before it compounds.

  • File late: the form can still be filed with additional fees; file as soon as the subscription money is in and evidenced.
  • Expect adjudication: Registrars have issued penalty orders for non-filing, so keep the bank evidence and board records ready.
  • Avoid strike-off: an unfiled declaration combined with no business activity can lead to removal of the company’s name; respond to any Registrar notice within its deadline.
  • Foreign subsidiaries: match the INC-20A amount with the inward remittance and the FC-GPR filing; see company registration for foreign nationals.
  • Annual compliance next: subsidiary compliance calendar and AOC-4 filing.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is Form INC-20A?
The declaration for commencement of business under section 10A of the Companies Act, 2013. A company with share capital files it to confirm that every subscriber has paid the value of the shares they agreed to take, before it starts business or borrows.
What is the due date for INC-20A?
Within 180 days of incorporation, for companies with share capital incorporated on or after 2 November 2018.
What is the penalty for not filing INC-20A?
Rs 50,000 on the company and Rs 1,000 per day on each officer in default, up to Rs 1,00,000. The Registrar can also start removing the company's name if it believes no business is being carried on.
Is INC-20A applicable to all companies?
It applies to companies having share capital incorporated on or after 2 November 2018. Companies without share capital and companies incorporated before that date are outside section 10A.
Who certifies INC-20A?
A practising chartered accountant, company secretary or cost accountant, with a director's digital signature, and with proof that the subscription money was received attached.
