Income Tax Notice for Property or Land Purchase (2026): Why You Got It, How to Reply and What Proof of Source of Funds to Send, show where every rupee came from.

A buyer usually gets an income tax notice after a property or land purchase because the registrar reported the deal to the Income Tax Department and the price does not match the income in the buyer's returns - or no return was filed. The notice is really one question: where did the money come from? A clear reply traces every payment to a bank account, a home loan, a gift, an earlier sale or declared income. If the source is not explained, the amount can be taxed as unexplained investment at about 78% (60% tax plus surcharge and cess).

/ Why buyers get notices

The registrar reports the deal.

  • SFT reporting: the registrar or sub-registrar reports property purchases and sales in the statement of financial transactions (SFT) - Rs 30 lakh or more up to 31 March 2026, and Rs 45 lakh or more (price or stamp duty value, including gifts and joint development agreements) from 1 April 2026 under rule 237 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026. The deal then shows in your Annual Information Statement (AIS).
  • Income does not match: the price is high compared with the income in your returns, or you did not file a return for that year.
  • Cash: cash payments raise a source-of-cash question. A seller cannot accept Rs 20,000 or more in cash for a property deal (section 269SS); the penalty is equal to the cash accepted.
  • Stamp duty value above the price: if the stamp duty value is higher than the price by more than the higher of Rs 50,000 and 10% of the price, the difference is taxed as the buyer's income (section 56(2)(x)).
  • Buyer TDS not deducted: 1% TDS on a purchase of Rs 50 lakh or more from a resident seller, reported in Form 141 (old 26QB), or TDS on the whole price, with no Rs 50 lakh threshold, when the seller is an NRI - from 1 October 2026 a resident individual or HUF buyer can do this with a PAN and Form 141 instead of a TAN (no TAN needed). A missing or short deduction leads to a TDS default notice.
  • Joint purchase: the AIS can show the full price against each buyer. Each co-owner should show their own share and where it came from.

/ Reply format

There is no prescribed format - this structure works.

  • Header: name, PAN, assessment year, notice section, DIN and date.
  • The purchase: property address, date of the deed, price, stamp duty value and your share if jointly bought.
  • Source of funds table: each payment with date, amount, bank account and source - savings, home loan, gift from a relative, sale of an earlier asset, agricultural income, NRE / NRO funds.
  • Proof for each line: bank statements, loan sanction and disbursement letter, gift deed with the donor's bank statement and PAN, earlier sale deed and capital gains working, land records for agricultural income, foreign remittance certificates for NRIs.
  • Close: ask for the proceedings to be dropped, and request a personal hearing by video if the officer is not satisfied.
  • Submit on e-Proceedings (or the compliance portal for e-campaigns). The reply cannot be edited once submitted, so check the figures first. Our free notice reply generator builds a first draft.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Why did I get an income tax notice after buying property or land?
Because the registrar reported the purchase to the Income Tax Department in the statement of financial transactions and it appears in your AIS. If the price does not match the income in your returns, you paid cash, the stamp duty value is much higher than the price, or buyer TDS was missed, the department asks you to explain the source of the money.
What is the limit for property purchase reporting to income tax?
Registrars report purchases and sales of Rs 30 lakh or more for deals up to 31 March 2026. From 1 April 2026, under rule 237 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026, the limit is Rs 45 lakh or more of price or stamp duty value, and gifts and joint development agreements are covered too. A purchase below the limit can still be questioned if it shows up in other information.
How do I reply to an income tax notice for a property purchase?
Check the DIN, note the deadline, then reply on e-Proceedings (or the compliance portal for an e-campaign) with a table of every payment, its date, the bank account and the source, backed by bank statements, the loan sanction letter, gift deeds and earlier sale papers. There is no prescribed format, and the reply cannot be edited after submission.
What happens if I cannot explain the source of money used to buy property?
The unexplained amount can be added to your income as unexplained investment and taxed at 60% plus a 25% surcharge and cess - about 78% - under section 115BBE, with penalty proceedings possible. That is why each payment should be traced to a documented source before replying.
I bought the property jointly. Do I explain the whole price?
Explain your own share. The AIS may show the full price against each buyer, so give feedback in the AIS if the figure is wrong and show in the reply how much each co-owner paid and from which account.
