/ Form 67 purpose + framework

What it does.

  • Form 67 = statement of foreign income + foreign tax paid - filed to claim FTC in Indian ITR.
  • Legal basis: Section 90 + Section 90A + Section 91 + Rule 128 Income Tax Rules.
  • Mandatory to claim FTC: no Form 67 = no FTC claim allowed (specific case-law + CBDT clarifications).
  • Must be filed BEFORE Indian ITR due date (31 July for individuals + 31 October for business / specific audit cases).
  • Separate Form 67 for each FY where FTC claimed.
  • Multiple foreign jurisdictions + income sources aggregated in single Form 67 per FY.

/ When Form 67 is needed

The applicability triggers.

  • Indian resident (ROR) with foreign-source income (salary, interest, dividend, capital gain, royalty, business, specific sources).
  • Foreign tax paid on same income (TDS, estimated tax, specific withholding).
  • Indian tax computed on worldwide income including foreign source.
  • Claim FTC in Indian ITR to prevent double taxation.
  • Specific examples: US K-1 income with 1446 withholding, UK dividend with specific tax, Singapore dividend, UAE specific tax, Australian trust distribution, Canadian employment income.

/ Rule 128 computation framework

How FTC is calculated.

  • Credit = lower of (a) foreign tax paid on specific income, (b) Indian tax on same income at specific rate.
  • Specific head-wise computation: salary / interest / dividend / capital gain / business / specific - credit computed per head.
  • Specific per-country computation if multiple foreign jurisdictions.
  • Specific currency conversion: SBI reference rate on specific date (date of payment / date of tax deduction / specific).
  • DTAA relief: specific DTAA Article 24 (relief from double taxation) governs.
  • Section 91 relief (for non-DTAA countries): proportionate credit without specific treaty.
  • Specific unused FTC carry-forward: generally NOT allowed in India (unlike US / UK where carry-forward exists).

/ Documentation required for Form 67

What to attach.

  • Form 67 itself (filed on e-filing portal).
  • Nature + amount of foreign source income (self-declaration).
  • Nature + amount of foreign tax paid (self-declaration + specific supporting).
  • Supporting documents retained (not uploaded with Form 67 but kept for audit):
  •   — Foreign tax return / tax calculation + specific tax paid certificate.
  •   — Specific withholding certificates (US 1042-S, UK CT61, Singapore Form IR8A, specific per jurisdiction).
  •   — Specific income statement from foreign payer (K-1 / Dividend voucher / P60 / specific).
  •   — Specific foreign bank statement showing receipt + specific tax deduction.
  •   — Specific currency conversion computation (SBI reference rate dates + specific calculations).

/ Step-by-step filing workflow

The e-filing process.

  1. Login India e-filing portal (incometax.gov.in).
  2. Navigate: e-File > Income Tax Forms > File Income Tax Forms.
  3. Select Form 67 - Foreign Tax Credit.
  4. Select AY + specific country(ies).
  5. Enter: foreign-source income (head-wise + country-wise + amount in INR using SBI reference rate).
  6. Enter: foreign tax paid (head-wise + country-wise + amount in INR).
  7. Compute FTC per Rule 128 (lower of foreign tax or Indian tax).
  8. Review + validate.
  9. e-Verify via DSC / Aadhaar OTP / specific.
  10. Receive acknowledgement.
  11. File Indian ITR with specific FTC claimed in applicable schedules.
  12. Retain all supporting documents for 6-year audit lookback (minimum).

/ Common errors + how to avoid

What to watch.

  • Filing Form 67 AFTER ITR due date (31 July / 31 October): FTC may be denied; specific condonation requests under Section 119(2)(b) in exceptional cases.
  • Not filing Form 67 at all (just claiming FTC in ITR): generally denied.
  • Wrong currency conversion: must use SBI reference rate on specific dates (date of tax payment / credit).
  • Not head-wise matching: FTC claimed for salary while foreign tax was on dividend - mismatch denies credit.
  • Not considering DTAA treaty rate: can affect specific tax base + specific credit.
  • Specific unused FTC claim as carry-forward (not allowed in India).
  • Not retaining specific supporting documents for audit lookback.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is Form 67?
Form 67 is statement of foreign income + foreign tax paid filed on India e-filing portal to claim Foreign Tax Credit (FTC) under Section 90/91. Mandatory - no Form 67 = no FTC. Must be filed BEFORE Indian ITR due date (31 July / 31 October). Separate per FY. Multiple foreign jurisdictions + income types aggregated in single form.
When is Form 67 due?
BEFORE Indian ITR due date for that FY. For individual non-audit: 31 July. For business / specific audit: 31 October. If specifically delayed under Section 119(2)(b): CBDT condonation may be sought (exceptional cases only). Default: late Form 67 = FTC denied.
How is FTC calculated under Rule 128?
Credit = lower of (a) foreign tax paid on specific income, (b) Indian tax on same income at specific rate. Head-wise + country-wise computation. Currency conversion at SBI reference rate on specific dates. Specific DTAA Article 24 (relief from double taxation) + Section 91 (non-DTAA countries) governing framework. Unused FTC: generally NOT carried forward in India.
Do I need foreign tax certificate for Form 67?
Form 67 itself doesn't require document upload. Supporting documents (foreign tax return / withholding certificates / income statements / bank statements / currency conversion) must be retained for 6-year audit lookback. If scrutiny: BQP + client produce documents in defence.
