/ Section 80GG eligibility

Who qualifies.

  • Individual (not HUF / firm).
  • Self-employed OR salaried but NOT receiving HRA.
  • Pays rent for residential accommodation.
  • Does NOT own residential property in city of employment / self-employment.
  • Spouse + minor child do NOT own residential property in city of residence.
  • Does NOT claim HRA under Section 10(13A).
  • File Form 10BA + claim in ITR.

/ Section 80GG deduction amount

The 3-way minimum.

  • Deduction = LOWER of three:
  •   — (a) INR 5,000 per month = INR 60,000 per year.
  •   — (b) 25% of adjusted total income (ATI).
  •   — (c) Actual rent paid MINUS 10% of ATI.
  • Adjusted Total Income = gross total income minus specific deductions (long-term capital gain, Section 56(2)(x), Chapter VI-A except 80GG itself).
  • Rate materially lower than HRA for employees - Section 80GG is a backstop, not a parallel.

/ Form 10BA mechanics

The declaration.

  • Form 10BA: self-declaration by taxpayer.
  • Content: name + PAN + address of property + name of landlord + rent amount + period + confirmation of no residential property ownership.
  • Submitted via Indian income tax e-filing portal before filing ITR.
  • Not CA-attested.
  • Supporting documents: rent receipts + rent agreement + landlord's PAN (if rent > INR 1 lakh per year).

/ Section 80GG vs HRA

The comparison.

HRA under Section 10(13A): for salaried employees receiving HRA from employer. Exemption = lower of (a) actual HRA, (b) rent paid - 10% of basic salary, (c) 50% of basic salary (metro) / 40% (non-metro). Typically much larger than Section 80GG.

Section 80GG: for self-employed OR salaried without HRA. Deduction capped at INR 60,000 per year typically. Materially smaller than HRA.

For freelancers / consultants / self-employed: Section 80GG is the only rent-related tax benefit (apart from Section 24 home loan interest if owned property, which disqualifies 80GG).

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Who can claim Section 80GG?
Individual (not HUF / firm) who: (a) pays rent for residential accommodation, (b) does NOT own residential property in city of employment / self-employment, (c) spouse + minor child also don't own residential property there, (d) does NOT receive HRA from employer. Must file Form 10BA + claim in ITR.
What's the maximum Section 80GG deduction?
Lower of: (a) INR 5,000 per month = INR 60,000 per year, (b) 25% of adjusted total income, (c) actual rent paid - 10% of adjusted total income. Typically capped at INR 60,000 per year. Much smaller than HRA for salaried employees.
Can I claim both HRA and Section 80GG?
No - mutually exclusive. If you receive HRA from employer (even partially), you claim HRA exemption under Section 10(13A). Section 80GG specifically for those who do NOT receive HRA. Choose one based on employment structure.
Do I need landlord's PAN for Section 80GG?
If annual rent exceeds INR 1 lakh (INR 8,333/month): yes - landlord's PAN mandatory on Form 10BA. Also required for HRA exemption above this threshold. Below INR 1 lakh / year: PAN not required.
Can freelancer claim Section 80GG?
Yes - self-employed freelancer paying rent for residential accommodation qualifies for Section 80GG (subject to other conditions). File Form 10BA + claim in ITR. Combined with Section 44ADA presumptive scheme (if eligible), can optimise tax position.
