/ Indian founders · US incorporation · FEMA ODI · Updated 2026-11-13

The Triangular Structure: Indian Founders, a US Parent and an Indian Subsidiary Under FEMA's Overseas Investment Rules (2026), the rule most founder diagrams leave out.

The structure most Indian startups copy is a triangle: founders living in India own a Delaware (or other US) parent, and the parent owns the Indian operating company. Under the FEMA Overseas Investment Rules, 2022, a resident individual's general permission covers only an operating foreign company that has no subsidiary or step-down subsidiary where the individual has control, and control starts at 10% of the votes, board control or control of management. So a founder-controlled US parent that owns an Indian company usually falls outside the automatic route and needs approval. Separately, the round-tripping rule allows no more than two layers of subsidiaries.

/ What founders can do

Options, in order of simplicity.

  • Seek approval up front: where the founders will hold control in the US parent that owns the Indian company, apply for approval through the authorised dealer bank before investing or swapping shares.
  • Indian company as the parent: an Indian company can set up a US subsidiary under the general route for Indian entities (Schedule I), within the two-layer limit, which avoids the individual-control restriction.
  • Build in India first, flip later if investors insist: many founders now keep the Indian company as the parent and only flip when a lead investor requires it, with the FEMA and tax costs priced in.
  • Already in a triangle? Map every share issue and transfer, check what was reported, and regularise with the bank and RBI (late submission fees or compounding) before a fundraise or exit.

/ Other points in the same structure

Founders ask about these too.

  • Flip share swap: taxable for Indian shareholders (12.5% on long-term gains on unlisted shares held over 24 months, at fair value); see the flip tax calculator.
  • Place of effective management: if the US company is run entirely from India, it can be treated as resident in India.
  • Transfer pricing: the Indian subsidiary charges the US parent cost plus a margin; from tax year 2026-27 a 15.5% safe harbour can apply. See the 2026 safe harbour.
  • Angel tax: no longer applies to share issues from assessment year 2025-26 onwards.
  • Founders' own returns: the US shares go in Schedule FA every year, and the annual performance report is due by 31 December.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Can Indian resident founders own a US company that owns an Indian subsidiary?
Not under the general permission if the founders have control in the US company. Schedule III of the Overseas Investment Rules lets a resident individual invest only in a foreign company that has no subsidiary or step-down subsidiary where the individual has control; otherwise approval is needed.
What counts as control for this rule?
10% or more of the voting rights, the right to appoint a majority of directors, or control of management or policy decisions.
What is the two-layer round-tripping rule?
Rule 19(3) bars a financial commitment in a foreign entity that has invested or invests into India where it results in more than two layers of subsidiaries.
Can an Indian company set up the US subsidiary instead?
Yes. An Indian company invests under the general route for Indian entities (Schedule I), subject to the two-layer limit, which avoids the individual-control restriction.
We already have a triangle. What now?
Map every share issue and transfer, check what was reported to the bank and RBI, and regularise through late submission fees or compounding before a fundraise or exit.
