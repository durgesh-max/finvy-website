Gift Deed to an NRI Son or Daughter (2026): Registration, FEMA Rules, Tax and Stamp Duty for Indian Property, registered, not just notarised.

Parents in India can gift a flat, house or commercial property to an NRI or OCI son or daughter: FEMA allows gifts of residential and commercial property between relatives without RBI approval, and the gift is tax-free for the child as a gift from a relative. For immovable property the gift must be made by a registered gift deed, signed by the parent and attested by two witnesses; a notarised deed is not enough. The child must accept the gift while the parent is alive, which can be done from abroad. Agricultural land, plantations and farmhouses cannot be gifted to an NRI or OCI. Stamp duty is set by each state and is often lower for family gifts. When the child later sells, the parent’s cost and holding period carry over.

/ Quick answers

What families ask.

  • Is a gift deed necessary? For immovable property, yes, and it must be registered. For money or shares, a gift deed is not legally required but is good evidence of a gift.
  • Can a gift deed be notarised instead of registered? Not for immovable property: a notarised gift deed does not transfer a flat or land. It can support a gift of money or movable assets.
  • Who can a gift deed be given to? Anyone can receive a gift, but only gifts from relatives (parents, spouse, siblings, children and others in the income tax list) are fully tax-free, and FEMA lets an NRI or OCI receive Indian property by gift only from a relative.
  • Can I gift property to my NRI son? Yes, residential or commercial property, with no RBI approval. Not agricultural land, plantations or farmhouses.
  • Does my son need to come to India? Usually not: he can accept the gift by signing at an Indian consulate or through a registered power of attorney holder, depending on the state’s registration practice.

/ Steps and later sale

Doing it properly.

  • Draft the deed: names, relationship, property description, that the gift is voluntary and without consideration, and acceptance by the child.
  • Pay stamp duty and register at the sub-registrar’s office where the property is, with the parent and two witnesses; check the state’s concessional rate for family gifts.
  • Acceptance from abroad: the child signs an acceptance at an Indian consulate or appoints a power of attorney holder; see power of attorney for NRIs.
  • Update records: mutation in local land and municipal records, society share certificate and utility accounts.
  • Later sale: the parent’s purchase cost and holding period count for capital gains, and the buyer deducts tax because the seller is an NRI; see NRI property sale.
  • Revocation: a completed gift generally cannot be revoked, except on conditions written into the deed or, for senior citizens, where a gift made on condition of care is not honoured under the senior citizens’ maintenance law.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Is a gift deed necessary to gift property to my son?
Yes for immovable property: a gift of a flat, house or land must be made by a registered gift deed signed by the giver and attested by two witnesses. For money or shares a gift deed is not required, though a simple deed or letter is useful evidence.
Can a gift deed be notarised instead of registered?
Not for immovable property. A notarised gift deed does not transfer ownership of a flat or land; it must be registered with the sub-registrar. Notarisation can support a gift of money or movable assets.
Can parents gift property in India to an NRI son or daughter?
Yes. Under FEMA, an NRI or OCI can receive residential or commercial property by gift from a relative without RBI approval. Agricultural land, plantations and farmhouses cannot be gifted to them.
Is a gift from parents to an NRI child taxed in India?
No. Gifts from relatives, including parents, are exempt from income tax in the receiver's hands. On a later sale, the parent's cost and holding period are used to work out capital gains.
Does the NRI child need to travel to India for the gift deed?
Usually not. The child can accept the gift by signing at an Indian consulate or through a registered power of attorney holder, depending on the state's registration practice; the parent and two witnesses attend the registration.
