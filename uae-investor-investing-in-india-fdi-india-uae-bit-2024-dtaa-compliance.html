/ UAE companies, family offices & investors · FDI · Updated 2026-11-12

Investing in India From the UAE: FDI Route, the India-UAE BIT 2024 and Tax Treaty Points (2026 Guide), protection signed, paperwork still yours.

UAE companies, free zone entities, family offices and individuals invest in India mostly under the automatic FDI route, with no prior approval in most sectors. Since 31 August 2024 their investments are protected by the India-UAE Bilateral Investment Treaty, signed on 13 February 2024, which notably covers portfolio investment as well as direct investment. Protection does not replace compliance: the Indian company must report the investment on Form FC-GPR within 30 days of allotting shares, issue shares at not less than fair value, and file the annual FLA return.

/ The FDI steps

For an investment into an Indian company.

  • Route: automatic route for most sectors; government approval for restricted sectors, and for investors with beneficial owners in countries sharing a land border with India (see Press Note 2 of 2026: the 10% test).
  • Pricing: shares issued to a non-resident at not less than fair value under an internationally accepted method, supported by a valuation report.
  • Reporting: Form FC-GPR within 30 days of allotment on the RBI FIRMS portal; transfers between residents and non-residents on Form FC-TRS.
  • Annual: the FLA return by 15 July each year (FLA guide), plus corporate and transfer pricing compliance if there are dealings with the UAE group.
  • Before investing: financial, tax and legal due diligence on the Indian company; see transaction due diligence in India.

/ Tax points

Dividends, exits and the treaty.

  • Tax treaty: dividends, interest and capital gains are governed by the India-UAE tax treaty together with Indian law; see India-UAE DTAA for rates and conditions.
  • Treaty access: claiming treaty rates needs a UAE tax residency certificate and Form 10F, and the investor must meet the treaty's residence and anti-abuse conditions.
  • Exits: plan the exit route (secondary sale, buy-back, IPO) at entry, because each is taxed and reported differently.
  • Repatriation: dividends and sale proceeds can be repatriated after tax; see repatriating profits to a foreign parent.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

When did the India-UAE bilateral investment treaty come into force?
On 31 August 2024. It was signed in Abu Dhabi on 13 February 2024 and replaced the 2013 agreement.
Does the India-UAE BIT cover portfolio investments?
Yes. Its asset-based definition of investment includes portfolio investment, which sets it apart from India's Model BIT approach.
Do UAE investors need approval to invest in an Indian company?
Not in most sectors, which are under the automatic route. Restricted sectors need government approval, as do investors with beneficial owners in countries sharing a land border with India.
What must the Indian company file when a UAE investor buys shares?
Form FC-GPR within 30 days of allotment, a valuation showing the price is not below fair value, and the annual FLA return by 15 July.
Can a UAE investor go straight to arbitration under the BIT?
Not straight away. The treaty requires local remedies to be pursued first, reported as three years, before investor-state arbitration.
