/ Trademark India for foreign applicant

Two routes.

Route 1: Madrid Protocol (international registration)

  • File a base application in home country (USPTO / UKIPO / EUIPO / etc.) then designate India via WIPO Madrid Protocol.
  • Single application covers India + other designated countries.
  • Timeline: 2-4 years from Madrid filing to Indian registration (India examines designations independently).
  • Benefits: simpler administration (single renewal, single modification), cost-effective for multi-country protection.
  • Drawbacks: dependent on home base registration first 5 years (central attack); Indian objections handled by Indian IP agent.

Route 2: Direct Indian application

  • File directly at Indian Trademark Registry via Form TM-A (national application).
  • Foreign applicant must appoint Indian attorney / agent.
  • Timeline: 18-36 months typical from filing to registration (varies by class + opposition).
  • Benefits: independent of home base, direct control, faster for Indian-specific priority.
  • Drawbacks: country-by-country costs if multi-national protection needed.

Which to pick:

  • Multi-country launch (India + 3+ others): Madrid.
  • India-only or India-priority: Direct.
  • Existing US / EU registration + want India expansion: Madrid designation if home registration is already granted.

/ Trademark workflow + cost

India specifics.

  1. Trademark search: free search on Indian TM Registry database (ipindiaonline.gov.in). Identify similar marks in your Nice class(es). Essential to avoid oppositions.
  2. Classification: pick Nice Class(es) — 45 total (34 goods + 11 services). Fee per class.
  3. Filing: TM-A (national) or WIPO Madrid designation. Government fee ~INR 4,500-9,000 per class (varies by applicant type: startup/MSME half-fee).
  4. Examination: Indian examiner reviews on absolute grounds (distinctiveness) + relative grounds (prior marks) in ~6-18 months.
  5. Examination report (if raised): respond within 1 month. If objection sustained, hearing before Trademark Hearing Officer.
  6. Publication: approved applications published in Trademark Journal.
  7. Opposition: 4-month window post-publication for third parties to oppose. If opposed → proceedings before Trademark Registry (can take 2-4 years).
  8. Registration: if no opposition / opposition dismissed, certificate issued.
  9. Validity: 10 years from filing, renewable indefinitely.

/ Patent India for foreign applicant

PCT national phase + direct.

Route 1: PCT National Phase Entry

  • File PCT international application at WIPO (claiming priority from US / UK / etc. patent).
  • Enter Indian national phase within 31 months of priority date.
  • Filing fee ~INR 1,600-8,000 (government) + attorney fee.
  • Timeline: 4-7 years from PCT filing to Indian grant (varies by field).
  • Most foreign-origin Indian patents use this route.

Route 2: Direct Indian application (Convention application)

  • Within 12 months of priority date in home country.
  • Independent Indian prosecution.
  • Faster if India is key market.

Patent workflow:

  1. Prior art search.
  2. Filing: Form 1 (Request for Patent) + Form 2 (Provisional/Complete Specification) + claims + drawings. Attorney fee significant.
  3. 18-month publication (automatic unless secrecy direction).
  4. Request for Examination within 48 months (Form 18).
  5. First Examination Report (FER) — respond within 6-12 months.
  6. Hearing (if needed) before Controller.
  7. Grant → publication → 20-year validity from filing date.
  8. Annuity fees every year from 3rd year.

Section 3 restrictions: certain subject matter not patentable in India — new form of known substance (Section 3(d)), method of agriculture, business methods per se, mathematical/business methods, computer programs per se, plants/animals (except microorganisms). Section 3(d) especially important for pharma patents.

/ Copyright + design + GI

Other IP categories.

Copyright:

  • Automatic protection on creation (no registration mandatory in India for enforceability).
  • Registration optional but useful as evidence of ownership + date.
  • Covers: original literary / artistic / musical / dramatic works + cinematograph films + sound recordings + software source code.
  • Term: author's life + 60 years for literary/musical works.
  • Foreign authors / corporations: Berne Convention + India IP agent for registration.

Design Registration:

  • Protects shape, configuration, ornamentation of articles.
  • Term: 10 years + 5-year renewal.
  • Priority claim within 6 months of foreign filing.

Geographical Indication (GI):

  • Mostly for Indian-origin products (Darjeeling tea, Basmati rice, Mysore silk).
  • Foreign GI possible for products uniquely associated with a foreign region.

/ IP enforcement + oppositions

What happens post-registration.

  • Infringement suit: civil remedy before District Court / High Court. Injunction + damages + account of profits.
  • Opposition (TM): as above, 4-month post-publication window.
  • Rectification: cancel registered mark on grounds of non-use (5 years) or improper registration.
  • Pre-grant opposition (Patent): before grant, by any person.
  • Post-grant opposition (Patent): within 1 year of grant.
  • Customs enforcement: register IP with Indian Customs (Form IEIR) to block counterfeit imports at border.
  • Criminal remedy (TM): Section 103-104 Trademarks Act — imprisonment + fine for counterfeiters.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Can a US / UK / Singapore company register trademark in India directly?
Yes - via two routes: (a) Madrid Protocol designating India via WIPO (requires home-country base registration), or (b) Direct Indian application via Form TM-A through an Indian trademark attorney. Both work. Madrid is cost-effective for multi-country protection; Direct is faster if India is the priority.
How long does Indian trademark registration take?
18-36 months typical from filing to registration if no opposition. Timeline: examination (6-18 months), publication (immediate after examination clearance), 4-month opposition window, registration certificate issuance. If opposed, add 2-4 years for opposition proceedings. Madrid Protocol designation similar - Indian examination independent.
Can a foreign company file a patent in India?
Yes - via (a) PCT national phase entry within 31 months of priority date, or (b) direct Indian application (Convention route) within 12 months of priority. Both require Indian patent agent. Timeline: 4-7 years from PCT filing to Indian grant typical. Section 3 restrictions apply - new form of known substance, business methods per se, software per se all excluded.
Does Section 3(d) really block pharma patents?
Yes for 'evergreening' claims. Section 3(d) prohibits patents on new forms (salts, polymorphs, isomers, etc.) of known substances unless they show significantly enhanced efficacy. Famous Novartis Glivec case - Supreme Court denied patent on imatinib mesylate crystalline form. Foreign pharma applicants should assess Section 3(d) carefully for secondary patents.
Is copyright registration mandatory in India?
No - copyright is automatic on creation (per Copyright Act). Registration optional but useful: provides prima facie evidence of ownership + date of creation + nature of work. Useful if enforcement litigation is likely. Foreign works protected under Berne Convention + Universal Copyright Convention automatically.
