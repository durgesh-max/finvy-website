/ PA vs PG - the distinction

What each does.

Payment Aggregator (PA):

  • Entity that onboards merchants to accept various payment instruments (cards, UPI, net banking, wallet) and settles funds to merchants.
  • Takes custody of funds for brief period (merchant's funds flow via PA's nodal account to merchant's bank).
  • Needs RBI authorisation (licence) under PSS Act 2007.
  • Example: Razorpay, Cashfree, Paytm Payment Services, PhonePe for Business.

Payment Gateway (PG):

  • Technology provider that routes payment data between merchant, customer, acquirer, issuer. Does NOT handle funds custody.
  • Does NOT need RBI authorisation separately but is subject to RBI guidelines (data localisation, security standards).
  • Example: pure PG function of certain gateways (though most Indian PGs are also PAs).

PA-CB (Payment Aggregator Cross-Border):

  • Specialised PA licence for cross-border payment flows: Indian consumer paying foreign merchant, or foreign consumer paying Indian merchant.
  • Separate authorisation framework (RBI October 2023 PA-CB circular).
  • Three sub-categories: PA-E (Export, for Indian merchants selling abroad), PA-I (Import, for Indian consumers buying abroad), PA-CB for both.
  • Enables UPI international linkages, card payments cross-border, international remittance for e-commerce.

/ Eligibility + net worth for PA

The capital requirement.

  • Company incorporated in India under Companies Act 2013 (private limited / public limited / unlisted / listed). Foreign fintech must have Indian subsidiary.
  • Net worth: minimum INR 15 crore in year 1 of application; INR 25 crore in year 3 and ongoing.
  • Net worth certification: CA certificate confirming net worth as of latest audited balance sheet date.
  • Promoters' background: fit and proper person test. Promoters / directors must not be disqualified; must have relevant financial / technology experience.
  • IT infrastructure: dedicated India-hosted infrastructure. Core payment systems hosted in India (data localisation).
  • Nodal / escrow account: specified bank for merchant funds; settlement to merchants within T+1 for most instruments.

/ Application + approval process

RBI PA licence workflow.

  1. Pre-application: structure Indian subsidiary (WOS of foreign parent typical); fund with min INR 15 crore net worth; set up IT infrastructure in India; appoint CTO + Compliance Officer + Chief Risk Officer.
  2. Application: Form A to RBI with supporting documents — company incorporation, MOA/AOA, net worth CA certificate, business plan, system architecture, security assessment, promoter / director KYC.
  3. RBI examination: typically 6-18 month review. Includes IT infrastructure inspection, business plan evaluation, promoter background check, legal + compliance review.
  4. In-principle approval: 1-year validity. Applicant must demonstrate operational readiness.
  5. Full authorisation (Certificate of Authorisation): upon RBI satisfaction. Entity becomes authorised PA.
  6. Ongoing compliance: monthly / quarterly reporting, annual CA certification of net worth, periodic IT audit, security incident reporting.

/ Key RBI PA / PG guidelines

The compliance stack.

  • Data localisation (RBI April 2018 circular): all payment data (card number, CVV, OTP, device data, user data) must be stored ONLY in India. Foreign fintech typically keeps India-only data stores for Indian users.
  • Card tokenisation (RBI September 2021): card storage prohibited by any entity except card networks + issuers. PA / PG must use network tokens for recurring payments.
  • Merchant onboarding (RBI March 2020 PA/PG circular): PA must conduct merchant KYC, maintain merchant database, submit to RBI on request.
  • Nodal account (RBI March 2020): merchant funds in nodal account; cannot be used for own purposes; T+1 settlement for most instruments.
  • Grievance redressal: standardised merchant + consumer complaint mechanism.
  • Security audit (annual): PCI DSS + CERT-In empanelled auditor annual audit.
  • Non-bank PPI (Prepaid Payment Instrument): separate licensing framework if issuing wallets.
  • UPI integration: NPCI approval + bank partnership for UPI handles.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is a Payment Aggregator (PA) licence in India?
RBI authorisation under Payment and Settlement Systems Act 2007 for entities that onboard merchants to accept various payment instruments (cards, UPI, net banking, wallet) and settle funds to merchants. Entity takes custody of funds briefly via nodal account before settling to merchant. Required for all PA activities since RBI March 2020 PA/PG circular. Minimum net worth INR 15 crore (year 1) scaling to INR 25 crore (year 3+).
Can a foreign fintech obtain Indian PA licence?
Yes - via Indian subsidiary. Foreign parent must incorporate Indian WOS (Pvt Ltd), capitalise it with min INR 15 crore net worth, set up India-based IT infrastructure (data localisation), appoint required officers (CTO, Compliance Officer, CRO), apply to RBI with Form A + documents. 6-18 month approval timeline typical.
What is PA-CB (Payment Aggregator Cross-Border)?
Specialised PA framework for cross-border payment flows - Indian consumer paying foreign merchant (PA-I Import) or foreign consumer paying Indian merchant (PA-E Export) or both (PA-CB). RBI October 2023 circular established the framework. Enables cross-border e-commerce, international UPI linkages, international card payments through authorised PA-CB entities.
What is India's data localisation for payment data?
RBI April 2018 directive: all 'payment data' (card number, CVV, device data, user data, OTPs) must be stored ONLY in India. Applies to PAs, PGs, card networks, banks, PPI issuers, any entity in payment ecosystem. Transit outside India permitted for processing but final storage must be India-only. Foreign fintech must architect India-only data stores for Indian users.
How much net worth does a foreign fintech need for Indian PA licence?
INR 15 crore (~USD 1.8M) minimum at time of application; must scale to INR 25 crore (~USD 3M) by end of year 3 and maintain. Net worth certified by CA via audited balance sheet. Separate from any float / nodal account balance - net worth is own capital of the licensed entity.
