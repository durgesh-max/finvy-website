High Value Transaction Notice from Income Tax (2026): Credit Card Spending, the SFT Limits, Why It Comes and How to Reply, show what paid for it.

A 'high value transaction' notice means a bank, card issuer, fund house, registrar or other reporting entity told the Income Tax Department about a large transaction in your name - and your return does not seem to explain how you paid for it. The usual triggers are credit card bills of Rs 10 lakh or more in a year, large cash deposits, fixed deposits, share or mutual fund purchases, property deals and foreign currency purchases. Most cases close once you show the source - salary, business income, savings, a loan or a gift - with documents, and reply on time.

/ What gets reported

The Statement of Financial Transactions limits.

TransactionReported when, in a financial year
Credit card bill paymentsRs 1 lakh or more paid in cash, or Rs 10 lakh or more paid by any mode
Cash depositsRs 10 lakh or more in savings accounts (a higher limit applies to current accounts)
Fixed / time depositsRs 10 lakh or more
Shares, mutual funds, bonds, debenturesPurchases of Rs 10 lakh or more
Immovable propertyPurchase or sale of Rs 30 lakh or more, reported as raised to Rs 45 lakh from 1 April 2026
Foreign currencyPurchases, including forex card loads and travellers' cheques, of Rs 10 lakh or more
  • Where it shows: reported transactions appear in your Annual Information Statement (AIS).
  • Why a notice follows: the department compares the AIS with your return; if spending or investment is high against declared income, or the return was not filed, it asks you to explain.

/ How to reply

Point by point, with documents.

  • Find the transaction in your AIS and check it is yours, correctly reported and not duplicated - an add-on card, a joint account or a family member's investment can be reported against you.
  • Compliance portal query: give feedback against each transaction - for example the information is correct and already covered in the return, relates to another person, or is duplicate - and add your explanation.
  • Show the source: salary credits, business receipts, savings built over earlier years, a loan, sale of an asset, or a gift from a relative with a gift deed and the giver's bank record.
  • Credit card bills: link the card payments to your bank account and show that account is funded from declared income; business expenses on a personal card should be backed by books or reimbursement records.
  • Section 133(6) letter or 148A notice: reply on the e-filing portal within the time given, with a covering explanation and the supporting papers.
  • If income was missed: a revised return (until 31 December 2026 for this year, with a fee after that up to 31 March 2027) or an updated return may be the cleaner route before a notice escalates.

/ What happens if it is not explained

Why the reply matters.

  • Unexplained investments, money or expenditure can be added under sections 69, 69A or 69C and taxed under section 115BBE at about 78% including surcharge and cess, with penalty exposure on top.
  • Ignoring a compliance query or a section 133(6) letter can lead to a reassessment notice under section 148A.
  • NRIs: large remittances or investments in India are usually explained by income earned abroad or savings - keep the foreign bank statements and remittance records ready.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is a high value transaction notice from income tax?
A query or notice asking you to explain a large transaction that a bank, card issuer, fund house, registrar or other entity reported in the Statement of Financial Transactions - such as credit card bills, cash deposits, fixed deposits, share or mutual fund purchases, property or foreign currency - because your return does not seem to account for it.
Can I get an income tax notice for credit card spending?
Yes. Credit card bill payments of Rs 10 lakh or more in a financial year by any mode, or Rs 1 lakh or more in cash, are reported to the department and appear in your AIS. If your declared income does not support that spending, you may be asked to explain how the bills were paid.
How do I reply to a high value transaction notice?
Check the transaction in your AIS, confirm it is yours and correctly reported, then explain the source with documents - salary, business income, savings, a loan, an asset sale or a documented gift. On the compliance portal give feedback against each transaction; for a section 133(6) or 148A notice, reply on the e-filing portal within the time given. There is no fixed format, but a point-by-point reply with papers works best.
What are the high value transaction limits for income tax reporting?
In a financial year: credit card bills of Rs 1 lakh in cash or Rs 10 lakh by any mode; cash deposits of Rs 10 lakh in savings accounts (higher for current accounts); fixed deposits of Rs 10 lakh; share, mutual fund, bond or debenture purchases of Rs 10 lakh; property of Rs 30 lakh, reported as raised to Rs 45 lakh from 1 April 2026; and foreign currency purchases of Rs 10 lakh.
Can the income tax department see or track my bank account?
Not in real time, but it sees a great deal. Banks report interest through TDS statements, and large transactions through the Statement of Financial Transactions, such as cash deposits adding up to INR 10 lakh or more in a year in savings accounts (INR 50 lakh in current accounts), fixed deposits of INR 10 lakh or more, and large credit card bill payments. All of this is linked to your PAN and shows up in your AIS, and mismatches with your return trigger e-campaign emails or notices. During an assessment or search the department can also ask a bank for full statements. Book a call if an AIS entry or a bank-related notice does not match your records.
Is money received from abroad taxable in India?
It depends on what the money is. An NRI's own savings sent home, or a gift from a relative, is generally not taxable; salary, business receipts or fees for services can be. Large inward remittances can be reported and questioned, so keep the foreign bank statements and the reason for the transfer.
Is buying gold taxable, and do I show it in my return?
Buying gold is not taxed in itself - what matters is that the money used can be explained from income or savings, because large purchases can be reported and questioned. Sellers must collect your PAN for cash purchases of Rs 2 lakh or more and cannot accept Rs 2 lakh or more in cash from one person for one transaction. Individuals with total income above Rs 1 crore report jewellery and bullion in Schedule AL. When you sell gold, the gain is a capital gain - long-term after 24 months, taxed at 12.5% without indexation.
