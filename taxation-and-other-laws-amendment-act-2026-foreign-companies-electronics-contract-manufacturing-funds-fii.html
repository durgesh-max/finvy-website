Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Act, 2026: What It Changes for Foreign Companies - Electronics Contract Manufacturing, Offshore Funds and Bond Investors, what it does, in plain terms.

The Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Act, 2026 received assent on 17 August 2026 and mostly applies from 1 April 2026. It gives foreign businesses and investors five things. A foreign company that keeps components in an Indian customs-bonded warehouse and sells them to an Indian contract manufacturer of electronics pays no Indian income tax on that income from 1 October 2026 until 31 March 2041. The existing exemption for supplying capital goods, equipment and tooling to such a manufacturer now runs to tax year 2040-41. Foreign institutional investors pay no tax on Indian government bond interest and gains from 1 April 2026. The safe harbour for offshore funds managed from India has fewer conditions. Foreign sellers of rough diamonds in notified zones are exempt, and the data-centre exemption wording is tightened. Separately, foreign investment in contract manufacturing is allowed up to 100% without approval. Each exemption needs prescribed information to be filed, so the structure and paperwork matter.

The contract manufacturer must be an Indian company producing them for the foreign company in a customs-bonded area.

/ Contract manufacturing in India: how it fits together

For foreign brands moving production.

  • Foreign investment: contract manufacturing counts as manufacturing, so 100% foreign ownership is allowed without approval, whether the Indian manufacturer acts as principal or as your agent.
  • Without an Indian entity: a foreign brand can buy finished goods from an Indian contract manufacturer and export them. Watch for a permanent establishment if your own people or stock in India go beyond purchasing; see the PE risk checker.
  • The new exemptions: for specified electronic goods, components you hold in a bonded warehouse and equipment you place with the manufacturer no longer create Indian income tax on that income, if the conditions and filings are met.
  • Customs and GST: in-bond manufacturing defers duty on imported inputs; exports are zero-rated. Get the customs set-up reviewed with the manufacturer.
  • Related-party pricing: if the manufacturer is your subsidiary, transfer pricing applies; see transfer pricing for foreign groups.
  • Company tax for an Indian manufacturing subsidiary: 25.17%; the 15% rate is closed to new companies.
  • Choosing the route: see China plus one: moving sourcing or manufacturing to India and the entry structure chooser.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is the Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Act, 2026?
A law that received assent on 17 August 2026 and mostly applies from 1 April 2026. It amends the Income-tax Act, 2025, the Finance Act, 2026 and the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007, with exemptions for foreign investors, offshore funds and electronics manufacturing.
Is a foreign company taxed in India on components kept in a bonded warehouse?
For specified electronic goods, income from storing components in a customs-bonded warehouse and selling them to an Indian contract manufacturer is exempt for income arising from 1 October 2026 until 31 March 2041, subject to prescribed information being furnished.
Can a foreign company use contract manufacturing in India without setting up there?
Yes. It can buy from an Indian contract manufacturer, and foreign investment in contract manufacturing is also allowed up to 100% without approval. Permanent establishment risk depends on what the foreign company's own people and stock do in India.
Do foreign investors pay tax on Indian government bonds?
Foreign institutional investors and the Bank for International Settlements pay no tax on interest or capital gains from Indian government securities for income arising on or after 1 April 2026, subject to prescribed information.
What changed in the safe harbour for offshore funds managed from India?
Four conditions were removed: the minimum of 25 investors, the 10% single-investor cap, the INR 100 crore minimum corpus and the 25% single-entity investment cap. Resident Indians may still hold no more than 5% of the corpus, and the Indian manager must meet registration and independence conditions.
