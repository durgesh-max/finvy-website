/ The CS compliance calendar

Monthly / quarterly / annual.

Annual filings:

  • AOC-4: financial statements filing within 30 days of AGM (typically 30 October).
  • MGT-7 / MGT-7A: annual return within 60 days of AGM (typically 29 November). MGT-7A for small companies / OPCs.
  • DIR-3 KYC: KYC for every DIN holder once every three financial years, by 30 June of the due year (G.S.R. 943(E), from 31 March 2026).
  • DPT-3: annual return of deposits + exempted deposits by 30 June.
  • Form 23B: appointment of auditor (first time + rotation).

Half-yearly filings:

  • PAS-6: Reconciliation of Share Capital Audit Report (for Rule 9B / unlisted companies with demat shares). Within 60 days of each half-year end (30 September, 31 March).
  • MSME Form 1: half-yearly return of outstanding dues to MSME suppliers over 45 days.

Event-based filings:

  • DIR-12: appointment / resignation of director within 30 days.
  • PAS-3: allotment of shares within 30 days.
  • MGT-14: board / shareholders' resolutions for specified matters within 30 days.
  • CHG-1 / CHG-4: creation / modification / satisfaction of charges within 30 days.
  • INC-22: change of registered office within 15 days.
  • SH-7: increase in authorised share capital within 30 days.
  • FC-GPR / FC-TRS: FEMA FDI reporting (coordinated with FEMA / RBI compliance).

Board + AGM meetings:

  • Minimum 4 board meetings per year with max 120-day gap.
  • AGM within 6 months of FY end + 15 months of previous AGM.
  • EGMs as needed.
  • Notice (21 days clear) + agenda + draft resolutions prepared by CS; dispatched via email/post.

/ Why foreign-shareholder subsidiaries especially need CS

Compliance stakes.

  • Related-party disclosures: every transaction with foreign parent / sister entities triggers Section 188 compliance + Audit Committee approval if above threshold. Mishandled RPT disclosures are a common audit / regulatory finding.
  • Section 185 inter-corporate loan: loans to directors (including common directors of parent) restricted. Common foreign-subsidiary compliance mistake.
  • Section 186 investment / loan limits: investment / loan given by Indian subsidiary limited to 60% of paid-up capital + free reserves + securities premium, or 100% of free reserves + securities premium (whichever higher). Common mishandled.
  • Managerial remuneration: Section 197 limits + Schedule V. Director remuneration from Indian subsidiary to foreign parent's expat executive requires compliance.
  • Independent director requirements: for public companies + certain large private companies.
  • CSR under Section 135: 2% CSR spend obligation if net worth INR 500 crore / turnover INR 1,000 crore / net profit INR 5 crore. Reporting via Form CSR-2.

Foreign-parent CFO + legal counsel typically unaware of these India-specific traps. CS integration prevents compliance surprises surfacing in annual audit.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What does a Company Secretary do for an Indian company?
Company Secretary (CS) handles all Companies Act 2013 compliance: board meetings, AGM, statutory filings (AOC-4, MGT-7, DPT-3, PAS-6, DIR-3 KYC (every three years), MSME Form 1), event-based filings (DIR-12, PAS-3, MGT-14, CHG forms), statutory registers (Members, Directors, Charges, RPT, Loans, Deposits), resolutions, Secretarial Audit for applicable companies. For foreign-shareholder subsidiaries, CS also handles FEMA FDI reporting coordination.
What annual compliance does a private limited company need, and can it be handled online?
Every year: at least four board meetings (two for a small company or OPC), the AGM by 30 September with audited accounts, AOC-4 (financial statements) within 30 days of the AGM, MGT-7 or MGT-7A (annual return) within 60 days of the AGM, ADT-1 when an auditor is appointed or re-appointed, DPT-3 by 30 June, MSME-1 by 30 April and 31 October where dues to micro or small suppliers are outstanding beyond 45 days, and DIR-3 KYC for each director once every three years by 30 June. On the tax side: the income tax return with tax audit where it applies, TDS returns, GST returns and, with foreign investment, FC-GPR / FC-TRS and the FLA return by 15 July. Annual ROC forms filed late attract an additional fee of Rs 100 a day per form, and repeated non-filing can lead to director disqualification. All of it can be handled online - board meetings by video conference, documents signed with DSCs and every form filed on the MCA portal - so the company's city does not matter. Can CS services be delivered remotely for foreign shareholders?
Yes - entirely remote. Board meetings via video conference (permitted under Companies Act with specific procedure), AGM physical or virtual (allowed), all filings electronic via MCA portal, statutory registers maintained in cloud. Foreign directors can sign resolutions digitally (DSC) or via physical signing + courier.
What is Secretarial Audit and when is it required?
Secretarial Audit under Section 204 Companies Act - mandatory for every listed company AND every unlisted public company with (a) paid-up capital >= INR 50 crore OR (b) turnover >= INR 250 crore, and also companies with outstanding loans or borrowings from banks or public financial institutions of INR 100 crore or more. Report in Form MR-3 filed with ROC. Verifies compliance with Companies Act, SEBI regulations, FEMA, applicable laws. Done by independent practising CS.
What filings does a private Indian subsidiary of a foreign parent need?
Annual: AOC-4, MGT-7, DPT-3. Every three years: DIR-3 KYC per director (by 30 June). Half-yearly: PAS-6 (if demat required under Rule 9B), MSME Form 1. Event-based: DIR-12 (director changes), PAS-3 (share allotment), MGT-14 (specific resolutions), CHG-1/CHG-4 (charges), FC-GPR / FC-TRS (FEMA FDI reporting). Board meetings minimum 4/year; AGM within 6 months of FY end.
What is ROC compounding, and when does a company need it?
Compounding under section 441 of the Companies Act, 2013 lets a company or an officer in default settle an offence by paying a compounding amount instead of facing prosecution. It applies only to offences punishable with a fine, or with a fine or imprisonment - not to offences carrying imprisonment alone or imprisonment and a fine - and generally not to a repeat of an offence compounded within the previous three years. The Regional Director (or an officer authorised by the Central Government) compounds offences where the maximum fine is up to INR 25 lakh; above that, the NCLT does. The application is made in e-form GNL-1 through the Registrar of Companies, usually after the default has been made good (the late return filed, the register updated). Check the route first: since the 2019 and 2020 amendments many defaults are no longer offences but civil penalties decided by the ROC as adjudicating officer under section 454, which is a different process. What is the time limit for filing CHG-1, and what if the charge was not registered in time?
A company must register a charge it creates - for example when it takes a secured bank loan or a vehicle loan - in Form CHG-1 within 30 days of creating it (Form CHG-9 for debentures); modifications are filed the same way and the charge is closed with CHG-4 once the loan is repaid. For charges created on or after 2 November 2018, the Registrar can allow a late filing up to 60 days from creation with an additional fee, and after that a further 60 days with an ad valorem fee, with lower multiples for small companies and OPCs. Beyond those windows the Registrar cannot accept the filing, and the delay has to be condoned by the Central Government (Regional Director) under section 87. An unregistered charge is not valid against a liquidator or other creditors, so lenders insist on it, and the company and its officers face penalties under section 86. How does a company change its registered office address?
The procedure depends on how far the office moves. Within the same city, town or village: a board resolution and Form INC-22 with proof of the new address and the owner's NOC, filed promptly - section 12(4) sets a short deadline (15 days after the 2017 amendment, though some guides still cite 30). Outside those local limits but within the same Registrar's jurisdiction: a special resolution of the shareholders, MGT-14 for the resolution and INC-22. To another Registrar's area in the same state: the special resolution, confirmation from the Regional Director in Form INC-23, then INC-22 after the confirmation order. To another state: a special resolution altering the memorandum, notice to creditors and a newspaper advertisement, Regional Director approval in INC-23, and filings with both Registrars. Update GST, PAN / TAN, bank, import-export code and letterheads afterwards; a mismatch between the registered office and the address on these registrations is a common cause of notices. WhatsApp +91 78018 87130 with the current and new address and the company's CIN, or book a call.
How do I change the name of a private limited company in India?
Under section 13, first reserve the new name with the ROC through the RUN service, then hold a board meeting and pass a special resolution of the shareholders approving the new name and the altered MOA and AOA. File the resolution in MGT-14 within 30 days and apply in INC-24; once approved, the ROC issues a fresh certificate of incorporation in the new name, and the change takes effect from that certificate. A company that has not filed its due annual returns or financial statements, or has defaulted on repaying deposits or debentures, cannot change its name until the default is cured. For two years after the change, letterheads, invoices and the signboard must show the former name alongside the new one. Then update PAN and TAN, the GST registration (a core-field amendment), bank accounts, IEC, licences, trademarks and contracts. Book a call to plan the change and the follow-on updates.
How does a private limited company increase its authorised capital?
Under section 61, if the articles allow it the shareholders pass an ordinary resolution at a general meeting to increase the authorised capital and alter the capital clause of the memorandum; if the articles are silent, they are first altered by special resolution (filed in MGT-14). Form SH-7 is then filed with the Registrar within 30 days of the resolution, attaching the resolution and the altered memorandum and articles, and the government filing fee and state stamp duty on the increase are paid with the form. Raising authorised capital only creates headroom - actually issuing shares is a separate step: a rights issue or private placement with board and shareholder approvals, PAS-3 within 30 days of allotment, and, where a foreign investor or NRI subscribes on a repatriation basis, FDI pricing at or above fair value and FC-GPR within 30 days of allotment. Plan the authorised capital for the next few funding rounds rather than raising it each time. What is the rights issue procedure for a private limited company?
A rights issue under section 62(1)(a) offers new shares to existing equity shareholders in proportion to their holdings. The board approves the issue, price and offer letter (raising authorised capital with SH-7 first if needed); the offer is sent to every shareholder and normally stays open for 15 to 30 days, with a right to renounce in favour of someone else unless the articles say otherwise. A private company can run a shorter offer period if at least 90% of members agree in writing or electronically. After the offer closes the board allots the shares and files PAS-3 within 30 days. Where a shareholder is foreign or an NRI on a repatriation basis, the price offered to them cannot be lower than the price offered to resident shareholders, and FC-GPR is due within 30 days of allotment. Book a call to run the issue and the filings.
How does a private placement under section 42 work, and what is Form PAS-4?
A private placement offers shares or convertible securities to identified persons, not to existing shareholders in proportion. It needs a special resolution, a valuation report from a registered valuer, and a private placement offer letter in Form PAS-4 addressed by name to each investor, with a record of the offers in PAS-5. Offers can go to at most 200 persons in a financial year (excluding qualified institutional buyers and employees under an ESOP), money must come by banking channels into a separate bank account, and allotment must be made within 60 days of receipt - otherwise the money is refunded within the next 15 days, with interest after that. The return of allotment in PAS-3 is due within 15 days of allotment. Foreign investors also need FEMA pricing and an FC-GPR within 30 days. Book a call before the term sheet is signed so the paperwork matches the round.
What is Form PAS-6 and does my Indian subsidiary need to file it?
PAS-6 is the Reconciliation of Share Capital Audit Report under Rule 9A - half-yearly filing within 60 days of each half-year end (30 September + 31 March). Mandatory for every unlisted public and private company (except small companies and government companies) that has shares in demat form post Rule 9B (private companies other than small companies had to demat their securities by 30 June 2025).
