/ Families planning gifts and investments between spouses, children and HUFs · Clubbing provisions · Updated 2026-11-29

Clubbing of Income in India (2026): Income of Spouse and Minor Child, Gift to Wife, Section 64, the INR 1,500 Per-Child Exemption and HUF Conversions, the gift is tax-free, the income from it may not be.

Clubbing rules make you pay tax on income that legally belongs to someone else: income from money or property you gave your spouse or son's wife without adequate consideration, most income of your minor child, and income from property you converted into HUF property. A gift to a relative is not taxed when received, but the income it earns can still be clubbed with the giver. Gifts to adult children are generally outside clubbing.

/ Whose income is clubbed

Section 64 of the Income-tax Act, 1961.

SituationClubbed with
Asset transferred to spouse without adequate considerationThe person who transferred it - income from the asset
Spouse paid salary or fees by your concern (you hold 20% or more)You, unless the spouse has technical or professional qualifications and the income is solely due to them
Asset transferred to son's wife without adequate considerationThe person who transferred it
Income of a minor childThe parent with the higher total income (or the parent maintaining the child if the marriage has ended)
Own property converted into HUF propertyThe individual who converted it
Gift to an adult son or daughterNot clubbed - taxed in the child's hands
  • Second-generation income: clubbing covers income from the transferred asset, not income earned later by reinvesting that income.
  • Both regimes: clubbing applies under the new and old tax regimes alike.
  • New Act: the same rules continue in the Income-tax Act, 2025 from tax year 2026-27, under new section numbers.

/ Minor children and planning

Exceptions, the exemption and what works.

  • Not clubbed: a minor child's income from manual work or from an activity involving the child's own skill, talent or specialised knowledge, and income of a minor with a disability of the kind covered by section 80U.
  • Exemption: the parent can claim up to INR 1,500 a year per minor child against the clubbed income.
  • Loans instead of gifts: a genuine loan on documented terms is not a transfer without consideration - interest, repayment and records matter.
  • Investing in the spouse’s own name from the spouse’s own income: no clubbing, so keep the money trail clean.
  • NRIs gifting to family in India: clubbing follows the giver’s residential status and the source of income - check before large gifts or property transfers.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is clubbing of income?
Clubbing means including someone else's income in your own taxable income. It applies mainly to income from assets you transferred to your spouse or son's wife without adequate consideration, your minor child's income, and income from property you converted into HUF property.
Is income from a gift to my wife taxable in my hands?
Yes. The gift itself is not taxed in her hands because she is a relative, but the income from the gifted money or asset - interest, rent, dividends or capital gains - is clubbed with your income. Income she earns by reinvesting that income is not clubbed.
Is a minor child's income clubbed with the parents?
Yes, with the parent whose total income is higher, or with the parent who maintains the child if the marriage has ended. Income from the child's own manual work, skill or talent is not clubbed, and nor is the income of a minor with a disability covered by section 80U.
How much of a minor child's income is exempt?
The parent in whose income it is clubbed can claim an exemption of up to INR 1,500 a year for each minor child under section 10(32).
Is income from a gift to an adult son or daughter clubbed?
No. Once the child is an adult, income from the gift is taxed in the child's own hands. The exception is a gift to a son's wife, where the income is clubbed with the giver.
