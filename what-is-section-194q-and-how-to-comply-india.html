What Is Section 194Q and How to Comply India, 0.1% TDS on purchase above INR 50 lakh.

Direct answer: Section 194Q is India Income Tax Act TDS provision effective 1 July 2021 requiring buyer to deduct 0.1% TDS when buying goods exceeding INR 50 lakh in a FY from a single seller. Applies to buyers with turnover > INR 10 crore in preceding FY. Interacts with Section 206C(1H) seller-side TCS (buyer's TDS prevails). Compliance via Form 26Q quarterly + Form 16A to seller.

/ Section 194Q at a glance

The core provisions.

  • Who deducts: buyer of goods with turnover / gross receipts / sales > INR 10 crore in immediately preceding FY.
  • What triggers: purchase of goods from any seller exceeding INR 50 lakh in aggregate during FY.
  • Rate: 0.1% on consideration exceeding INR 50 lakh (not on the entire amount - only on excess).
  • Section 206AA override: if seller lacks Indian PAN, higher of 0.1% / 5% applies - typically 5%.
  • Deduction timing: credit of amount in books OR payment, whichever earlier.
  • Interaction with Section 206C(1H) seller TCS: if both 194Q (buyer) + 206C(1H) (seller) apply, buyer's 194Q TDS prevails (no 206C(1H) TCS on same transaction).

/ Threshold applicability analysis

Who falls under 194Q.

  • Buyer's turnover > INR 10 crore in preceding FY: mandatory applicability.
  • Specific aggregation across buyer group: 194Q applies entity-wise, not group-wise (each legal entity assessed separately).
  • Buyer type: company / LLP / partnership / HUF / individual / specific entity.
  • Specific sector carve-outs: specific exempt purchases + specific government purchases.
  • Trading goods vs capital goods: 194Q applies to purchases - specific analysis for leased / specific scenarios.
  • Import of goods: 194Q applies to purchases from Indian sellers; imports have specific TCS under 206C(1H) seller-side + specific customs duty.

/ Compliance workflow for buyer

Step-by-step.

  1. FY-start: identify sellers from whom cumulative purchases likely to exceed INR 50 lakh. Historical data + budget / forecast.
  2. Monitor cumulative purchases: track per seller + per FY running total.
  3. Trigger: when cumulative purchase crosses INR 50 lakh in FY: deduct 0.1% on amount in excess of INR 50 lakh (or 5% if seller lacks PAN).
  4. Pay TDS: by 7th of following month (June: 30th).
  5. Quarterly return: Form 26Q filed within 30 days of quarter-end (July 31 / October 31 / January 31 / May 31 for Q4).
  6. Form 16A to seller: TDS certificate within 15 days of quarterly return filing.
  7. Section 206AA / 206AB: if seller inactive filer or lacks PAN: higher rate applicability check.
  8. Reconciliation: Form 26AS / AIS reconciliation + specific credit flow to seller.

/ Interaction with Section 206C(1H) seller TCS

The parallel regime.

  • Section 206C(1H) effective 1 October 2020: seller collects 0.1% TCS on sale of goods above INR 50 lakh in FY from single buyer. Seller turnover > INR 10 crore in preceding FY.
  • Section 194Q effective 1 July 2021: parallel buyer-side TDS.
  • Overlap: both buyer (194Q) + seller (206C(1H)) qualify + transaction crosses INR 50 lakh.
  • Resolution: proviso to Section 206C(1H) - if buyer deducts 194Q TDS, seller is NOT required to collect 206C(1H) TCS on same transaction. 194Q TDS prevails.
  • Documentation: buyer confirms 194Q TDS deduction to seller + specific declaration.
  • Specific administrative framework + CBDT Circulars for operationalisation.

/ Common compliance errors + risks

What to avoid.

  • Not identifying 194Q applicability + missing TDS deduction.
  • Wrong rate: deducting on entire amount vs only excess of INR 50 lakh.
  • Not applying Section 206AA 5% for sellers lacking PAN.
  • Not applying Section 206AB higher rate for seller as 'specified person' (non-filer).
  • Missing Form 26Q quarterly filing.
  • Mismatched Form 16A causing seller's Form 26AS reconciliation issues.
  • Double TDS + TCS on same transaction (should not happen if 194Q + 206C(1H) interaction correctly managed).
  • Section 201 default: TDS not deducted / short deducted - buyer treated as 'assessee in default' + interest + penalty.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is Section 194Q?
Section 194Q is Indian Income Tax Act TDS provision (effective 1 July 2021): buyer with turnover > INR 10 crore in preceding FY must deduct 0.1% TDS when cumulative purchases from a single seller exceed INR 50 lakh in a FY. Deducted on amount exceeding INR 50 lakh (not entire). Section 206AA 5% if seller lacks Indian PAN.
How does 194Q interact with 206C(1H)?
If both apply (buyer qualifies for 194Q + seller qualifies for 206C(1H) + transaction crosses INR 50 lakh): buyer's 194Q TDS prevails. Seller NOT required to collect 206C(1H) TCS on same transaction (proviso to Section 206C(1H)). Buyer confirms 194Q deduction to seller + specific declaration maintained.
When must 194Q TDS be deducted?
At credit of amount in buyer's books OR payment to seller, whichever is EARLIER. Pay TDS by 7th of following month (except June: 30th). Quarterly return Form 26Q within 30 days of quarter-end. Form 16A to seller within 15 days of Form 26Q filing.
Does 194Q apply to imports?
194Q applies to purchases FROM Indian sellers. For imports from foreign sellers: 194Q NOT applicable (different jurisdictions). Specific customs duty + specific IGST on import + specific Section 206C(1H) seller TCS by foreign seller doesn't apply either. Imports have specific separate framework.
