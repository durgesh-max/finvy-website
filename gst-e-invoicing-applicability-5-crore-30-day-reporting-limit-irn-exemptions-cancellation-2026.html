/ Manufacturers, traders, service providers and exporters · GST e-invoicing · Updated 2026-11-28

GST E-Invoicing (2026): Who Must Issue E-Invoices (INR 5 Crore Turnover), Which Documents, Exemptions, the 30-Day Reporting Limit, IRN Cancellation and Penalties, once you cross the line, every B2B invoice needs an IRN.

If your aggregate turnover crossed INR 5 crore in any financial year since 2017-18, your B2B invoices, credit and debit notes, exports and SEZ supplies must be reported to the Invoice Registration Portal to get an IRN and QR code. An invoice without an IRN is not a valid invoice, and the buyer’s input tax credit is at risk. If your AATO is INR 10 crore or more, invoices must be reported within 30 days of their date - late ones are rejected by the portal.

/ Who and what is covered

Applicability, documents and exemptions.

PointRule
ThresholdAggregate turnover above INR 5 crore in any financial year from 2017-18 (from 1 August 2023)
DocumentsTax invoices, credit notes and debit notes
Supplies coveredB2B, exports, supplies to SEZ units or developers, deemed exports
Not coveredB2C invoices (separate dynamic QR code rules apply to the largest businesses)
Exempt classesSEZ units, insurers, banks, NBFCs and financial institutions, goods transport agencies, passenger transport, multiplex admission, government departments and local authorities
30-day limitAATO INR 10 crore and above: report within 30 days of invoice date (from 1 April 2025; INR 100 crore and above since 1 November 2023)
CancellationIRN can be cancelled within 24 hours on the IRP; no partial cancellation
  • Once in, always in: the test looks at every year since 2017-18, so a single year above the threshold brings you in even if turnover later falls.
  • Auto-flow: e-invoice data populates GSTR-1 and Part A of the e-way bill, which reduces mismatches when set up correctly.

/ Getting compliant, and fixing gaps

Setup, late invoices and notices.

  • Check applicability now: compute aggregate turnover (all GSTINs on the same PAN, India-wide) for each year since 2017-18.
  • Set up: generate IRNs through your billing or ERP software, a GST Suvidha Provider, or directly on the IRP, and train staff on the 30-day and 24-hour limits.
  • Invoices issued without IRN: where e-invoicing applied, the invoice is treated as not issued - correct the position with the buyer quickly to protect their credit.
  • Rejected for being late: plan billing so invoices reach the IRP within 30 days; get advice on how to regularise a missed invoice.
  • Penalties and notices: invoices not issued as required attract penalty under section 122; e-invoice gaps also show up in GSTR-1, e-way bill and GSTR-2B mismatches.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Who has to issue e-invoices under GST?
Registered persons whose aggregate turnover exceeded INR 5 crore in any financial year from 2017-18, unless they fall in an exempt class such as SEZ units, insurers, banks and financial institutions, goods transport agencies, passenger transport and government departments.
Which documents need an e-invoice?
Tax invoices, credit notes and debit notes for B2B supplies, exports, supplies to SEZs and deemed exports. Invoices to unregistered consumers (B2C) are not reported for IRN.
What is the 30-day rule for e-invoices?
Businesses with annual aggregate turnover of INR 10 crore or more must report an invoice to the IRP within 30 days of its date, from 1 April 2025. Later invoices are rejected by the portal. The limit applied to INR 100 crore and above from 1 November 2023.
What happens if an e-invoice is not generated?
Where e-invoicing applies, an invoice without an IRN is not a valid invoice, so the buyer's input tax credit is at risk and penalty under section 122 can apply. Correct it with the buyer as soon as possible.
Can an e-invoice be cancelled?
Yes, the IRN can be cancelled on the IRP within 24 hours of generation, but only in full. After that, the correction is made through a credit note or an amendment in GSTR-1.
