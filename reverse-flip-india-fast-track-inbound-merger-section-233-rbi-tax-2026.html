Reverse Flip to India by Fast-Track Merger: Section 233, RBI Approval and Tax (2026), the Regional Director, not the NCLT.

Since 17 September 2024, a foreign holding company can merge into its wholly owned Indian subsidiary through the fast-track merger route under section 233 of the Companies Act, 2013, approved by the Regional Director instead of the NCLT, with prior RBI approval. That makes an inbound merger a practical way to bring a Delaware, Singapore or UK parent back to India, alongside the share-swap route. The legal steps are now shorter; the decisive question is usually tax, for the Indian entities and for the foreign shareholders in their home countries.

/ Fast-track merger steps

In outline.

  1. Confirm eligibility: the foreign company is the holding company and the Indian company its wholly owned subsidiary; check the latest form of the rules (amended again in September 2025).
  2. RBI approval: obtain prior approval and meet the FEMA (Cross Border Merger) Regulations, 2018 for the assets, liabilities and shares that move.
  3. Scheme and approvals: board and shareholder approvals, creditor approvals and notices as section 233 requires, then the Regional Director's confirmation.
  4. Shares and filings: the Indian company issues shares to the foreign parent's shareholders, with FDI reporting for non-resident shareholders and ODI or other FEMA reporting where residents are involved.
  5. Close out abroad: deal with the foreign entity's dissolution, contracts, IP and bank accounts in its home jurisdiction.

/ Tax points

Where the cost sits.

  • Indian tax neutrality: an amalgamation into an Indian company can be exempt for the amalgamating company and its shareholders only if the statutory definition of amalgamation is met (including the shareholder-continuity condition).
  • Foreign shareholders: their home country decides whether receiving Indian shares is taxable; US holders, for example, need a reorganisation analysis.
  • ESOPs and SAFEs: map how options, warrants and convertibles in the foreign parent convert into the Indian company.
  • Losses and stamp duty: check the carry-forward of losses, state stamp duty on the transfer and GST on any assets or services moving.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Can a Delaware company merge into its Indian subsidiary?
Yes. Since 17 September 2024, a foreign holding company can merge into its wholly owned Indian subsidiary through the section 233 fast-track route, with prior RBI approval and Regional Director confirmation instead of the NCLT.
Is the fast-track reverse flip tax-free?
On the Indian side it can be tax-neutral if the statutory definition of amalgamation is met. Foreign shareholders' home-country tax is separate and often the bigger question.
Do we need RBI approval for an inbound merger?
Yes. The rules require prior RBI approval, and the merger must meet the FEMA (Cross Border Merger) Regulations, 2018.
Merger or share swap: which is better?
It depends on the shareholder base and tax. A merger avoids keeping a foreign parent; a share swap can be simpler where the merger conditions are not met. Model both.
How long does the fast track take?
Reported timelines are a matter of months, shorter than the NCLT route, but RBI approval and foreign-law steps can extend it.
