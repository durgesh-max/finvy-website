/ Why 15CA + 15CB exist

Section 195 + Rule 37BB.

Section 195 Income Tax Act: any person making payment to a non-resident that is chargeable to tax in India must deduct TDS. Rule 37BB (introduced 2013, revised repeatedly) operationalises this via Form 15CA (payer declaration) and Form 15CB (CA certificate on taxability and rate).

The AD bank processing the SWIFT asks for 15CA acknowledgement AND (where applicable) 15CB before releasing foreign exchange. No 15CA/15CB → no wire.

The four Parts of Form 15CA:

  • Part A: for remittances up to INR 5 lakh in a FY, aggregate per payee. No 15CB needed; only self-declaration.
  • Part B: for remittances above INR 5 lakh where an order under Section 195(2)/(3) or 197 has been obtained (NIL / lower deduction certificate).
  • Part C: for remittances above INR 5 lakh where Form 15CB (CA certificate) is attached. This is the standard case.
  • Part D: for remittances not chargeable to tax under the Act (list of 33 specified categories).

/ When Form 15CB is mandatory

The core use case.

Form 15CB (CA certificate) is required when:

  • Remittance is > INR 5 lakh aggregate per payee per FY, AND
  • The remittance is chargeable to tax in India, AND
  • No Section 195(2)/(3) / 197 order has been obtained.

Standard scenarios needing 15CB:

  • Dividend from Indian subsidiary to foreign parent — Section 195 TDS 20% (10% under treaty with TRC).
  • Royalty / FTS (Fees for Technical Services) to foreign licensor — Section 115A 20% (10% under treaty).
  • Interest on ECB / NCD to foreign lender — Section 195 20% (treaty rate may apply).
  • Capital gains on Indian securities to foreign investor — Section 112A / 115AD rates.
  • Branch office remittance to head office.
  • Service fee to foreign vendor — nature-dependent (FTS vs business income under treaty).
  • Salary to foreign consultant / independent contractor — Section 192 vs Section 194R / 195 depending on employment vs independent.
  • Reimbursement of expenses — chargeability depends on facts.

/ Form 15CB mechanics

What the CA certifies.

Form 15CB is a CA certificate stating:

  • Nature of remittance (dividend / royalty / FTS / interest / capital gain / business income / salary / reimbursement, etc.).
  • Chargeability under the Act (Section 9, Section 115A, Section 195, specific sections).
  • Applicable DTAA, if any, and treaty rate.
  • TDS deducted at applicable rate.
  • Reasons supporting the taxability / non-taxability conclusion.

Key documents needed:

  • Invoice / agreement supporting the remittance.
  • Payee's Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) — mandatory for claiming DTAA benefit.
  • Payee's Form 10F — electronic Form 10F now mandatory via e-filing portal (post Oct 2023). Even if TRC provides most info, Form 10F additional.
  • Payee's PAN (if available) — without PAN, Section 206AA kicks in: rate = higher of (a) treaty rate, (b) 20%.
  • No-PE declaration from payee (if claiming business income exemption under treaty).

/ Common 15CA / 15CB mistakes

What foreign payees should push back on.

  • Payer withholding at 20% ignoring treaty: when TRC + Form 10F are available, treaty rate (usually 10-15%) applies. Over-withholding delays payee's refund / clog in Indian tax system.
  • Payer claiming 'reimbursement' without substance: reimbursement must be bare cost, no mark-up, documented back-to-back. Else chargeable as business income / FTS.
  • Missing Form 10F: post Oct 2023, electronic Form 10F mandatory. Even non-residents without Indian PAN must obtain India e-filing PAN temporarily to file Form 10F. CBDT clarifications ongoing.
  • Service classification errors: payer calls it 'consultancy' (FTS 10% treaty), CA re-classifies as 'business income' (nil if no PE). Taxability can shift by INR 10 lakh+ on INR 1 crore remittance.
  • PE / fixed-place analysis skipped: foreign vendor rendering services remotely but has frequent India presence (frequent travel, demo deployment, long on-site engagement) → service PE / fixed-place PE → business income taxable.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

When is Form 15CB mandatory?
When remittance exceeds INR 5 lakh aggregate per payee per FY AND the remittance is chargeable to tax in India AND no Section 195(2)/(3)/197 order has been obtained. For smaller amounts (up to INR 5 lakh in FY), only Form 15CA Part A (self-declaration) is required; no CA certificate.
What is Form 10F and is it still required?
Yes - Form 10F is a declaration by the non-resident payee confirming details (name, country of residence, PAN or substitute, treaty eligibility, no PE). Post October 2023, electronic Form 10F is mandatory via Indian e-filing portal. Even non-residents without Indian PAN must obtain temporary India e-filing registration to file Form 10F. Required alongside TRC for DTAA benefit.
What is TRC and why is it needed?
Tax Residency Certificate - issued by the tax authority of the payee's country of residence confirming the payee is a tax resident there. Mandatory for claiming DTAA benefit (treaty rate) under Section 90(4). Without TRC, no treaty rate - standard Section 195 rate (20%) applies.
What's the TDS rate if payee doesn't have PAN?
Section 206AA kicks in - rate is higher of: (a) rate under the Act / applicable Section 195, (b) rate under treaty (if TRC + Form 10F available), (c) 20%. Effectively 20% for most remittances. Payee should obtain PAN or at minimum file Form 10F even temporarily.
