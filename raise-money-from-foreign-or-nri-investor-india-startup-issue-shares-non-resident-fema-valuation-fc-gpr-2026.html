Raising Money From a Foreign or NRI Investor (2026): How an Indian Startup Issues Shares to a Non-Resident - FDI Route, Valuation, 60-Day Allotment and FC-GPR, the order matters; most problems come from doing it out of order.

An Indian startup can take money from a foreign investor or an NRI by issuing shares, compulsorily convertible preference shares or convertible notes, in most sectors without government approval. The order is fixed: check the sector and the investor’s country, get a valuation (the price cannot be below fair value), receive the money into the company’s bank account, allot within 60 days of receiving it, and report to the RBI in Form FC-GPR within 30 days of allotment. Investors linked to China or another country sharing India’s land border need approval unless their land-border ownership is within 10% and non-controlling. An NRI or OCI investing on a non-repatriation basis is treated like a domestic investor, with far less paperwork. The angel tax on premium above fair value has been abolished, so a high price is no longer an income-tax problem.

/ Instruments and traps

What founders get wrong.

  • Instruments: equity shares and compulsorily convertible preference shares or debentures count as foreign direct investment when the conversion price or formula is fixed upfront. Optionally convertible instruments are debt and fall under borrowing rules. See CCPS and CCDs and SAFE vs convertible note.
  • Money before paperwork: money that arrives before approvals are in place, or sits more than 60 days without allotment, creates a FEMA breach that needs regularising.
  • Late FC-GPR: costs a late submission fee and can hold up the next round’s due diligence; see the late fee calculator.
  • Downstream investment: once foreign-owned and controlled, the startup’s own investments in other Indian companies follow FDI rules; see downstream investment.
  • US parent instead? Some founders raise through a US holding company; that changes tax and FEMA for everyone. See US parent with an Indian subsidiary.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Can an Indian startup take investment from a foreign investor?
Yes. In most sectors a foreign investor can subscribe to equity shares or compulsorily convertible instruments under the automatic route, without government approval, subject to the sector cap and the pricing and reporting rules.
What is the minimum price for issuing shares to a foreign investor?
The issue price cannot be below the fair value set by a chartered accountant, SEBI-registered merchant banker or practising cost accountant using an internationally accepted method. The report is usually expected to be no older than 90 days.
How long does a company have to allot shares after receiving foreign investment?
60 days from receiving the money. If shares are not allotted by then, the money must be refunded within 15 days.
When is Form FC-GPR due?
Within 30 days of allotting the shares, filed on the RBI's portal through the company's bank.
Does an NRI investment need FC-GPR?
Only if it is on a repatriation basis. An NRI or OCI investing on a non-repatriation basis is treated as a domestic investor.
Can a startup take money from an investor linked to China?
Direct investment from China or Hong Kong needs government approval. Under Press Note 2 of 2026, an investor incorporated elsewhere whose land-border beneficial ownership is within 10% and non-controlling can use the automatic route.
Is angel tax still a problem when raising from foreign investors?
No. The income-tax charge on share premium above fair value was abolished from FY 2024-25. The FEMA pricing floor still applies.
