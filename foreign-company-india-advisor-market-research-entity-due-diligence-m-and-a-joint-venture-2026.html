Working with an Indian CA as a Foreign Company (2026): Market Research, Entity Set-Up, Due Diligence, M&A, Joint Ventures and Partnerships, what foreign businesses ask an Indian professional for, and how the first call works.

Foreign companies and investors usually come to an Indian chartered accountant with one of eight questions. Is there a market for us in India? Which entity or place of business should we set up? Is this Indian company, supplier or partner genuine? What will we find in due diligence before we buy or invest? How do we acquire an Indian company, or sell one? How should a joint venture be structured? How do we appoint a distributor or licensee, or set up a capability centre? And what will tax, FEMA and the filings cost us each year? Each answer depends on your sector, your home country's treaty with India and who your Indian counterparty is. Buying shares from an NRI or foreign company? Deduct tax first The buyer must withhold Indian tax on the seller’s gain: 12.5% on unlisted shares held over 24 months, up to 30% or 35% if held for less, plus surcharge and cess. A CA computation, a Form 128 certificate or a treaty can reduce it: TDS on buying shares from a non-resident.
Selling a foreign holding company with Indian value underneath? India can tax the sale of foreign company shares if Indian assets exceed INR 10 crore and are at least 50% of its assets.
