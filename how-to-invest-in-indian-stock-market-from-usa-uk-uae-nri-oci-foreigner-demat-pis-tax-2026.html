How to Invest in the Indian Stock Market From the USA, UK, UAE or Anywhere Abroad (2026): NRIs, OCIs and Foreign Nationals - Accounts, KYC, Limits and Tax, the accounts, the paperwork, and the tax on both sides.

You can buy listed Indian shares from abroad through an Indian broker, a demat account and an Indian bank account that the rules allow for this. NRIs and OCI cardholders use an NRE account with the bank’s portfolio investment route if they want to send the money back abroad freely, or an NRO account if they do not. Since 2026, any foreign individual can also buy listed Indian shares on a repatriable basis. Each person can hold less than 10% of a company. From 3 December 2026, KYC can be completed by video from most countries, without visiting India. India taxes the gains at 12.5% (held over 12 months, above INR 1.25 lakh a year) or 20% (shorter), and the broker deducts tax at sale. Your home country may also tax them. Indian mutual funds are the trap: US investors face PFIC rules, and UK investors usually pay income tax on the gains.

/ Step by step

From no account to your first trade.

  • 1. PAN: an Indian PAN is needed. NRIs, OCIs and foreigners can apply from abroad; see PAN from abroad.
  • 2. Bank account: NRE with portfolio investment permission (repatriable) or NRO (non-repatriable). Foreign nationals open the designated rupee account.
  • 3. Demat and trading account: with a broker that serves non-residents. From 3 December 2026, KYC can be done by video from FATF-compliant countries. See KYC from abroad.
  • 4. Fund and trade: send money through banking channels; buy on the exchange through the linked bank route. NRIs are not allowed to day-trade or short-sell in the cash segment.
  • 5. Tax at sale: the broker deducts TDS on the gain. File an Indian return to recover any excess and to carry forward losses.
  • 6. Report at home: declare the account and gains where you live, and claim credit for the Indian tax where a treaty allows it.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Can an NRI invest in the Indian stock market?
Yes. An NRI invests in listed Indian shares through a demat and trading account linked to an NRE account with the bank's portfolio investment route (repatriable) or an NRO account (non-repatriable).
Can a US citizen or foreigner buy Indian shares?
Yes. OCI cardholders invest like NRIs, and since 2026 any individual resident outside India can buy listed Indian shares on a repatriable basis through a designated rupee account, below 10% of a company each. Citizens of countries sharing a land border with India face approval rules.
Can I open an Indian demat account from abroad?
Yes. From 3 December 2026, NRIs, OCI cardholders and foreign nationals in FATF-compliant countries can complete KYC digitally by video without visiting India.
How are Indian shares taxed for someone living abroad?
India taxes long-term gains on listed shares at 12.5% above INR 1.25 lakh a year and short-term gains at 20%, plus surcharge and cess, with tax deducted by the broker. The country where you live may tax the gain too, usually with credit for the Indian tax.
Should a US resident buy Indian mutual funds?
Usually not without advice. Indian mutual funds are generally PFICs for US taxpayers, which can mean heavy tax and reporting, and many Indian fund houses do not accept US residents. Direct shares avoid PFIC.
Can foreigners invest in Indian mutual funds?
NRIs and OCI cardholders can, after KYC, paying from an NRE or NRO account; some fund houses do not accept investors living in the US or Canada. The 2026 opening for other foreign individuals covers listed shares through a repatriable rupee account; for mutual fund units, foreign nationals who are not NRIs or OCIs generally use the foreign portfolio investor route, which needs registration through a custodian. Check with the fund house and your bank before investing, and check how your home country taxes foreign funds.
Can an OCI cardholder start a SIP in Indian mutual funds?
Yes. OCI cardholders invest like NRIs: complete KYC (video KYC from most countries from 3 December 2026), link an NRE or NRO account, and choose a fund house that accepts residents of your country.
Can I do a SIP in India from the UK?
An NRI in the UK can, if the fund house accepts UK residents, but most Indian funds are non-reporting offshore funds for UK tax, so gains are taxed as income at your marginal rate. Check HMRC's reporting fund list first.
