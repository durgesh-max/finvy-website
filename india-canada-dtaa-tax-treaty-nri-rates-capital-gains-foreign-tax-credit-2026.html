India-Canada Tax Treaty (DTAA) for NRIs in Canada (2026): Rates on Interest, Dividends, Rent and Capital Gains, and How to Claim, what India deducts and what Canada credits.

Under the India-Canada tax treaty, India can tax Indian income of a Canadian resident, but the treaty caps the rate on some income: 15% (company holding at least 10%) / 25% (others) on dividends and 15% on interest, with royalties at 10% to 15%, depending on the type. The treaty rate applies only when it is lower than the Indian rate, and only if you give the payer a tax residency certificate from Canada and Form 10F (now Form 41). Rent from Indian property and capital gains on Indian property and shares remain taxable in India; Canada then taxes the same income as part of your worldwide income and gives a foreign tax credit for the Indian tax.

/ Treaty rates vs Indian rates

Use the lower one.

The Indian rate applies if it is lower than the treaty cap. Interest on NRE accounts and FCNR deposits is tax-free in India for NRIs, so the treaty matters mainly for NRO interest, dividends, rent and gains.

/ Capital gains and property

India keeps the right to tax.

The India-Canada treaty lets each country tax capital gains under its own domestic law, so India taxes gains on Indian property and Indian shares, and Canada taxes the same gain as a Canadian resident's worldwide income with credit for the Indian tax.

  • Rent from Indian property is taxed in India (tenant deducts about 31.2% TDS); see NRI rental income.
  • Selling Indian property: long-term gains at 12.5% for NRIs, with the buyer deducting TDS unless you obtain a lower deduction certificate; see the NRI property TDS calculator.

/ How to claim and avoid paying twice

Step by step.

  • Get a tax residency certificate from the Canada tax authority for the relevant year.
  • Fill Form 10F (Form 41 under the Income-tax Rules, 2026) on the Indian e-filing portal; you need a PAN.
  • Give both to your Indian bank, tenant, company or buyer before payment, or claim the lower rate and refund in your Indian return.
  • Report the income in Canada and claim the foreign tax credit for Indian tax actually paid, within the treaty cap.
  • Canada's foreign tax credit is claimed on the T1 return; Indian property and accounts may also need form T1135 if the cost of specified foreign property exceeds CAD 100,000.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is the India-Canada DTAA rate on dividends?
15% (company holding at least 10%) / 25% (others) under the treaty. Indian domestic tax on dividends to non-residents is 20% plus surcharge and cess, so the lower of the two applies.
What TDS applies on NRO interest for an NRI in Canada?
The bank deducts 30% plus surcharge and cess by default. With a tax residency certificate, Form 10F (Form 41) and PAN, the treaty rate of 15% applies.
Is NRE interest taxable for an NRI living in Canada?
NRE and FCNR interest is exempt in India for NRIs, but Canada taxes its residents on worldwide income, so it is usually reportable there.
How do I avoid double tax on Indian income in Canada?
Pay Indian tax at the lower of the treaty and Indian rate, then claim a foreign tax credit in Canada for the Indian tax.
Does India tax capital gains of a Canadian resident?
Yes. Under the India-Canada treaty each country may tax capital gains under its domestic law, so gains on Indian property and shares are taxed in India, and Canada gives a foreign tax credit.
For an individual shareholder, which dividend rate applies?
The treaty cap for non-corporate shareholders is 25%, which is higher than the Indian 20% rate, so the Indian rate (plus surcharge and cess) applies.
