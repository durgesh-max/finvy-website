GST Registration for a Foreign Company in India (2026): Non-Resident Taxable Person (REG-09) for Exhibitions, Events and Short Projects, OIDAR, Reverse Charge, Advance Deposit, GSTR-5, for foreign businesses supplying in India without an Indian office.

A foreign company or individual that occasionally supplies goods or services in India without a fixed place of business, for example selling at a trade exhibition, running an event or delivering a short project, must register under GST as a non-resident taxable person (NRTP). The application goes in Form GST REG-09 at least five days before business starts. It needs an authorised signatory resident in India with a PAN and an advance deposit of the estimated GST for the period. Registration lasts up to 90 days and can be extended once for another 90. Returns are filed in GSTR-5 by the 13th of the following month, or within seven days after registration ends. Input tax credit is generally not available on purchases in India, except IGST on goods you import. Any unused deposit is refunded after the final return. For a continuing business in India, a subsidiary or branch is usually more efficient than repeated NRTP registrations.

/ Steps and timeline

From application to refund.

  • Documents: passport or incorporation documents, tax identification from your home country, authorised signatory's PAN and Aadhaar or other KYC, details of the supply and place in India.
  • Apply: Form GST REG-09 at least five days before starting; pay the advance deposit of estimated tax; supplies begin only after the certificate is issued.
  • Invoice and collect GST: charge IGST or CGST and SGST as applicable on Indian sales.
  • File GSTR-5: by the 13th of the following month, or within seven days after registration expires; late fees and interest apply for delays.
  • Extend if needed: once, for up to 90 days, in Form REG-11 with an additional deposit.
  • Close and refund: after the final return, claim the balance of the advance deposit.
  • Income tax: sales at an event can create Indian tax exposure; check your treaty and permanent establishment position; see the PE risk checker.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Who is a non-resident taxable person under GST?
A person outside India who occasionally supplies goods or services in India, as principal or agent, without a fixed place of business or residence in India, such as a foreign company selling at a trade show.
Does a foreign company need GST registration in India?
It depends on what it supplies and to whom. A foreign company that occasionally makes taxable supplies in India without a fixed place of business - for example selling at an exhibition or carrying out a short project - must register as a non-resident taxable person (REG-09, with an advance deposit). A foreign provider of online services (OIDAR) supplying Indian consumers or other non-business recipients registers under the simplified scheme in REG-10 and pays IGST - see our OIDAR guide. Services supplied to GST-registered Indian businesses are usually taxed under reverse charge in the hands of the Indian recipient, so the foreign supplier does not register for them, and goods shipped to India bear IGST at customs paid by the importer.

How does a foreign company register for GST as an NRTP?
By filing Form GST REG-09 at least five days before starting business, with an Indian-resident authorised signatory holding a PAN and an advance deposit of the estimated tax.
How long is NRTP registration valid?
For the period applied for, up to 90 days, extendable once for up to another 90 days with an additional deposit.
What return does an NRTP file?
GSTR-5, by the 13th of the following month or within seven days after the registration expires, whichever is earlier.
Can an NRTP claim input tax credit?
Generally not on purchases in India; credit is available on IGST paid on goods the NRTP imports.
