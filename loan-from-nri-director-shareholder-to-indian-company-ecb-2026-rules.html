Loan from an NRI Director or Foreign Shareholder to an Indian Company (2026): ECB Rules, Director Loan Exemption, TDS on Interest and Filings, for NRI founders in the Gulf, US, UK and Singapore funding their Indian company.

Yes, an NRI director or a foreign shareholder can lend money to an Indian private company. Under the revised External Commercial Borrowing (ECB) framework in force from 16 February 2026, any person resident outside India, including an individual NRI, can be a recognised lender. There is no longer any need to hold a minimum equity stake. The loan must have a minimum average maturity of three years and be priced in line with the market (at arm's length for a related party). It can be in foreign currency or rupees and can be repaid abroad. Before drawdown the company obtains a Loan Registration Number (LRN) by filing Form ECB 1 through its bank, then files Form ECB 2 within 7 days after each month with a drawdown or repayment. Under company law, a loan from a director (in a private company, also a director's relative) who declares in writing that the money is not borrowed is not a deposit; it is disclosed in the annual DPT-3. The company deducts TDS on interest paid to the NRI, at the treaty rate where a tax residency certificate is provided.

/ The 2026 ECB rules in practice

What changed on 16 February 2026.

  • Who can lend: recognised lenders now include persons resident outside India, which can include individuals, so an NRI director in Dubai, London or Singapore can lend directly. The earlier minimum shareholding test for foreign equity holders no longer applies.
  • How much: the higher of USD 1 billion of outstanding ECB, or total outstanding borrowing (foreign and domestic) of up to 300% of net worth on the last audited balance sheet.
  • Maturity: three years minimum average maturity (manufacturing companies can borrow for one to three years up to USD 150 million outstanding).
  • Cost: no fixed all-in-cost ceiling for loans of three years or more; the rate must be in line with market conditions, and at arm's length where the lender is a related party. Transfer pricing applies if the lender is an associated enterprise.
  • End-use: working capital and general corporate purposes are generally allowed; the negative list includes real estate business (with exceptions such as construction-development and own-use premises), chit funds and Nidhi companies, agriculture and plantations, trading in transferable development rights, investing in securities other than strategic corporate actions, and on-lending for these.
  • Reporting: monthly ECB 2 returns were replaced by event-based returns within 7 days of the end of the month in which a drawdown or repayment happens. From 1 April 2026 each late ECB 2 under an LRN counts as a separate instance for the late submission fee.
  • Non-repatriation rupee loans from NRIs under the 2026 regulations are framed for resident individual borrowers, with repayment only to the NRO account. For a company, use ECB, a properly documented director loan that also meets FEMA, or non-repatriable equity; confirm the route with your bank before the money moves.

/ Tax and company law

Interest, TDS and deposits.

  • TDS on interest: interest paid to a non-resident is subject to withholding under section 393(2) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (section 195 of the 1961 Act). Interest on a foreign-currency loan is taxed at 20% under the domestic rule (plus surcharge and cess); a tax treaty can lower this, for example 12.5% under the India-UAE treaty and 15% under the US, UK and Singapore treaties for non-bank lenders, with a tax residency certificate and Form 10F (Form 41 under the new rules). Remittances need Form 15CA/15CB (Forms 145/146).
  • Interest deduction: where interest to a non-resident associated enterprise exceeds INR 1 crore, the thin-capitalisation cap (30% of EBITDA) can restrict the deduction.
  • Deposit rules: under rule 2(1)(c)(viii) of the Companies (Acceptance of Deposits) Rules, 2014, money from a director who gives a written declaration that it is not borrowed is not a deposit. A plain loan from a shareholder who is not a director or relative needs to fit the member-deposit route or be structured differently. All exempt receipts are reported in Form DPT-3.
  • Already received the money without an LRN? Stop further drawdowns, obtain the LRN, and regularise the delay through the bank (late submission fee) or compounding; see FEMA compounding.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Can an NRI director give a loan to an Indian private company?
Yes. Since 16 February 2026 any person resident outside India, including an individual NRI, can lend as a recognised ECB lender. The company needs a Loan Registration Number through its bank before drawdown, a three-year minimum average maturity and a market-level interest rate.
Is a loan from a director a deposit under the Companies Act?
No, if the director gives a written declaration that the money is their own and not borrowed (rule 2(1)(c)(viii) of the Deposit Rules). In a private company, loans from a director's relatives are also covered. The amount is still reported in DPT-3.
Does the company need RBI approval for a loan from a foreign shareholder?
Not usually. ECB within the limits and conditions is under the automatic route: the company files Form ECB 1 with its authorised dealer bank to get an LRN, then Form ECB 2 for each month with a drawdown or repayment.
Can an NRI lend from an NRO account without ECB?
The 2026 non-repatriation NRI loan route is framed for resident individual borrowers. For a company, use ECB, a director loan that also meets FEMA, or non-repatriable equity, and confirm with the bank first.
What TDS applies on interest paid to an NRI lender?
Withholding under section 393(2) of the Income-tax Act, 2025: 20% plus surcharge and cess on interest on a foreign-currency loan under domestic law, or the lower treaty rate (for example 12.5% for UAE, 15% for US, UK and Singapore non-bank lenders) with a TRC and Form 10F.
