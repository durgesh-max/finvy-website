Indirect Transfer Tax in India (2026): When Selling a Foreign Holding Company Is Taxed in India - the 50% and INR 10 Crore Tests, Exemptions and What Buyers Must Withhold, offshore share sales with Indian value underneath.

India can tax the sale of shares in a foreign company if those shares get most of their value from assets in India. The rule applies when the Indian assets are worth more than INR 10 crore and make up at least 50% of the foreign company’s total assets. Only the part of the gain that relates to the Indian assets is taxed, at India’s capital gains rates: 12.5% for holdings over 24 months, plus surcharge and cess. Small shareholders are outside the rule: those with no more than 5% and no management or control in the previous 12 months. The buyer, even a foreign one, must withhold the Indian tax, and the Indian company underneath has its own reporting duty. A tax treaty can remove the charge in some cases. This is the rule that followed the Vodafone case; it applies to transfers from 28 May 2012.

/ The tests

Is an offshore sale taxable in India?

StepTestResult
1Is the company being sold outside India, and does it own Indian assets directly or through other companies?If not, the rule does not apply
2Are the Indian assets worth more than INR 10 crore at fair market value?If not, outside the rule
3Are the Indian assets at least 50% of the value of all its assets?If not, outside the rule
4Does the seller, with its associated enterprises, hold more than 5% or have management or control in the last 12 months?If neither, the small shareholder exemption applies
5Is an exemption available: investor in a Category I foreign portfolio investor, qualifying foreign merger or demerger, or a tax treaty?If yes, no Indian tax or reduced tax
6If still taxable, how much of the gain relates to India?Gain × value of Indian assets ÷ value of all assets

Values are taken at fair market value without deducting liabilities, normally at the end of the last accounting period before the sale. If the book value of assets has grown by 15% or more since then, the date of the sale is used.

/ Who has to do what

Seller, buyer and the Indian company.

  • Seller: pays capital gains tax on the India-related part of the gain, gets an accountant’s valuation report under the prescribed method, and files an Indian tax return. A PAN is needed.
  • Buyer: withholds Indian tax on that gain before paying, even if both buyer and seller are outside India. See TDS on buying shares from a non-resident.
  • Indian company: reports the transfer and the group shareholding to the tax department: within 90 days of the deal if control of the Indian company changes, otherwise within 90 days after the financial year ends. The old form was Form 49D; it is reported as Form 163 under the 2026 rules.
  • Penalty on the Indian company: 2% of the transaction value if control changed and the report was not filed; INR 5 lakh in other cases.

/ Practical points

What deal teams should check early.

  • Rates: 12.5% on long-term gains (held over 24 months); short-term gains at normal rates, 35% for a foreign company; plus surcharge and 4% cess.
  • Treaties: several Indian treaties give the right to tax shares of a non-Indian company only to the seller’s home country, which can switch the Indian charge off. Others, including the US and UK treaties, let India tax under its own law. The seller needs a tax residency certificate and must pass anti-abuse tests.
  • Group restructurings: an internal transfer is still a transfer. Foreign mergers and demergers are exempt only if their conditions are met, so check before moving a holding company within the group.
  • Funds: investors in Category I foreign portfolio investors are outside the rule. Private equity and venture funds exiting through an offshore holding company are not automatically exempt.
  • Founders and investors living outside India: selling shares of a Delaware or Singapore parent whose value sits in an Indian subsidiary can be taxed in India, unless the 5% exemption or a treaty applies. Founders resident in India are taxed here on the sale in any case. See US parent with an Indian subsidiary.
  • Dividends: a dividend paid by the foreign company outside India is not caught by this rule.
  • Old transactions: transfers before 28 May 2012 are no longer taxed under this rule, following the 2021 amendment.
  • Sale agreements: the price mechanics, the withholding clause, the tax indemnity and who obtains the valuation should be settled before signing.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is indirect transfer tax in India?
India taxes the gain on a sale of shares or an interest in a foreign company or entity if the shares derive their value substantially from assets located in India. Only the part of the gain attributable to the Indian assets is taxed.
When does a foreign company derive substantial value from Indian assets?
When the Indian assets are worth more than INR 10 crore and represent at least 50% of the value of all the assets of the foreign company, measured at fair market value without deducting liabilities.
What is the indirect transfer tax rate in India?
India's capital gains rates apply to the India-related part of the gain: 12.5% for long-term gains (held over 24 months), and normal rates for short-term gains, 35% for a foreign company, plus surcharge and 4% cess.
Who is exempt from India's indirect transfer rules?
A seller who, with associated enterprises, holds no more than 5% and has no right of management or control in the 12 months before the transfer; investors in Category I foreign portfolio investors; qualifying foreign mergers and demergers; and sellers protected by a tax treaty.
Does the buyer have to withhold tax on an indirect transfer?
Yes. The buyer must deduct Indian tax on the taxable gain before paying the seller, even when both are outside India.
Does the Indian company have to report an indirect transfer?
Yes. The Indian company through which the foreign company holds its Indian assets must report the transfer: within 90 days of the deal if control of the Indian company changes, otherwise within 90 days after the end of the financial year.
