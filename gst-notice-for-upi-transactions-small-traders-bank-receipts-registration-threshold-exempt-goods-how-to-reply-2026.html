/ Shopkeepers, vendors, freelancers and small service providers · GST notices from UPI and bank data · Updated 2026-11-28

GST Notice for UPI Transactions (2026): Why Small Traders Get Them, Registration Thresholds, What Is Not Turnover, Exempt Goods and How to Reply, UPI receipts are not automatically taxable turnover.

Tax departments now compare UPI and bank receipts with GST registrations. If your receipts in a year cross the registration threshold - INR 40 lakh for most goods-only businesses, INR 20 lakh for services - you may get a notice proposing registration, tax, interest and penalty. It is a proposal, not a final demand: personal receipts are not turnover, a person selling only exempt goods such as vegetables or milk need not register, and a documented reply can close or reduce it.

/ What the department looks at - and what it misses

Turnover vs receipts.

Receipt typeCounts towards GST turnover?
Sales of taxable goods or servicesYes
Sales of exempt goods or servicesCounts in aggregate turnover, but a person supplying only exempt items need not register
Money from family, friends or own other accountsNo - not a supply
Loans, refunds, reimbursements, deposits returnedNo
Cash sales not seen in UPI dataYes - include them in your own working
Registration thresholdINR 40 lakh for goods-only suppliers in most states; INR 20 lakh for services; lower in some special category states
  • Mixed accounts are the problem: when business and personal money run through the same UPI ID, the data overstates turnover - separate them in your reply with statements and explanations.
  • Several years at once: these notices often cover multiple financial years; check each year against the threshold separately.

/ How to reply

Before the deadline in the notice.

  • Read what is proposed: registration only, or tax, interest and penalty too - and for which years.
  • Build a year-wise working: total receipts, minus personal and non-business credits, split into taxable and exempt sales, compared with the threshold.
  • Reply in writing with evidence: bank and UPI statements, purchase bills, stock records, and proof that items sold are exempt.
  • If you were liable: register, consider the composition scheme if eligible, and pay what is genuinely due - paying tax and interest before a formal show cause notice limits penalty in non-fraud cases.
  • Do not just switch to cash: turnover is taxable however it is received, and cash raises other questions.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Why did I get a GST notice for UPI transactions?
Tax departments compare UPI and bank receipts with GST registrations. If your receipts in a year exceed the registration threshold and you are not registered, the system flags you and a notice proposes registration and tax. The notice is based on receipts, which may include money that is not turnover.
Is all money received on UPI taxable under GST?
No. Only consideration for supplies of goods or services is turnover. Money from family or friends, transfers between your own accounts, loans, refunds and reimbursements are not turnover, and exempt goods such as fresh vegetables, fruit and milk are not taxed.
What is the GST registration limit for small shops and service providers?
INR 40 lakh of aggregate turnover in a year for those supplying only goods in most states, and INR 20 lakh for services, with lower limits in some special category states. A person supplying only exempt goods or services does not need to register.
How do I reply to a GST notice based on UPI data?
Reply in writing before the deadline with a year-wise working that removes personal and non-business receipts and separates exempt sales, supported by bank and UPI statements, purchase bills and stock records. If tax is genuinely due, register and pay it with interest.
Should I stop accepting UPI payments to avoid GST notices?
No. Turnover is taxable whether it is received by UPI or in cash, and moving to cash does not change your liability. Keep business and personal receipts in separate accounts instead.
