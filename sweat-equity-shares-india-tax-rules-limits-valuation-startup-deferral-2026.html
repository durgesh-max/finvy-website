Sweat Equity Shares in India (2026): Tax on Allotment, the Startup Deferral, Limits, Valuation and Lock-In, taxed when you get them, not only when you sell.

Sweat equity shares are shares a company gives its directors or employees at a discount, or for their know-how, intellectual property or work, instead of cash. They are taxed as salary when allotted: the fair market value on the allotment date, less anything the person paid, is a perquisite taxed at slab rates, and the company deducts tax. At an eligible startup holding the Inter-Ministerial Board certificate, that tax can be deferred until the earliest of 48 months after the end of the relevant assessment year, the sale of the shares, or leaving the company. Company law caps sweat equity at 15% of paid-up capital (or INR 5 crore worth) a year and 25% in total, or 50% for a DPIIT startup in its first ten years, with a three-year lock-in and a registered valuer’s report. When the shares are sold later, the value already taxed becomes the cost, so it is not taxed twice.

/ How it is taxed

Allotment, deferral, sale.

StageTax
AllotmentFair market value on the allotment date minus the amount paid is a perquisite, taxed as salary at slab rates; the company deducts TDS
Eligible startupTax and TDS can be deferred until 14 days after the earliest of: 48 months from the end of the relevant assessment year, sale of the shares, or leaving the company
SaleCapital gain = sale price minus the fair market value taxed at allotment; holding period runs from allotment
Unlisted shares held over 24 months12.5% long-term capital gains plus surcharge and cess; shorter holding at slab rates
  • The cash problem: tax is due on shares that cannot be sold for three years. That is why the startup deferral matters; check that the company has the Inter-Ministerial Board certificate, not only DPIIT recognition. See the startup tax holiday and IMB certificate.
  • Sweat equity for IP: where a founder gives the company intellectual property in exchange for shares, the transfer of the IP can itself be taxable for the founder. Value and document it carefully.
  • Fair market value: for unlisted shares, the value is set by a category I merchant banker under the income-tax rules, which can differ from the company-law valuation. Get both right.
  • Non-resident recipients: issue to a person outside India is reported to the RBI in Form ESOP within 30 days; India taxes the perquisite to the extent it relates to work in India.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What are sweat equity shares?
Shares a company issues to its directors or employees at a discount, or for consideration other than cash such as know-how, intellectual property or value additions, under section 54 of the Companies Act, 2013.
How are sweat equity shares taxed in India?
On allotment, the fair market value of the shares on that date minus any amount paid is taxed as a salary perquisite at slab rates, with TDS by the company. On a later sale, the gain over that value is a capital gain.
Can tax on sweat equity be deferred?
Yes, at an eligible startup holding the Inter-Ministerial Board certificate. Tax can be paid within 14 days of the earliest of 48 months from the end of the relevant assessment year, the sale of the shares, or leaving the company.
What is the limit on sweat equity shares?
In a year, 15% of paid-up capital or shares worth INR 5 crore, whichever is higher. In total, 25% of paid-up capital, or 50% for a DPIIT-recognised startup in its first ten years.
Is there a lock-in on sweat equity shares?
Yes, three years from the date of allotment.
Can a founder get sweat equity shares?
Yes. Sweat equity can go to directors, including founder-directors, for their work, know-how or IP. ESOPs, by contrast, generally cannot be given to promoters except in startups within ten years of incorporation.
