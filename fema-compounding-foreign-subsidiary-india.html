/ Common FEMA breaches foreign-subsidiary carries

What needs compounding.

  • FC-GPR not filed / late filed: 30-day window from receipt of inward FDI remittance. Common breach when funds come in but filing is forgotten / delayed.
  • FC-TRS not filed / late filed: transfer of shares between resident and non-resident within 60 days. Missed when secondary share transfers happen.
  • FLA Return not filed: annual filing by 15 July (see our FLA guide). Multi-year non-filing common.
  • Form ODI Part II (APR - Annual Performance Report) not filed: for Indian company's overseas investment. Annual by 31 December.
  • Pricing guideline breach: FDI issue / FC-TRS done at price below RBI pricing guidelines (DCF for unlisted) or above (for FC-TRS to non-resident).
  • Sector-cap breach: foreign investment exceeded sector FDI cap (approval route sector but handled as automatic).
  • Delay in share allotment post inward remittance: shares must be allotted within 60 days (previously 180 days). Delays trigger compounding.
  • Reporting delays on External Commercial Borrowings (ECB): Form ECB / ECB-2 monthly reports.
  • Press Note 3 breach: investment from land-bordering country without prior approval.
  • Section 10 of FEMA: AD-category banking violations.

/ The compounding mechanism

Section 13 FEMA Act.

Section 13 of FEMA Act 1999 + FEMA (Compounding Proceedings) Rules 2000 permit voluntary compounding of contraventions. Key features:

  • Voluntary application: filed by the contravener (Indian subsidiary / Indian branch / Indian individual) to RBI's Compounding Authority (Chief General Manager or Regional Director).
  • Admission of contravention: application must contain full disclosure of the breach + supporting documents.
  • Compounding fee: as determined by Compounding Authority. Based on guidelines + amount involved + nature of breach + delay.
  • Timeline: 180 days from acknowledgement of application — typical 90-180 days.
  • Hearing: usually physical or video hearing with contravener's representative + CA. Opportunity to present mitigating facts.
  • Compounding order: specifies compounding fee + payment timeline (usually 15 days).
  • Payment made → contravention compounded → closed: no further prosecution under FEMA.

Benefits of voluntary compounding:

  • Materially lower fee than if caught during audit / scrutiny.
  • Closes exposure permanently — no further prosecution.
  • Avoids reputational damage.
  • Required for M&A / IPO / future FDI — without clean compounding history, future transactions may be blocked by AD bank.

/ Compounding fee structure

What to budget for.

Compounding fee is at Compounding Authority's discretion but follows published guidelines (RBI Master Direction on Compounding):

  • Base fee: proportional to amount involved + nature of breach.
  • FC-GPR / FC-TRS late filing (minor delay): typically INR 10,000-50,000 per instance.
  • FLA / APR non-filing (per year): typically INR 10,000-50,000 per year.
  • Pricing guideline breach: higher — up to multiples of the pricing variance.
  • Sector cap breach / Press Note 3 breach: significant — can be 100%+ of amount involved; may require unwinding.
  • Multi-year / multi-instance breaches: compound; often grouped under single application for cost efficiency.

Rough range for typical foreign-subsidiary cleanup: INR 50,000 - INR 10 lakh depending on scope and complexity. Material certainty achieved for small outlay.

/ Compounding application workflow

90-180 day execution.

  1. Audit of FEMA compliance history: review all FC-GPR, FC-TRS, FLA, APR, Form ODI, ECB filings for 5+ year history. Identify breaches + severity.
  2. Fact pattern + chronology: for each breach, draft chronology with dates, amounts, parties, delay period, reason (if any).
  3. Supporting document collection: FIRC (Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate), bank statements, board resolutions, share certificates, AD bank correspondence.
  4. Compounding application drafting: comprehensive application covering all identified breaches, admission of facts, mitigating circumstances, prayer for compounding.
  5. Submission to Compounding Authority: via AD-I category bank. Along with application fee + supporting documents.
  6. Acknowledgement: RBI acknowledges + schedules hearing (typically within 60-90 days).
  7. Hearing: in-person or video hearing with CA representation. BQP partner attends. Specific questions on breach + mitigating facts.
  8. Compounding order: within 180 days of acknowledgement. Specifies fee + payment deadline.
  9. Fee payment: via RTGS to designated government account within 15 days.
  10. Compliance certificate: Compounding Authority issues closure certificate — breach formally compounded. File copy forever.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is FEMA compounding?
Voluntary settlement mechanism under Section 13 of FEMA Act 1999 to regularise historical FEMA breaches. Contravener (Indian subsidiary / Indian company / Indian individual) files application with RBI Compounding Authority admitting the breach, pays compounding fee as determined, and the contravention is formally compounded - no further prosecution. Materially cheaper and more certain than being caught in audit / scrutiny.
When should an Indian subsidiary pursue voluntary compounding?
When FEMA breaches are discovered - typically during pre-IPO due diligence, pre-M&A due diligence, auditor review, or internal compliance review. Voluntary compounding is cheaper than compounding driven by RBI inspection. Compounding should be pursued before any formal RBI notice because once a notice is issued, lower-fee voluntary route may close.
How much does FEMA compounding cost?
Discretionary - set by Compounding Authority based on guidelines + amount + nature + delay. Rough ranges: FC-GPR/FC-TRS late filing INR 10,000-50,000 per instance; FLA / APR non-filing INR 10,000-50,000 per year; pricing guideline breach higher. Total cleanup for typical foreign-subsidiary: INR 50,000 - INR 10 lakh. BQP engagement fee separate.
What breaches are typically compounded for foreign-subsidiary companies?
Most common: missed/late FC-GPR filing after inward FDI remittance; late FC-TRS on share transfer; non-filing of FLA Return (annual); non-filing of Form ODI Part II/APR for Indian company's overseas investment; pricing guideline breach on share issue to non-resident; delays in share allotment post remittance; Press Note 3 breach (investment from land-bordering country without approval).
How long does FEMA compounding take?
90-180 days from application submission to compounding order. Includes application review (30-60 days), hearing scheduling (another 30-60 days), hearing + order drafting (30-60 days). Fee payment within 15 days of order. Timing varies by Compounding Authority's docket + complexity of breach.
