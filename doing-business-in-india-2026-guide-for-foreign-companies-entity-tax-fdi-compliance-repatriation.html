Doing Business in India 2026: A Guide for Foreign Companies - Entity, FDI, Tax Rates, Compliance, Hiring, Banking and Getting Money Out, the facts in one place, and who to call.

Most foreign companies do business in India through a wholly owned Indian private limited company. Foreign ownership of 100% is allowed without approval in most sectors. Investors from countries that share a land border with India, and a few capped sectors, need government approval first; since Press Note 2 of 2026, land-border ownership within 10% and non-controlling no longer triggers it. The company pays about 25% tax under the 22% regime; a foreign company’s branch pays 35% plus surcharge and cess. Dividends, royalties and service fees paid abroad carry Indian withholding tax of 20% plus surcharge and cess, usually cut to 5% to 15% by a tax treaty. Every company needs a director resident in India, a statutory audit, an annual tax return and RBI reporting of foreign investment. India has a new Income-tax Act, 2025 from 1 April 2026, has abolished the equalisation levy and has not enacted Pillar Two. This page sets out the facts and links to the detailed guide for each step.

/ The first year

What a new Indian subsidiary does.

  • Before incorporation: name approval, a resident director, the registered office, and FDI route check.
  • Incorporation and bank account: the parent remits share capital through banking channels; the bank issues the foreign inward remittance certificate and KYC.
  • Within 30 days of issuing shares: Form FC-GPR to the RBI through the bank, with a valuation certificate. Late filing costs a fee; see the late fee calculator.
  • Registrations: PAN, TAN, GST where needed, payroll registrations (provident fund, state taxes) as staff join.
  • Intercompany agreements: service, licence and cost-sharing agreements priced at arm’s length.
  • Year end, 31 March: audit, board and shareholder meetings, company law filings, the FLA return by 15 July, income-tax return and transfer-pricing report. See the annual compliance calendar.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Can a foreign company own 100% of an Indian company?
Yes, in most sectors under the automatic route without prior approval. Investors from countries sharing a land border with India, and some capped or regulated sectors, need government approval first; under Press Note 2 of 2026, an investor whose land-border beneficial ownership is within 10% and non-controlling can use the automatic route.
What is the best way for a foreign company to start doing business in India?
For selling, hiring or building a team, usually a wholly owned private limited company. An employer of record suits a few early hires, and a liaison office suits representation without income.
How long does it take for a foreign company to set up a company in India?
Incorporation through the MCA's SPICe+ form usually takes a few weeks once documents are ready. The usual delays are apostilled or consularised parent documents, digital signatures and DINs for directors, the resident director, and the bank account; the FC-GPR filing with the RBI follows within 30 days of issuing shares to the parent.
Does a foreign company need a local partner in India?
No, in most sectors. 100% foreign ownership is allowed under the automatic route without a local partner; a few sectors have caps or need government approval, and investors linked to countries sharing a land border with India need approval unless the stake is within 10% and non-controlling (Press Note 2 of 2026).
Does a foreign company need GST registration to sell to Indian customers?
It depends on what it sells and to whom. A foreign supplier of online services to Indian consumers must register for GST under the OIDAR rules; services sold to Indian businesses are usually taxed under reverse charge by the Indian buyer; goods are taxed at import. A subsidiary selling in India registers like any Indian company.
What is the corporate tax rate in India for a foreign company?
An Indian subsidiary under the 22% regime pays about 25.17% including surcharge and cess. A branch of a foreign company pays 35% plus surcharge and cess.
Does an Indian subsidiary need an Indian director?
It needs at least one director who stayed in India for 182 days or more in the previous calendar year. A private company needs at least two directors in total.
What filings does a foreign-owned Indian company make every year?
Statutory audit, company law annual filings, an income-tax return, a transfer-pricing report and master file where applicable, GST returns if registered, and the FLA return to the RBI by 15 July.
How does a foreign parent take money out of India?
Through dividends, royalties and service fees, or a share buy-back or capital reduction. Each carries Indian tax, usually reduced by a tax treaty, and needs Forms 145 and 146 for the bank.
Has India introduced the global minimum tax or a digital services tax?
No. India has not enacted Pillar Two, and it abolished the equalisation levy: the 2% levy ended on 1 August 2024 and the 6% levy on 1 April 2025.
