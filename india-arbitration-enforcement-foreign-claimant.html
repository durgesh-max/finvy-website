/ India + New York Convention

Enforcement framework.

India is a signatory to the New York Convention on Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards. Enforcement in India governed by:

  • Arbitration & Conciliation Act 1996: Section 47-49 (Part II) governs foreign award enforcement.
  • Section 48: grounds on which enforcement may be refused — parties under incapacity, invalid agreement, no proper notice, scope exceeded, incorrect tribunal composition, award not binding, public policy of India.
  • Section 49: once satisfied with Section 47 requirements, award deemed to be a decree of Indian court — enforceable like any domestic judgment.

Reciprocity: India has notified specific countries as reciprocating territories (US, UK, Singapore, UAE, France, Germany, Netherlands, Japan, Russia, Hong Kong, among others). Awards from reciprocating territories enforceable under Part II.

Enforcement workflow:

  1. File petition in Indian High Court having jurisdiction over respondent / assets.
  2. Submit certified award + arbitration agreement + award debt confirmation.
  3. Court examines Section 48 grounds; respondent may object.
  4. If court satisfied: award declared enforceable + decree issued.
  5. Execution: attachment of assets, bank account garnishment, winding-up petition if company respondent.

Timeline: 12-36 months for enforcement decree + execution, depending on court workload + opposition.

/ Section 48 defences India respondents raise

What to anticipate.

  • Public policy of India: most common defence. Courts have interpreted narrowly post-2015 amendment — limited to fundamental policy of Indian law, interest of India, justice / morality. Shri Lal Mahal case (2014) narrowed scope.
  • Fraud / corruption in award making: Section 48(2)(b)(ii) — if award involved fraud or corruption. Hard to establish.
  • No proper notice of proceedings: respondent not notified / unable to present case.
  • Scope beyond submission: award covers matters outside arbitration agreement.
  • Invalid arbitration agreement: no valid underlying agreement.
  • Patent error / excess of jurisdiction: tribunal exceeded authority.

Section 34 (Part I - domestic awards): broader grounds including patent illegality, but Section 34 doesn't apply to foreign awards.

/ Asset tracing + attachment

Finding Indian assets.

  • MCA records: Indian company respondent's directorship, cross-ownership, group structure. All public via MCA portal.
  • Immovable property: state revenue department records (land / building titles). State-specific portals (Mumbai, Delhi, Bangalore have online property searches).
  • Bank account identification: via judgment-debtor examination post-decree.
  • Shareholding in Indian listed companies: NSDL / CDSL (depository) + stock exchange records.
  • Foreign assets held by Indian respondent: FEMA ODI filings (via Form ODI / APR) show overseas investments.
  • Attachment before judgment: Section 9 Arbitration Act / Order 38 CPC — interim attachment to prevent asset dissipation during enforcement proceedings.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Can a foreign arbitral award be enforced in India?
Yes - under Part II of Arbitration & Conciliation Act 1996 (Sections 47-49), awards from New York Convention signatory countries (US, UK, Singapore, UAE, France, Germany, Netherlands, Hong Kong, among 50+ reciprocating territories) are enforceable in India. Foreign claimant files petition in High Court having jurisdiction over respondent / assets. Section 48 provides narrow refusal grounds (public policy, fraud, no notice, scope excess).
Which arbitration seats are most commonly enforced in India?
SIAC (Singapore), LCIA (London), ICC (various seats including Paris / Geneva), HKIAC (Hong Kong), DIAC / ADGMAC / DIFC-LCIA (UAE), SCCA (Saudi Arabia), AAA (US). India is also a seat for ICA (Indian Council of Arbitration), MCIA (Mumbai Centre for International Arbitration), DAC (Delhi Arbitration Centre). Foreign-seated awards enforceable; India-seated awards executed as domestic decrees under Part I.
How long does India enforcement take?
12-36 months typical from petition filing to decree + execution. Depends on: respondent's opposition intensity, court workload, interim measures (attachment pre-decree), appeals (within 30 days of enforcement order). India's Supreme Court has emphasised faster enforcement in recent judgments but delay remains structural issue.
Can I get interim measures / asset attachment in India before enforcement?
Yes - Section 9 Arbitration Act permits interim measures by Indian court including before / during arbitration + post-award / pre-enforcement. Can include: asset attachment, injunction against disposal, preservation of evidence, bank account freeze. Order 38 Rule 5 CPC also available for attachment before judgment in execution proceedings.
How is 'public policy of India' interpreted in enforcement refusal?
Narrowly post-2015 Arbitration Amendment Act + Shri Lal Mahal case (2014). Limited to: (a) fundamental policy of Indian law, (b) interest of India, (c) justice or morality. NOT a general review of award merits. Earlier wide interpretation (Phulchand Exports / Patel Engineering) overruled. India now generally pro-enforcement.
