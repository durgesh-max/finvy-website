Income Tax Outstanding Demand (2026): What It Means, How to Respond (Agree, Partly Agree, Disagree), Section 245 Refund Adjustment, Interest, Rectification and Appeal, answer it, don’t ignore it.

An outstanding demand is tax the department says you still owe for a past year - raised by a 143(1) intimation, an assessment or a penalty order. Left alone, it carries interest at 1% per month and can be recovered from your future refunds under section 245. Many demands are wrong - tax already paid, TDS credit missed, a processing error - and can be disputed online, rectified or appealed.

/ Where the demand comes from, and what fixes it

Cause first, remedy second.

Common causeUsual remedy
Tax already paid but challan not matchedRespond that the demand is paid, with challan details; rectification if needed
TDS / TCS credit not given (missing in Form 26AS or claimed in wrong year)Get the deductor to correct the TDS return; then rectification
Arithmetic or processing error in the 143(1) intimationRectification (section 154, now section 287)
Disallowance or addition in an assessment orderAppeal to CIT(A) within 30 days of service; seek stay of demand
Demand already reduced by an appeal or rectification orderRespond with the order so the record is updated
Demand is correctPay it (to stop 1% per month interest) or seek instalments
  • Find the source: every demand has an assessment year, a section and a reference to the intimation or order that raised it - get that document before replying.
  • Old demands: legacy demands from years ago often relate to processing errors or TDS mismatches and can still be disputed with evidence.

/ Responding, refund adjustment, interest and stay

Sections 156, 220(2) and 245.

  • Respond online: on the e-filing portal you can say the demand is correct, partly correct or disputed, and give the reason (paid, rectification filed, appeal filed, stay granted, credit not given) with supporting details.
  • Section 245 intimation: before setting a refund off against a demand, the department sends an intimation; reply within the time stated, or the refund can be adjusted.
  • Interest: a demand not paid within the time in the notice of demand (section 156) carries interest at 1% per month under section 220(2).
  • Pending appeal: apply for a stay of demand; under CBDT guidelines a stay is generally granted on payment of a part of the disputed demand.
  • Don’t just agree to clear the screen: agreeing to a wrong demand allows adjustment of your refunds and makes later correction harder.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What does outstanding demand mean in income tax?
It is tax, interest or penalty the department says you still owe for a past year, raised by a 143(1) intimation, an assessment order or a penalty order. It shows against your PAN until it is paid, reduced by rectification or appeal, or recorded as disputed.
What should I do if I get an outstanding demand notice?
Find the intimation or order that raised it and check the cause. If it is correct, pay it to stop interest. If it is wrong, respond online saying it is disputed with the reason, then file a rectification for processing or credit errors or an appeal against an assessment order.
Can the department adjust my refund against an old demand?
Yes. Under section 245 it can set off a refund against a demand for another year after sending you an intimation. Reply to the intimation within the time given if the demand is wrong, already paid or under appeal.
Is there interest on an unpaid income tax demand?
Yes. If the demand is not paid within the time in the notice of demand, interest at 1% per month runs under section 220(2) until it is paid or reduced.
I already paid the tax but it still shows as outstanding. What now?
Respond that the demand is paid, with the challan details (BSR code, date, serial number). If the payment was made under the wrong year or head, a correction or rectification is needed.
