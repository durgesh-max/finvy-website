/ What triggers FEMA ODI

The scope.

Overseas Direct Investment (ODI) under FEMA (Overseas Investment Rules 2022) is triggered by:

  • Indian resident individual investing in equity, debentures, or convertible instruments of a foreign company.
  • Indian company investing in equity or convertible instruments of a foreign company (strategic or financial).
  • Setting up a wholly-owned subsidiary or joint venture outside India.
  • Acquiring an existing foreign company (M&A).
  • Investing in overseas funds as an LP.
  • Flipping an Indian startup to an overseas parent (share-swap).

For Indian individuals: Automatic Route up to USD 250,000 per financial year under LRS (Liberalised Remittance Scheme). For Indian companies: Automatic Route up to 400% of Indian net worth.

Specific sector carve-outs trigger Approval Route: real estate (prohibited), financial services (specific restrictions), chit funds (prohibited). Press Note 3 (eased for non-controlling stakes within 10% by Press Note 2 (2026)) of 2020 governs investment into India, not ODI itself: it matters when the overseas entity has an owner from a land-border country and invests back into India, for example after a flip.

/ What a BQP FEMA ODI engagement covers

The full workflow.

  1. Pre-investment scoping — eligibility under Automatic Route vs Approval Route, LRS limit check, Press Note 3 analysis if any beneficial owner from land-border country.
  2. Valuation certificate — the price must be on an arm's length basis using an internationally accepted pricing methodology, supported by a valuation report where the rules require one.
  3. Authorised Dealer bank co-ordination — outward remittance via your Indian bank's forex desk (HDFC, ICICI, Axis, SBI).
  4. ODI reporting — reported in Form FC (which replaced Form ODI Part I in August 2022) through the AD bank when the investment is made; RBI allots a UIN; evidence of investment within six months; Form APR every year by 31 December.
  5. UIN (Unique Identification Number) — allotted when the first Form FC is reported; all later reporting for that foreign entity uses it.
  6. Annual Performance Report (APR) — filed by 31 December every year for each foreign entity, based on its latest accounts (limited exceptions, such as holdings under 10% without control, or an entity under liquidation).
  7. Disinvestment reporting — if the Indian investor sells or exits the overseas entity, the disinvestment is reported through the AD bank within 30 days (Form ODI Part III no longer exists).
  8. FDI reporting — if the overseas entity in turn invests back into India, that is treated as FDI and separate filings apply.

/ Common FEMA ODI mistakes

What we clean up.

  • Form FC not reported, or reported late. Late reporting is a breach. Regularise within three years by paying the RBI late submission fee; beyond that, compounding under FEMA is needed.
  • UIN never obtained. Investor remitted the money but the investment was never reported, so no UIN exists. Years later, the structure is quietly non-compliant.
  • APR not filed annually. A dormant overseas entity still needs an APR unless an exception applies. Missing APRs compound.
  • Press Note 3 missed on the way back in. If the overseas entity has a beneficial owner from China, Pakistan, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Myanmar or Afghanistan and invests into India (common after a flip), that investment needs government approval.
  • Pricing not at arm's length. No valuation, or a valuation that does not use an internationally accepted method, is a common gap.
  • LRS limit breach. Individuals investing above USD 250,000 per financial year under Automatic Route without additional approval.
  • Disinvestment not reported. Exit happened but the disinvestment was never reported to the AD bank.

Clean-up: the RBI late submission fee where the filing is within three years of its due date, otherwise a compounding application under FEMA section 15. We handle both routes end-to-end.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is FEMA ODI and when is it required?
Overseas Direct Investment under FEMA (Overseas Investment Rules 2022). Required whenever an Indian resident individual or Indian company invests in equity or convertible instruments of a foreign company. Each investment is reported in Form FC through the AD bank when it is made (a UIN is allotted), and an Annual Performance Report is filed by 31 December each year.
What is the LRS limit for overseas investment?
USD 250,000 per financial year per Indian resident individual under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS). Covers investment, education, medical, travel, maintenance of relatives abroad. Investment-specific sub-limits apply (e.g., equity investment capped at the LRS limit itself).
What is Form ODI Part I vs Part II vs Part III?
Since the Overseas Investment Rules and Directions of August 2022 the old Form ODI parts are replaced: Form FC reports each financial commitment (first investment and later ones under the same UIN), Form APR is the annual performance report due 31 December, Form FLA is the annual return on foreign assets and liabilities, and a sale or exit (disinvestment) is reported through the AD bank within 30 days.
What is Annual Performance Report (APR)?
An annual filing under FEMA ODI framework reporting the overseas entity's performance (financial results, operations, dividend/royalty flows back to India). Filed by 31 December every year, based on the foreign entity's latest accounts. Required even if the entity is dormant, with limited exceptions (for example a holding under 10% without control, or an entity under liquidation).
What happens if I miss FEMA ODI filings?
Late ODI reporting (Form FC, the Annual Performance Report and other overseas investment filings) can be regularised within three years of the due date by paying the RBI late submission fee: INR 7,500 plus 0.025% of the amount for each year of delay for filings that report a flow of money, and a flat INR 7,500 for the APR. After three years the breach must be compounded with the RBI; penalties under Section 13 apply to breaches that are not regularised. BQP handles both routes.
