/ Small businesses · GST · Updated 2026-11-25

GST Composition Scheme (2026): Turnover Limit Rs 1.5 Crore, Rates, Service Providers at 6%, Rules, Returns and Who Should Opt In, lower tax and fewer returns, at the cost of input tax credit.

The GST composition scheme lets small businesses pay tax at a flat rate on turnover instead of charging regular GST: 1% for traders and manufacturers, 5% for restaurants and 6% for service providers. It is open to businesses with turnover up to ₹1.5 crore in the previous year (₹75 lakh in some special category states), and up to ₹50 lakh for service providers. In return, a composition taxpayer cannot claim input tax credit, cannot collect GST from customers, issues a bill of supply instead of a tax invoice and cannot make inter-state outward supplies of goods. It files a quarterly payment statement (CMP-08) and one annual return (GSTR-4).

/ Quick answers

What small businesses search for.

  • What is the composition scheme turnover limit? ₹1.5 crore aggregate turnover in the previous financial year for traders, manufacturers and restaurants (₹75 lakh in some special category states); ₹50 lakh for service providers under the separate services composition.
  • What are the composition rates? 1% (0.5% CGST + 0.5% SGST) for manufacturers and traders, 5% for restaurants not serving alcohol, and 6% (3% + 3%) for other service providers.
  • Can service providers opt in? Yes, under the services composition at 6% if turnover was up to ₹50 lakh. Restaurants use the main scheme.
  • How do I opt in? File Form CMP-02 on the GST portal before the start of the financial year (for a new registration, choose composition in the registration application).
  • What returns are due? CMP-08 to pay tax by the 18th of the month after each quarter, and the annual GSTR-4. The GSTR-4 due date was moved to 30 June after the year in 2024; some guides still show 30 April, so check the portal.
  • How do I check if a supplier is under composition? Search the GSTIN on the GST portal; the taxpayer type shows Composition.

/ Who cannot opt in

Exclusions and exits.

  • Excluded: businesses making inter-state outward supplies of goods, non-resident and casual taxable persons, and manufacturers of notified goods such as ice cream, pan masala and tobacco products.
  • E-commerce: eligibility for sellers supplying through e-commerce operators is reported inconsistently and has changed over time; confirm before opting in.
  • All or nothing: every registration under the same PAN must opt for composition together.
  • Crossing the limit: once turnover crosses the limit during the year, the scheme ends and regular GST applies; file CMP-04 to leave voluntarily.
  • Sole proprietors and small firms: see proprietorship registration and partnership firm registration.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is the turnover limit for the GST composition scheme?
Rs 1.5 crore aggregate turnover in the previous financial year for traders, manufacturers and restaurants (Rs 75 lakh in some special category states), and Rs 50 lakh for service providers under the services composition scheme.
What are the GST composition scheme rates?
1% of turnover for manufacturers and traders, 5% for restaurants not serving alcohol, and 6% for other service providers, split equally between CGST and SGST.
Can a service provider opt for the composition scheme?
Yes. A service provider with turnover up to Rs 50 lakh in the previous year can pay 6% under the services composition scheme, without input tax credit and without charging GST to customers.
What returns does a composition taxpayer file?
CMP-08 by the 18th of the month after each quarter to pay tax, and the annual GSTR-4. The GSTR-4 due date was moved to 30 June after the year in 2024; confirm the current date on the GST portal.
Can a composition dealer claim input tax credit?
No. Composition taxpayers cannot claim input tax credit or collect GST from customers; they issue a bill of supply instead of a tax invoice.
