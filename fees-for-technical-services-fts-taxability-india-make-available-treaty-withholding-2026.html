Fees for Technical Services (FTS) in India (2026): Definition, When Payments to Foreign Companies Are Taxable, the 'Make Available' Test and Withholding, taxable in India even if the work is done abroad, unless a treaty says otherwise.

Fees for technical services (FTS) means payment for managerial, technical or consultancy services, other than construction, assembly or mining projects and salary. When an Indian business pays FTS to a foreign company, the income is treated as arising in India even if the services are rendered entirely outside India and the foreign company has no presence here. Under Indian law it is taxed at 20% on the gross amount plus surcharge and cess, and the Indian payer must withhold tax under section 393(2) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (formerly section 195). Tax treaties usually cut this to 10-15%, and some treaties (with the US, UK, Singapore and Canada) tax technical services only if they make available know-how that the Indian buyer can apply on its own; otherwise the payment is business profit, taxable in India only if the supplier has a permanent establishment here.

/ Is it FTS, and is it taxable?

Three questions.

Is it a managerial, technical or consultancy service (not construction/mining or salary)?Domestic FTS: taxable in India at 20% grossCheck royalty or business-profits rules instead
Does the supplier's treaty have an FTS article?Treaty rate applies (often 10-15%) if lower and documents are givenNo separate FTS article: check the royalty and business-profits articles (for example India-Australia)
Does the treaty require the service to 'make available' know-how (US, UK, Singapore, Canada)?Taxable only if technical knowledge or skill is transferred so you can use it independently (for example, training your team)Routine services that do not transfer know-how fall outside FTS: business profits, taxable only with a PE

The contract wording often decides the tax.

Whether a service "makes available" know-how, or is plain business income, depends on what is delivered. A review before signing (and before the first payment) avoids withholding at 20% when the treaty may allow nil. WhatsApp CA Durgesh Chavda.

/ How the Indian payer withholds

Process and documents.

  • Withhold under section 393(2) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 at the lower of the Act rate (20% plus surcharge and cess) and the treaty rate.
  • For the treaty rate, collect the supplier's tax residency certificate, Form 41 (formerly 10F) and PAN; keep a no-PE declaration where relevant.
  • If you agree to bear the tax, the payment is grossed up, so the tax cost rises.
  • Deposit the tax, file the quarterly statement (Form 144) and certify the remittance on Forms 145/146 (formerly 15CA/15CB).
  • GST is separate: imported services are taxed under reverse charge; see RCM on import of services.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What are fees for technical services under Indian tax law?
Consideration for managerial, technical or consultancy services, excluding construction, assembly, mining or similar projects and income taxed as salary.
Is FTS paid to a foreign company taxable in India if the work is done abroad?
Under Indian law, yes: FTS payable by an Indian business is treated as arising in India whether or not the services were rendered in India. A tax treaty may reduce or remove the tax.
What is the tax rate on FTS paid to non-residents?
20% on the gross amount plus surcharge and cess under Indian law; treaties commonly cap it at 10-15%.
What does 'make available' mean?
Under treaties such as India-US, India-UK, India-Singapore and India-Canada, technical services are taxable as FTS only if they transfer technical knowledge, skill or know-how that lets the recipient apply it independently. Services that do not, are business profits taxable only with a PE in India.
Which documents does the Indian payer need for the treaty rate?
The supplier's tax residency certificate, Form 41 (formerly 10F) and PAN, plus the remittance forms 145/146 (formerly 15CA/15CB).
