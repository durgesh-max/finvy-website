GIFT City for NRIs (2026): Dollar FDs, IFSC Funds, Insurance and Listed Securities - What Is Tax-Free in India and When It Actually Helps, for NRIs in the UAE, US, UK, Singapore, Canada and Australia.

Yes, NRIs can invest through GIFT City, India's International Financial Services Centre (IFSC) in Gujarat. Everything there is held in foreign currency, usually US dollars. The main options are foreign-currency fixed deposits with the IFSC banking units of Indian and foreign banks, IFSC mutual funds, retail schemes and AIFs (AIFs typically need around USD 150,000), securities on the IFSC exchanges, and life insurance from IFSC insurance offices. Several of these are exempt from Indian tax for non-residents. Interest on IFSC banking unit deposits has long been exempt for non-residents and not-ordinarily-resident individuals, and the Income-tax Act, 2025 groups the IFSC exemptions in a schedule. But your home country may still tax the income: the US, UK, Canada and Australia tax residents on worldwide income. GIFT City is therefore most attractive for NRIs in the UAE and other no-tax countries, for returning NRIs in their RNOR years, and for anyone who wants dollar exposure with an Indian bank.

/ Practical points

Before you invest.

  • KYC: passport, overseas address proof, PAN or tax residency details; banks may onboard you remotely.
  • Funding: transfer from abroad, or from NRE/FCNR accounts where the bank permits; money stays in foreign currency.
  • Compare with FCNR deposits: FCNR(B) interest is also exempt for non-residents and RNORs; compare rates, tenure and flexibility; see NRE, NRO and FCNR.
  • Returning to India: once you become resident and ordinarily resident, exemptions tied to non-resident status stop, and residents can only invest within the LRS rules.
  • US persons: check PFIC and reporting rules before buying any non-US fund.
  • Home-country reporting: declare accounts and income where required (for example FBAR or T1135).

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Can NRIs invest in GIFT City?
Yes. NRIs can open foreign-currency deposits with IFSC banking units and invest in IFSC mutual funds, retail schemes, AIFs, exchange-traded products and insurance, all in foreign currency.
Is GIFT City FD interest tax-free for NRIs?
Interest on deposits with IFSC banking units is exempt in India for non-residents and not-ordinarily-resident individuals, but your country of residence may still tax it.
Are GIFT City funds tax-free for NRIs?
Income of specified IFSC funds attributable to non-resident unit holders is exempt in India subject to conditions; check that the scheme qualifies and how your home country taxes it.
Is GIFT City worth it for NRIs in the US or UK?
Often less so: US and UK residents are taxed on worldwide income, and US persons face PFIC rules on foreign funds. It is most useful for NRIs in the UAE and other no-tax countries, and for returning NRIs in RNOR years.
What is the minimum investment in GIFT City?
It depends on the product and provider; AIFs typically need around USD 150,000, while bank deposits and some retail schemes have lower minimums.
