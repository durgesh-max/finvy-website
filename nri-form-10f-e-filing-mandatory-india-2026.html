Form 10F, what every NRI claiming DTAA needs.

/ The e-Form 10F mandate

What changed in 2023.

Earlier: Form 10F was a self-declaration by non-resident payee, submitted on plain paper to the Indian payer, kept in the payer's file. From 16 July 2022 (CBDT Notification 03/2022), Form 10F became electronic — must be filed on the Indian Income Tax e-filing portal (incometax.gov.in).

Initial relaxation (October 2022 - March 2023, extended to September 2023): non-residents without an Indian PAN could continue to file manual Form 10F. Post 1 October 2023: full electronic filing mandatory for all non-residents claiming DTAA benefit.

CBDT clarified (via Notification/Circular July 2024): non-residents without Indian PAN can obtain a temporary online account on the Indian e-filing portal using their foreign Tax Identification Number + country of residence — and file electronic Form 10F from that account.

/ When Form 10F is required

The use cases.

  • Any payment from India to non-resident claiming DTAA treaty rate: dividend, royalty, FTS, interest, capital gain, service fee. See our Form 15CA/CB guide.
  • NRI filing Indian ITR: when claiming reduced tax rate under DTAA (e.g., long-term capital gains at treaty rate).
  • NRI claiming lower TDS deduction: when seeking refund of excess Section 195 TDS deducted at 20% default when treaty rate was 10-15%.
  • Foreign company receiving India-source income: dividend from Indian subsidiary, royalty from Indian licensee, service fee from Indian customer.

Without Form 10F + TRC, Indian payer must apply Section 195 default rate (20%) or Section 206AA PAN-absent rate (20%+). No treaty relief.

/ What Form 10F contains

The nine items.

  • Status of the taxpayer (individual / company / firm / other).
  • Nationality / country of incorporation / registration.
  • Taxpayer's Identification Number in home country (TIN / SSN / NIN / foreign PAN equivalent).
  • Period for which residential status (per TRC) applies.
  • Address in home country.
  • Indian PAN (if available) or foreign TIN as substitute.
  • Confirmation that the person is a tax resident of home country.
  • Confirmation that beneficial ownership of income is with the declarant (anti-treaty-shopping provision).
  • Signature of declarant (or authorised signatory for companies).

Validity: Form 10F declaration is tied to the TRC date range — typically 1 year (TRCs are usually annual).

/ Non-PAN NRI route - step-by-step

e-Filing without Indian PAN.

  1. Visit India e-filing portal: incometax.gov.in.
  2. Click 'Register' as 'Taxpayer' → select 'Non-Resident Not Holding and Not Required to Have PAN'.
  3. Enter: name + date of birth + foreign TIN (passport number acceptable where no formal TIN) + country of residence + email + mobile.
  4. OTP verification on email + mobile.
  5. Login credentials created; account linked to foreign TIN.
  6. Navigate to 'e-File → Income Tax Forms → File Income Tax Forms → Form 10F'.
  7. Complete the 9 fields.
  8. Upload TRC from home country tax authority (scanned PDF).
  9. Submit with Aadhaar OTP (not required for non-residents) OR using the account-level digital signature if available.
  10. Acknowledgement number generated — retain + share with Indian payer.

Form 10F now visible to Indian payer when they look up the non-resident's Indian tax profile for Form 15CB signing.

/ Common Form 10F mistakes

Where NRIs trip.

  • Missing TRC: Form 10F cannot substitute TRC. Both are needed. TRC = tax authority of home country confirms residence; Form 10F = non-resident's own declaration about residence + treaty eligibility + beneficial ownership.
  • Stale TRC/Form 10F: validity is calendar year (or TRC's stated period). Must be renewed annually. Common for Indian payer to use last year's Form 10F in the new FY → invalid.
  • Beneficial ownership issue: NRI claiming treaty benefit but income actually flows to someone else (treaty shopping). GAAR / principal purpose test may deny treaty benefit even with TRC + Form 10F.
  • Wrong country of residence: NRI has moved countries but TRC + Form 10F reflect old country. Must match current residence as of income accrual period.
  • Form 10F filed too late: Form 10F must be in place BEFORE Indian payer makes payment / files Form 15CB. Late filing = payer deducts at non-treaty rate; NRI must claim refund later.
  • Account not accessible: non-resident forgot login or lost email access. CBDT support slow; prevention via proper credential management.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Is Form 10F mandatory for NRIs in 2026?
Yes - mandatory in electronic form on India's income tax e-filing portal for every non-resident claiming DTAA treaty rate on India-source income (dividend, royalty, FTS, interest, capital gain, service fee). Post 1 October 2023, no manual filing permitted. Non-residents without Indian PAN can register online as 'Non-Resident Not Holding and Not Required to Have PAN' and file Form 10F from that account.
Can an NRI file Form 10F without an Indian PAN?
Yes. Register on India's e-filing portal (incometax.gov.in) using foreign TIN (or passport number where no TIN exists) + country of residence + email + mobile. OTP verification creates account. File Form 10F from that account. CBDT clarified this specific route in Notification/Circular July 2024.
What is the validity of Form 10F?
Tied to the associated TRC - typically 1 year. Both must be refreshed annually. Form 10F stays filed on the portal; new one must be filed for each new FY where DTAA benefit continues to be claimed.
What happens if an NRI doesn't file Form 10F?
DTAA treaty rate denied. Indian payer must apply Section 195 default rate (20%) or Section 206AA rate (20%+) where applicable. NRI can later claim refund by filing Indian ITR + Form 10F + TRC, but immediate TDS deduction is at higher rate.
Does Form 10F need to be in English?
The India e-filing portal is in English. Form 10F is filled in English. Supporting TRC (from home country) can be in any language but English translation (certified) recommended for Indian payer / tax officer reference.
Does BQP help NRIs with Form 10F + TRC + DTAA claim?
Yes - standard NRI advisory service.
