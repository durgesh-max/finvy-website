/ Companies appointing a statutory auditor · Company law · Updated 2026-11-25

Form ADT-1 Filing Procedure (2026): Auditor Appointment Within 15 Days, First Auditor, Five-Year Term, Rotation and ADT-3, every appointment needs a filing trail.

Form ADT-1 is the notice a company files with the Registrar of Companies within 15 days of the meeting at which it appoints its statutory auditor. Under section 139(1) of the Companies Act, 2013, the auditor is appointed at the first annual general meeting for a term of five years, and rule 4(2) of the Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules, 2014 requires the ADT-1 filing. A new company’s first auditor is appointed by the board within 30 days of incorporation; whether ADT-1 is needed for that appointment has long been debated, and since MCA’s May 2025 form changes most practitioners file it as the cautious course.

/ Quick answers

What people search for.

  • What is the purpose of ADT-1? To inform the Registrar that an auditor has been appointed, with the auditor’s details, the period of appointment and the auditor’s written consent and eligibility certificate.
  • When is ADT-1 due? Within 15 days of the meeting at which the auditor is appointed.
  • Is ADT-1 mandatory for the first auditor? Rule 4(2) refers to appointments under section 139(1), so filing for a first auditor under section 139(6) was traditionally treated as not required. After MCA notification G.S.R. 359(E) of 30 May 2025, ICSI has asked MCA to clarify, and filing ADT-1 for the first auditor is now the safer practice.
  • How long is the auditor’s term? Five years from the first AGM (to the conclusion of the sixth AGM), then reappointment subject to rotation limits.
  • Who must rotate auditors? Listed companies, unlisted public companies with paid-up share capital of ₹10 crore or more, private companies with paid-up share capital of ₹50 crore or more, and companies with public borrowings of ₹50 crore or more: an individual auditor for one term of five years, a firm for two terms, then a five-year gap.
  • What if the auditor resigns? The auditor files ADT-3 with the company and the Registrar within 30 days, and the board fills the casual vacancy.

/ Getting it right

Common issues.

  • Consent and certificate: obtain the auditor’s written consent and certificate of eligibility under section 141 before the appointment.
  • Late filing: ADT-1 can be filed late with additional fees that rise with the delay; file promptly.
  • Term tracking: note the five-year term end and the rotation date in the compliance calendar.
  • Foreign-owned subsidiaries: group auditors often audit the Indian subsidiary; check independence and rotation limits.
  • Company secretarial support: see company secretarial services and MGT-14 for resolutions that must be filed.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is Form ADT-1?
The notice of appointment of auditor that a company files with the Registrar of Companies within 15 days of the meeting at which the auditor is appointed, under section 139(1) and rule 4(2).
Is ADT-1 mandatory for the first auditor?
Traditionally it was treated as not required, because rule 4(2) refers to appointments under section 139(1) and the first auditor is appointed under section 139(6). After MCA's May 2025 notification (G.S.R. 359(E)), ICSI has sought clarification, so filing ADT-1 for the first auditor is the safer course.
What is the due date for ADT-1?
Within 15 days of the meeting, usually the AGM, at which the auditor is appointed.
What happens if ADT-1 is not filed?
File it now, late. A late ADT-1 carries an additional fee that grows with the delay, and failing to comply with the auditor appointment rules in section 139 can lead to penalties under section 147 for the company and every officer in default. It also blocks the next step: the AOC-4 asks for the SRN of the ADT-1, so the financial statements cannot be filed cleanly until it is in place. First check what actually happened. If the auditor was appointed at the AGM and only the form was missed, a late ADT-1 with the board or AGM resolution and the auditor's consent and eligibility certificate is enough. If no auditor was ever appointed, the appointment has to be fixed first (by the board for a first auditor, or at a general meeting), and only then is ADT-1 filed. How do I check if ADT-1 was filed for my company?
The company's filing history on the MCA website lists each e-form with its SRN and date, and the auditor details shown in the company's master data come from the latest ADT-1. If the auditor shown is an old firm, or no auditor appears at all, the form was probably not filed. What is the term of a statutory auditor?
Five years from the conclusion of the first AGM to the conclusion of the sixth AGM, with reappointment subject to rotation rules for listed and larger companies.
Who files ADT-3?
The resigning auditor, with the company and the Registrar, within 30 days of resignation.
