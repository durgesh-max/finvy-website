/ Indian companies, foreign parents and their shareholders · Companies Act section 90 · Updated 2026-11-28

Significant Beneficial Owner (SBO) in India (2026): Section 90, the 10% Tests, BEN-1 Declaration, BEN-2 Filing, BEN-4 Notice and Foreign-Owned Subsidiaries, trace the chain to a real person.

Every Indian company must identify its significant beneficial owners - the individuals who ultimately hold 10% or more of its shares, votes or dividend rights, or exercise significant influence or control - and report them to the Registrar. The individual declares in BEN-1 within 30 days, and the company files BEN-2 within 30 days of receiving it. For a subsidiary of a foreign group, that means tracing ownership up through every layer to the people at the top.

/ Who is an SBO, and what is filed

Section 90 and the SBO Rules.

PointRule
SBO tests (any one)At least 10% of shares; at least 10% of voting rights; right to at least 10% of distributable dividend; significant influence or control
HoldingDirect or indirect, alone or together with others
BEN-1Declaration by the individual to the company within 30 days of becoming an SBO or of any change
BEN-2Return by the company to the Registrar within 30 days of receiving BEN-1
BEN-3Register of SBOs kept by the company
BEN-4Notice by the company to non-individual members holding 10% or more, to identify SBOs
ExemptionsCertain government holdings and specified regulated investment vehicles
  • Not just shareholders: control through agreements, board rights or voting arrangements can make someone an SBO without 10% of the shares.
  • Indirect holdings: where a company, trust, partnership or fund sits in the chain, look through it to the individuals behind it.

/ Foreign-owned subsidiaries

Where most errors happen.

  • Trace every layer: for an Indian subsidiary of a foreign company, identify the individuals who control the foreign parent, layer by layer.
  • Send BEN-4 early: the Indian company should formally ask its corporate shareholders for SBO details and record the replies.
  • Keep it consistent: SBO details should match FEMA filings (FC-GPR / FLA), bank KYC and foreign registers such as the UK PSC register.
  • Changes in the group: a restructuring, share transfer or new investor above the threshold restarts the 30-day clock.
  • Consequences: the company, its officers and the individual face penalties under section 90, and the company can seek tribunal orders restricting rights on shares where the SBO is not disclosed.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Who is a significant beneficial owner in India?
An individual who, directly or indirectly, holds at least 10% of a company's shares, voting rights or right to distributable dividend, or who exercises significant influence or control over the company.
What is the difference between BEN-1 and BEN-2?
BEN-1 is the declaration the individual gives to the company within 30 days of becoming an SBO or of any change. BEN-2 is the return the company files with the Registrar within 30 days of receiving BEN-1.
Does a subsidiary of a foreign company need to file BEN-2?
Yes, if any individual meets an SBO test through the foreign group. The Indian company must trace ownership through each layer of the foreign parent to the individuals at the top.
What is Form BEN-4?
A notice the company sends to non-individual members holding 10% or more of its shares, asking for information needed to identify significant beneficial owners.
What happens if SBO details are not filed?
The company, its officers in default and the individual face penalties under section 90, and the company can apply to the tribunal for orders restricting rights attached to the shares.
