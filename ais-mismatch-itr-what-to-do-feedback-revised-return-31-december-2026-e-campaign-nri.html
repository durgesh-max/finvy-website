AIS Mismatch With Your ITR (2026): What to Do, AIS Feedback Options, Revised Return by 31 December and Tax Department Messages, fix the return, not just the statement.

An AIS mismatch means the Annual Information Statement on the income tax portal shows income or transactions that your return does not. Banks, brokers, employers, registrars and buyers of property report to the tax department, and its systems compare that data with your ITR. A gap can lead to an SMS or email, a reduced refund, an adjustment under section 143(1) or a notice. If the AIS entry is wrong, give feedback on the portal. If your return is wrong, correct the return: for FY 2025-26 you can revise it without a fee until 31 December 2026.

/ Quick answers

What people ask after seeing a mismatch.

  • Is an AIS mismatch a notice? Not by itself. It is information the department holds. It can lead to an e-campaign message, a refund adjustment, a section 143(1) intimation or, for large gaps, scrutiny or reassessment.
  • The AIS entry is wrong. What do I do? Open AIS on the e-filing portal and submit feedback on that entry, for example that it is duplicate, belongs to another PAN or year, or is not fully correct. Keep evidence.
  • I missed income in my return. What now? For FY 2025-26, file a revised return; it is free until 31 December 2026 and allowed with a fee until 31 March 2027. For older years, an updated return (ITR-U) with additional tax may be the route.
  • Does feedback fix my return? No. Feedback corrects the information trail; your return must still show the right income.
  • I got an SMS or email about a mismatch. Log in to the e-filing portal and check the compliance section. Do not click links in messages; go to the portal directly.

/ AIS feedback options

Choose the one that matches the facts.

Feedback optionWhen to use it
Information is correctThe entry is right and is in your return
Information is not fully correctThe amount or details are partly wrong; enter the correct value
Information relates to other PAN or yearThe transaction is not yours, or belongs to another tax year
Information is duplicateThe same transaction appears twice (check records first: two similar entries can be genuine)
Information is deniedYou did not undertake the transaction at all
Income is not taxableShown for income items, for example exempt income such as NRE interest

The department may confirm your feedback with the bank, broker or other reporting source. See Form 26AS vs AIS vs TIS for how the statements fit together.

/ Common mismatches

Where the gaps usually come from.

  • Savings and deposit interest left out of the return, or reported on a cash basis while the bank reports on accrual.
  • Share and mutual fund sales where the AIS shows sale value but not cost; the return should show the gain worked out from cost.
  • Property sale: the buyer reports the sale and TDS; NRIs often miss reporting the gain or claiming the exemption.
  • NRIs: NRE interest appearing in AIS (exempt; use 'income is not taxable'), NRO interest and TDS, and income of a family member shown against your PAN.
  • Rent, dividends and foreign remittances reported by tenants, companies and banks.
  • Duplicate entries when one transaction is reported by two sources.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What should I do if my AIS does not match my ITR?
Find out which side is wrong. If the AIS entry is wrong, submit feedback on it on the e-filing portal with evidence. If your return left out income, correct the return: for FY 2025-26 a revised return is free until 31 December 2026.
What are the AIS feedback options?
Information is correct; information is not fully correct; information relates to other PAN or year; information is duplicate; information is denied; and, for income items, income is not taxable.
Does AIS feedback correct my income tax return?
No. Feedback corrects the information the department holds, and it may be checked with the reporting source. Your return must still show the correct income, so revise it if it is wrong.
Can an AIS mismatch lead to a notice?
Yes. Unresolved mismatches can lead to e-campaign messages, adjustments in a section 143(1) intimation, a reduced refund, or for large or unexplained gaps, scrutiny or reassessment.
NRE interest shows in my AIS. Is it taxable?
NRE interest is exempt for an NRI. Choose the feedback 'income is not taxable' for that entry and do not include it as taxable income in the return, but keep account statements as proof.
