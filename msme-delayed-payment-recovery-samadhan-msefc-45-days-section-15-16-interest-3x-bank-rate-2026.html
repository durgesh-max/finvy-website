/ Micro and small suppliers with dues stuck · MSMED Act sections 15 to 23 · Updated 2026-11-28

MSME Delayed Payment Recovery (2026): The 45-Day Rule, Interest at Three Times the RBI Bank Rate, MSME Samadhan and MSEFC Conciliation and Arbitration, the law is on the small supplier’s side - use it.

If you are a micro or small enterprise, a buyer must pay you within the agreed period - never more than 45 days from acceptance, or 15 days if nothing was agreed. Late payment carries compound interest with monthly rests at three times the RBI bank rate, and you can refer the dispute to the Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council through MSME Samadhan for conciliation and arbitration.

/ Your rights under the MSMED Act

Sections 15 to 23.

SectionRule
15 - payment periodAs agreed in writing, but not more than 45 days from acceptance or deemed acceptance; 15 days if no agreement
16 - interestCompound interest with monthly rests at three times the RBI bank rate, from the due date
17 - liabilityBuyer liable for the principal with that interest
18 - referenceEither party can refer to the MSEFC: conciliation first, then arbitration; award within 90 days of reference
19 - challenging an awardBuyer must deposit 75% of the amount awarded to apply to set it aside
22 - disclosureBuyers must disclose unpaid MSE dues and interest in their annual accounts
23 - taxInterest payable under section 16 is not deductible for the buyer’s income tax
  • Who benefits: micro and small suppliers - not medium enterprises. Courts have tied eligibility to Udyam registration at the time of the contract, so check your registration date.
  • Separate tax push: section 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act stops buyers deducting purchases from micro and small suppliers until they pay within the MSMED Act period.

/ Recovering your dues

From reminder to award.

  • Build the file: Udyam certificate, purchase order or contract, invoices, delivery challans, acceptance or inspection proof, ledger confirmation and correspondence.
  • Demand in writing: a notice citing sections 15 and 16, with the principal and interest computed, often prompts payment.
  • File on MSME Samadhan: the reference goes to the MSEFC of the state where the supplier is located; attend conciliation with a clear computation.
  • Arbitration and award: if conciliation fails, the Council arbitrates or refers the matter; an award is enforceable like a decree.
  • Buyer challenges: a buyer seeking to set aside the award must first deposit 75% of the amount.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is the 45-day payment rule for MSMEs?
Under section 15 of the MSMED Act, a buyer must pay a micro or small supplier by the agreed date, which cannot be more than 45 days from acceptance or deemed acceptance of the goods or services. If there is no written agreement, payment is due within 15 days.
What interest can an MSME claim on delayed payment?
Compound interest with monthly rests at three times the bank rate notified by the RBI, from the due date until payment, under section 16 of the MSMED Act.
How do I file an MSME delayed payment complaint?
File a reference on the MSME Samadhan portal to the Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council of your state, with your Udyam certificate, contract, invoices and acceptance proof. The Council tries conciliation first and then arbitration.
Can a medium enterprise use MSME Samadhan?
No. The payment protection in sections 15 to 23 is for micro and small suppliers. Courts have also linked eligibility to Udyam registration at the time of the contract.
What happens if the buyer challenges the MSEFC award?
To apply to set aside the award, the buyer must first deposit 75% of the amount awarded, which strongly favours the supplier.
