Income Tax Notice Under Section 133(6) and Summons Under Section 131 (2026): What They Mean, How They Differ From Assessment Notices, and How to Reply, answer exactly what is asked, on time.

A notice under section 133(6) is a request for information - from you or from a third party such as your bank - to verify a transaction or support an inquiry. A summons under section 131 goes further: the officer has the powers of a civil court and can require you to attend, produce books and give a statement on oath. Neither is an assessment by itself, but what you send or say becomes the record for whatever follows.

  • Why 133(6) notices come: to verify AIS or SFT entries, property purchases, cash deposits, large payments, or a counterparty’s claims about you.
  • No proceeding pending? An inquiry under 133(6) can still be made, with higher approval - so a notice does not mean an assessment has started.

/ How to reply

Precise, documented, on time.

  • Read the questions carefully: answer each point asked, with supporting documents - no more and no less.
  • Match your records: check the figures against your return, AIS, bank statements and books before you reply.
  • Reply within the deadline through the e-proceedings facility or as the notice directs; if you need more time, ask for an adjournment before the date.
  • Summons under 131: attend or seek a new date in writing; give statements carefully, as they are on oath; ask for a copy.
  • Third-party notices: a bank, employer or buyer may be asked about you - keep your own explanation ready in case an assessment follows.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is a notice under section 133(6) of the Income-tax Act?
It is a request from an income tax authority for information on specific points, sent to the taxpayer or to a third party such as a bank. It is used to verify transactions or support an inquiry and is not an assessment by itself.
How do I reply to a section 133(6) notice?
Answer each question asked, attach supporting documents, check the figures against your return, AIS and bank statements, and submit within the deadline through the e-proceedings facility or as the notice directs. Ask for more time before the deadline if needed.
What is a summons under section 131?
A summons issued using the powers of a civil court, requiring you to attend, produce books or documents and give a statement on oath. Books produced can be impounded, and keeping them beyond fifteen days, excluding holidays, needs higher approval.
What happens if I ignore a 133(6) notice or 131 summons?
Not complying can attract penalty under section 272A, and the officer may move to a best-judgment assessment or reassessment using the information already available.
Does a 133(6) notice mean my case is under scrutiny?
Not necessarily. It can be issued for an inquiry even when no proceeding is pending. But the reply forms part of the record, so it should be accurate and complete.
