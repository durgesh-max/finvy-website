/ Startups and private companies raising money · Companies Act section 42 · Updated 2026-11-28

Private Placement of Shares Under Section 42 (2026): Identified Investors, the 200-Person Limit, PAS-4, Separate Bank Account, 60-Day Allotment and PAS-3, money in, shares out, inside 60 days.

When a private company issues shares to new investors, it usually does so by private placement under section 42 of the Companies Act. The offer goes only to identified investors (not more than 200 in a year), is approved by special resolution, is made through a PAS-4 offer letter, and the money must come from the investor’s bank account into a separate bank account. Shares must be allotted within 60 days of receiving the money, and the return of allotment (PAS-3) filed within 15 days of allotment.

/ The section 42 process

Step by step.

StepRule
Identify investorsNames recorded before the invitation; not more than 200 persons in a year (excluding QIBs and ESOP employees)
ValuationIssue price supported by a registered valuer’s report
ApprovalBoard approval and a special resolution of shareholders (MGT-14 filed)
Offer letterForm PAS-4 to each identified investor; record of offers in PAS-5
MoneyFrom the investor’s own bank account, never cash, into a separate bank account; not usable before allotment
AllotmentWithin 60 days of receiving the money
If not allottedRefund within 15 days after the 60 days, or interest at 12% a year from the 60th day
Return of allotmentForm PAS-3 within 15 days of allotment
  • No public marketing: the offer cannot be advertised or marketed to the public, and it cannot be renounced to someone else.
  • Shareholder agreement: investor rights (board seats, vetoes, anti-dilution) need matching changes in the articles.

/ Foreign investors, tax and common mistakes

Where rounds get stuck.

  • Foreign investors: the price must meet FEMA pricing guidelines, the sector must permit foreign investment, and FC-GPR must be filed within 30 days of allotment.
  • Angel tax: the section 56(2)(viib) charge on premium was abolished from assessment year 2025-26, but valuation still matters for FEMA and company law.
  • Money before paperwork: receiving funds before the special resolution and PAS-4 is a common breach - sequence the steps.
  • Missed 60 days: refund with interest, or regularise with advice - do not simply allot late.
  • Convertible notes and CCPS: convertible instruments follow the same private placement route, with conversion terms fixed upfront.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is private placement under section 42?
An offer of shares or other securities by a company to a select group of identified persons, not more than 200 in a financial year (excluding QIBs and ESOP employees), made through a PAS-4 offer letter after a special resolution, without public advertisement.
What is the time limit for allotment in a private placement?
Shares must be allotted within 60 days of receiving the application money. If not, the money must be refunded within the next 15 days, failing which interest at 12% a year is payable from the 60th day.
When is PAS-3 due?
The return of allotment in Form PAS-3 must be filed with the Registrar within 15 days of allotment.
Can investors pay in cash or into the normal company account?
No. Subscription money must come from the investor's own bank account, by cheque, draft or other banking channel, into a separate bank account opened for the issue. It cannot be used until shares are allotted.
What extra steps apply to foreign investors?
The issue price must meet FEMA pricing guidelines, the sector must allow foreign investment under the automatic or approval route, and FC-GPR must be filed within 30 days of allotment.
