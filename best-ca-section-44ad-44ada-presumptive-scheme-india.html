/ Section 44AD (small business)

The core.

  • Available for: resident individual + HUF + Partnership firm (not LLP) + eligible business.
  • Turnover threshold: INR 2 crore standard; INR 3 crore if 95%+ digital receipts (post Finance Act 2024).
  • Deemed profit: 8% of gross receipts (6% for digital receipts).
  • Exempt from: tax audit under Section 44AB (section 63 of the 2025 Act), books of accounts under Section 44AA.
  • Advance tax: payable in single instalment by 15 March of FY (vs 4 instalments standard).
  • Must declare 8%/6% deemed profit; cannot declare lower without tax audit.
  • Opt-in binding for 5 years - if opt-out any year, 5-year lockout from re-opting.

/ Section 44ADA (specified professionals)

The professionals.

  • Available for: resident individual + HUF (specific professionals - not entity).
  • Covered professions: legal, medical, engineering, architectural, accountancy, technical consultancy, interior decoration, other notified professions.
  • Turnover threshold: INR 50 lakh standard; INR 75 lakh if 95%+ digital receipts.
  • Deemed profit: 50% of gross receipts.
  • Exempt from tax audit + books of accounts.
  • Advance tax: single instalment by 15 March.
  • Cannot declare lower than 50% without tax audit.

/ When presumptive vs normal regime

The decision.

  • Presumptive makes sense: Business with gross margin above deemed % (actual profit > 8%/6% for 44AD, > 50% for 44ADA). Deemed profit is cap on tax; actual higher margin isn't taxed.
  • Normal regime makes sense: Business with gross margin BELOW deemed % (actual profit lower than 8%/50%). Normal regime allows reporting actual lower profit with proper books + audit if above threshold.
  • Also consider: compliance burden (presumptive is much simpler), advance tax structure, cash flow, deductibility of specific expenses.

/ Common presumptive scheme mistakes

Where filings trip.

  • Turnover exceeded in later year: must exit presumptive + apply normal regime + tax audit if applicable.
  • Opt-out triggers 5-year lockout: cannot opt back into presumptive for 5 FYs after opt-out. Material if circumstances fluctuate.
  • 95%+ digital receipts threshold: higher INR 3 crore / INR 75 lakh thresholds apply only if 95%+ receipts digital. Cash-heavy business doesn't qualify for higher limit.
  • Declaring profit below deemed %: triggers tax audit + books of accounts obligation. Common for low-margin business - must choose: deemed % profit OR normal regime with audit.
  • Non-specified profession under 44ADA: software consultancy / management consultancy / specific tech services - borderline cases. CBDT clarifications matter.
  • Firm vs individual: 44ADA for individuals + HUF only (not firms / LLPs). 44AD covers partnership firm + individual + HUF.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What's the Section 44AD turnover threshold for 2026?
INR 2 crore standard; INR 3 crore if 95%+ receipts digital (post Finance Act 2024 enhancement). Deemed profit 8% of gross receipts (6% for digital). Available for resident individual + HUF + Partnership firm (not LLP) + eligible business.
What's the Section 44ADA turnover threshold for 2026?
INR 50 lakh standard; INR 75 lakh if 95%+ receipts digital. Deemed profit 50% of gross receipts. Available for resident individual + HUF specified professionals (legal, medical, engineering, architectural, accountancy, technical consultancy, interior decoration + notified).
Can I declare profit lower than 8% or 50%?
Yes - but triggers tax audit under Section 44AB + books of accounts under Section 44AA + advance tax in 4 instalments. Essentially exits presumptive scheme. If business genuinely has lower margin, normal regime + audit is appropriate. Just presumptive declaration below deemed % without audit is non-compliant.
Can my LLP opt into Section 44AD?
No - LLP specifically excluded. 44AD covers: resident individual + HUF + Partnership firm (not LLP). For LLP, normal regime applies + tax audit if turnover > INR 1 crore (standard INR 10 crore for 95%+ digital). Consider conversion to partnership firm if presumptive beneficial.
What's the 5-year lockout rule under 44AD?
If taxpayer opts out of presumptive scheme (declaring lower profit or using normal regime), they cannot opt back into 44AD for 5 FYs following opt-out. Section 44AD(4). Must think long-term before opt-out. Doesn't apply to 44ADA.
