/ Dividend Repatriation Playbook · Updated 2026-10-23

CA for India Dividend Repatriation to Foreign Parent (Step-by-Step), post-DDT dividend tax + treaty rate execution.

Indian subsidiary paying dividend to foreign parent: post Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT) abolition April 2020, dividend taxed in shareholder's hands via Section 195 TDS. Default 20% + surcharge + cess; 10-15% under DTAA with TRC + Form 10F. Form 15CA + 15CB mandatory. Here's the complete step-by-step execution framework.

/ Pre-dividend declaration

Preparation.

  1. Verify distributable profits: Section 123 Companies Act 2013 - dividend can only be declared out of current profit or accumulated past profit; must pass solvency test.
  2. Board resolution (final dividend requires shareholder approval; interim dividend board-only).
  3. Section 115QA buyback vs dividend decision for large repatriation.
  4. Specific Section 180 Companies Act compliance if dividend > specific threshold.
  5. Transfer Pricing + arm's-length verification for related-party context.
  6. Beneficial ownership of foreign parent + Press Note 3 compliance.

/ Foreign parent documents

What to collect.

  • TRC (Tax Residency Certificate): from foreign parent's home country tax authority - annual.
  • Form 10F: electronic filing on India e-filing portal.
  • No-PE declaration: foreign parent confirms no Permanent Establishment in India.
  • Foreign PAN: if available (not strictly required; Section 206AA 20% applies if missing).
  • Beneficial ownership declaration: anti-GAAR / PPT compliance.

/ Form 15CA + 15CB execution

The CA certification.

  1. Form 15CA Part C (standard for dividend above INR 5 lakh): e-filed on Indian income tax portal by Indian subsidiary.
  2. Form 15CB: Chartered Accountant certificate stating: chargeability under Section 195, DTAA applicable, treaty rate, TDS deducted.
  3. CA (ICAI member) signs with UDIN.
  4. Treaty rate verification: country-specific DTAA + beneficial ownership + PPT.
  5. Standard DTAA dividend rates:
  6.   — USA: 15% (25% ownership) / 25% (otherwise).
  7.   — UK: 10% (10% ownership) / 15% (otherwise).
  8.   — Singapore: 10% (25% ownership) / 15% (otherwise).
  9.   — UAE: 10%.
  10.   — Mauritius: 5% (10% ownership) / 15% (otherwise).
  11.   — Netherlands / Germany / France: 10%.

/ TDS deposit + filing + remittance

The execution.

  1. TDS deposit by 7th of next month via Challan ITNS 281 under Section 195.
  2. Form 27Q quarterly return by 31 July / 31 October / 31 January / 31 May.
  3. Form 16A issued to foreign payee within quarterly timeline.
  4. Remittance via AD-I category bank: Form A2 + Form 15CA acknowledgement + Form 15CB attached.
  5. Bank releases SWIFT after documents verified.
  6. Foreign parent receives dividend net of TDS.
  7. Foreign parent claims FTC in home country tax return for Indian TDS paid.

/ Common dividend repatriation mistakes

Where companies trip.

  • Section 206AA 20% rate when foreign parent PAN missing + TRC/Form 10F also missing.
  • Over-withholding at 20% default when treaty rate (10-15%) applies with proper documentation.
  • Section 194 (resident dividend TDS) confused with Section 195 (non-resident). Different rates + specific sections.
  • Form 10F expired - TRC + Form 10F must be current for FY of dividend.
  • Section 194 10% TDS on dividend to Indian shareholder confused with Section 195 for non-resident shareholder.
  • Form MGT-14 if specific threshold crossed - specific resolution filing.
  • Specific Section 123 Companies Act compliance missed - solvency + distributable profit verification.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Is dividend from Indian subsidiary to foreign parent taxable in India?
Yes - post DDT abolition April 2020, dividend taxable in foreign parent's hands. Section 195 TDS at 20% default + 10-15% treaty rate under DTAA with TRC + Form 10F. Indian subsidiary deducts TDS + deposits + Form 27Q + Form 16A to foreign parent.
What's the typical DTAA dividend rate?
Varies by country: USA 15% (25% ownership) / 25% otherwise; UK 10% (10% ownership) / 15% otherwise; Singapore 10% (25% ownership) / 15% otherwise; UAE 10%; Mauritius 5% (10% ownership) / 15% otherwise; Netherlands/Germany/France 10%. TRC + Form 10F + beneficial ownership required to claim treaty rate.
Can we use buyback instead of dividend?
Yes - alternative for large one-time repatriation. Section 115QA buyback tax 20% + surcharge on distributed income paid by Indian subsidiary. Receipt in parent's hands = capital gain but Section 10(34A) exempts buyback-related income. Materially different cash flow + tax position. Max 10-25% buyback per Companies Act + 2-year gap between buybacks.
What if TRC/Form 10F is missing?
Section 206AA + Section 195 default: 20% + applicable surcharge + 4% cess. Treaty rate denied. Foreign parent can later claim refund via Indian ITR but immediate TDS at higher rate. Best practice: TRC + Form 10F obtained before dividend declaration.
