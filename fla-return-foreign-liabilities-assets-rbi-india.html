FLA Return, RBI's annual foreign-investment filing.

FLA (Foreign Liabilities and Assets) Return is an annual mandatory filing to the RBI under FEMA by every Indian company, LLP, or AIF that has received foreign direct investment (FDI) OR made outbound investment (ODI) in any prior financial year. Due 15 July each year. Non-compliance can trigger FEMA compounding + penalty. Many Indian companies with foreign shareholders miss this filing. Here's the practitioner's guide.

/ Who must file FLA Return

The scope.

FLA Return is mandatory under FEMA Master Direction on Reporting under Foreign Exchange Management Act, for every:

  • Indian company that has received FDI (inbound foreign equity investment) in the current or any previous FY AND still has outstanding foreign liability (foreign-shareholder balance) at the end of the FY.
  • Indian company that has made ODI (outbound overseas investment) in the current or any previous FY AND still has outstanding foreign asset at the end of the FY.
  • LLP with foreign capital contribution.
  • AIF, Partnership firm, Public Private Partnership with foreign liabilities / assets.

Even if there is no fresh transaction in the current FY, if the opening balance of foreign liability / asset exists, FLA Return is mandatory.

Exempt (no FLA Return required):

  • Companies with no foreign shareholder and no overseas investment.
  • Companies where all foreign shareholding has been fully exited (nil opening balance for the FY).
  • Non-reporting companies specifically identified by RBI.

/ Due date + filing route

15 July via FLAIR portal.

Due date: on or before 15 July of the year following the FY. For FY 2024-25, due by 15 July 2025. For FY 2025-26, due by 15 July 2026.

If audited financial statements are not available by 15 July, the FLA Return can be filed with provisional (unaudited) numbers — a revised FLA Return with audited numbers must then be filed within 30 days of signing of audited financials.

Filing route:

  1. FLA Return filed on the FLAIR portal (RBI's Foreign Liabilities and Assets Information Reporting system) at flair.rbi.org.in.
  2. First-time users register on the portal with company's PAN + entity type + authorised person details.
  3. Fill the online FLA Return form with company's shareholding pattern, foreign shareholders' details, outbound investments, equity + reserves + surplus, borrowing, assets.
  4. Attach supporting documents: audited balance sheet (or provisional), auditor's report, share capital register.
  5. Submit with DSC of the authorised signatory.
  6. Download acknowledgement.

/ Information required in FLA Return

What RBI wants.

Core sections of FLA Return:

  • Section 1: identification details (PAN, CIN, incorporation date, main activity, SBI RRN if applicable).
  • Section 2: equity position — paid-up capital, foreign shareholder equity breakdown, resident shareholder equity. End-of-FY numbers.
  • Section 3: foreign direct investment inward — foreign shareholder-wise paid-up equity, country of residence, breakup of equity, preference shares, convertible instruments.
  • Section 4: outbound direct investment — details of overseas subsidiaries / JVs with equity + loans given.
  • Section 5: other foreign liabilities — External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs), trade credit, foreign bank loans.
  • Section 6: foreign assets — overseas bank accounts, foreign securities, loans given to foreign parties.
  • Section 7: income & dividends — dividend paid to foreign shareholders in FY, dividend received from overseas subsidiaries.

/ Non-compliance consequences

What breaks.

  • FEMA compounding: non-filing is a FEMA breach under Section 13. Compounding application can be filed voluntarily to pay reduced penalty (approximately INR 10,000-50,000 per year of non-filing, scaling with company size).
  • Compounding + penalty: for persistent non-filing, RBI can impose compounding fee + penalty up to 3 times the amount involved.
  • Operational: AD banks may hesitate to process future foreign remittances (inward FDI or outward ODI dividend repatriation) if FLA Return history is non-compliant.
  • Diligence red flag: in M&A diligence, future FDI, future ODI, auditor review — FLA Return non-compliance surfaces and must be fixed.

Clean-up: file all missed FLA Returns for prior years (if the data can be reconstructed), then apply for compounding on FEMA breaches. Typical compounding timeline: 90-180 days from application to RBI order.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is FLA Return?
Foreign Liabilities and Assets Return - annual filing to RBI under FEMA by Indian companies / LLPs / AIFs with foreign direct investment (inward FDI) or outbound overseas investment (ODI). Reports foreign shareholder equity, dividend paid to foreign shareholders, overseas subsidiary investments, ECBs, foreign assets. Due 15 July each year via FLAIR portal (flair.rbi.org.in).
Who is required to file FLA Return?
Every Indian company / LLP / AIF with (a) foreign shareholder equity outstanding at end of FY, OR (b) outbound overseas investment outstanding at end of FY. Even companies with no fresh transaction in the current FY must file if the opening balance of foreign liability / asset exists. Companies with no foreign shareholder and no outbound investment are exempt.
What is the FLA Return due date for FY 2025-26?
15 July 2026 for FY 2025-26 (FY 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026). Can be filed with provisional (unaudited) financials if audited not available by 15 July; revised FLA Return with audited numbers must then be filed within 30 days of audit completion.
Where do I file FLA Return?
FLAIR portal (Foreign Liabilities and Assets Information Reporting) at flair.rbi.org.in. First-time users register with PAN + entity type + authorised person details. Fill online form, attach audited (or provisional) financials, submit with DSC. Download acknowledgement.
What happens if I miss FLA Return?
FEMA breach under Section 13. Voluntary compounding application reduces penalty - approximately INR 10,000-50,000 per year of non-filing. Persistent non-filing can attract higher penalty up to 3 times the amount involved. Also blocks AD banks from processing future foreign remittances. Clean up: file all missed years + apply compounding.
