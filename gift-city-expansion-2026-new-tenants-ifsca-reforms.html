/ GIFT City Status · Updated 2026-10-22

GIFT City 2026, new tenants + IFSCA reforms.

GIFT City (Gujarat International Finance Tec-City) IFSC has grown substantially since 2022-2023 initial scaling. As of October 2026: 500+ IFSC entities (AIFs, banks, insurance, broking, reinsurance), several hundred-billion dollar AUM, new product categories operational via IFSCA regulations. Here's the current state + what new foreign + Indian financial-sector entrants should know.

/ Current state in Q4 2026

The ecosystem.

  • 500+ IFSC entities operational: alternative investment funds, foreign bank branches, insurance intermediaries, broker-dealers, re-insurers, finance companies, aircraft leasing, ship leasing, bullion exchange.
  • Several hundred billion USD AUM across IFSC AIFs + banks + related products.
  • Resident entity cost base: office space + manpower materially cheaper than Mumbai / Bengaluru / Singapore equivalent. Tax regime (Section 10(4D)) adds further advantage.
  • Infrastructure: fully operational since early 2020s; now mature.
  • Regulatory stability: IFSCA (International Financial Services Centres Authority) established 2020; now mature with specific regulations for each product.

/ Key IFSCA regulatory reforms 2024-2026

What's changed.

  • Expanded AIF framework: new fund categories permitted; retail AIF discussions; INR-INR AIF framework (previously limited to foreign currency denomination).
  • Family office framework: specific IFSCA rules for family offices operating from GIFT City.
  • IBU (IFSC Banking Unit) expansion: more Indian + foreign banks setting up IBU - cross-border lending, treasury, trade finance.
  • Insurance + re-insurance: expanded IRDAI-IFSCA framework for cross-border insurance; Indian re-insurer and foreign re-insurer presence.
  • Fintech sandbox: specific IFSCA fintech sandbox for cross-border fintech experimentation.
  • Aircraft + ship leasing: dedicated regulatory framework; several lessors operational.
  • Bullion Exchange: IIBX operational; foreign bullion inflows + Indian jewellery sector integration.
  • Ancillary services: expanded scope for law firm presence, accounting / consulting firm presence, R&D centre presence.

/ Tax regime maturity

Section 10(4D) + related.

  • Section 10(4D): specified income of specified fund (including Category III AIF) in IFSC - exempt. Covers capital gain, dividend, interest - subject to conditions.
  • Section 115U / 115UA: pass-through for Category I + II AIFs.
  • Dividend from Indian company: specific carve-outs for IFSC fund.
  • GST: specific exemptions for IFSC services; co-ordinated with CGST Act.
  • Stamp duty: Gujarat state exemption for IFSC entities on specific instruments.
  • BEPS Pillar 2 interaction: Section 10(4D) exemption creates low Pillar 2 ETR risk for in-scope groups; QDMTT top-up may apply. See Pillar 2 guide.

/ For foreign + Indian entrants

Decision factors.

  • Foreign asset manager: IFSC AIF structure provides LP-friendly tax regime + regulatory arbitrage vs Mumbai AIF.
  • Indian family office: specific IFSCA family office framework + cross-border investment flexibility.
  • Indian reinsurer: cross-border insurance via IFSC with specific regulatory + tax regime.
  • Foreign fintech: fintech sandbox for cross-border experimentation + eventual full IFSC licence.
  • Aircraft lessor: dedicated regulatory framework + Indian + foreign airline customer base.
  • Bullion dealer: IIBX membership + import channel optimisation.
  • Service provider (law / consulting / audit): ancillary services licence to serve IFSC entities.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What's the current state of GIFT City IFSC in October 2026?
Mature ecosystem - 500+ IFSC entities operational across AIFs, foreign banks, insurance, broker-dealers, reinsurance, bullion exchange, aircraft/ship leasing. Several hundred billion USD AUM. IFSCA regulatory framework mature with specific rules for each product category. Resident entity cost base + tax regime (Section 10(4D)) remains advantageous vs Mumbai / Bengaluru / Singapore equivalents.
What recent IFSCA reforms affect foreign entrants?
Expanded AIF framework (new fund categories + INR denomination), family office framework, IBU (IFSC Banking Unit) expansion for Indian + foreign banks, insurance + reinsurance expansion, fintech sandbox for cross-border experimentation, aircraft + ship leasing frameworks, Bullion Exchange (IIBX) operational, ancillary services (law + consulting) expanded scope.
How does Section 10(4D) interact with BEPS Pillar 2?
Section 10(4D) provides tax exemption for specified income of IFSC fund - resulting in low Pillar 2 ETR for groups using IFSC. For in-scope MNC groups (consolidated revenue EUR 750M+), QDMTT (Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax) may apply to raise Indian ETR to 15% minimum. Impact: GIFT City tax advantage preserved for small/mid groups; large groups face Pillar 2 top-up.
Can a foreign asset manager run an IFSC AIF without Indian presence?
No - IFSC AIF must have Indian-incorporated AMC / Investment Manager + Indian trustee + Indian fund as the SPV. Foreign asset manager typically owns 100% of Indian AMC (which is IFSC-registered). Combined with offshore feeder fund (Mauritius / Singapore) for international LPs, full cross-border structure operational.
What's the fastest way to enter GIFT City for a foreign entity?
