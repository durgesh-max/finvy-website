GST DRC-01C and DRC-01B Notice Reply (2026): ITC Mismatch (GSTR-2B vs GSTR-3B) and Liability Mismatch (GSTR-1 vs GSTR-3B) - Rules 88D and 88C, Part B Reply, Seven-Day Window, reconcile, then pay or explain in seven days.

DRC-01C and DRC-01B are system-generated intimations on the GST portal, not show cause notices: DRC-01C flags input tax credit claimed in GSTR-3B that is higher than the credit shown in GSTR-2B, and DRC-01B flags output tax shown in GSTR-1 that is higher than the tax paid through GSTR-3B. You have seven days to either pay the difference with interest through DRC-03 or explain it in Part B of the form. Ignoring them can block your next GSTR-1 and usually leads to a proper notice later, so a quick, reconciled reply matters.

/ The two intimations

What each one means.

FormRuleWhat it flagsCommon genuine reasons
DRC-01CRule 88DITC claimed in GSTR-3B is more than ITC available in GSTR-2B beyond the prescribed limitSupplier filed GSTR-1 late, import IGST, ITC of an earlier period claimed now, ISD credit, timing of the 2B cut-off
DRC-01BRule 88CTax liability in GSTR-1 / IFF is more than tax paid in GSTR-3B beyond the prescribed limitInvoice reported in GSTR-1 but tax paid in a later 3B, amendments, advances adjusted, typing errors in GSTR-1

/ How to respond

Within seven days, on the portal.

  • Reconcile first: compare GSTR-3B with GSTR-2B (for DRC-01C) or GSTR-1 (for DRC-01B) invoice by invoice for the months shown, and work out how much is a timing difference and how much is a real excess.
  • If the excess is real: pay it with interest under section 50 through DRC-03 and quote the DRC-03 reference in Part B.
  • If it is explained: give the reason in Part B - for example supplier filed late and the credit appears in a later GSTR-2B, IGST on imports, or tax paid in the next month's GSTR-3B - and keep the reconciliation on file.
  • Part payment and part explanation are allowed; the reply must cover the full difference.
  • Find the form in the notices and intimations area of your GST portal login and the registered email; the reply is filed against the same reference number.

/ If you ignore it

Why a reply matters.

  • The portal can stop you filing GSTR-1 for the next period until you pay or reply, which delays your customers' credit.
  • The difference stays on record and is a common starting point for a scrutiny notice (ASMT-10) or a demand notice (DRC-01) later.
  • A short, reconciled Part B reply now is far easier to defend than a demand two years later.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is a DRC-01C notice in GST?
A system-generated intimation under rule 88D that the input tax credit you claimed in GSTR-3B is higher than the credit available in your GSTR-2B by more than the prescribed limit. It is not a show cause notice, but you must pay the excess or explain it within seven days.
How do I reply to a DRC-01C ITC mismatch notice?
Reconcile GSTR-3B with GSTR-2B for the months shown. Pay any genuine excess with interest through DRC-03 and quote it in Part B, and explain the rest in Part B - for example supplier filed late and the credit appears in a later GSTR-2B, IGST on imports, or ITC of an earlier period. Reply within seven days on the GST portal.
What is DRC-01B?
An intimation under rule 88C that the tax liability you reported in GSTR-1 (or IFF) is higher than the tax you paid through GSTR-3B by more than the prescribed limit. Pay the shortfall with interest through DRC-03 or explain the difference in Part B within seven days.
What happens if I do not reply to DRC-01C or DRC-01B?
The portal can block your next GSTR-1 until you pay or reply, and the unexplained difference often leads to a scrutiny notice (ASMT-10) or a demand notice (DRC-01) later.
Can I explain part of the difference and pay the rest?
Yes. Part B lets you show an amount paid through DRC-03 and give reasons for the remaining difference; the reply should account for the whole amount in the intimation.
