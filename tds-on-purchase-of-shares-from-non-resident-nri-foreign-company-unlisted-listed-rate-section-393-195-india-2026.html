TDS on Purchase of Shares From a Non-Resident (2026): Rates for Unlisted and Listed Shares, Who Deducts, Treaty Relief and the Lower Deduction Certificate, what the buyer must withhold before paying an NRI or foreign seller.

If you buy shares of an Indian company from a non-resident, you must deduct Indian tax from the price before you pay. This applies whether the seller is an NRI or a foreign company, and whether the buyer is in India or abroad. The tax is on the seller’s capital gain: 12.5% if unlisted shares were held for more than 24 months, and the normal rate if held for less (up to 30% for an individual, 35% for a foreign company), plus surcharge and 4% cess. If the gain is not worked out and certified, the tax office can expect deduction on the whole price. The fixes are a Chartered Accountant’s computation of the gain, a lower or nil deduction certificate (Form 128), or a tax treaty claim with a tax residency certificate. A buyer who does not deduct can be made to pay the tax, interest and a penalty. On-market sales of listed shares are different: the NRI’s bank or broker deducts the tax.

/ Step by step

What the buyer does.

  • 1. Confirm the seller is non-resident for tax, and get PAN, tax residency certificate and Form 41 (formerly Form 10F) if a treaty is claimed.
  • 2. Work out the gain: sale price less cost, with the holding period. For a non-resident who bought unlisted shares in foreign currency, the computation rules differ from a resident’s; have a CA certify it.
  • 3. Decide the amount to deduct: tax on the certified gain, or on the figure in a lower or nil deduction certificate. See Form 128, formerly Form 13.
  • 4. Get a TAN if you do not have one. A foreign buyer needs one too. The no-TAN route that began on 1 October 2026 covers only property bought by individuals and HUFs.
  • 5. Deduct, deposit and report: deduct at payment, deposit the tax by the 7th of the next month, file the quarterly statement in Form 144 (formerly 27Q) and give the seller the TDS certificate.
  • 6. FEMA side: price within the fair-value rule, Form FC-TRS within 60 days, and Form 145 and 146 for the bank if money is remitted abroad. See transferring shares between residents and non-residents.

/ Practical points

Where deals go wrong.

  • Non-resident buyer, non-resident seller: the duty to deduct still applies when the shares are of an Indian company. A foreign buyer needs a PAN and TAN in India for this.
  • Treaty relief: sellers in Mauritius, Singapore or Cyprus keep exemption only for shares acquired before 1 April 2017, and must pass the treaty’s anti-abuse tests. The US and UK treaties let India tax the gain. A few treaties, such as the Netherlands, still exempt some share sales. Get this checked before agreeing to deduct nothing.
  • Fair value matters twice: if unlisted shares are sold below fair value under the tax rules, the seller is taxed as if the fair value was received and the buyer can be taxed on the discount. FEMA sets its own price limits. See FEMA valuation reports.
  • Escrow, earn-outs and deferred payments: tax is deducted when each amount is paid or credited, so the share purchase agreement should say how.
  • Indemnity is not a substitute: the tax office recovers from the buyer first. A tax indemnity from a seller who has left India is hard to enforce.
  • Seller’s refund: if too much is deducted, the seller recovers it by filing an Indian return. That takes months, which is why a lower deduction certificate before closing is worth the effort.
  • Selling the foreign holding company instead: a sale of an overseas company can also be taxed in India if most of its value comes from Indian assets. Ask before assuming an offshore deal is outside India.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Is TDS applicable on purchase of shares from a non-resident?
Yes. A buyer of shares of an Indian company from a non-resident must deduct Indian tax on the seller's capital gain before paying, under section 393 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (formerly section 195). On-market sales of listed shares by NRIs are handled by the NRI's bank or broker.
What is the TDS rate on sale of unlisted shares by a non-resident?
12.5% on long-term gains (shares held more than 24 months) and normal rates on short-term gains, up to 30% for an individual and 35% for a foreign company, plus surcharge and 4% cess. A treaty may reduce this.
Is TDS deducted on the full sale price or only on the gain?
Tax is due on the capital gain. To deduct on the gain only, the buyer needs a reliable computation, normally certified by a Chartered Accountant, or a lower or nil deduction certificate in Form 128. Without either, the tax office can expect deduction on the whole payment.
Does a foreign buyer have to deduct TDS when buying Indian shares from another foreign seller?
Yes. The duty applies to any person paying a non-resident a sum taxable in India, including a non-resident buyer. The buyer needs an Indian PAN and TAN to deposit the tax.
Can a tax treaty remove the TDS?
Sometimes. Sellers in Mauritius, Singapore and Cyprus are exempt only for shares acquired before 1 April 2017 and must meet anti-abuse tests; the US and UK treaties allow India to tax the gain. The seller must give a tax residency certificate and Form 41.
What happens if the buyer does not deduct TDS?
The buyer can be treated as in default and asked to pay the tax, with interest and a penalty, even though the gain was the seller's.
