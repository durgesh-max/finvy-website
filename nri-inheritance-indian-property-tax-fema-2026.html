NRI inheriting Indian property, tax + FEMA + mutation in one guide.

NRI inheriting immovable property / Indian financial assets / Indian bank balance from Indian parents or relatives — the sequence of actions, tax position, FEMA treatment, mutation / succession formalities, and eventual sale / repatriation. India has no inheritance tax, but mishandled succession formalities can block sale or repatriation for years. Here's the complete framework.

/ Tax treatment of inheritance

No inheritance tax.

  • India abolished estate duty in 1985. No inheritance tax / estate tax on Indian assets.
  • Section 56(2)(x) specifically exempts gifts under will or by way of inheritance — inherited property is not treated as 'Income from Other Sources' in NRI's hands.
  • No immediate tax liability at the time of inheritance. The property / asset moves into NRI's hands at its historical cost (for later capital gains purposes under Section 49(1): cost = cost in previous owner's hands + period held by previous owner counted for holding-period calculation).
  • Income from inherited property is taxable in NRI's hands post-inheritance: rental income, interest on inherited FD, dividend on inherited shares.
  • US estate tax: if NRI is US tax person, inherited Indian assets may be included in US estate for US estate tax purposes on NRI's own death — separate planning needed. See our US estate tax guide.

/ Mutation + legal transfer

What NRI must do.

  1. Death certificate: obtain from Municipal Corporation / Panchayat.
  2. If deceased left a Will (testamentary succession):
  3.   — Original Will + witness confirmation.
  4.   — Probate from Indian High Court if (a) deceased was Christian / Parsi / Jew, or (b) immovable property in Mumbai / Chennai / Kolkata (mandatory jurisdictional probate requirement). Elsewhere, probate optional for Hindu / Muslim / Sikh / Jain testators but strengthens title.
  5.   — Timeline: 6-24 months for probate.
  6. If no Will (intestate succession):
  7.   — Hindu Succession Act 1956 for Hindu / Buddhist / Jain / Sikh.
  8.   — Indian Succession Act 1925 for Christian / Parsi / Jew.
  9.   — Muslim Personal Law for Muslim.
  10.   — Succession Certificate from District Court (for movable assets — bank balance, shares).
  11.   — Legal Heir Certificate from Revenue Authority (for immovable property mutation).
  12.   — Timeline: 6-18 months.
  13. Mutation (immovable property): application to Municipal Corporation / Revenue Department with death certificate + Will / Succession Certificate / Legal Heir Certificate + NOC from co-heirs + property documents. Mutation updates municipal records to NRI's name.
  14. Bank / demat / MF transfer: with Succession Certificate + ID documents.
  15. NRO account: NRI should have NRO account to hold inherited Indian funds. Operations limited to local use + repatriation under FEMA.

/ FEMA + repatriation

Taking inherited money out of India.

  • Inherited property / funds held in NRO account: fully permissible under FEMA.
  • Repatriation of inherited funds abroad: NRI can repatriate up to USD 1 million per FY (combined limit for all remittances from NRO / inheritance / sale proceeds) under FEMA Current Account Rules + Schedule III. Requires Form A2 declaration + Form 15CA + 15CB (CA certificate).
  • Documents for repatriation: proof of inheritance (Will / probate / succession certificate), source documentation (bank statements showing credit), tax clearance (ITR / assessment order where income involved).
  • Beyond USD 1M/year: requires RBI prior approval under FEMA Current Account Rules.
  • Inherited immovable property: can be held by NRI indefinitely. Sale proceeds repatriable up to USD 1M/year.
  • Agricultural land / plantation / farmhouse: NRI can INHERIT but cannot BUY. Can hold inherited agricultural land but with restrictions on sale (sale only to Indian resident).

/ Sale of inherited property + capital gains

Later disposal.

  • Capital gain calculation: sale price - indexed cost (indexation from original acquisition date by previous owner per Section 49(1) + Section 48).
  • Holding period: period held by previous owner + period held by NRI (combined). If combined > 24 months (property), gain is Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG).
  • LTCG tax rate: Finance Act 2024 changed - 12.5% without indexation OR 20% with indexation (whichever lower) for property acquired before 23 July 2024; 12.5% without indexation for property acquired after 23 July 2024.
  • STCG: at slab rate.
  • TDS under Section 195: buyer deducts 20% LTCG / 30% STCG from sale proceeds. Lower Deduction Certificate (LDC) available under Section 197. See our LDC guide.
  • Section 54 reinvestment exemption: if NRI reinvests LTCG in another residential property in India within 1 year back / 2 years forward (purchase) / 3 years forward (construction), LTCG exempt subject to limits.
  • Section 54EC bonds: INR 50 lakh in REC / NHAI / PFC / IRFC bonds within 6 months.
  • Section 54F: full LTCG exemption on sale of any long-term asset (not residential property) if NRI invests net consideration in one residential property in India.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Is there inheritance tax on Indian property for NRI heir?
No - India abolished estate duty in 1985 and has no inheritance tax. Section 56(2)(x) Income Tax Act specifically exempts inheritance from being treated as 'Income from Other Sources'. NRI inheriting Indian property faces no immediate tax liability at inheritance; only future income from the property (rent, interest, dividend) + capital gains on later sale are taxable.
How does an NRI legally transfer inherited Indian property to their name?
Depends on whether a Will exists. With Will: obtain probate from Indian High Court (mandatory for Mumbai/Chennai/Kolkata immovable; optional elsewhere for Hindu/Muslim/Sikh/Jain). Without Will: Succession Certificate from District Court (for movable) + Legal Heir Certificate from Revenue Authority (for immovable mutation). Timeline: 6-24 months. Then mutation with Municipal Corporation/Revenue Department updates records to NRI's name.
Can an NRI repatriate inherited Indian funds abroad?
Yes - up to USD 1 million per FY (combined limit for all remittances from NRO account / inheritance / property sale proceeds) under FEMA Current Account Rules + Schedule III. Requires Form A2 declaration + Form 15CA + 15CB (CA certificate). Beyond USD 1M/year requires RBI prior approval. Inheritance documentation (Will/probate/succession certificate) must support the repatriation.
Can an NRI inherit agricultural land in India?
Yes - NRI can inherit agricultural land/plantation/farmhouse. Cannot BUY new agricultural land (FEMA restriction). Can hold inherited agricultural land but sale restricted to Indian resident buyer (cannot sell to another NRI). This is a specific FEMA restriction - plan the sale accordingly.
What's the capital gain tax when NRI later sells inherited property?
Under Section 49(1) + Section 48: cost = cost in previous owner's hands (indexed if applicable). Holding period = combined (previous owner + NRI). If > 24 months: LTCG. Post Finance Act 2024, LTCG rate: 12.5% without indexation OR 20% with indexation (whichever lower) for property acquired before 23 July 2024; 12.5% without indexation thereafter. Section 54 / 54F / 54EC reinvestment exemptions available.
