Startup India Complete Guide 2026

Everything Indian startup founders need to know about Startup India — DPIIT recognition, tax exemptions (Section 80-IAC), the end of angel tax, Seed Fund Scheme, government tenders, IPR rebates. Updated for 2026.

DPIIT Recognition

Get DPIIT recognition.

DPIIT (Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade) recognises eligible startups under Startup India. Free, quick, and unlocks multiple benefits.

  • Eligibility: incorporated less than 10 years ago; turnover up to ₹200 crore in any financial year under the February 2026 rules; entity is Pvt Ltd / LLP / Partnership
  • Application: online at startupindia.gov.in (free)
  • Documents: incorporation certificate, brief about business, intellectual property if any
  • Processing: 2-7 days
  • Validity: until 10 years from incorporation or turnover above ₹200cr, whichever is earlier (20 years and ₹300cr for deep tech, February 2026 rules)

Section 80-IAC tax exemption

Income tax exemption.

Eligible DPIIT-recognised startups get 100% income tax exemption on profits for 3 consecutive years (out of first 10 years).

  • Eligibility: DPIIT-recognised + Inter-Ministerial Board approval
  • Exemption: 100% on profits for 3 of first 10 FYs
  • Choice of years: founder selects which 3 years
  • Apply: online via Income Tax Department
  • Conditions: must be Pvt Ltd or LLP; turnover < ₹100cr in any of 3 FYs claimed

Angel tax (abolished)

Section 56(2)(viib) abolished.

Angel tax used to tax share premium above fair value; DPIIT-recognised startups could be exempted under conditions. It was abolished for all companies from AY 2025-26 (FY 2024-25), so it matters now only for rounds raised before that.

  • Section 56(2)(viib) (abolished from FY 2024-25): if share premium > FMV, excess taxed as 'income from other sources'
  • DPIIT-recognised startups exempt if conditions met
  • Conditions: paid-up capital + share premium ≤ ₹25cr (post-issue); investor disclosure

Startup India Seed Fund Scheme

Government seed funding.

Direct seed funding scheme by DPIIT for early-stage startups.

  • Total corpus: ₹945cr deployed across 300+ incubators
  • Per-startup: up to ₹20L grant for proof-of-concept; up to ₹50L convertible debenture for market entry
  • Eligibility: DPIIT-recognised; incorporated < 2 years
  • Apply via DPIIT-listed incubators

IPR benefits

IPR fast-track.

DPIIT-recognised startups get patent fee rebates and expedited examination.

  • 80% rebate on patent filing fees
  • 50% rebate on trademark filing
  • Expedited patent examination
  • Free assistance from empanelled facilitators

Public procurement

Government contracts.

DPIIT-recognised startups get easier access to government tenders.

  • Exemption from prior-experience and prior-turnover criteria
  • Listed on GeM (Government e-Marketplace)
  • Self-certification under labour and environmental laws

Startup tax holiday: 3 years out of 10 An eligible company or LLP incorporated by 31 March 2030 pays no tax on business profits for any 3 consecutive years out of its first 10, with an Inter-Ministerial Board certificate. The 22% regime and MAT can wipe out the benefit: startup tax holiday (80-IAC, now section 140).
Founders: salary or dividend? Salary is deductible for the company and taxed once; a dividend comes out of profit already taxed at about 25% and is taxed again. Loans from your own company can be taxed as a deemed dividend: founder salary vs dividend.
Converting your LLP into a company? It is tax-free only if partners get shares in proportion to capital accounts and nothing else, and keep 50% of the votes for five years. A funding round can break it: LLP to private limited conversion.
Giving shares for work or IP? Sweat equity is taxed as salary when allotted, though eligible startups can defer the tax for up to 48 months. Limits: 15% a year, 25% in total (50% for DPIIT startups), three-year lock-in: sweat equity shares: rules and tax.
Raising from an investor outside India? Valuation floor, money into the company account, allotment within 60 days, Form FC-GPR within 30 days; NRIs investing on a non-repatriation basis count as domestic: raising from a foreign or NRI investor, step by step.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is Startup India?
Government initiative launched 2016 to promote startups via DPIIT recognition, tax exemptions, IPR fast-tracking, and Seed Fund Scheme.
Who is eligible for DPIIT recognition?
Indian Pvt Ltd / LLP / Partnership; incorporated < 10 years; turnover up to ₹200cr in any FY (₹300cr and 20 years for deep tech, February 2026 rules); working on innovation/scalability/employment.
How long does DPIIT recognition take?
2-7 days from online application at startupindia.gov.in. Free, no professional fees needed.
What is Section 80-IAC?
100% income tax exemption on profits for 3 consecutive FYs (out of first 10) for DPIIT-recognised startups approved by Inter-Ministerial Board.
What is angel tax?
Abolished from FY 2024-25 by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024. It was a tax under Section 56(2)(viib) — if share premium received from resident investor exceeds Fair Market Value, the excess is taxed as 'income from other sources'. DPIIT-recognised startups could be exempted before it was abolished; it now matters only for older rounds.
How much money does Startup India Seed Fund give?
Up to ₹20L grant for proof-of-concept and up to ₹50L as debt or convertible debentures for market entry, routed through selected incubators to young DPIIT-recognised startups. The Seed Fund portal closed to new applications on 31 May 2026; check seedfund.startupindia.gov.in for any reopening, and look at state startup grants and the Fund of Funds-backed VC funds meanwhile.
Raising your first money? Looking at the Startup India Seed Fund? Up to Rs 20 lakh grant and Rs 50 lakh debt via incubators, but the 2026 call closed in May: SISFS 2026 status and alternatives.
