Startup Funding Stages in India (2026): Pre-Seed, Seed, Series A and Beyond, Government Funding Schemes, Instruments and the Compliance Each Round Needs, each round brings new paperwork, not just new money.

Indian startups usually raise money in stages: friends and family or pre-seed, a seed round from angels and seed funds, then Series A and later rounds from venture capital and growth funds. Alongside private money, founders can use government routes such as the Startup India Seed Fund (through incubators), funds backed by the ₹10,000 crore Fund of Funds 2.0, credit guarantees and state schemes. Every round also needs company-law and, for foreign investors, FEMA filings, which is where most founders need a CA.

/ Government funding schemes

Non-dilutive and fund-of-funds routes.

  • Startup India Seed Fund Scheme: grants and debt for early DPIIT startups through selected incubators; the 2026 call closed in May 2026 per published guides; see SISFS 2026 status.
  • Fund of Funds 2.0: a ₹10,000 crore corpus notified in April 2026 that invests through SEBI-registered AIFs, so startups raise from those funds.
  • Credit Guarantee Scheme for Startups: collateral-free loans from lenders backed by a government guarantee.
  • Tax holiday: eligible DPIIT startups can apply for section 80-IAC; start with DPIIT recognition.
  • State schemes: many states offer seed grants, sustenance allowances and incubation support.

/ Compliance at every round

What has to be filed when money comes in.

  • Valuation: a valuation report is needed for share issues, under the income-tax rules and, for foreign investors, FEMA pricing; see FEMA valuation reports.
  • Private placement: shares are offered under section 42 with an offer letter (PAS-4), money into a separate bank account, and the return of allotment (PAS-3) filed with the Registrar within 15 days of allotment; the money cannot be used until PAS-3 is filed.
  • Foreign or NRI investors: allot within 60 days of receiving the money and file FC-GPR within 30 days of allotment; see raising money from a foreign investor and the FC-GPR checklist.
  • Instruments: CCPS, CCDs and SAFE-style notes each have different tax and FEMA treatment; see CCDs and CCPS and SAFE vs convertible note.
  • Angel tax: the section 56(2)(viib) tax on share premium was abolished by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024 from FY 2024-25.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What are the stages of startup funding in India?
Usually pre-seed (founders, friends and family, early angels), seed (angels, angel networks, seed funds), Series A (venture capital), Series B and later (growth funds) and pre-IPO rounds.
What government funding schemes are available for startups in India?
The Startup India Seed Fund Scheme through incubators (2026 call closed in May 2026 per guides), funds backed by Fund of Funds 2.0, the Credit Guarantee Scheme for Startups, the section 80-IAC tax holiday and state schemes.
Which instruments do Indian startups use to raise money?
Equity shares, compulsorily convertible preference shares (CCPS), compulsorily convertible debentures (CCDs) and SAFE-style convertible notes, each with different tax and FEMA treatment.
What compliance is needed when a startup raises funding?
A valuation report, a private placement under section 42 with an offer letter and separate bank account, the return of allotment (PAS-3) within 15 days, and for foreign investors allotment within 60 days and FC-GPR within 30 days of allotment.
Is angel tax still applicable in India?
No. The angel tax under section 56(2)(viib) was abolished by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024 from FY 2024-25.
