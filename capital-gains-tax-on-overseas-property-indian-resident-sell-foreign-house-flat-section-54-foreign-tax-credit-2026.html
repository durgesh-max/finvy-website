/ Indian residents and returning NRIs with property abroad · Capital gains on foreign real estate · Updated 2026-11-28

Capital Gains Tax on Overseas Property for Indian Residents (2026): Selling a Foreign House or Flat, Rates, Section 54, Foreign Tax Credit and Schedule FA, taxed in India too, with credit for tax paid abroad.

If you are resident and ordinarily resident in India, a gain on selling a house or flat abroad is taxable in India, even if the other country also taxes it. Held more than 24 months, it is a long-term gain - taxed at 12.5%, or at 20% with indexation for property bought before 23 July 2024 if you are an individual or HUF - with credit for tax paid abroad. The section 54 and 54F reinvestment exemptions work only if the new house is in India.

/ How India taxes the sale

Status first, then the rules.

PointPosition
Resident and ordinarily resident (ROR)Gain taxable in India (worldwide income)
RNOR or NRI in the year of saleGenerally not taxable in India
Long-termHeld more than 24 months
Long-term rate12.5% without indexation; resident individuals and HUFs may choose 20% with indexation for land or buildings bought before 23 July 2024
Short-termAdded to income and taxed at slab rates
Section 54 / 54FOnly if the new residential house is in India
Tax paid abroadForeign tax credit under the DTAA or section 91, claimed with Form 67
  • Currency conversion: cost and sale price in foreign currency are converted at the rates the rules prescribe - the rupee movement can increase the Indian gain.
  • Returning NRIs: selling while still RNOR can keep the gain outside Indian tax - timing matters.

/ Reporting and planning

Before and after the sale.

  • Schedule FA: the property should have been reported as a foreign asset each year you held it as a resident; missing years can draw Black Money Act penalties.
  • Schedule CG and FSI: report the gain in capital gains and foreign source income, with the foreign tax credit claim.
  • Credit limits: the credit is limited to the Indian tax on that income; timing differences between the two countries’ tax years need care.
  • Bringing money to India: through banking channels; FEMA treatment depends on how the property was acquired - check before the sale.
  • Inherited property abroad: the previous owner’s cost and holding period apply.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Do I have to pay capital gains tax in India on property sold abroad?
Yes, if you are resident and ordinarily resident in India in the year of sale, because residents are taxed on worldwide income. If you are RNOR or non-resident that year, foreign capital gains are generally not taxed in India.
What is the tax rate on long-term capital gains from overseas property?
12.5% without indexation for property held more than 24 months. Resident individuals and HUFs may instead choose 20% with indexation for land or buildings acquired before 23 July 2024. Short-term gains are taxed at slab rates.
Can I claim section 54 exemption if I buy a house abroad?
No. Section 54 and section 54F require the new residential house to be in India. Reinvesting the gain in a house in India can qualify, subject to the usual conditions.
What if I already paid capital gains tax in the other country?
You can claim foreign tax credit under the tax treaty or section 91, by filing Form 67, up to the Indian tax on that gain.
Do I need to report overseas property in my ITR?
Yes. A resident and ordinarily resident must report foreign property in Schedule FA every year it is held, and the sale in Schedule CG and Schedule FSI in the year of sale.
