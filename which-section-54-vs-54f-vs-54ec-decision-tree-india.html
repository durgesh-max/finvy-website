/ The 4-question decision tree

Pick based on asset + goal.

Q1: What's the asset being sold?

  • Residential property → Section 54 (if reinvesting in residential property) OR Section 54EC (if bonds OK) OR mix.
  • Non-residential asset (shares, MF, gold, commercial, land) → Section 54F (if reinvesting in residential property) OR Section 54EC (if bonds OK).

Q2: Where do you want to reinvest?

  • Residential property in India + want full exemption → Section 54 (from residential) or Section 54F (from non-residential).
  • Bonds + want simpler + no property hassle → Section 54EC (any long-term asset to specific bonds).
  • Multiple residential properties → only Section 54 permits two (with specific conditions); Section 54F allows only ONE.

Q3: What's the gain amount vs net consideration?

  • Gain > INR 50 lakh → 54EC bonds alone insufficient; need 54 or 54F in combination.
  • Gain = INR 50 lakh or less → 54EC alone works (simpler).
  • Section 54F requires FULL net consideration investment (not just gain) - evaluate feasibility.

Q4: Timing window?

  • 54 / 54F: 2 years purchase forward / 3 years construction forward / 1 year back.
  • 54EC: 6 months forward only (bonds).
  • Deposit in Capital Gain Account Scheme (CGAS) if investment delayed - mandatory by 31 July of next ITR year.

/ Section 54 vs 54F key differences

The nuances.

  • 54: residential property sold → residential reinvested. Exempt amount = CAPITAL GAIN (not consideration). Can reinvest in TWO properties (if capital gain <= INR 2 crore + specific conditions).
  • 54F: non-residential (shares, MF, gold, commercial, land) sold → residential reinvested. Exempt amount proportional to NET CONSIDERATION invested. Only ONE residential property.
  • 54: standard for property sellers.
  • 54F: standard for ESOP exercise + listed share sale + MF redemption + specific long-term asset sale.

/ Section 54EC bonds specific

The simpler option.

  • Investment in specific bonds: REC (Rural Electrification Corporation), NHAI (National Highways Authority), PFC (Power Finance Corporation), IRFC (Indian Railway Finance Corporation).
  • Investment within 6 months of asset transfer.
  • Maximum INR 50 lakh per taxpayer per FY (cross-year ceiling).
  • Lock-in 5 years.
  • Interest rate: specific (currently ~5-5.5%).
  • Simpler + safer than property reinvestment.
  • No property management hassle.

/ Common decision mistakes

Where taxpayers trip.

  • 54F confusion with 54: forgetting net consideration vs gain difference. 54F requires FULL consideration investment; 54 only requires gain investment.
  • Multiple properties under 54F: 54F allows only ONE residential property; 54 allows up to TWO (specific conditions).
  • 54EC above INR 50 lakh: single-year cross-year ceiling - cannot invest INR 1 crore in bonds in single FY.
  • Property sold within 3 years: clawback - prior exemption added back.
  • Not depositing in CGAS: if investment delayed beyond ITR deadline, must deposit in Capital Gain Account Scheme by 31 July of next year. Otherwise exemption lost.
  • Foreign residential property: 54 + 54F require Indian residential property. Foreign property doesn't qualify.
  • Combination of 54/54F + 54EC: can combine - e.g., 54EC INR 50 lakh + 54F for balance. Common strategy for large gains.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What's the main difference between Section 54 and 54F?
Section 54: residential property sold reinvested in residential property. Exempt amount = GAIN only. Section 54F: any long-term asset (NOT residential property) - shares, MF, gold, commercial, land - sold reinvested in residential property. Exempt amount proportional to NET CONSIDERATION invested (not just gain). 54F requires FULL consideration, not just gain.
Can I combine Section 54F + 54EC?
Yes - standard strategy for large gains. Example: INR 2 crore LTCG from share sale → INR 50 lakh in 54EC bonds (max limit) + balance INR 1.5 crore invested in residential property under 54F. Both exemptions can be claimed simultaneously. Also 54 + 54EC combination works.
What's the Section 54EC INR 50 lakh limit?
Maximum INR 50 lakh per taxpayer per FY (not per transaction). Cross-year ceiling if gain spans across FYs. Also includes aggregate across different 54EC-eligible bonds (REC + NHAI + PFC + IRFC). Any excess above INR 50 lakh is NOT exempt.
Can NRI claim Section 54 / 54F / 54EC?
Yes - all three available to NRIs. Specific: Section 54 / 54F require Indian residential property reinvestment. 54EC bonds (REC / NHAI / PFC / IRFC) available to NRI. Combined with LDC under Section 197 for TDS reduction - BQP integrated planning.
What if I can't invest within 2/3 year window?
Deposit in Capital Gain Account Scheme (CGAS) by 31 July of next year's ITR deadline. Deposited amount counts as 'invested' for purposes of claiming exemption. Must actually use CGAS deposit for Section 54/54F investment within 2/3 year window from original sale date. If unused, exemption clawed back + taxed as deemed capital gain.
