/ Irish companies and NRIs in Ireland · DTAA · Updated 2026-11-24

India-Ireland DTAA (2026): Tax Treaty Rates of 10% on Dividends, Interest, Royalties and Technical Fees, Article 8 and How to Claim, a straightforward 10% treaty, with leasing and shipping questions to watch.

The India-Ireland double tax avoidance agreement, signed in 2000 and effective from 2001, caps Indian tax on dividends, interest, royalties and fees for technical services paid to an Irish resident at 10%, against India’s domestic withholding of around 20% plus surcharge and cess. Interest paid to certain government and financial institutions is exempt. Article 8 deals with profits from shipping and air transport, which matters for airlines and the aircraft leasing businesses that make Ireland a common counterparty for Indian carriers. To get the treaty rate, the Irish recipient gives the Indian payer a tax residency certificate, Form 41 (formerly Form 10F) and usually a PAN.

/ Quick answers

What Irish companies and NRIs ask.

  • What are the treaty rates? Up to 10% on dividends, interest, royalties and fees for technical services paid by an Indian payer to an Irish resident beneficial owner.
  • Is any interest exempt? Yes, interest paid to certain government bodies and financial institutions named in the treaty.
  • What does Article 8 cover? Profits from operating ships and aircraft in international traffic; check the treaty text for how it allocates taxing rights and whether leasing income falls inside it.
  • How are aircraft lease rentals treated? Depending on the lease terms and treaty definitions, payments by Indian airlines to Irish lessors can be argued as royalties or business profits, so the treaty position needs reviewing for each lease.
  • Does the MLI apply? Check the synthesised text; the principal purpose test and beneficial ownership rules apply to treaty claims.

/ Claiming and planning

Documents and credit.

  • Documents: an Irish tax residency certificate, Form 41 and a PAN or the documents allowed instead; see tax residency certificates.
  • Substance: keep evidence of real activity in Ireland, since treaty benefits can be denied to conduit arrangements.
  • Leasing and shipping: review each contract against Article 8 and the royalty definition before fixing the withholding rate.
  • Credit in Ireland: Indian tax at the treaty rate is generally creditable against Irish tax on the same income.
  • Indian residents with Irish income claim foreign tax credit in India for Irish tax.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What are the India-Ireland DTAA rates?
Dividends, interest, royalties and fees for technical services paid by an Indian payer to an Irish resident are taxed in India at up to 10%, with interest to certain government and financial institutions exempt. Without the treaty, withholding is usually 20% plus surcharge and cess.
What is Article 8 of the India-Ireland DTAA?
The article on shipping and air transport, covering profits from operating ships and aircraft in international traffic. Check the treaty text for how it allocates taxing rights and whether leasing income is included.
How does an Irish company claim the treaty rate in India?
Give the Indian payer an Irish tax residency certificate, Form 41 (formerly Form 10F) and a PAN or the documents allowed instead, before tax is deducted, and keep evidence of substance in Ireland.
Are aircraft lease rentals to Irish lessors taxed in India?
It depends on the lease terms and the treaty definitions: such payments can be argued as royalties or as business profits, which changes the Indian tax position, so each lease needs review.
