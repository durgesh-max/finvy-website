ESOP Buyback Taxation in India (2026): Shares Bought Back, Vested Options Cashed Out, and the 1 April 2026 Change, what you sell back decides how it is taxed.

An ESOP buyback is taxed in one of two ways, depending on what the company buys back. If it buys back shares you already received on exercise, the gain over the share’s fair market value on the exercise date is a capital gain for buybacks on or after 1 April 2026: for unlisted shares held over 24 months, 12.5% without indexation; otherwise at your slab rate. (Between 1 October 2024 and 31 March 2026, buyback money was taxed as a dividend instead.) If it pays you cash for vested options you never exercised, most employers deduct tax as salary, but a 2026 Bangalore tribunal ruling held such a payment to be a capital gain, so the position is disputed. Remember the perquisite tax you paid at exercise: that value is your cost, so it is not taxed twice.

/ Quick answers

What employees ask when a buyback is announced.

  • What is an ESOP buyback? The company (or an investor or ESOP trust it arranges) buys back employees’ shares or vested options for cash, giving liquidity before an IPO or sale.
  • Is ESOP buyback money taxable in India? Yes. Shares bought back on or after 1 April 2026 give a capital gain; cash for unexercised options is usually taxed as salary by the employer, though one tribunal has held it to be a capital gain.
  • What is my cost? For shares you exercised, the fair market value on the exercise date (the value already taxed as a perquisite), not the exercise price you paid.
  • Long-term or short-term? Unlisted shares are long-term after 24 months from allotment (12.5%); listed shares after 12 months (12.5% above INR 1.25 lakh). Shorter holdings are short-term.
  • Do the promoter rules apply to me? Only if you count as a promoter, which for this purpose includes holding more than 10% of the company. Ordinary employees pay normal capital gains rates.

By what is bought back and when.

/ Practical points

Before you accept the buyback offer.

  • Ask what is being bought back: shares or options. The offer letter decides the tax treatment and whether the company deducts TDS as salary.
  • Keep the exercise-date valuation report: it fixes your cost; without it you may be taxed on more than your real gain.
  • Option cash-outs: if TDS is deducted as salary and you want to take the capital gains position, take advice first; it can be contested.
  • Employees abroad: non-resident employees face TDS at non-resident rates on the capital gain and may get treaty relief; their home country may also tax the gain.
  • Reinvestment: Section 54F can exempt a long-term gain on shares if the net consideration goes into one residential house in India; 54EC bonds do not apply to share gains.
  • Companies running a buyback: follow the Companies Act buyback rules (or arrange a secondary purchase), value the shares properly and plan the TDS; see issuing ESOPs in India.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

How is ESOP buyback taxed in India in 2026?
If the company buys back shares you received on exercise, on or after 1 April 2026 the gain over the fair market value on the exercise date is a capital gain: 12.5% for unlisted shares held over 24 months, otherwise at slab rates. Cash paid for vested options never exercised is usually taxed as salary, although a 2026 Bangalore tribunal ruling treated it as a capital gain.
What is an ESOP buyback?
A company, an investor or an ESOP trust buying back employees' shares or vested options for cash, usually to give employees liquidity before an IPO or acquisition.
Is ESOP buyback taxed as dividend?
Only for company buybacks between 1 October 2024 and 31 March 2026. From 1 April 2026 buybacks are taxed as capital gains again.
What is the cost of ESOP shares for capital gains?
The fair market value on the date of exercise, which was already taxed as a perquisite, not the exercise price you paid.
Is cash for unexercised ESOPs salary or capital gain?
Disputed. Employers usually deduct tax as salary. A Bangalore tribunal held in 2026 that it is a capital gain because the perquisite rule taxes only shares allotted on exercise. Take advice before filing on that basis.
