/ Why Rule 9B blocks fundraising

The specific trigger.

  • Rule 9B (Companies PAS Second Amendment Rules 2023, effective 27 October 2023 for most companies): mandatory dematerialisation of unlisted shares for most companies (except small / government / Nidhi).
  • Specific obligation: company must facilitate demat; any new allotment / transfer / buyback / transmission can ONLY be in demat form.
  • Investor's closing condition typically: 'all outstanding shares to be in demat form before Closing'.
  • If company has physical-share holders at closing: new allotment to investor BLOCKED.
  • Deal pauses until all shareholders in demat - 60-120 days typical for complete demat with shareholder cooperation.

/ 60-120 day remediation workflow under deal pressure

Fast-track execution.

  1. Week 1: Compliance gap assessment + shareholder-wise physical/demat status + ISIN application if not already (if ISIN exists, skip).
  2. Week 2-3: ISIN approval from NSDL/CDSL (if applied).
  3. Week 2-4: Depository Participant (DP) appointment + company's DP for facilitating demat.
  4. Week 3-6: Shareholder communication - formal letter + email + phone follow-up to all physical-share holders. Templates + repeat follow-ups.
  5. Week 4-10: Shareholders open demat accounts (if not already) + submit Dematerialisation Request Form (DRF) + physical share certificate to their DPs. Repeat follow-up for non-responsive shareholders.
  6. Week 6-12: Company RTA + DP verification + demat confirmation. Shares credited to shareholders' demat accounts.
  7. Week 10-14: Physical certificates cancellation + Form PAS-6 half-yearly filing if window.
  8. Parallel: Lost certificate issuance under Rule 6 for shareholders with lost certificates.

/ Dealing with unresponsive shareholders

The hard case.

  • Document attempts: RPAD letters + emails + phone follow-ups + newspaper notice (if specific requirement).
  • Prove company's 'reasonable efforts' to facilitate demat.
  • For truly unresponsive shareholders: deal closing may proceed with specific indemnity + escrow structure (shareholders remain physical; specific carve-out).
  • Alternative: company buyback of unresponsive shareholder's shares (specific valuation + Section 115QA buyback tax 20%).
  • Specific SEBI SBEB framework for employee ESOP shares.
  • For unresponsive angel: specific negotiation + specific premium offer.

/ Related compliance

What also triggers.

  • Form PAS-6 half-yearly filing: for companies in demat form, mandatory half-yearly reconciliation (29 November + 30 May). Catch-up on missed periods.
  • FEMA FC-TRS filing: for share transfers involving non-resident during demat process.
  • Section 62(1)(b) + preferential allotment compliance: for new investor allotment post-demat completion.
  • Form PAS-3 share allotment filing: within 30 days of new allotment.
  • Investor's post-closing covenants: ongoing Form PAS-6 + Rule 9B compliance for new company state.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

How long does Rule 9B remediation take under deal pressure?
60-120 days typical. ISIN application 15-30 days + DP appointment + shareholder communication + 60+ days for shareholder-side demat opening + DRF submission + company confirmation. Faster possible with responsive shareholders + if ISIN already exists. For unresponsive shareholders: specific indemnity/escrow may bypass completion.
What if shareholders refuse to demat?
Document reasonable efforts (RPAD letters + emails + phone + newspaper notice). Options: (a) continue chase, (b) buyback unresponsive shareholder's shares (specific valuation + Section 115QA 20% buyback tax), (c) specific indemnity/escrow with investor accepting specific carve-out. Investor's acceptance varies by specific percentage of unresponsive + relationship.

What's buyback tax Section 115QA?
20% + surcharge on distributed income paid by Indian company doing buyback. Receipt in shareholder's hands: capital gain but Section 10(34A) exempts buyback-related income (so no further shareholder tax typically). Companies Act limits: max 10-25% buyback per limit + 2-year gap between buybacks + Section 68 Companies Act conditions.
