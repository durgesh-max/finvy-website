Australian Citizen or Resident Selling Property in India (2026): Who Can Buy, TDS, Repatriation and Australian Capital Gains Tax, India taxes first; the ATO gives an offset.

An Australian citizen of Indian origin, or an Indian living in Australia, can sell a house, flat or commercial property in India to a resident Indian, an NRI or an OCI holder; agricultural land only to a resident Indian citizen. India taxes the gain first: the buyer deducts TDS, 12.5% plus surcharge and cess on long-term gains, unless you get a lower deduction certificate (Form 128), and up to USD 1 million a year can be sent to Australia from your NRO account. If you are an Australian tax resident, the ATO taxes the gain in dollars: half the gain is taxable if you held the property for at least 12 months, and if you bought it before moving to Australia your cost is its market value on the day you became resident. The Indian tax comes off through the foreign income tax offset. People on temporary visas are generally not taxed in Australia on gains on Indian property.

/ Quick answers

What Australia-based sellers ask.

  • Can an Australian citizen sell property in India? Yes. As a person of Indian origin (with or without OCI) you can sell residential or commercial property to a resident Indian, an NRI or an OCI. Agricultural land, plantations and farmhouses can be sold only to an Indian citizen resident in India.
  • How much TDS will the buyer deduct? 12.5% plus surcharge and cess on long-term gains (held over 24 months); 30% plus surcharge and cess on short-term gains. A Form 128 certificate brings it down to the real tax.
  • Do I pay tax in Australia? If you are an Australian resident for tax (and not a temporary resident), yes: the gain goes in your return, halved if you held the property for at least 12 months, and the Indian tax is offset.
  • I bought the flat before moving to Australia. What is my cost? For Australian tax, generally its market value on the day you became an Australian resident, so only the growth since then is taxed. Get a valuation for that date.
  • I am on a temporary visa. Am I taxed? Temporary residents are generally taxed in Australia only on taxable Australian property, so a gain on Indian property is usually outside Australian tax; India still taxes it.

/ Steps

Selling from Australia.

  • 1. Documents: title deed or inheritance papers, mutation, tax receipts, PAN (Form 95 for foreign citizens).
  • 2. Value the property at the date you became an Australian resident, if bought before then.
  • 3. Apply for Form 128 before the sale; see the TDS and LDC calculator.
  • 4. Sign in person or by power of attorney (notarised and apostilled in Australia, adjudicated in India); the buyer deducts TDS.
  • 5. Receive proceeds in your NRO account and remit with Forms 145 and 146, up to USD 1 million a year.
  • 6. File the Indian return to recover excess TDS, then report the gain in your Australian return and claim the foreign income tax offset.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Can an Australian citizen sell property in India?
Yes. An Australian citizen of Indian origin, with or without an OCI card, can sell residential or commercial property in India to a resident Indian, an NRI or an OCI. Agricultural land, plantations and farmhouses can be sold only to an Indian citizen resident in India.
Do Australian residents pay tax on selling property in India?
Yes. India taxes the gain first (12.5% plus surcharge and cess for long-term gains, through TDS). Australia then taxes the gain in Australian dollars, halved if held at least 12 months, with a foreign income tax offset for the Indian tax.
What is my cost base if I bought the property before moving to Australia?
Generally the market value of the property on the day you became an Australian resident for tax, so only the increase after that date is taxed in Australia.
Are temporary visa holders in Australia taxed on Indian property gains?
Generally no. Temporary residents are taxed in Australia only on taxable Australian property, so a gain on Indian property usually falls outside Australian tax. India still taxes it.
How much can I transfer from India to Australia after the sale?
Up to USD 1 million per financial year from your NRO account, with Form 145 and a chartered accountant's certificate in Form 146.
