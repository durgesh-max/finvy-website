GST Summons under Section 70, Inspection and Search under Section 67 (2026): What to Do, Your Rights, GSTR-3A, DRC-07 and E-Way Bill Penalties, prepare before you appear.

A GST summons under section 70 requires you to appear, give a statement or produce documents before a GST officer; an inspection or search under section 67 means officers can visit your premises and take documents or goods. Both are serious: statements are recorded in a judicial proceeding and are used later in show cause notices. Respond on time, carry only what is asked, keep copies of everything, and take professional help before the first appearance.

/ Your position on a summons

Guidelines officers must follow.

  • Records already online: CBIC Instruction 03/2022-23 says summons should not be used to call for records already available on the GST portal.
  • Senior management: the same instruction says chairpersons, managing directors, CEOs and CFOs should not ordinarily be summoned in the first instance.
  • Reference number: check the document identification or reference number on the summons; CBIC's Circular 249/06/2025-GST treats a communication with a reference number (RFN) as not needing a separate DIN.
  • Your statement: answer factually, do not guess figures, ask to read the statement before signing, and keep a copy; retractions later carry little weight.
  • Arrest: arrest is possible only for specified offences under section 132 with the Commissioner's authorisation - if you fear arrest, take legal advice at once.

/ After the visit or statement

What usually follows.

  • A show cause notice (DRC-01) under section 73 or 74, often preceded by DRC-01A - reply on time with documents.
  • Voluntary payment with interest can reduce penalty at the right stage - weigh it before the notice is issued.
  • Seized goods can be released provisionally on a bond and security; seized documents must be returned if not relied on.
  • If an order is passed, the DRC-07 summary starts the three-month appeal clock.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is a GST summons under section 70?
An order from a GST officer requiring you to appear, give evidence or produce documents. The officer has the powers of a civil court and the inquiry is treated as a judicial proceeding, so statements recorded can be used later in a show cause notice or prosecution.
How do I reply to a GST summons?
There is no fixed reply format. Check the reference number, date and documents asked for, then either appear with those documents or write asking for a short adjournment with reasons. Prepare a factual statement with your adviser, read it before signing, and keep copies of everything submitted.
Can a GST officer summon the CEO or managing director?
CBIC Instruction 03/2022-23 says senior management such as chairpersons, managing directors, CEOs and CFOs should not ordinarily be summoned in the first instance, and that summons should not be used to call for records already on the GST portal.
What happens during a GST inspection or search?
Officers visit on written authorisation (INS-01) from a Joint Commissioner or above, inspect records and stock, and may seize documents or goods. Ask to see the authorisation, check the panchnama and inventory before signing, and take copies of seized documents; seized goods can be released provisionally on a bond and security.
What is a GSTR-3A notice?
A notice under section 46 to someone who has not filed a GST return, asking them to file within 15 days. If the return is not filed, the officer can make a best judgment assessment under section 62; that order is withdrawn if the return is filed within 60 days (a further 60 days with an additional late fee).
What does an assessment order and DRC-07 mean?
DRC-07 is the summary of a demand order showing the tax, interest and penalty payable after a show cause notice is decided. It starts the three-month time limit to appeal in APL-01, with the pre-deposit, or the amount must be paid.
What is the penalty for moving goods without an e-way bill?
Under section 129 the goods and vehicle can be detained. Where the owner comes forward, the penalty is 200% of the tax payable on the goods (for exempt goods, 2% of their value or Rs 25,000, whichever is less); where the owner does not come forward, a higher penalty applies. The officer issues a notice in MOV-07 and passes an order in MOV-09, and the goods are released once the penalty is paid. If the detention was wrong - for example a minor clerical error in the e-way bill - reply to the MOV-07 notice with the invoice and documents, and appeal the MOV-09 order in APL-01 if needed, with a pre-deposit of 25% of the penalty.
