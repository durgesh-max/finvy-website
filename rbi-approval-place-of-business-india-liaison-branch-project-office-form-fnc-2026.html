/ Foreign companies · Liaison office · Branch office · Updated 2026-11-28

RBI Approval to Establish a Place of Business in India (2026): Liaison, Branch or Project Office - Eligibility, Form FNC, Who Needs RBI Approval, After-Approval Filings and How to Close the Office, for foreign companies opening an office in India.

A foreign company that wants a liaison office, branch office or project office in India applies in Form FNC through an Indian authorised dealer (AD Category-I) bank. The bank approves most cases under the RBI's 2016 regulations. Under those regulations a branch office needs profits in each of the last five years and a net worth of at least USD 100,000. A liaison office needs profits in the last three years and a net worth of at least USD 50,000. A parent's letter of comfort can fill the gap. RBI approval (with government consultation) is needed for applicants from Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Afghanistan, Iran, China, Hong Kong or Macau, for the defence, telecom, private security and broadcasting sectors, and for non-profits. After approval, the office gets a unique identification number, registers with the ROC in Form FC-1 within 30 days, and files an Annual Activity Certificate by 30 September each year. RBI's October 2025 draft would drop the net worth and profit tests, but as of the latest reports the 2016 rules still apply.

/ The process

From application to operating.

  • Documents: Form FNC, certificate of incorporation and constitutional documents, last audited financial statements (or a CPA-certified net worth), board resolution, KYC of the parent and its authorised signatory, and a letter of comfort if needed; foreign documents notarised and apostilled or consularised.
  • AD bank review: the bank checks eligibility and KYC and approves, or forwards to the RBI where RBI approval is required; the RBI allots a unique identification number.
  • After approval: Form FC-1 with the Registrar of Companies within 30 days; PAN and TAN; bank account; GST registration for a branch or project office that makes taxable supplies.
  • Every year: audited accounts and the Annual Activity Certificate by 30 September to the AD bank and the income-tax department; a branch files an income-tax return. See annual activity certificate.
  • Closing or converting: closure through the AD bank with auditor's certificates, or conversion to a subsidiary; see liaison vs branch vs subsidiary.

/ What is changing

RBI's draft regulations.

  • Status: RBI issued draft 'Establishment in India of a branch or office' Regulations in October 2025; reports in May 2026 said they were not yet notified, so check the current position when you apply.
  • Proposed: no profit or net worth test, a negative list of prohibited activities instead of a permitted list, no tenure limit for liaison offices, more offices without prior RBI approval, and a simpler route for changes of ownership.
  • Unchanged in principle: AD bank processing, security-related approvals for certain countries and sectors, and annual reporting.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

How does a foreign company get approval for a place of business in India?
It applies in Form FNC through an AD Category-I bank in India, which approves most liaison, branch and project office applications under the 2016 regulations; some countries and sectors need RBI approval.
What is a liaison office in India and what is it allowed to do?
A liaison office is a foreign company's representative office in India. Under the 2016 FEMA regulations it may only represent the parent or group companies, promote exports from and imports into India, promote technical or financial collaborations, and act as a communication channel between the parent and Indian businesses. It cannot trade, manufacture, render paid services or earn any income in India; its expenses are met entirely from remittances from the head office. For example, a foreign machinery maker can run a liaison office in Mumbai that meets Indian buyers and passes enquiries to head office, while head office signs and invoices the contracts directly. If the office starts negotiating or concluding contracts, it risks creating a taxable permanent establishment, so the activity needs watching. Where the plan is to sell or earn in India, a branch office or a subsidiary is usually the right vehicle - book a call to choose between them.
Is a liaison office taxable in India?
A liaison office cannot earn income in India, so it normally has no business income to tax. The risk is a permanent establishment: if its staff negotiate or conclude contracts, take orders or do more than the preparatory or auxiliary work the RBI approval allows, the tax department can treat it as a permanent establishment and tax the profits attributable to it. It still needs a PAN and TAN, deducts TDS on salaries and rent, and files Form 49C each year along with the Annual Activity Certificate. Book a call to review the office's activities before an assessment does.
What are the eligibility criteria for a liaison or branch office?
Under the 2016 rules, a branch office needs profits in the preceding five years and net worth of at least USD 100,000; a liaison office needs profits in the preceding three years and net worth of at least USD 50,000. A parent's letter of comfort can help.
Who needs RBI approval rather than just the bank?
Applicants from Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Afghanistan, Iran, China, Hong Kong or Macau; offices in defence, telecom, private security or information and broadcasting; and non-profit organisations.
What is filed after approval?
Form FC-1 with the ROC within 30 days, PAN and tax registrations, and each year the audited accounts and Annual Activity Certificate by 30 September.
How do we close a liaison office or branch office in India?
Closure runs through the same AD Category-I bank that handled the office, which checks the papers and reports the closure to the RBI. Before applying, wind down the office's activity, settle employees and creditors, dispose of or transfer its assets, bring all Annual Activity Certificates up to date (missing years are the most common hold-up) and close out the income-tax position, including the final return or Form 49C and any pending assessments. The application to the bank usually carries a copy of the RBI or AD bank approval (and UIN), an auditor's certificate showing how the amount to be remitted abroad was worked out, backed by a statement of assets and liabilities and how the assets were disposed of, confirmation that all Indian liabilities are met or provided for, confirmation that no legal proceedings stand in the way, and - where the office was registered with the Registrar of Companies as a foreign company - the ROC filings for its closure. The balance is then remitted to the head office with Forms 145 and 146 (old 15CA / 15CB) where they apply. Converting the office into a subsidiary is an alternative where the business is staying in India. RBI's October 2025 draft proposes removing the profit and net worth tests, but as of the latest reports the 2016 regulations were still in force; check before applying.
