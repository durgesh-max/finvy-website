/ NRIs and OCI cardholders abroad · Inherited shares and mutual funds in India · Updated 2026-11-28

NRI Inheriting Indian Shares and Mutual Funds (2026): Transmission With or Without a Nominee, Holding on Non-Repatriation Basis, Tax on Sale and Repatriation, claim first, then decide whether to sell.

Inheriting a parent’s Indian shares or mutual funds is not taxed in India, but the holdings must be transmitted to you before you can do anything with them. With a registered nominee the process is short; without one, legal heir documents are needed. As an NRI or OCI heir you hold them on a non-repatriation basis, pay capital gains on sale using your parent’s cost and holding period, and repatriate the proceeds from your NRO account within the annual limit.

Getting the holdings into your name.

Mutual fund unitsThe AMC or its registrarDeath certificate, transmission request, claimant KYC and bank details
No nominee registeredSame channelsLegal heir documents - succession certificate, probate or letter of administration, or the simplified documents the rules allow, with consent of other heirs
  • Your own accounts first: an NRI heir needs an NRO-linked demat (and NRI KYC with the fund houses) to receive the holdings.
  • Nominee vs owner: a nominee receives the holdings, but other legal heirs may still have a claim under succession law - settle that within the family.

/ Tax and repatriation

When you sell.

  • No tax on inheritance: receipts by inheritance are outside the gift tax rule.
  • Cost and holding period: on sale, your cost is the previous owner’s cost and the holding period includes theirs.
  • Rates: listed equity shares and equity funds - long-term gains at 12.5% above the annual exemption, short-term at 20%; TDS is deducted by the broker or fund house on NRI sales.
  • Repatriation: sale proceeds land in your NRO account; you can repatriate up to USD 1 million per financial year from NRO balances with Forms 15CA / 15CB.
  • Your home country: report the inherited assets and gains where you live, and use the DTAA (with a TRC and Form 10F) to avoid double tax.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Is inheritance of shares or mutual funds taxed in India for an NRI?
No. India has no inheritance tax, and assets received by inheritance are excluded from the gift tax rule. Tax arises only when you sell, as capital gains.
How does an NRI claim a deceased parent's demat shares?
Apply to the parent's depository participant with the death certificate, the transmission form and your KYC, with an NRO-linked demat account to receive the shares. If no nominee was registered, legal heir documents are also needed.
What if no nominee was registered on the shares or mutual funds?
The claimant must produce legal heir documents - a succession certificate, probate or letter of administration, or the simplified documents allowed by the rules, usually with consent of the other legal heirs.
How are capital gains calculated on inherited shares?
Your cost is the previous owner's cost and the holding period includes their holding. Listed equity and equity funds held long term are taxed at 12.5% above the annual exemption; short-term gains at 20%.
Can an NRI repatriate money from selling inherited shares?
Yes. Sale proceeds are credited to the NRO account, and up to USD 1 million per financial year can be repatriated from NRO balances with Forms 15CA and 15CB.
