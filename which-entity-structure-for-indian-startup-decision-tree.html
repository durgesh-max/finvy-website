Which Entity Structure for Indian Startup, Pvt Ltd vs LLP vs Partnership vs Sole Proprietor.

Indian startup choosing entity structure faces four main options: Private Limited Company, Limited Liability Partnership (LLP), Partnership firm, Sole Proprietor. Each has distinct implications for liability, tax, fundraising, compliance, ESOPs, M&A exit. Here's the BQP decision-tree framework for Indian founders in 2026.

/ The 5-question decision tree

Pick based on answers.

Q1: Will you raise external VC / angel funding in next 24 months?

  • Yes → Pvt Ltd (VCs almost exclusively invest in Pvt Ltd).
  • No → continue to Q2.

Q2: Will you grant ESOPs to employees?

  • Yes → Pvt Ltd (ESOP mechanism cleanest in Pvt Ltd under Section 62(1)(b)).
  • No → continue to Q3.

Q3: Will multiple founders / partners split profits + liability?

  • 2+ founders + want limited liability → LLP (simpler compliance than Pvt Ltd + limited liability + pass-through tax).
  • 2+ founders + OK with unlimited liability → Partnership (simplest + lowest compliance).
  • Solo founder → continue to Q4.

Q4: Solo founder - need liability shield?

  • Need liability shield + growth plan → OPC (One Person Company) OR Pvt Ltd with nominee shareholder.
  • Low-liability business + simplicity priority → Sole Proprietor (just PAN + GST + business registration).

Q5: Need IPO exit in 5-7 years?

  • Yes → Must be Pvt Ltd. LLP must convert to Pvt Ltd pre-IPO.
  • No → previous answer stands.

/ Partnership vs Sole Proprietor

For specific scenarios.

  • Partnership: 2+ partners + unlimited liability. Simple + cheapest to set up (partnership deed registration). Pass-through tax (profits taxed in partners' hands). No fundraising + no ESOP. Suitable for small-to-medium professional practice, trading, specific low-risk business.
  • Sole Proprietor: 1 person + unlimited liability. Simplest: PAN + GST + Shops & Establishment. Business income taxed as individual slab rate. Section 44AD / 44ADA presumptive scheme attractive if gross receipts under thresholds. No fundraising + no ESOP + no shield. Suitable for freelancers / consultants / small retail / specific trading.
  • HUF (Hindu Undivided Family): specific family-business option. Taxed as separate entity + family-member-based structuring. Specific tax planning benefits. Not for new-age startup typically.

/ Common entity choice mistakes

Where founders trip.

  • Starting as LLP when planning external VC: VCs require Pvt Ltd. LLP to Pvt Ltd conversion is specific transaction with tax + FEMA complications. Start as Pvt Ltd if VC planned.
  • Starting as Partnership for 'simplicity' with growth plan: unlimited liability + no ESOP + conversion complexity.
  • Section 115BAA / 115BAB opt-in not evaluated: Pvt Ltd + 115BAA 22% effective 25.17% often beats LLP 29% + AMT 18.5%. Specific analysis needed.
  • Sole proprietor crossing Section 44AB (section 63 of the 2025 Act) tax audit threshold: INR 1 crore turnover triggers tax audit. Formal entity (LLP / Pvt Ltd) may be more cost-effective at that scale.
  • IPO plan ignored: must be Pvt Ltd for IPO. LLP conversion pre-IPO adds 60-120 days + complexity.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Should startup choose Pvt Ltd or LLP in 2026?
If raising external VC/angel funding + ESOPs + IPO plan: Pvt Ltd. If 2+ founders + no external funding + limited liability + simpler compliance: LLP. If simple professional practice + no growth plan: Partnership. If solo + minimal liability + simplicity: Sole Proprietor. BQP decision-tree analysis per specific context.
Does Pvt Ltd always need to opt into Section 115BAA?
Not always. 115BAA 22% effective 25.17% + no MAT vs standard 25% + 15% MAT. For most mature profitable companies 115BAA wins. For companies with substantial SEZ 10AA / scientific research / carry-forward losses - standard regime may be better. Decision modelling critical.
Can I start as sole proprietor and convert later?
Yes - sole proprietor to Pvt Ltd conversion is standard business transfer. Specific tax implications: Section 47 may not fully apply; capital gain on business transfer. BQP handles conversion with Section 47 planning + specific tax structuring. Easier if planned early.
What's HUF suitability for new-age startup?
HUF is traditionally family-business option. Specific tax planning benefits (separate PAN + separate basic exemption). Not typically used for new-age startup due to specific family structure + growth constraints + fundraising limitations. For specific family-business + professional practice contexts.
