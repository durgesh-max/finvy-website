/ Statutory + corporate validation

The baseline.

  • MCA registration: CIN + current status + registered office + directors + paid-up capital + authorised capital.
  • MOA + AOA review: object clause + capacity to perform proposed contract.
  • GST registration verification + current status + state-wise registrations (if multi-state operations).
  • PAN verification against Income Tax database.
  • IEC (Import Export Code) if cross-border trade involved.
  • Udyam MSME + category.
  • Specific sector licences: RBI (financial), SEBI (securities), FSSAI (food), CDSCO (pharma), Pollution Control Board (environmental), Factory licence (manufacturing), Shops & Establishment (service / retail), professional licences.
  • Last 3 years AOC-4 + MGT-7 filing status.
  • DIR-3 KYC status of all directors.
  • Section 164 disqualified director check.

/ Financial health

The money view.

  • Last 3 years' audited financials (MCA public filings): revenue trend, gross margin, EBITDA margin, net profit, net worth, debt/equity, interest coverage.
  • Auditor's qualifications or notes (going concern, pending litigation disclosure, related-party transactions).
  • DPT-3 return showing deposits + external borrowings.
  • CIBIL commercial credit score (if accessible).
  • Bank statement review for 3-6 months (if counterparty cooperates in DD).
  • Working capital analysis: debtor days + creditor days + inventory days = cash conversion cycle.
  • Pending Form 15CA / 15CB (Forms 145/146 from 1 April 2026) history indicating cross-border payments.

/ Litigation + compliance history

The risk view.

  • Pending Income Tax notices + demands + appeals (via Form 26AS [Form 168 from tax year 2026-27] + ITR disclosures + Section 143(2) / 148 history).
  • Pending GST notices + demands (via GST portal + ASMT-10 / DRC-01 history).
  • Civil litigation: Supreme Court / High Court / District Court search by party name.
  • NCLT proceedings: insolvency, oppression / mismanagement, Section 241-242 petitions.
  • DRT (Debt Recovery Tribunal) proceedings.
  • Criminal proceedings: FIRs, chargesheets, convictions (CrPC + specific legislation like PMLA / Prevention of Corruption Act).
  • Consumer forum cases.
  • Labour tribunal / IRPL cases.
  • SEBI violations / fines (for listed / proximate-to-listed companies).
  • FEMA breaches / RBI compounding history.
  • Winding up / insolvency references.

/ FCPA + UK Bribery Act + anti-corruption DD

The compliance view.

For foreign principals subject to extraterritorial anti-bribery regimes (US FCPA, UK Bribery Act 2010, French Sapin II, Brazilian Clean Companies Act), Indian counterparty DD includes:

  • Government relationship mapping: counterparty's revenue from government contracts, licensed sectors, politically exposed persons (PEPs) in management / ownership.
  • Red-flag transaction screening: unusual payments to agents, intermediaries, consultants without clear business purpose; cash transactions in regulatory sectors; third-party intermediary payments.
  • Beneficial Ownership mapping: PEPs in ownership chain + ultimate controller.
  • Press / public record check: adverse media search; corruption allegations.
  • FCPA / Bribery Act training evidence: counterparty's internal compliance programme.
  • Specific Prevention of Corruption Act (India) history: pending cases under Section 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 13 of PCA.
  • Sanctions screening: OFAC SDN, UN consolidated list, EU, UK HM Treasury, Indian MHA + MEA lists.
  • Press Note 3 beneficial ownership: any BO link to China, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Bhutan, Myanmar, Afghanistan (for FDI into India from foreign principal).

/ Deliverable + decisions

What foreign client gets.

  • Counterparty DD Report (10-20 pages): all five workstreams + overall risk rating (Low / Medium / High) + go/no-go recommendation.
  • Pre-contract checklist: representations + warranties + indemnities to include; termination triggers; audit rights; compliance covenants.
  • Post-signing monitoring plan: periodic verification of continuing compliance.
  • Scope-based: Light (statutory + public records, 3-5 days), Full (+ financial + litigation + press, 5-10 days), Enhanced (+ FCPA / BO / sanctions / adverse media + interview, 10-15 days).

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

When should I run counterparty DD before signing with Indian company?
Before any material / recurring / long-term commitment: MSA with recurring revenue over USD 50K/year, JV / partnership agreement, supply contract above USD 100K/year, channel partner / distributor agreement, licensing / franchise agreement, equity investment, acquisition. Also mandatory under: UK Modern Slavery Act, German Supply Chain Act (LkSG), EU CSDDD for in-scope companies.
How long does Indian counterparty DD take?
5-10 business days typical. Light tier (statutory + public records only): 3-5 days. Full tier (+ financial + litigation history): 5-10 days. Enhanced tier (+ FCPA + beneficial ownership + sanctions + adverse media + optional interview): 10-15 days.
What's the difference between counterparty DD and supplier verification?
Supplier verification focuses on operational capability + delivery capacity + first-PO risk - includes site visit + factory audit + trade references (see our supplier verification guide). Counterparty DD focuses on contractual commitment risk + compliance + reputation - more legal/compliance oriented, less operational. Both can be combined for comprehensive due diligence.
What's checked in FCPA / Bribery Act DD specifically?
Government revenue exposure, PEP ownership, unusual third-party payments, red-flag transactions, adverse media / corruption allegations, prevention of corruption Act (India) history, beneficial ownership chain, sanctions screening (OFAC, UN, EU, UK, Indian MHA). Output formatted to satisfy US DOJ / SEC / UK SFO / French PNF due-diligence standards for extraterritorial anti-corruption enforcement.
