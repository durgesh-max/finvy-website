/ Private companies and their shareholders · Companies Act sections 62 and 63 · Updated 2026-11-28

Rights Issue and Bonus Issue of Shares (2026): Section 62(1)(a) and Section 63 Procedure, Timelines, PAS-3, Tax and Foreign Shareholders, new money from existing owners, or reserves turned into shares.

A rights issue offers new shares to existing shareholders in proportion to what they hold - the simplest way to raise money from the current owners. A bonus issue gives free shares by converting free reserves or securities premium into share capital. Both need the authorised capital to cover the new shares and a PAS-3 return within 30 days of allotment.

/ Rights issue vs bonus issue

Sections 62(1)(a) and 63.

PointRights issue (s.62(1)(a))Bonus issue (s.63)
Money inYes - shareholders pay for the sharesNo - reserves are capitalised
Who gets itExisting equity shareholders, in proportionExisting shareholders, in proportion
ApprovalBoard resolutionBoard recommendation and general meeting approval; articles must allow
Offer window15 to 30 days; shorter in a private company with 90% members’ consentNot applicable
RenunciationAllowed unless the articles say otherwiseNot applicable
SourceFresh subscription moneyFree reserves, securities premium or capital redemption reserve - not revaluation reserves
ConditionsAuthorised capital headroomPartly paid shares fully paid; no default on deposits, debt or statutory employee dues; not in lieu of dividend
TimelineAllot after the offer closesImplement within two months of approval; cannot be withdrawn once announced
FilingPAS-3 within 30 days of allotmentPAS-3 within 30 days of allotment
  • Check authorised capital first: increase it (and file SH-7) before the allotment if the new shares would exceed it.
  • Rights vs private placement: offering shares to someone other than existing shareholders in proportion is a private placement under section 42, with its own process.

/ Tax and foreign shareholders

Before you price it.

  • Bonus shares: no tax on receipt for ordinary shareholders; cost is nil and the holding period runs from allotment, which matters on a later sale.
  • Rights at a low price: a proportionate rights issue is generally neutral, but disproportionate subscription or renunciation at below fair value can raise tax questions - plan the pricing.
  • Non-resident shareholders: rights shares to non-residents cannot be priced below the price offered to residents, the sector must allow foreign investment, and FC-GPR is filed within 30 days.
  • Renounced rights: a renouncee who is new to the company should be checked for FEMA and KYC before allotment.
  • Records: update the register of members, issue share certificates or credit demat accounts, and keep board and meeting minutes.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is a rights issue in a private company?
An offer of new shares to existing equity shareholders in proportion to their holdings, made by a letter of offer open for 15 to 30 days, approved by the board. Shareholders can renounce their entitlement unless the articles prohibit it.
Can a private company keep the rights issue open for less than 15 days?
Yes, if at least 90% of the members consent in writing or electronically, under the exemption available to private companies.
From which reserves can bonus shares be issued?
From free reserves, the securities premium account or the capital redemption reserve. Bonus shares cannot be issued out of revaluation reserves.
What is the time limit to complete a bonus issue?
The bonus issue must be implemented within two months of the board approval, or of shareholder approval where that is required, and once announced it cannot be withdrawn.
Are bonus shares taxable?
Receiving bonus shares is generally not taxed for ordinary shareholders. Their cost is treated as nil and the holding period runs from the date of allotment, which affects capital gains on a later sale.
