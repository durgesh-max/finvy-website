/ EOR vs own Indian subsidiary

The decision.

EOR is right if:

  • 1-15 Indian hires over next 12-24 months.
  • Early market exploration; India is one of several hiring geographies.
  • Need to onboard a specific candidate quickly (1-3 weeks).
  • Don't want to deal with Indian compliance (payroll, PF, ESI, PT, gratuity, labour law).

Own Indian subsidiary (Pvt Ltd WOS) is right if:

  • 15+ Indian hires or multi-year India plan.
  • Need to invoice Indian customers (EOR doesn't give you an invoicing entity).
  • Want direct ESOP grants to Indian employees (easier via Indian subsidiary).
  • Want to control the brand presence / IP / data residency in India.
  • Need to amortise setup cost over longer horizon.

Cost comparison (rough):

  • EOR: ~USD 100-300/employee/month fee + employee cost. For 10 engineers at USD 20K/year each, EOR cost ~USD 15-30K/year.
  • Own subsidiary: setup USD 2-5K one-time + annual compliance USD 5-10K/year + optional resident director nominee USD 2-3K/year. Break-even vs EOR around 8-12 Indian hires.

/ What EOR handles

The compliance stack.

  • Employment contract: EOR issues to employee; mirrors foreign hirer's commercial terms. India-compliant (Shops & Establishment, applicable state's labour laws).
  • Monthly payroll: compute gross → deductions (PF 12% + ESI 0.75% if applicable + PT state-specific) → TDS Section 192 → net pay. Credit on 1st of month.
  • PF (Provident Fund): 12% employer + 12% employee contribution. EOR files Form 5 (new employee) + ECR (electronic challan return) monthly.
  • ESI: if applicable (gross salary <= INR 21,000/month, often not applicable for mid-senior roles).
  • Professional Tax: state-specific, max INR 2,500/year.
  • TDS Section 192: monthly deduction + quarterly Form 24Q filing.
  • Gratuity: provision in books; payable on exit after 5 years of continuous service.
  • Form 16: issued to employee annually (by 15 June).
  • Statutory compliance: labour welfare fund, bonus, leave encashment, maternity benefit, Shops & Establishment renewal, labour returns.

/ PE risk - the critical issue

What breaks the model.

EOR assumes the Indian employee works 'for' the foreign company. The foreign company pays, directs work, provides tools. India's tax authority can argue:

  • Dependent Agent PE: Indian employee habitually concludes contracts in India on behalf of foreign company → Dependent Agent PE under Article 5 of most DTAAs → foreign company has Indian taxable presence → profit attribution + Indian corporate tax.
  • Service PE: Indian employees provide services in India to foreign group companies for > specified days per 12-month period (varies by treaty, 90 days in India-US DTAA, 183 days in some others) → Service PE → same consequence.
  • Fixed Place PE: if Indian employee works from a specific premises (foreign company's leased office, cowork) and this is held at foreign company's disposal → Fixed Place PE.

Risk mitigation:

  • Keep Indian employees in support / development roles, not sales / contract-signing.
  • Decision-making authority stays with foreign HQ.
  • Avoid giving Indian employees authority to bind foreign company contractually.
  • Document role as 'back-office support' / 'development'.
  • Review PE risk annually with tax advisor.

For sales / client-facing / contract-authority roles, own Indian subsidiary is safer than EOR (because invoicing is through the Indian entity).

/ Independent contractor alternative

The 1099 / Form 16A path.

Instead of EOR, foreign company can engage Indian individuals as independent contractors:

  • Contract directly with Indian individual as consultant / freelancer.
  • Pay via wire / wire aggregator / PayPal / Deel / Wise.
  • Contractor is self-employed; files own Indian ITR, pays own tax + GST (if applicable).
  • No PF / ESI / Section 192 TDS obligation on foreign company.

But:

  • Mis-classification risk: if the arrangement is really an employment (regular hours, exclusive, directed work, tools provided), labour authority can re-characterise as employment → back-dated PF + ESI + gratuity + Section 192 TDS.
  • GST risk: if contractor's turnover exceeds INR 20 lakh / 40 lakh threshold, they must register for GST + charge GST on invoice. If export of service under GST rules (place of supply outside India, payment in foreign currency, zero-rated), LUT bond + no IGST charged.
  • No employee benefits: contractor has no gratuity, no PF, no leave encashment, no bonus — may limit ability to hire mid-senior talent.

/ Who to pick as EOR

What matters.

  • Compliance track record: PF / ESI / Section 192 TDS on time, Form 24Q filed quarterly without defaults.
  • Transparency: itemised monthly invoice (base pay / PF / ESI / PT / TDS / fee).
  • Payroll portal: employee can see payslip + Form 16 + PF balance.
  • HR support: offer letters, onboarding, policy drafts, exit handling.
  • Legal support: labour law notice handling, PE-risk analysis, specific-event advisory (maternity, resignation, PIP).
  • Scale: can grow with you from 1 to 50+ Indian hires without renegotiation.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is an Employer of Record (EOR) in India?
EOR is a third-party entity that becomes the legal employer of your Indian hires. EOR handles Indian employment contract, monthly payroll, PF, ESI, Professional Tax, Section 192 TDS, Form 24Q, Form 16, gratuity, labour law compliance. You pay EOR a monthly fee + the employee's cost; the employee effectively works for your company. Fast path to hire Indian talent without setting up your own Indian entity.
When should I use EOR vs own Indian subsidiary?
EOR for 1-15 Indian hires + short/medium-term India plan + no need to invoice Indian customers. Own subsidiary for 15+ hires + long-term plan + Indian customer invoicing + direct ESOP grants + brand/IP presence. Break-even cost around 8-12 Indian hires: EOR cost ~USD 100-300/employee/month scales linearly; own subsidiary has fixed setup + annual compliance that amortises.
What is PE (Permanent Establishment) risk with EOR?
If Indian employees working through EOR take decisions / sign contracts / provide client-facing services on behalf of the foreign company, India's tax authority can argue the foreign company has a Dependent Agent PE / Service PE / Fixed Place PE in India - creating Indian taxable presence + profit attribution. Mitigation: keep EOR employees in support/development roles, decision authority at foreign HQ, no contract-signing authority.
Can I hire Indian contractors directly instead of using EOR?
Yes - as independent contractor / consultant. Contract directly, pay via wire. No PF / ESI / Section 192 TDS on foreign company. But risk of mis-classification: if arrangement is really employment (regular hours, exclusive, directed), labour authority can re-characterise with back-dated obligations. Appropriate for genuine freelancers / consultants, not for exclusive full-time workers.
How quickly can I onboard someone via EOR in India?
1-3 weeks from offer acceptance: EOR drafts employment contract (India-compliant), employee signs, PF registration + bank account linkage, payroll setup, Section 192 TDS registration if first employee. First salary credit on 1st or 7th of next month depending on cycle. Much faster than setting up your own entity (which takes 30-45 days before you can start payroll).
