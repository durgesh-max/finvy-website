SOFTEX Form Replaced by EDF (2026): How Software and IT Service Exporters Declare Exports Under the FEMA Export Regulations From 1 October 2026, one declaration for software and services.

The SOFTEX form is gone. From 1 October 2026 the FEMA (Export of Goods and Services) Regulations, 2026 treat software as a service, and software and service exports are declared on the Export Declaration Form (EDF) instead - within 30 days from the end of the month in which the invoice is raised. Small receipts and individual exporters got relief on 2 November 2026; companies with larger invoices still run the EDF routine, and SOFTEX forms left open from earlier months still need closing.

  • Realisation is unchanged in principle: export proceeds must still be realised and repatriated within the period allowed (nine months from the invoice, twelve for rupee invoices, per the current rules) - the EDF is how the bank tracks it.
  • Open SOFTEX forms: invoices raised before 1 October 2026 under SOFTEX should be followed up with the bank or STPI so the export is shown as realised and closed.
  • GST is separate: export of services is still zero-rated under GST with an LUT or IGST refund; the EDF does not replace GST invoices or the LUT.

/ Who has to do what

By type of exporter.

  • Freelancers and individual consultants: give the bank complete payment details (purpose code, invoice and client) for each receipt - the EDF is then deemed filed.
  • Companies, LLPs and firms with receipts up to INR 10 lakh: the same deemed filing applies to those remittances.
  • Larger receipts: file the EDF within 30 days from the end of the invoice month and get it certified by the bank (or STPI for a domestic tariff area unit).
  • STPI and SEZ units: keep the unit-level reporting your scheme requires alongside the EDF.
  • Everyone: track realisation invoice by invoice so nothing stays outstanding past the deadline.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Is the SOFTEX form still required?
No. The FEMA (Export of Goods and Services) Regulations, 2026, in force from 1 October 2026, discontinued SOFTEX. Software is treated as a service and software exports are declared on the Export Declaration Form (EDF).
What is the due date for the EDF for software exports?
Within 30 days from the end of the month in which the invoice is raised. It is certified by the bank or, for a unit in a domestic tariff area, by STPI.
Do freelancers have to file the EDF?
Under the RBI amendment of 2 November 2026, a resident individual exporting services is deemed to have filed the EDF once they give the bank the information needed to receive and credit the payment, such as the purpose code, invoice and client details.
Is there relief for small export receipts?
Yes. For exporters other than individuals, the EDF is deemed filed for inward remittances up to INR 10 lakh. Larger receipts follow the normal EDF process.
What happens to SOFTEX forms filed before October 2026?
They do not disappear. Invoices declared on SOFTEX before 1 October 2026 should be followed up with the bank or STPI so the export is shown as realised and closed; an open form can lead to bank or RBI queries.
