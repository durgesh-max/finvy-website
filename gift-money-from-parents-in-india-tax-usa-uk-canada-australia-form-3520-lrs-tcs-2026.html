Gift Money From Parents in India While Living Abroad (2026): Is It Taxable in the USA, UK, Canada or Australia? Form 3520, LRS and TCS, usually tax-free, but not paperwork-free.

Money your parents in India give you is not taxed as income in India, the US, the UK, Canada, Australia, Singapore or the UAE. India exempts gifts from relatives, and none of these countries taxes the person receiving a gift. The catches are paperwork and cash flow: a US person must file Form 3520 if gifts from non-US parents exceed USD 100,000 in a year, with penalties of up to 25% for missing it; your parents can send up to USD 250,000 each per year under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme, but the bank collects 20% TCS on gift remittances above INR 10 lakh a year, which they get back through their Indian return. Income you later earn on the money is taxed where you live.

/ Quick answers

What children abroad and their parents ask.

  • Do I pay tax on gift money from my parents in India? No, as income: India exempts gifts from parents and other relatives, and the US, UK, Canada, Australia, Singapore and UAE do not tax the recipient of a gift.
  • Do I have to report it? In the US, yes, on Form 3520 if gifts from non-US persons exceed USD 100,000 in the year. The UK, Canada, Australia, Singapore and UAE have no gift-reporting form for the recipient, but banks may ask where large sums came from.
  • How much can my parents send? Up to USD 250,000 per parent per financial year (April to March) under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme.
  • Why did the bank deduct 20%? That is TCS on gift remittances above INR 10 lakh in a year. It is not a final tax: your parents claim it against their tax or as a refund in their Indian return.
  • Is there a cheaper route? A rupee gift into your NRO account in India attracts no TCS; moving it abroad from the NRO account then needs Forms 145 and 146 within the USD 1 million a year limit.

/ India side

What your parents need to know.

  • LRS limit: USD 250,000 per resident individual per financial year, so two parents can send up to USD 500,000 between them.
  • TCS: 20% on the amount of gift remittances above INR 10 lakh in a financial year (counting all their LRS remittances). It shows in their Form 26AS and is adjusted in their return; it is not an extra tax.
  • No gift tax in India for a gift to a child, and no tax for the child as a relative. Income on the money while it sits in India in the parent’s name stays taxable in the parent’s hands.
  • Rupee route: parents can gift into your NRO account in India; see gifts to NRI relatives.
  • Students: money for a child’s education abroad has different TCS rules; see parents paying for study abroad.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Do I pay tax on gift money from my parents overseas?
Not as income. India exempts gifts from parents and other relatives, and the US, UK, Canada, Australia, Singapore and UAE do not tax the person receiving a gift. Income you later earn on the money is taxable where you live.
Do I need to file Form 3520 for money from my parents in India?
Yes, if you are a US person and gifts from non-US persons, including your parents, exceed USD 100,000 in the calendar year. There is no tax, but the penalty for not filing can reach 25% of the gift.
How much money can parents in India send to their child abroad?
Up to USD 250,000 per parent per financial year under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme. The bank collects 20% TCS on gift remittances above INR 10 lakh in the year, which the parent claims back in their Indian return.
Can I receive money from my parents tax free in the UK?
Yes. The UK does not tax the recipient of a gift, and inheritance tax concerns the giver; parents who are not UK residents giving non-UK assets are generally outside it.
Is a gift from parents taxable in Canada or Australia?
No. Neither country taxes the recipient of a gift. In Canada, an Indian account in your name above CAD 100,000 goes on Form T1135; in Australia, keep records of the source.
