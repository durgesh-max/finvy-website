Finding a Distributor or Business Partner in India (2026): Where to Look, How to Check Them, Agreement Terms, Tax and Product Registrations, for foreign brands and manufacturers selling into India.

Most foreign companies start selling in India through a distributor who buys and resells your product, an agent on commission, a licensee or franchisee, or a contract manufacturer. They set up their own entity later. Good leads come from your home country's trade service and embassy commercial section, Indian export promotion councils and industry associations, trade fairs and existing customers. Before signing, check the partner: company and GST records, financial statements, litigation and references. Then sign an agreement covering territory and exclusivity, minimum purchases, pricing, trademark ownership, termination and arbitration. India has no statute that pays distributors compensation when an agreement ends, so the contract decides. A buy-sell distributor rarely creates tax exposure for you, but an agent who habitually concludes contracts in your name can create a permanent establishment.

/ Checking a partner

Before the first shipment.

  • Company records: registration, directors, charges and filing history from the MCA portal; GST registration status and return filing; import-export code for importers. See verify an Indian company.
  • Financials: filed financial statements, working capital, credit references and payment history with other suppliers.
  • Litigation and reputation: court records, regulatory actions, news and customer references.
  • Capability: warehousing, sales team, coverage of the states you care about, after-sales service.
  • Deeper checks: site visits and background reports when the commitment is large; see supplier and vendor verification.

/ Agreement, tax and registrations

Put it in writing.

  • Territory and exclusivity: by state or channel, with minimum purchase targets that end exclusivity if missed.
  • Pricing: you can recommend resale prices, but fixing them can raise Competition Act issues.
  • Trademark: register your mark in India in your own name before appointing anyone; see IP for foreign companies.
  • Termination: notice period, buy-back of stock, return of materials; India has no statutory goodwill compensation for distributors.
  • Disputes: arbitration with a neutral seat; payment terms backed by letters of credit or advance payment where credit is uncertain.
  • Product registrations: depending on the product, BIS certification, FSSAI (food), CDSCO (medical devices and drugs), Legal Metrology labelling, and extended producer responsibility for packaging, e-waste or batteries.
  • Tax: payments for goods do not attract Indian withholding; commissions to an Indian agent carry GST; royalties and service fees from India to you carry withholding tax.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

How do I find a distributor in India?
Through your home country's trade service and embassy commercial section, Indian export promotion councils and industry associations, trade fairs, and referrals from existing customers. Shortlist two or three and check each before negotiating.
How do I check an Indian distributor before signing?
Review MCA company records, GST registration and return filing, filed financial statements, litigation, credit references and capability, and visit if the commitment is significant.
Does appointing a distributor create a tax presence in India?
A buy-sell distributor usually does not. An agent who habitually concludes contracts on your behalf can create a permanent establishment, making part of your profit taxable in India.
Do Indian distributors get compensation when the agreement ends?
There is no statute giving goodwill compensation in India; the agreement decides notice, stock buy-back and any payment.
Should I register my trademark in India first?
Yes. Register it in your own name before appointing a distributor or licensee, so the brand stays yours if the relationship ends.
