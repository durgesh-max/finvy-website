/ Private and public companies · Companies Act sections 61 and 64 · Updated 2026-11-28

Increase Authorised Share Capital (2026): Section 61, Ordinary Resolution, Altering the Memorandum, Form SH-7 Within 30 Days and Doing It Before a Fundraise, raise the ceiling before you issue shares.

Authorised capital is the ceiling on the shares a company can issue. Before an allotment, conversion of notes or preference shares, or a bonus issue that would cross that ceiling, the company must increase its authorised capital under section 61. That needs permission in the articles, an ordinary resolution in a general meeting (special, if the articles say so), a change to the capital clause of the memorandum, and Form SH-7 filed with the Registrar within 30 days.

/ The steps

Sections 61 and 64.

StepRule
Check the articlesThey must authorise an increase; amend them first if they do not
Board meetingApprove the proposal and call a general meeting
General meetingOrdinary resolution, unless the articles require a special resolution; 21 clear days notice or shorter notice with members’ consent as the Act allows
Alter the memorandumReplace the capital clause with the new authorised capital
Form SH-7File with the Registrar within 30 days of the resolution, with the altered memorandum (and articles if changed)
MGT-14Also within 30 days if a special resolution was passed
Then allotOnly after the increase is effective, issue shares within the new limit
  • Authorised vs paid-up: authorised capital is the maximum; paid-up capital is what investors and founders have actually subscribed and paid.
  • Stamp duty and fees: the increase attracts stamp duty and Registrar fees that depend on the amount and the state - plan the size of the increase with that in mind.

/ Timing it with a round

Where founders get stuck.

  • Sequence: increase authorised capital, then pass the private placement resolutions, receive the money and allot - in that order.
  • Convertibles and CCPS: keep enough headroom for conversion of notes, CCPS and the ESOP pool, so a later conversion does not stall.
  • Bonus issues: the authorised capital must cover the bonus shares before the board allots them.
  • Classes of shares: if adding preference shares or a new class, the memorandum and articles must reflect it.
  • Late SH-7: filing after 30 days attracts additional fees and keeps the allotment exposed - file promptly.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

How do I increase the authorised share capital of a private limited company?
Check that the articles allow it, hold a board meeting to call a general meeting, pass an ordinary resolution (or a special resolution if the articles require), alter the capital clause of the memorandum, and file Form SH-7 with the Registrar within 30 days of the resolution.
What is the due date for Form SH-7?
Within 30 days of the shareholders' resolution approving the increase. If a special resolution was passed, MGT-14 is also due within 30 days.
Is a special resolution needed to increase authorised capital?
Not usually. An ordinary resolution in a general meeting is enough, unless the company's articles require a special resolution.
What is the difference between authorised and paid-up capital?
Authorised capital is the maximum share capital the company can issue under its memorandum. Paid-up capital is the part actually allotted to and paid for by shareholders.
Do I need to increase authorised capital before a funding round?
Yes, if the new shares, conversions or ESOP pool would take total issued capital above the current authorised capital. The increase must be effective before allotment.
