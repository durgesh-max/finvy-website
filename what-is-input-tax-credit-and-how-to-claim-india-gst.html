What Is Input Tax Credit and How to Claim India GST, ITC framework + conditions + reconciliation.

Direct answer: Input Tax Credit (ITC) under Indian GST allows a registered taxpayer to claim credit of GST paid on business inputs (goods/services) against GST payable on outward supplies. Key conditions (Section 16 CGST Act): valid invoice + goods/services received + supplier has filed GSTR-1 + GSTR-3B / payment made + specific restrictions under Section 17(5) blocked credits. Claim via GSTR-3B + reconcile with GSTR-2B monthly. Here's the complete framework.

/ ITC fundamental framework

The core concept.

  • ITC = GST paid on inputs (goods purchased + services received) that becomes credit against GST payable on outputs (sales).
  • Legal basis: Section 16 CGST Act 2017 + CGST Rules 36-45.
  • Chain: supplier charges GST → registered buyer pays → buyer claims ITC against output GST liability.
  • Net GST payable = Output GST - ITC claimed.
  • Prevents cascading tax (tax on tax).
  • Essential for competitive pricing + specific sector cost structure.

/ Section 16 ITC conditions

What must be satisfied.

  • Possession of tax invoice / debit note: valid tax invoice (Section 31 CGST Rule 46 compliant).
  • Receipt of goods / services: physical receipt of goods OR constructive receipt (bill-to-ship-to acceptable per Section 16(2)).
  • Supplier has filed GSTR-1: outward supplies reported.
  • Supplier has paid tax: GSTR-3B filed + tax paid to government.
  • Recipient has filed GSTR-3B: ITC claim reported.
  • Payment made to supplier: within 180 days of invoice date. If not: ITC reversible + specific interest.
  • No Section 17(5) blocked credit: specific blocked categories (vehicles for personal use, food/beverage, specific scenarios).
  • Section 16(4) time limit: ITC must be claimed by earlier of (a) specified date following FY (currently 30 November following FY), (b) date of filing annual return.

/ Section 17(5) blocked credits

What ITC is NOT available.

  • Motor vehicles for personal transportation (except specific business use like taxi/bus operation, driving school, resale).
  • Food and beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, health services (except specific scenarios).
  • Membership of clubs + health + fitness centres.
  • Travel benefits for employees (except specific scenarios).
  • Works contract services + goods used for construction of immovable property (except specific plant + machinery).
  • Goods / services used for personal consumption.
  • Goods lost / stolen / destroyed / written off / gifted / free samples.
  • Services for CSR activities.
  • Tax paid under Section 74 (fraud).

/ GSTR-2B reconciliation

The matching process.

  • GSTR-2B: auto-populated statement showing ITC available based on supplier's GSTR-1 + GSTR-3B.
  • Generated monthly for recipient based on supplier filings.
  • Rule 36(4) imposes 2A/2B rule: ITC claim restricted to invoices appearing in 2B + specific cap.
  • Monthly reconciliation: recipient's books vs GSTR-2B.
  • Discrepancies: (a) supplier not filed GSTR-1 → ITC not in 2B → follow up with supplier, (b) supplier not paid tax → ITC may be challenged, (c) wrong invoice details → correction / amendment.
  • Monthly discipline critical for timely ITC claim + specific defence in GST audit.

/ Claim process via GSTR-3B

The monthly filing.

  1. Download GSTR-2B from GST portal (auto-populated).
  2. Reconcile against books of accounts.
  3. Identify ITC available: 2B + specific Rule 36(4) cap.
  4. Classify: eligible ITC + ineligible ITC (Section 17(5) blocked) + proportionate reversal (specific business + specific exempt supply).
  5. Enter ITC in GSTR-3B Table 4 (ITC claim section).
  6. Compute output tax liability + ITC + net payable.
  7. Pay net tax via electronic cash ledger / specific challan.
  8. File GSTR-3B by 20th of following month.
  9. Specific annual reconciliation in GSTR-9 (Table 6-8).

/ Common ITC compliance errors + risks

What to avoid.

  • Claiming ITC on Section 17(5) blocked items.
  • Section 16(4) time-barred ITC claim (beyond 30 November following FY).
  • ITC on exempt supply inputs (requires proportionate reversal under Rule 42/43).
  • Not reversing ITC on supplier non-payment within 180 days.
  • Rule 36(4) cap breach (ITC claim exceeding 2B + specific cap).
  • Not reconciling monthly 2B vs books.
  • Not following up with supplier for GSTR-1 filing delay (ITC appears in subsequent 2B).
  • Not maintaining proper tax invoice as per Section 31 / Rule 46.
  • Section 73 / Section 74 scrutiny + ASMT-10 scrutiny + specific reversal + specific interest + specific penalty.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is Input Tax Credit (ITC) under GST?
ITC = GST paid on business inputs (goods / services) that becomes credit against GST payable on outward supplies. Section 16 CGST Act. Net GST payable = Output GST - ITC claimed. Essential for competitive pricing. Conditions: valid invoice + goods/services received + supplier filed GSTR-1 + payment made within 180 days + no Section 17(5) blocked + Section 16(4) time limit.
What's the time limit for claiming ITC?
Section 16(4) time limit: ITC must be claimed by earlier of (a) specified date following FY (currently 30 November following FY), (b) date of filing annual return. Beyond this: ITC lapses + cannot be claimed. Monthly reconciliation + specific Rule 36(4) cap discipline critical for complete claim.
What's Section 17(5) blocked credit?
Specific categories where ITC NOT available: motor vehicles for personal transportation + food/beverages/outdoor catering + club memberships + travel benefits (specific) + works contract for immovable property + personal consumption + CSR activities + Section 74 tax (fraud). Specific exceptions for business-use scenarios.
How does GSTR-2B work?
