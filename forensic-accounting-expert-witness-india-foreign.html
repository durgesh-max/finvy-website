/ Forensic investigation scope

What we investigate.

  • Fraud investigation: identification of fraudulent transactions, misrepresentation of financial statements, revenue recognition fraud, expense manipulation, inventory fraud, procurement fraud.
  • Embezzlement + asset misappropriation: cash theft, invoice manipulation, vendor kickbacks, ghost employees, payroll fraud.
  • Related-party transaction fraud: round-tripping, over-invoicing, under-invoicing between related entities for tax / money laundering purposes.
  • Books of account tampering: forged / altered entries, back-dated transactions, reversal fraud.
  • Bribery / corruption investigation: in support of FCPA / UK Bribery Act / Indian Prevention of Corruption Act investigations.
  • Money trail tracing: fund flow analysis across bank accounts + entities + jurisdictions. Trace Indian layer in international flows.
  • Insolvency fraud: pre-insolvency diversion of assets, preferential transactions, fraudulent trading under IBC 2016.

/ Expert witness services

Testimony + reports.

  • Expert reports: comprehensive forensic reports admissible in foreign courts (US federal / state, UK commercial courts, Singapore International Commercial Court, DIFC/ADGM, LCIA, ICC, SIAC, HKIAC arbitration).
  • Rebuttal reports: critique of opposing expert's methodology, assumptions, data.
  • Deposition support: preparation for and defence of deposition.
  • Expert testimony: in-person or video testimony in trial / arbitration hearing.
  • Pre-trial support: trial exhibits, demonstratives, cross-examination preparation.
  • Settlement support: financial analysis supporting mediation / settlement positions.

Qualifications accepted:

  • Chartered Accountant (ICAI, India) — equivalent to US CPA / UK ICAEW / Canadian CPA for most foreign court purposes.
  • CA / forensic specialisation certifications (where applicable).
  • Testimony experience in prior cross-border matters.

/ Damage + loss quantification

The valuation layer.

  • Lost profits: but-for analysis quantifying what the plaintiff would have earned absent the alleged wrongful conduct.
  • Business valuation: at specific dates, using DCF / market comparable / asset approach.
  • Diminution in value: before vs after valuation when wrongful conduct damaged value.
  • Unjust enrichment: defendant's gain from wrongful conduct.
  • Reasonable royalty: for IP infringement — what reasonable royalty would have been.
  • Pre-judgment interest: typically from date of wrongful conduct to judgment, at applicable rate.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Can an Indian CA serve as a financial expert witness in US / UK courts?
Yes - Chartered Accountant (ICAI) qualification is accepted as expert financial credential by US federal / state courts, UK commercial courts, Singapore International Commercial Court, DIFC/ADGM, major arbitration tribunals (SIAC, LCIA, ICC, HKIAC). CA report typically accepted under Federal Rules of Evidence 702 (Daubert) / UK equivalent. Prior cross-border testimony experience strengthens admissibility.
What's the difference between forensic accounting and audit?
Audit = reasonable assurance that financial statements are free of material misstatement; follows standardised auditing procedures. Forensic accounting = investigation of specific fraudulent / wrongful conduct; uses legal + accounting + investigation methods tailored to the case. Audit is backward-looking general purpose; forensic is specific-purpose and often prospective (admissible in court).
Can forensic investigation be done remotely?
Partially. Document review, data analytics, financial modelling, interviews can be remote. Site visits (physical records review, interviews of specific witnesses, inventory counts) often require in-person India presence - handled by BQP team in India. Final report + expert testimony delivered via video + in-person for trial / hearing as needed.
How long does forensic engagement take?
Varies widely. Initial scoping (1-2 weeks). Document / data collection (2-6 weeks depending on access). Analysis + report drafting (4-12 weeks for complex matters). Expert report filing + reply filings (2-4 weeks each). Deposition (1-5 days depending on scope). Trial / hearing (variable). Overall 3-12 months for most matters; longer for complex cross-border cases.
