/ Private limited company founders and directors · Companies Act section 185 and Income-tax section 2(22)(e) · Updated 2026-11-28

Loan to Director From a Company (2026): Section 185 Restrictions, the Private Company Exemption, Director Loans to the Company and Deemed Dividend Under 2(22)(e), the company is not the founder’s wallet.

A company generally cannot lend to its directors or their relatives under section 185 of the Companies Act - but many private companies qualify for an exemption, and loans to entities in which a director is interested are allowed with a special resolution. Even where company law allows it, a loan from a closely held company to a 10% shareholder can be taxed as a deemed dividend. Money a director lends to the company is fine, if the paperwork shows it is not from borrowed funds.

/ Company to director: section 185

What is barred, what is allowed.

SituationPosition
Loan, guarantee or security to a director, a director of the holding company, their partners or relatives, or their firmsProhibited, unless an exception or exemption applies
Loan to a company or body in which a director is interestedAllowed with a special resolution, if used for the borrower’s principal business (section 185(2))
Loan to a managing or whole-time directorAllowed under conditions of service for all employees, or a scheme approved by special resolution
Holding company to its wholly owned subsidiaryAllowed if used for the subsidiary’s principal business
Lending company in ordinary courseAllowed at not less than the bank rate
Private company exemptionNo body corporate shareholder; borrowings from banks, FIs and bodies corporate below twice paid-up capital or INR 50 crore (lower); no repayment default
  • Check the exemption every time: a new corporate investor, more borrowing or a default can take away the private company exemption.
  • Consequences: breaching section 185 exposes the company, the officers in default and the director who received the loan to penalties.

/ Director to company, and the tax side

Deposits, deemed dividend and cash.

  • Director lending to the company: not a deposit if the director gives a written declaration that the money is not from borrowed funds (for private companies, a director’s relative too); disclose it and report it in the annual return of money not treated as deposits.
  • Deemed dividend: a loan or advance from a closely held company to a shareholder with at least 10% voting power, or to a concern in which that shareholder has a substantial interest, is taxed as dividend in the recipient’s hands to the extent of accumulated profits.
  • Repaying does not undo it: deemed dividend is triggered when the loan is given, even if repaid later.
  • No cash: loans of INR 20,000 or more cannot be accepted or repaid in cash.
  • Founder salary instead? Salary, dividend or a properly approved loan each have different tax results - plan before money moves.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Can a director take a loan from a private limited company?
Generally not under section 185, unless the company qualifies for the private company exemption - no body corporate shareholder, borrowings from banks, financial institutions and bodies corporate below twice paid-up capital or INR 50 crore (whichever is lower), and no repayment default - or another exception applies.
Can a company give a loan to another company in which a director is interested?
Yes, under section 185(2), after a special resolution of the lending company, provided the loan is used by the borrower for its principal business activities.
Can a director lend money to the company?
Yes. The money is not treated as a deposit if the director gives a written declaration that it is not out of borrowed funds. In a private company, a director's relative can also lend on the same basis.
What is deemed dividend under section 2(22)(e)?
A loan or advance by a closely held company to a shareholder with at least 10% voting power, or to a concern in which that shareholder has a substantial interest, is taxed as dividend in the recipient's hands to the extent of the company's accumulated profits, even if it is later repaid.
What happens if section 185 is breached?
The company, its officers in default and the director who received the loan face penalties. The loan should be reviewed and regularised with advice.
