Do You Have to Pay Tax on Cash Deposits? Income Tax Notice for Cash Deposit (2026): the Rs 10 Lakh Reporting Limit, How to Reply, Section 69A Tax and Cash Limits (40A(3), 269ST), explain the source before it becomes income.

An income tax notice for cash deposits usually starts with your bank's report: large cash deposits are reported in the Statement of Financial Transactions, show up in your Annual Information Statement (AIS), and the department asks you to explain them if your return does not. Most cases are settled by showing where the cash came from - business sales, money withdrawn earlier and redeposited, a documented gift or the sale of an asset. If the source is not explained, the deposit can be taxed as unexplained money under section 69A at about 78% including surcharge and cess.

/ Why the notice comes

How the department finds the cash.

StepWhat happens
Bank reportsCash deposits of Rs 10 lakh or more in a financial year in savings accounts (a higher limit applies to current accounts) are reported in the Statement of Financial Transactions
AIS shows itThe reported amount appears in your Annual Information Statement for that year
Mismatch checkIf your return does not show income or sources that match, the system flags it
Query or noticeAn e-campaign or compliance portal query, a section 133(6) letter asking for details, or for older years a reassessment notice under section 148A

/ How to reply

Show the source, with papers.

  • Compliance portal query: give feedback against the transaction - for example the information is correct and already covered in the return, relates to someone else, or is duplicated - and add the explanation and documents.
  • Business cash: link deposits to sales in your books, GST returns and cash book.
  • Redeposited withdrawals or savings: show earlier withdrawals from your accounts and why the cash was held.
  • Gifts and family money: a gift from a relative is exempt, but keep a gift deed and the giver's bank or source evidence.
  • Sale of an asset or other income: sale deed, receipts, agricultural income records or similar.
  • If the return missed income: a revised or updated return may be the cleaner route before a notice escalates.

/ If it is not explained

Why a careful reply matters.

  • Unexplained money can be added under section 69A and taxed under section 115BBE at about 78% including surcharge and cess, with penalty exposure on top.
  • Separate rules restrict cash itself: receiving Rs 2 lakh or more in cash from one person in a day or for one transaction, and taking or repaying loans or deposits of Rs 20,000 or more in cash, carry penalties equal to the amount.
  • Reply within the time given; ignoring a section 133(6) letter or a portal query can lead to reassessment.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Why did I get an income tax notice for a cash deposit?
Banks report large cash deposits - Rs 10 lakh or more in a financial year in savings accounts, with a higher limit for current accounts - in the Statement of Financial Transactions. The amount shows in your AIS, and if your return does not explain it, the department asks for the source through the compliance portal, a section 133(6) letter or a reassessment notice.
What is the cash deposit limit in a savings account to avoid an income tax notice?
There is no limit on depositing your own explained money, but cash deposits of Rs 10 lakh or more in a financial year in savings accounts are reported to the department. Deposits below that can still be questioned if they do not match your income.
How do I reply to an income tax notice for cash deposit?
Explain the source with documents: business sales linked to your books and GST returns, earlier withdrawals redeposited, a documented gift from a relative, sale of an asset, or other income. On the compliance portal give feedback against the transaction and attach the explanation; for a section 133(6) or 148A notice, reply on the e-filing portal within the time given.
What happens if I cannot explain a cash deposit?
It can be added as unexplained money under section 69A and taxed under section 115BBE at about 78% including surcharge and cess, and penalty can follow. Where income was simply left out of the return, a revised or updated return may limit the damage.
What is the penalty under section 271AAC on unexplained income?
Where income is added as unexplained cash credits, investments, money or expenditure (sections 68 to 69D) and taxed under section 115BBE, a further penalty of 10% of that tax can be levied under section 271AAC, on top of the tax at 60% plus surcharge and cess (about 78%). It does not apply to the extent the income was declared in the return and the tax paid before the end of the year. The Income-tax Act, 2025 has corresponding provisions from 1 April 2026.
Is a loan from a friend taxable or treated as income?
A genuine loan is not income, but if the department questions it you must show three things under section 68: the lender's identity, their capacity to lend, and that the loan is genuine - a written loan note, the transfer through the bank, the lender's bank statement or return, and repayments through the bank. If you cannot, the amount can be taxed as unexplained credit at about 78%. Taking or repaying a loan of Rs 20,000 or more in cash is barred and attracts a penalty equal to the amount.

Do I have to pay tax on cash deposits?
Not on the deposit itself - depositing cash is not income. What matters is where the money came from. Cash from salary or income already taxed, earlier withdrawals put back, a gift from a relative or the sale of an asset you report is not taxed again; cash from business sales is part of your business income and taxed through your return. The risk is a deposit you cannot explain: it can be taxed as unexplained money under section 69A at about 78% including surcharge and cess, plus penalty. Deposits of Rs 10 lakh or more in a year in savings accounts are reported to the department and appear in your AIS, so keep the source papers ready. What are the cash payment and cash receipt limits under income tax?
The main limits (old section numbers, carried into the Income-tax Act, 2025): a business cannot deduct an expense paid in cash above INR 10,000 to one person in a day (INR 35,000 for payments to goods transporters) under section 40A(3), subject to the Rule 6DD exceptions, and cash paid for a capital asset above that limit does not count for depreciation. Nobody may receive INR 2 lakh or more in cash from one person in a day, for one transaction or for one event or occasion (section 269ST); the penalty is equal to the amount received. Loans, deposits or advances for property of INR 20,000 or more cannot be taken or repaid in cash (sections 269SS and 269T), with a penalty equal to the amount. Cash donations above INR 2,000 get no 80G deduction, and health insurance premium paid in cash gets no 80D deduction (a preventive health check-up excepted). Cash deposits themselves are not taxed, but large ones are reported to the department and must be explained.
