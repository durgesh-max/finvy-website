What Is Section 115BAA and Should I Opt In India, 22% corporate tax + MAT exemption + specific trade-offs.

Direct answer: Section 115BAA is a concessional corporate tax regime offering 22% rate (plus 10% surcharge + 4% cess = effective ~25.17%) with Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT) exemption. Opt-in by Indian domestic company that forgoes specific deductions (Section 32(1)(iia) additional depreciation, Section 10AA SEZ profits, Section 35 R&D, Section 33AB tea/coffee reserve, specific state incentives). Once opted: PERMANENT (cannot opt out). Here's the complete analysis.

/ Section 115BAA at a glance

The regime.

  • Effective: FY 2019-20 onwards (introduced by Taxation Laws Amendment Act 2019).
  • Rate: 22% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess = effective ~25.17%.
  • Who can opt: Indian domestic company only (not foreign company + not LLP + not individual).
  • MAT exemption: companies opting into 115BAA are EXEMPT from Minimum Alternate Tax (Section 115JB).
  • MAT credit lapse: existing MAT credit under Section 115JAA LAPSES on opt-in - specific loss if large MAT credit balance.
  • Opt-in: via Form 10-IC filing before due date of ITR for first FY of opt-in.
  • Permanent: once opted, cannot opt out in subsequent years - PERMANENT choice.

/ Specific deductions forgone

The trade-off.

  • Section 10AA: SEZ unit profit deduction - not available.
  • Section 32(1)(iia): additional depreciation 20% on new plant/machinery - not available.
  • Section 32AD: investment allowance - not available.
  • Section 33AB: tea / coffee / rubber reserve - not available.
  • Section 33ABA: site restoration fund - not available.
  • Section 35(1)(ii) / (iia) / (iii): R&D expenditure specific weighted deduction - not available.
  • Section 35AD: specified business deductions - not available.
  • Section 35CCC / 35CCD: skill development specific deductions - not available.
  • Chapter VI-A Section 80 deductions (specific): most not available including 80-IA / 80-IAB / 80-IAC / 80-ID / 80-IE / 80-JJAA (except 80-JJAA + 80-M available).
  • Set-off of brought-forward loss attributable to above deductions - not available.

/ Who should opt in

The clear-yes profile.

  • Profitable established companies with turnover > INR 400 crore (base rate 30% without 115BAA, so 22% is material saving).
  • Companies without specific 10AA SEZ / 35 R&D / 32(1)(iia) depreciation claims.
  • Companies previously paying MAT (effective rate 15% + surcharge + cess = ~17-18%) but having book profit higher than ITA computed - 115BAA MAT exemption + specific other benefits.
  • Companies in services / consumer / most sectors without specific depreciation-intensive capex.
  • Standard profitable SMEs + mid-market Indian companies.

/ Who should NOT opt in

The clear-no profile.

  • SEZ units claiming Section 10AA deduction - specific tax holiday more valuable.
  • New manufacturing companies eligible for Section 115BAB 15% - lower rate preferred.
  • R&D-heavy companies claiming Section 35 weighted deductions.
  • New plant/machinery capex companies claiming Section 32(1)(iia) additional depreciation.
  • Companies with large MAT credit balance under Section 115JAA (credit lapses on opt-in).
  • Companies in specific infrastructure / specific sector with Section 80-IA / 80-IAC / 80-IAB claims.
  • Companies currently in loss - 115BAA offers no benefit (no tax savings if no profit).

/ Who should analyse case-specifically

The judgment call.

  • Companies with turnover < INR 400 crore (base rate 25% without 115BAA - savings between 25% and 22% = 3% - specific analysis).
  • Specific mixed-income companies (part SEZ + part non-SEZ).
  • Companies planning major capex (future depreciation claims impact).
  • Companies with specific brought-forward losses (specific set-off implications).
  • Companies with specific MAT credit balance that could be utilised in future.
  • Companies in specific sectors with specific state incentive implications.

/ Case study comparison

Specific numbers.

  • Example 1 (favour 115BAA): Services company, turnover INR 50 crore, profit INR 10 crore, no 10AA / 35 / 32(1)(iia). Without 115BAA (25% rate): tax ~INR 2.5 crore + surcharge + cess. With 115BAA (22%): tax ~INR 2.2 crore + surcharge + cess. Saving ~INR 30 lakh annually.
  • Example 2 (favour 115BAA): Large manufacturing company, turnover INR 500 crore, profit INR 50 crore, no specific capex. Without 115BAA (30% rate): tax ~INR 15 crore. With 115BAA (22%): tax ~INR 11 crore. Saving ~INR 4 crore annually.
  • Example 3 (against 115BAA): SEZ company, 10AA deduction INR 20 crore, residual profit INR 5 crore. Without 115BAA: SEZ deduction saves ~INR 5 crore tax + 25% on residual = INR 1.25 crore. With 115BAA: SEZ deduction LOST + 22% on INR 25 crore = INR 5.5 crore. Loss ~INR 4.25 crore annually.
  • Example 4 (new mfg - choose 115BAB): New manufacturing, turnover INR 100 crore, profit INR 20 crore, meets 115BAB conditions. 115BAA 22%: tax ~INR 4.4 crore. 115BAB 15%: tax ~INR 3 crore. Choose 115BAB (saves INR 1.4 crore annually vs 115BAA).

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is Section 115BAA?
Section 115BAA is concessional corporate tax regime offering 22% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess = effective ~25.17% for Indian domestic companies. MAT (Section 115JB) exempted. In exchange: specific deductions forgone (Section 10AA SEZ, 32(1)(iia) additional depreciation, 35 R&D, 80-IA/IAC/IAB, specific). Once opted: PERMANENT - cannot opt out.
Should I opt into Section 115BAA?
Clear yes if: (a) profitable established, (b) turnover > INR 400 crore (base rate 30% so 22% is material saving), (c) no SEZ / R&D / specific depreciation-intensive claims, (d) small MAT credit balance. Clear no if: (a) SEZ unit, (b) new manufacturing (prefer 115BAB 15%), (c) R&D-heavy, (d) large MAT credit, (e) specific 80 deductions. Case-by-case otherwise.
What's the difference 115BAA vs 115BAB?
115BAA: 22% rate - any Indian domestic company. 115BAB: 15% rate - new manufacturing company commencing manufacturing on/before specific date (date subject to extension; current limit was 31 March 2024, extended). Specific conditions: incorporated after 1 October 2019 + new infrastructure + no specific pre-existing operations + specific manufacturing-only focus. 115BAB is lower rate but strict conditions.
Can I opt out of 115BAA later?
No - Section 115BAA opt-in is PERMANENT. Once opted via Form 10-IC: cannot opt out in subsequent FYs. Specific finality. Decision must be considered carefully with 3-5 year future projection. BQP conducts specific long-term tax projection before opt-in recommendation.
