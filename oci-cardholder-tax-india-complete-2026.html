OCI cardholder tax, what residence status really means.

OCI (Overseas Citizen of India) card gives Indian-origin persons lifetime multi-entry visa + near-citizen rights. But OCI status does NOT change your Indian tax residence — tax residence is determined only by Section 6 physical presence in India. OCI holder can be NRI (most common), RNOR, or Resident for a given FY. Here's the complete 2026 tax framework for OCI cardholders.

/ OCI card vs tax residence

They are separate.

OCI card is an immigration/visa document:

  • Issued by Government of India, Ministry of Home Affairs.
  • Lifetime multi-entry Indian visa (no need for Indian visa before each trip).
  • Near-equal civil rights with Indian citizens (except voting, government jobs, agricultural land purchase, political office).
  • Does NOT confer Indian citizenship.
  • Does NOT determine Indian tax residence.

Indian tax residence is determined by Section 6 Income Tax Act:

  • Resident: in India for 182 days or more in the year, or 60 days or more plus 365 days in the previous four years. For an OCI holder or Indian citizen visiting India, the 60 days becomes 182 days, or 120 days if Indian income (other than from foreign sources) exceeds INR 15 lakh; in that 120-day case the person is resident but not ordinarily resident.
  • Non-Resident (NRI): neither test satisfied.
  • RNOR (Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident): resident this FY but non-resident for 9+ of 10 preceding FYs, OR physically in India <= 729 days in 7 preceding FYs. See our RNOR guide.

So an OCI holder physically present in India for most of the FY = Resident; one who visits briefly = NRI. OCI card itself is tax-neutral.

/ Taxability depends on status

Three different regimes.

  • OCI = Resident: global income taxable in India. World-wide investment income, overseas rental, foreign employment salary, foreign capital gain — all taxable at slab rate (foreign tax credit under Section 90/91 for DTAA).
  • OCI = RNOR: India-source + foreign-source income from business controlled from India + professional set up in India. Foreign-source investment / overseas employment / foreign capital gain remain exempt. See RNOR guide. RNOR status typically available for 2-3 years post-return to India.
  • OCI = NRI: only India-source income taxable — Indian employment, Indian business, Indian property rental, India-source capital gain, Indian bank interest. Foreign income / foreign investment income not taxable in India.

Many OCI holders maintain NRI status throughout life (short Indian visits only) → only India-source income taxable. Significant tax planning benefit vs full-resident status.

/ Key tax touch-points for OCI holder

What they typically own in India.

  • Indian bank account: NRO (for Indian income - rent, dividend, interest), NRE (for foreign source - repatriable), FCNR (foreign currency fixed deposit, repatriable). Not regular savings (that's for residents).
  • Indian property: OCI can purchase residential / commercial property (not agricultural). Rental income taxable in India (standard deduction 30% + municipal tax + interest on home loan deduction).
  • Indian mutual fund / shares: via NRO or NRE demat. Capital gain on sale: LTCG 12.5% (post Finance Act 2024) or STCG 20% for listed equity. Interest / dividend taxable.
  • Indian ITR filing: mandatory if India-source income exceeds basic exemption (INR 4 lakh under the new regime, INR 2.5 lakh under the old; non-residents cannot use the rebate that makes resident income up to INR 12 lakh tax-free). ITR-2 or ITR-3 depending on nature.
  • Form 10F (Form 41 from 1 April 2026) + TRC: for DTAA treaty rate claim. See Form 10F guide.
  • Section 54 (section 82 of the 2025 Act) reinvestment: available on sale of Indian property (same rules as resident).
  • Repatriation: up to USD 1M/year from NRO under FEMA; unlimited from NRE / FCNR.

/ Specific OCI complications in 2026

Current traps.

  • PAN requirement: OCI typically needs Indian PAN for ITR filing, property purchase, financial transactions above INR 2.5 lakh. Apply in Form 95 (formerly Form 49AA), the PAN form for foreign citizens including OCI cardholders.
  • PAN-Aadhaar linking: NRIs are EXEMPT from mandatory PAN-Aadhaar linking (Notification 37/2017). OCI should ensure PAN is marked NRI in database. See our PAN-Aadhaar guide.
  • The 120-day rule: an OCI holder visiting India with Indian income above INR 15 lakh becomes resident, but not ordinarily resident, after 120 days in India in the year if they also spent 365 days in India in the previous four years. The separate ‘deemed resident’ rule in section 6(1A) applies only to Indian citizens not taxed in any other country, so it does not apply to OCI holders, who are foreign citizens.
  • US estate tax: if OCI is US tax person, worldwide assets (including Indian) in US estate. See US estate tax guide.
  • FBAR + Form 8938: US tax person with foreign financial accounts must report. India NRO / NRE / MF holdings disclosable.
  • LRS on Indian income: cannot use LRS as OCI (LRS is for Indian residents only). Repatriation of Indian income via NRO - USD 1M/year route instead.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Does OCI card make me an Indian tax resident?
No - OCI is an immigration document, not a tax status. Indian tax residence is determined solely by Section 6 Income Tax Act physical presence tests (182 days or more in the year; for a visiting OCI holder the 60-day test becomes 182 days, or 120 days where Indian income exceeds INR 15 lakh). Most OCI holders residing abroad maintain NRI status; only short visitors to India stay NRI by default.
Is OCI the same as dual citizenship?
No. India does not allow dual citizenship. OCI is a lifelong registration for foreign citizens of Indian origin that gives multiple-entry, multi-purpose visa-free travel and residence, and parity with NRIs in many economic matters, but no Indian passport, no right to vote, no government jobs and no right to buy agricultural land.
When does an OCI card need to be re-issued after a new passport?
Only once, when a new passport is issued after you turn 20, so that your adult photograph is captured. For other new passports, including those issued to minors, you upload a copy of the new passport and a recent photo on the OCI portal (ociservices.gov.in), within three months under the current guidance. The earlier rule requiring re-issue after age 50 has been dropped, and existing physical OCI cards remain valid.
Does an OCI card need to be renewed?
No. OCI is lifelong and has no expiry or renewal. What changes with a new passport is the update: you upload the new passport and a recent photo on the OCI portal, and a fresh OCI card is needed only once, for the first new passport issued after you turn 20. Rules on this have changed several times, so check the OCI portal or your Indian mission before you travel.
What about OCI for a minor child who gets a new passport, for example in the USA?
For children under 20, a new passport no longer needs a new OCI card: you upload the child's new passport details and photo on the OCI portal through the Indian mission or its service provider in your country. Carry both the old passport shown on the OCI card and the new passport until the update is confirmed.
What do OCI cardholders need to carry when travelling to India?
The OCI card and the current passport whose details are linked to it on the OCI portal. If the new passport has not yet been updated, carry the old passport shown on the OCI card as well. OCI cardholders need no visa and do not have to register with the police or the FRRO, however long they stay.
What are OCI cardholders not allowed to do in India?
They need special permission for research, missionary or religious preaching work, mountaineering, journalism and visits to protected or restricted areas, and they cannot vote, hold most government posts or buy agricultural land, plantations or farmhouses. Business, work, study and owning residential or commercial property are allowed.
Have the OCI card rules been tightened, and what changed in 2025-26?
Yes. A Ministry of Home Affairs notification of August 2025 lets the government cancel OCI registration if the holder is sentenced to imprisonment of two years or more, or is named in a chargesheet for an offence punishable with seven years or more, as reported in the press; reports say this covers convictions abroad for offences also recognised in Indian law. Separately, the Citizenship (Amendment) Rules, 2026 moved OCI applications online and offer an electronic e-OCI document alongside the physical card. The money side is unchanged: residence for tax still depends on days in India, and OCI holders can keep NRE, NRO and FCNR accounts and buy residential and commercial property. Check the current MHA rules and your mission before travelling if you face any criminal case.
Does getting an OCI card change my Indian tax?
No. Tax depends on residence, measured by days in India, not on OCI status. An OCI holder living abroad is generally a non-resident and taxed only on Indian income; one who spends enough days in India becomes resident like anyone else.
Does the 120-day rule or deemed residency apply to an OCI holder?
The 120-day rule can. Since the Finance Act 2020, an OCI holder or Indian citizen visiting India with Indian income above INR 15 lakh is resident, but not ordinarily resident, after 120 days in India in the year, if they also spent 365 days in India in the previous four years. Deemed residency under section 6(1A) applies only to Indian citizens who are not taxed in any other country, so it does not apply to OCI holders.
I am a US citizen living in India for more than 6 months. Am I taxed in India?
Very likely yes. Indian tax residence depends on days, not citizenship: staying 182 days or more in India in a financial year (April to March) makes you resident. A longer-term resident becomes resident and ordinarily resident, taxed in India on worldwide income - US salary, US interest and dividends, 401(k) and IRA income as it becomes taxable, and gains on US shares - and must report US accounts and assets in Schedule FA. In the first years after moving, you may be resident but not ordinarily resident (RNOR), which keeps most foreign income outside Indian tax. The US keeps taxing you on worldwide income as a citizen, so double tax is avoided through foreign tax credits: claim credit in India with Form 44 (old Form 67) for US tax on US-source income where the treaty allows, and use Form 1116 or the foreign earned income exclusion in the US for Indian tax on Indian income. Can OCI holder buy agricultural land in India?
No - OCI cannot buy agricultural / plantation / farmhouse. Can inherit (same as any NRI). Can purchase residential / commercial property freely. For agricultural land purchase, OCI must have RBI prior approval (rarely granted).
Does OCI holder need to link PAN with Aadhaar?
No - NRIs including OCI holders are EXEMPT from mandatory PAN-Aadhaar linking under CBDT Notification 37/2017. However, PAN database must correctly reflect NRI status; if PAN was issued when holder was resident and status changed to NRI, update required. Common issue - PAN marked as resident but holder is actually OCI/NRI results in PAN inoperative notice.
Does OCI holder need to file Indian ITR?
Only if India-source income exceeds basic exemption limit (INR 4 lakh under the new regime or INR 2.5 lakh under the old; the rebate that makes resident income up to INR 12 lakh tax-free is not available to non-residents). Income types that typically trigger ITR: Indian property rent, Indian capital gains, Indian bank interest above INR 10K, India salary. Even where ITR not mandatory, filing often beneficial to claim Section 195 TDS refund.
Does an OCI cardholder need to file FAST-DS 2026?
Only if there is something to declare. FAST-DS covers foreign assets or income that should have been reported in an Indian return for a year in which you were resident and ordinarily resident. An OCI holder who has been non-resident throughout had no foreign-asset reporting duty and does not need it; one who lived in India for some years and kept foreign accounts may. Declarations close on 31 December 2026.
What TDS applies when an OCI holder sells property in India?
A non-resident OCI holder is treated like an NRI: the buyer deducts TDS on the full amount paid (unless the seller has a lower deduction certificate in Form 128), at 12.5% plus surcharge and cess for long-term gains (held over 24 months) and 30% plus surcharge and cess for short-term gains. A lower deduction certificate (Form 128, formerly Form 13) can cut the TDS to the tax actually due.
Do US citizen OCI holders report Indian bank accounts to the IRS?
Yes. US citizens report foreign accounts on the FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) when the combined balance exceeds USD 10,000 at any time in the year, and on Form 8938 above its thresholds. Indian mutual funds are usually PFICs for US tax and need Form 8621.
